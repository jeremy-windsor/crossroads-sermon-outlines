Crushed Joy

Josh Wyatt uses Psalm 51:15-17 to contrast the many ways a wrong spirit resists correction with the one right movement of repentance: breaking toward God. David cannot bargain for cleansing through sacrifice or past obedience; he needs the sacrifice God supplies in Jesus. That grace restores an opened mouth, surrendered usefulness, and 'crushed joy'—humble praise that remains honest about sin and confident in mercy.

August 24, 2026 · Josh Wyatt · 44:30

Start With Me

Josh Wyatt traces David's plea in Psalm 51 from forgiveness to inward re-creation: God must create a pure heart, renew a steadfast spirit, restore salvation's joy, and then use the forgiven sinner. Following the new-creation theme from Genesis through Ezekiel, Jesus, Paul, and Revelation, Josh distinguishes spiritual transformation from behavior-only repair, warns about unnoticed drift, explains Psalm 51:11 in David's old-covenant setting, and points David's blood guilt and fear of lost presence to Jesus, the innocent King who suffered to bring the guilty to God.

Born Bent

Elijah Stanley follows David's confession in Psalm 51:3–5 from the collapse of self-defense, through the way tolerated compromise formed David's later fall, to sin's deepest character as rebellion against God and its reach into human nature. The sermon then turns to Jesus as the greater David: the sinless King bears judgment, creates clean hearts, preserves his people, and calls guilty sinners out of hiding into grace.

August 3, 2026 · Josh Wyatt · 43:25

When Excuses Die

Josh Wyatt opens Psalm 51 by contrasting curated highlights with David's unfiltered confession, then reconstructs the Bathsheba cover-up and Nathan's confrontation to argue that true repentance begins when excuses die. David brings no defense, résumé, or promise—only guilt—and reaches for God's unfailing covenant love. Josh applies that responsibility to temptation, sexual integrity, confession, inward cleansing, daily mercy, and the testimony of people Jesus has washed.

July 27, 2026 · Josh Wyatt · 48:50

Family Resemblance

Josh presents Hebrews 13 as the family resemblance produced by the gospel, not an entrance fee for earning God's favor. Because Jesus gives his people new hearts, the church is called to sustain brotherly love, welcome strangers, remember prisoners and the mistreated, honor marriage and sexual holiness, reject the love of money, imitate faithful leaders without idolizing them, submit to accountable shepherding, and bear Christ's disgrace outside the camp. The sermon closes by locating every command in the unchanging Christ, inviting hearers to repent and trust the risen Shepherd, and praying for power to walk in his grace.

July 20, 2026 · Josh Wyatt · 47:40

Keep Running!

Josh Wyatt presents Hebrews 12 as a word for weary Christians: the life of faith is an agonizing relay that must be run with eyes fixed on Jesus. The witnesses testify that God is faithful, unnecessary weights and entangling sin must be put away, and Christ’s cross and promised joy make endurance possible. Josh then distinguishes punishment, consequences, and a Father’s loving discipline; calls the church to pursue peace, holiness, grace, and mutual care; warns against bitterness and Esau’s short-sighted trade; and closes by contrasting Sinai with Zion. Believers can keep running because Jesus has crossed the finish line and is bringing them into an unshakeable kingdom.

July 13, 2026 · Josh Wyatt · 48:50

Walk By Faith

Josh Wyatt defines authentic faith as confident obedience to God’s word despite circumstances and consequences, then follows Hebrews 11 from Abel, Enoch, and Noah through Abraham, Joseph, Moses, Jericho, Rahab, the judges, and believers who suffered without earthly deliverance. Their different outcomes reveal victory both over suffering and through it. Every witness finally points to Jesus, the author and perfecter of faith, who calls his people to leave the old life, keep marching, pass God’s promises forward, and walk with him through suffering to glory.
