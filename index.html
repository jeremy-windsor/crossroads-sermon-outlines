August 31, 2026 · Josh Wyatt · 49:00

Crushed Joy

Josh Wyatt uses Psalm 51:15-17 to contrast the many ways a wrong spirit resists correction with the one right movement of repentance: breaking toward God. David cannot bargain for cleansing through sacrifice or past obedience; he needs the sacrifice God supplies in Jesus. That grace restores an opened mouth, surrendered usefulness, and 'crushed joy'—humble praise that remains honest about sin and confident in mercy.

August 24, 2026 · Josh Wyatt · 44:30

Start With Me

Josh Wyatt traces David's plea in Psalm 51 from forgiveness to inward re-creation: God must create a pure heart, renew a steadfast spirit, restore salvation's joy, and then use the forgiven sinner. Following the new-creation theme from Genesis through Ezekiel, Jesus, Paul, and Revelation, Josh distinguishes spiritual transformation from behavior-only repair, warns about unnoticed drift, explains Psalm 51:11 in David's old-covenant setting, and points David's blood guilt and fear of lost presence to Jesus, the innocent King who suffered to bring the guilty to God.

August 10, 2026 · Elijah Stanley · 43:45

Born Bent

Elijah Stanley follows David's confession in Psalm 51:3–5 from the collapse of self-defense, through the way tolerated compromise formed David's later fall, to sin's deepest character as rebellion against God and its reach into human nature. The sermon then turns to Jesus as the greater David: the sinless King bears judgment, creates clean hearts, preserves his people, and calls guilty sinners out of hiding into grace.

August 3, 2026 · Josh Wyatt · 43:25

When Excuses Die

Josh Wyatt opens Psalm 51 by contrasting curated highlights with David's unfiltered confession, then reconstructs the Bathsheba cover-up and Nathan's confrontation to argue that true repentance begins when excuses die. David brings no defense, résumé, or promise—only guilt—and reaches for God's unfailing covenant love. Josh applies that responsibility to temptation, sexual integrity, confession, inward cleansing, daily mercy, and the testimony of people Jesus has washed.
