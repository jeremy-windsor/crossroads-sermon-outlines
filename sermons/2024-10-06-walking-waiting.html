Overview

  1. 00:20 Walking and waiting for sixty years

    Scripture: James 5:8 Romans 13:12 2 Timothy 4:7–8

    • Steve ran into some resistance this week, and it has made him even more passionate about what he prepared, because it really needs to be heard. Still, he wants to talk about what is on his heart more than preach it, not talking down to anyone but drawing on 60 years of walking with Jesus.
    • More than walking, he has been waiting for Jesus’ promised return. Didn’t Jesus say he would come soon? Didn’t his half brother James say his coming was at hand, and Paul that the day is almost here? Like every Christ follower trying to fight the good fight, finish the race, and keep the faith, as we walk and wait we long for his appearing.

  2. 02:01 Not yearning to distraction, not yawning in indifference

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:52 1 Thessalonians 4:16

    • That longing is how Jesus wants it: not yearning to the point of distraction, as if his return were the only thing we think about, but not yawning to the point of indifference either. Jesus clearly said he is coming soon, and the Bible gives clear signs meant to suggest it really is almost time. And it is, because it is always almost time.
    • Jesus wants us always looking for signs that his return is nearer than ever, and he said they would increase exponentially as a signal to anyone who lifts up their head that our redemption is drawing near. What fascinates Steve about our time is that more of these signs are happening in closer proximity than ever before.
    • If they really are signs, his return could happen, as Paul said, in the twinkling of an eye. Maybe the voice of the archangel and the trumpet of God are warming up even now. A twinkling, Steve says, is less than a full twink; call it half a twink, not enough time for one decent blink, and we could be in the presence of Jesus.

  3. 04:30 Not a theological gnat-swallowing contest

    • So let’s talk about it, not to the point of distraction, but for the reason Jesus gave the signs: so we would be ready, because even with increasing signs he will come at a time we don’t expect.
    • End-times talk isn’t an academic itch for Steve. He doesn’t care about identifying key players or plotting dates, and he has little patience with dogmatic theorists who parse prophecy like a theological gnat-swallowing contest. That bores him; Jesus’ soon return doesn’t bore him at all, “that puppy flips all my switches.” What bores him is people strumming the same tune on a one-string guitar about their chosen position. Give it up.
    • He will unpack a few interpretations, but don’t race up to him in the lobby expecting him to spend three minutes convincing you his is right. As long as we agree Jesus is coming in a physical, visible return, and soon, we’re good. Most of the rest is above his pay grade, and yours too.

    1. 07:00 Not the street-corner sign guy

      Scripture: Matthew 24:36

      • Maybe you worry that passion for Jesus’ return will turn you into the guy on the street corner with the hand-painted “The end is at hand” sign, who doesn’t shave or shower because it’s all ending anyway, so why give a rip about your B.O.
      • Let Steve assure you: the New Testament mentions Jesus’ return nearly 300 times, one in every 13 verses, and never so we can speculate about the logistics or timing. Jesus himself said even he didn’t know when; only the Father did. The whole point is that we stay passionate about living for Jesus and energized about following him until that twinkling twinks.

  4. 09:01 “Yes, I am coming soon”

    Scripture: Revelation 22:20 2 Peter 3:8

    • “Soon” was the last word Jesus spoke on earth. Not “It is finished” from the cross, nor “I am with you always” at the climax of the Great Commission, but when he appeared to a lonely disciple on the island of Patmos and said, “Yes, I am coming soon.”
    • People wrestle with that: it has been 2,000 years, and by any human standard that isn’t soon. Steve agrees, but Jesus isn’t human. The Bible says to the Lord a thousand years is like a day and a day like a thousand years. He transcends time; as far as he is concerned, he walked out of the tomb two days ago. Even if he doesn’t come for another thousand years, it will still be soon.

    1. 10:51 Maranatha: “Even so, come”

      Scripture: Revelation 22:20

      • But we humans are human, John included. Alone, afraid, locked in a damp, musty cell, John didn’t say, “A week from Tuesday would be better; I’ve got this golf thing.” He shouted, “Even so, come, Lord Jesus.” Let’s do this.
      • John’s response became the rallying cry of the church. In Aramaic, the language Jesus spoke, it is one word, the most popular prayer of the first century and many centuries after, cried out whenever believers gathered: Maranatha. You’ve seen it on lapel pins, bumper stickers, T-shirts, and song titles. You can’t pray it without passion and righteous zeal oozing out: come, Jesus, please just come.

  5. 12:39 The disciples wanted a date

    Scripture: Matthew 24:3 Matthew 24:36 Acts 1:6–7

    • The disciples were caught up in it too. On the Mount of Olives, with Jesus to themselves, they asked, “Tell us, Jesus, when will this happen? What will be the sign of your coming and of the end of the age?” After three and a half years at his feet hearing truth never before revealed, they wanted more. Steve thinks they wanted a date, even though Jesus had told them no one knows, not the angels in heaven, not even the Son, only the Father.
    • Just before he ascended they asked again: “Lord, are you at this time going to restore the kingdom?” Same question, same answer: it is not for you to know the times or dates the Father has set by his own authority. That desire to know when has been a constant drumbeat through church history.

  6. 14:47 1 Thessalonians 5: about times and dates

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5 1 Thessalonians 5:1

    • Last time, Paul had just finished a powerful teaching on the resurrection of the dead: the Jesus who died and rose will bring with him those who have fallen asleep, the sky will split open, an angel will shout, the trumpet will sound, and we will see Jesus. Paul told them what and even how, but not when.
    • So what do the Thessalonians ask first? When. And like every good prophet, Paul doesn’t answer: “Now, brothers and sisters, about times and dates we do not need to write to you.” Why? Because nobody knows.

    1. 16:14 Steve tips his hand

      • Steve hopes and passionately believes Jesus is coming back in his generation, as every prophet of God through church history has believed. From his study he believes the necessary prophecies have been fulfilled: the Jews have returned to their homeland, the gospel is preached around the world, political changes in Russia and Europe have reset the landscape for the Antichrist, and the world is in a horrific moral decline, with an exponential increase in seismic activity and war all over the world. He doesn’t see how anyone can look at these converging signs and not sense that maybe God is warming up the bus.
      • He believes that, but he doesn’t know it, and he isn’t supposed to; not even Jesus knew.

    2. 17:57 Relax: God’s got this

      Scripture: Psalm 2:4

      • Steve thinks Paul is saying: relax, God’s got this. The world may seem to be spinning out of control, but it isn’t. The return of Jesus is more than a wish; it is our blessed hope. It may feel like the world is crumbling, like things you held are being ripped from your not-yet-cold dead hands, or that unless this election goes your way we are all doomed. The truth isn’t even close.
      • To all your hand-wringing the Bible says, “He who sits in the heavens laughs.” Not because he doesn’t care or is mocking you, or isn’t broken by what breaks your heart, but because he has everything under control, with the wisdom, power, and dominion to direct all the evil and chaos of this bizarre world to his perfect and righteous conclusion.

  7. 19:46 Like a thief in the night

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:2–3

    • The day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night, not because he is sneaky, but because even with all we know about his return, we can’t know everything, and what irritates many of us most is that we don’t know when.
    • Some say the sky is falling, Chicken Little; others say peace and safety, all is well. That is when destruction will come on them suddenly, like labor pains on a pregnant woman, and they will not escape. But you know very well, Paul says, because you have been taught. We haven’t been chasing the identity of the Beast or the end-times timeline; we have stayed focused on the truths we can and should know well.

    1. 21:21 Those who will not escape

      Scripture: Matthew 24:40–41 Luke 17:26–29

      • Paul isn’t talking about believing people. These are people who refused to believe and never confessed Jesus as the Christ, going about their business in willful ignorance, oblivious to the signs flashing around them, when out of nowhere the Lord comes and then leaves, like a thief. In an instant the entire Christian community, maybe one or two billion, is swept away, and the unbeliever is surrounded by destruction with no way of escape. There never was, and now there never will be.
      • It isn’t a pretty picture, but it is fundamentally true. Jesus described the same moment: two men in a field, one taken and one left; two women grinding at the mill, one taken and one left. As in the days of Noah, people were eating, drinking, and marrying until the flood came and destroyed them all; as in the days of Lot, they were buying, selling, planting, and building until fire and sulfur rained down.
      • That awful truth ought to strike fear in the heart of everyone here who does not yet believe, because as soon as Jesus rescues his holy ones, severe destruction is left in its wake.

    2. 24:17 Children of the light

      Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:4–5

      • If that scares you as a believer, it shouldn’t. If you have received Jesus as Lord and Savior and seek to follow him in all your ways, you are Steve’s brother or sister. You are not in darkness, so this day should not surprise you like a thief; you are all children of the light and of the day. Amazing, reassuring words.
      • With that relief comes a truckload of responsibility. Because you are children of the light, Paul gives three responsibilities, all about being ready.

  8. 25:40 First: be alert

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:6

    • Unlike chapter 4, “asleep” here doesn’t mean dead. It means uninformed, unimpressed, maybe uninvolved: you know these things because you’ve been taught well, but you don’t much care. Paul says that is unacceptable; when it comes to the end times, always remain alert.

    1. 26:28 The ten virgins

      Scripture: Matthew 25:1 Matthew 25:13

      • Jesus said at the end time the kingdom of heaven will be like ten virgins who went out to meet the bridegroom. A bridesmaid’s duty was to wait for the bridegroom and light his way to the feast with an oil-soaked rope in a dish on a long pole; if you accepted, you were to have enough oil. But the bridegroom was a long time coming, and what happened then is what Steve fears is happening to the church: they all became drowsy and fell asleep, five ran out of oil, and when the groom arrived they were locked out of the banquet forever.
      • The roster: the bridegroom is Jesus; the waiting virgins are professing Christians who claim to love the promise of his return and look ready but aren’t. They fell asleep when it counted because they had no oil. The moral: keep watch, because you do not know the day or the hour.

    2. 28:39 Every day is game day: carry the oil

      Scripture: Luke 12:38

      • We must practice every day as though it were game day. Thinking there will be a more convenient time to get ready speaks to the real condition of your heart. Following Jesus isn’t just singing the songs or hanging with other believers; you’ve got to carry the oil. Have you got oil? Without your own oil you aren’t keeping watch, and you could miss the thrill of seeing Jesus and fail at your primary assignment, to light the way for him when he comes.
      • Jesus ends the story: it will be good for those servants whose master finds them ready, even if he comes in the second or third watch of the night. Even if he shows up later than you expected, your job isn’t to predict the time; it is to be ready no matter the time. He is coming, and our world has never been closer to his return. So while we wait, we stay alert and watch for him.

  9. 30:53 Second: be self-controlled

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:7–8 Luke 21:34

    • Those who sleep, sleep at night, and those who get drunk, get drunk at night; but since we belong to the day, let us be self-controlled, putting on faith and love as a breastplate and the hope of salvation as a helmet. Jesus put it this way: be careful, or your hearts will be weighed down with dissipation, drunkenness, and the anxieties of life, and that day will close on you unexpectedly like a trap.
    • Paul says thief; Jesus says trap; the application is the same. If you are lazy, walking in ungodliness, or unfaithful, you won’t be ready and could be caught short, maybe even left behind. Stay faithful. Temptations, pressures, cynics, and scoffers will try to convince you this end-times talk is a myth and make you quit living like a believer. When those attacks come, put on the helmet of the hope of salvation and hold in place the breastplate of faith and love.

  10. 33:08 Third: be encouraged

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:9–11

    • Why? Because God did not appoint you to suffer wrath but to receive salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ, who died for us so that whether we are awake or asleep we may live together with him. Therefore encourage one another and build each other up. That is Paul’s third charge: be alert, be self-controlled, be encouraged.
    • It is the second time in less than 12 verses that Paul says it, so the promise of Jesus’ soon return is clearly meant to encourage every believer. It isn’t a discouragement, a rebuke, or a reprimand; Paul means these words to lift our hearts, not burden them.

    1. 34:22 Why aren’t we encouraged?

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 2:9 Joel 2:11 Revelation 6:16

      • Many people, including many believers, aren’t encouraged by the second coming. Steve thinks much of it is fear: thief in the night, sudden destruction, unavoidable labor aren’t attractive terms. Some of that is to be expected, fear of the unknown or of missing out. Many have told him they want to get married or have a baby first, and he remembers feeling that. It’s understandable, but it isn’t logical, for no eye has seen, no ear has heard, no mind has conceived what God has prepared for those who love him.
      • Others really ought to be afraid. If you aren’t a follower of Jesus Christ, you have every reason to be. Joel 2:11 says the day of the Lord is great and dreadful; who can endure it? If he hasn’t forgiven your sins, it is a day of monumental destruction, and Scripture says some will call on the rocks and mountains to fall on them to escape it. But if you are a Christian, be encouraged, for reasons Paul gave long before Steve did.

    2. 37:07 Reason one: not appointed to wrath

      Scripture: Daniel Revelation

      • If you have given Jesus your life, mind, body, soul, and spirit, God hasn’t appointed you to suffer the coming wrath but to receive salvation. First, that means when Steve stands before the Lord, before one hint of his slander, rebellion, or disobedience is mentioned, Jesus will step in front of him and say, “Father, he’s one of mine,” and the Father will say, “Why didn’t you say so? Welcome home, son.”
      • Second, you will be saved from the wrath about to unfold on earth. Not everyone agrees, and that’s okay, but Steve believes Jesus will come for his church before the tribulation; others say we will endure it, which makes for a robust conversation. The Lord gave the signs so we would be alert; in the New Testament’s 260 chapters, his return and its signs are mentioned 318 times, to encourage, not discourage.
      • Steve believes the seven-year tribulation comes after Jesus takes his church. Imagine one or two billion God-fearing, taxpaying, government-respecting, morally upright believers suddenly gone; that will trigger chaos. Into it steps the Antichrist, whose first big act is a peace treaty with Israel. Halfway through he breaks it, and a greater tribulation lasts another three and a half years. It is prophesied in Daniel and described in greater detail in Revelation. There is no small disagreement about how these events unfold, but vast agreement that these are the events, and Steve really doesn’t think believers will be here for it.

    3. 41:31 Now is the time

      • More encouragement: during that awful period there will also be a great revival, as multitudes from every tribe, people, language, and nation turn to worship Jesus. But don’t forget the chaos. Now is the time for your salvation, before the tribulation breaks out without warning. Make sure your heart is right with God, that you are truly saved by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone. Please don’t wait. Do it right now.

    4. 42:34 Reason two: vindicated

      Scripture: Philippians 2:10–11

      • On that day you will finally be vindicated. You have been reviled, mocked, and called every name for living for Jesus, but as Paul told the Philippians, every knee will bow, in heaven, on earth, and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.
      • Every being that ever breathed, every angel, every departed saint, everyone living, even the tortured residents of hell, yes, even Satan, will bow and declare what you have believed all along. Every scoffer and cynic who sneered at your choices, mocked you as a fool, dismissed you as an intellectual lightweight, or despised your children will join in unison: he is risen and he is Lord. In that moment everything you lived for will be vindicated.

    5. 44:47 Reason three: with him forever

      Scripture: John 14:2–3

      • One more reason: from that moment you will live together with Jesus forever. According to Steve’s dictionary, forever means forever: always with him, never apart. You are why he endured the wrath, the beatings, the scourging, the nine-inch nails, and the spear in his side, so you and he could live together forever.
      • He said he was going away to prepare a place for you, and then he would come back and take you to be with him, so you will always be where he is. That is why he is coming: not to play the thief, the great destroyer, or to fill your heart with fear, but so you will be with him and he with you.

  11. 46:38 If this book says it

    • Steve believes every word of God’s book is true. If it says Jesus is coming again, he is. If it says disaster is on the way, it is. If it says the only way to avoid that disaster is faith in Jesus Christ, then that is the only way. And if it says God wants nothing more than for you to be with him forever, then he wants nothing more.

    1. 47:29 Restless until we see him

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 2:9

      • Until then, if you feel restless in your relationships, your family, or maintaining your purity, priorities, and commitments, God wants you to feel that, because you are not yet with him, and he made you to be with him. Your heart won’t find its rest, and that longing won’t be satisfied, until you see Jesus face to face. He made you for pleasures this world can’t provide and a glory you can’t imagine: no eye has seen, no ear has heard.
      • That day is coming. You will see Jesus face to face and join the redeemed from every tribe and nation, singing and shouting praise to the God who made you and saved you. You will know what your mind couldn’t contain, see beauty and colors you never knew existed, hear music you could never conceive, walk on streets of gold, and meet Peter, James, and John. But most of all, you will see Jesus.
      • In that moment your wandering, restless, meaningless life under the sun will find its ultimate meaning in him. All the so-called missed experiences you passed up to follow Jesus won’t mean a ding-dong thing. When you see Jesus you will know the fullness of everything you longed for, and nothing this life offers will begin to compare to being with Jesus. For how long? Forever.

  12. 51:54 Closing prayer and the Lord’s Supper

    • Steve prays that we can’t begin to imagine the glory God has in store, yet we get caught up in petty longings and empty pursuits while he keeps saying, “Look to me.” One day all will be known, every question answered, and it will be worth it all.
    • He leads the church into communion as Jesus said to do, noting that remembering him through the bread and the cup proclaims his coming, and thanks Jesus, who reduced himself to mere humanity and was rejected so we could be with him.
