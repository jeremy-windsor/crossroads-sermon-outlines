  1. 00:06 Week three of Misquoted

    Scripture: Matthew 7

    • Today is week three of the five-week series Misquoted, looking at texts often twisted out of context, sometimes innocently, so that God’s word is misused, misapplied, and misquoted.
    • In week one, many Christians had taken Matthew 7, where Jesus teaches us how to judge rightly, as a command never to judge anyone for anything. That can’t be true, because Scripture consistently calls believers to kill sin in their own lives, which is one way we love God, and to help others do the same, which is one way we love our neighbor. Knowing God’s view of sin and its consequences, how loving would it be to let another drown in it? We are ordered to be loving truth tellers who listen to our own sermons before we preach them.

    1. 01:18 Last week: follow your heart

      Scripture: Jeremiah 17:9

      • Last week’s misquote was that the best thing you can do is be who you are and follow your heart. Anyone who has spent time seeking Jesus knows he didn’t say drop your nets and follow your heart; he said drop your nets and follow me, deny yourself and come follow me. The heart is deceitful. A Christian who follows their heart will look more like the world; a Christian who follows Jesus will look more like a citizen of heaven.
      • We are called to love God, to love who God made us to be, to love our spouses, children, and families, and to love one another as brothers and sisters in Christ. God is love, so those who claim to love God take on the family resemblance. But there is one thing we are commanded not to love: the world. The Christian is daily learning to turn a deceitful heart into a dedicated heart.

  2. 02:36 Warm-up misquote: “God is love”

    Scripture: 1 John 4:8

    • Many misquotes could have made the series. One is from 1 John 4:8, where John simply states that God is love. As a warm-up before the real deal, Josh notes how many grab that phrase and make massive claims like “God loves you just the way you are.” That is what Satan does: he takes a truth and inserts a dab of deception so it still sounds good.
    • Josh suspects some actually mean that love is God: whatever or whoever we love is God, and you’d better show proper respect. Many shove God into the box of their experiences with human love, as though he is just a father with a software update, Father 2.0. Please don’t. God describes his own love and doesn’t need your help; if you shape it, you’ll miss its eternal, divine, unmatched, unconditional character. Don’t shape his love; read of his love and become a scholar of it.
    • Yes, God loves you no matter where you are, where you come from, or what you’ve done, but you make a grave mistake if you think he intends to leave you as he found you. He loves who he created you to be and will wash all this stuff off, if you’ll let him, so you can look like him.

    1. 04:57 Not just acceptance but redemption

      Scripture: Romans 5:6–7

      • His story isn’t just acceptance; it’s redemption. Romans 5 says that at just the right time, when we were still powerless, Christ died for the ungodly; very rarely will anyone die for a righteous person. God loved you in a way no one else would.
      • Josh asks the men: would you take a bullet for your wife? He would; quick math says the world is better off with her than with him, and he still doesn’t know how he managed to catch that one. Now, would you take a bullet for a death row inmate? He highly doubts it, but that’s the kind of love God had for you. You weren’t the bride; you were the inmate. You wouldn’t save a wicked person, but God did, and by the power of his word and the might of his Spirit he is teaching the ungodly to be godly, reshaping and transforming them to be holy.

    2. 06:37 God’s love is an action: keep reading

      Scripture: 1 John 4:8–10

      • God is love, and we witness that love in a person, Jesus. God’s love isn’t a feeling he has about you; it is an action he took for you. You can’t talk about God’s love and leave out Jesus. That is why you must keep reading your Bible and stop picking verses to put on a T-shirt, which Josh admits he is guilty of today.
      • The next verse usually clears everything up: 1 John 4:8–10 says that whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love; God showed his love by sending his one and only Son, and this is love, not that we loved God but that he loved us and sent his Son as an atoning sacrifice for our sins. That is why we preach Christ crucified and lift our hands in praise. What comfort to rest in the love of God.

  3. 07:50 Warm-up misquote: “With God all things are possible”

    Scripture: Matthew 19:26

    • Another misquote that didn’t make the series is from Matthew 19, where Jesus says that with God all things are possible. The misquote is that God will help you accomplish your dreams: you’re Batman, and God is Robin.
    • First, God doesn’t play a supporting role in anything. He is King of kings and Lord of lords, the Alpha and Omega, the great I Am, who holds everything in his hand. God doesn’t open for anyone; he is the main event. Nothing is too hard for the Lord, but that doesn’t mean he desires to bring you wealth, health, or success. What he is bringing you has nothing to do with your business or your 401(k).

    1. 09:00 The rich young man

      Scripture: Matthew 19:16–26

      • In Matthew 19 Jesus is responding to a man who asked, “Good teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?” He already had a successful business and everything else, and he set his eyes on eternal life not because he wanted heaven’s treasures but because he wanted his earthly treasures never to fade. Jesus told him to sell everything, give to the poor, and come follow him, and he went away sad, because he knew it was going to end.
      • Jesus was explaining that salvation is impossible for humans to acquire by works: with human beings this is impossible, but with God all things are possible. Jesus isn’t your dream catcher, golden goose, co-pilot, sixth man, cheerleader, or right-hand man; he is your Lord and Savior, Master and friend, fighting for your soul, not your success.
      • So many misquotes happen when people forget Christianity isn’t just about how to live now but how to live forever. Jesus isn’t part of your story; by his great mercy you are part of his. Praise God, he did the impossible by saving you from death and hell through his death and resurrection.

  4. 10:41 A text to memorize: Hebrews 12

    Scripture: Hebrews 12:1–2

    • Josh reads a text he will return to several times and challenges the church to memorize it, from Hebrews 12: since we are surrounded by such a great cloud of witnesses, let us throw off everything that hinders and the sin that so easily entangles, and run with perseverance the race marked out for us, fixing our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith, who for the joy set before him endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.

  5. 11:23 Today’s misquote: “God won’t give you more than you can handle”

    Scripture: 1 Peter 3:15

    • Warmed up, here is today’s misquote: “God won’t give you more than you can handle.” In a highly spiritual culture people cling to toilet-seat book encouragements and toss them around Christian lobbies. Every Christian feels an odd pressure to have answers. Paul says to give a reason for your faith, and you should be prepared, but you aren’t everyone’s Christian counselor. Most situations simply call for you to intercede: “I may not have words for you, but can I pray for you?”
    • The saddest part is that people don’t study the encouragements they offer. Paul’s words are often twisted to comfort those who are suffering, a friend who lost a spouse or parents who lost a child. Josh’s heart breaks for them too, but is Scripture twisted out of context truly providing comfort, or accomplishing something else?

    1. 12:46 1 Corinthians 10 is about temptation, not suffering

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 10 1 Corinthians 10

      • In 1 Corinthians, Paul has just pointed to himself as a mature Christian, not arrogantly but to teach, saying self-discipline and self-control are crucial to serving God better. Then in chapter 10 he uses Israel’s history as an example of immaturity: no humility, no self-control, overconfidence. Paul good, Israel bad.
      • It is simple: no believer can claim they were overpowered by temptation. “The devil made me do it”? No, he didn’t. You had a way out and chose to do it. Satan can’t even make an unbeliever sin; do you think he has more power over you as a believer? You sinned because you were willing to.
      • That is the problem with the misquote: in 1 Corinthians 10 Paul is writing about temptation, not suffering, so using a verse on temptation to comfort someone dealing with loss misses it.

    2. 13:58 Sin is like a creepy ex

      Scripture: Genesis 4:7

      • Temptation is the line between sin and holiness, the decision point that leads to evil or good. God explains this to Cain in Genesis: if you do what is right, will you not be accepted? But if not, sin is crouching at your door; it desires to have you, but you must rule over it.
      • Josh asks who has had a bad breakup that led to a creepy-ex situation, and jokes he won’t make anyone raise a hand for being the creepy one. Sin is like a creepy ex: hiding behind the bush when you pull out for work, peeking into your cubicle, driving by in the evening to see if the lights are on. Sin wants you.

    3. 15:03 God always provides a way out

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 10:13

      • But what God says, what Jesus teaches, and what Paul reminds us is that we have power over sin if we cling to him and his word. And you don’t really know what you’re made of until you’re tested; it’s one thing to think you’re strong and another to have evidence. God permits temptation because he knows how much we can take, and he always provides a way of escape if we trust him and take it. The believer who thinks he can stand may fall, but the one who runs to the arms of Jesus and prays for strength will stand.
      • So when you’re faced with temptation, are you looking to see who’s around, or looking for the emergency exit? Josh wrestled with that all week; the church gets two minutes, hopefully longer.

    4. 16:01 Josh’s catfish moment

      • Josh remembers the exact moment, while overcoming addiction, that he first experienced Paul’s way out. Until then all he had ever seen was the way in; he never looked for a way out or knew there was one. He just took the bait.
      • That day he felt like a catfish floating by a log under the surface, eyeing the shiny worm of temptation. For the first time, instead of chomping down and ending up gutted, breaded, and dipped in hot oil, he paused, prayed, and walked away. He looked up and asked, “God, what do you think?” and realized he already knew. So he asked, “God, can you help me walk away from this? I keep hearing about this power you have. Do you have it? Because I need some.” Now he knows what it looks like; he and God are dangerous together, eliminating sin in his life, and he has proof of a strength he didn’t think he had.

    5. 17:40 Verse 12: stay humble

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 10:12 Proverbs 16:18

      • In comes Paul’s warning in verse 12: if you think you’re standing firm, be careful that you don’t fall. Humility seems to be the way out of temptation and the safeguard against needing more of it. If you think you’re standing firm, maybe you just haven’t been tempted like those who fell. What if temptation increases? Many have said, “I’ll never do that,” and then been swept away.
      • Pride goes before destruction, a haughty spirit before a fall. It’s easy to see God working in your life and become puffed up, taking credit for his efforts in your heart and mind, when it could easily be you lying on the ground. Keep your head on a swivel, and remember there is always a way out.

    6. 18:54 From overconfidence to no confidence

      • Paul’s warning serves both those who think they stand strong and those who can hardly stand at all, and Josh is sure both camps are here: some think they’ll never fall; others feel they’ll never stand again. To the victorious: stay humble. To the defeated: find comfort in the arms of the one who gave his life for you, fix your eyes on Jesus, and keep getting back up with his help. Take his hand time and time again.

  6. 19:46 Trials and temptations: attitude is the key

    Scripture: James 1:2–4

    • There are two ways a believer is tested: trials and temptations. You can turn them into triumphs, and the key to victory is attitude. James, the brother of Jesus and then servant of God, writes to Jewish Christians around Palestine: consider it pure joy whenever you face trials of many kinds, because the testing of your faith produces perseverance; let perseverance finish its work so you may be mature and complete, not lacking anything.
    • The end game is spiritual maturity. The journey feels like climbing a mountain while people throw stones and all kinds of havoc tries to bring you down; how do we reach the summit of the Christian life? Notice it’s not if trials come but when. They come in many forms, some as temptation, some simply from living in a broken world with broken people, but however they come, James says we can join Christ as overcomers.

    1. 21:17 Four keys to success in trials

      Scripture: James 1:2

      • There are four keys: a joyful attitude, a knowledge of the outcome, a surrender to the process, and a trust in the one who began the work in us.
      • A trial is something God either causes or allows so that you might respond in faith and be conformed to the image of Christ, a divine disturbance intended to help you grow up. “Many kinds” means various or diverse: circumstantial, financial, relational, emotional, loneliness, depression, rejection, loss. It could be a final exam you prepare for or a pop quiz out of nowhere. Whatever the specifics, James says consider it pure joy, not half joy and half self-pity, not half a cup of joy and half a cup of victim mentality.

  7. 22:36 Key one: a joyful attitude

    • Josh starts with the joyful attitude believers are supposed to have.

    1. 22:41 Don’t be surprised

      Scripture: 1 Peter 4:12 John 16:33 Acts 14:21–22

      • Peter says, “Dear friends, do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal that has come on you to test you, as though something strange were happening to you.” Josh suggests our attitude problem begins before the trial shows up, because we’re shocked. If you expect the Christian life to be easy, you’re in for a rude awakening. God isn’t a heavenly helicopter parent making sure his children never get an owie on the Slip ’N Slide.
      • In John Jesus says, “In this world you will have trouble.” Can you imagine being discipled by Paul? You can, because it’s in Acts 14: they preached the gospel, won a large number of disciples, then returned to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch strengthening them, and their ministry tagline was, “We must go through many hardships to enter the kingdom of God.” You can’t expect everything to go your way or treat hardship as something weird.

    2. 24:20 Comparing trials kills joy

      • The quickest way to kill joy in trials is to compare your trials to others’. Welcome envy, bitterness, anger, and resentment: “This person lost their job and is whining, and I lost my wife.” Your loss may be much worse, but that attitude won’t bear fruit. You both lost something; pray together and mourn together.

    3. 24:54 Consequences aren’t divine trials

      • The Christian also can’t treat the outcome of bad decisions as divine trials. There is a difference between God sending a trial or allowing a temptation for your growth and the consequence of being foolish, though you can still grow walking through consequences with the Lord.
      • Victory over a trial can bring great encouragement, but defeat can bring great discouragement. People tell Josh they keep getting hit with the same trials, as if God is a bully picking on them. If you failed a test, would you expect to move on to the next unit? No, you’d retake it, and some might need to be held back. Walk through it and ask what didn’t work.

    4. 26:04 Our greatest obstacle is our attitude

      Scripture: James 1:2

      • It’s easy to fall into grumbling and resent our troubles: this isn’t what I signed up for. Yes, it is, and Josh is sorry if no one told you. Our greatest obstacle to Christian maturity is our attitude toward our trials and temptations. Consider it pure joy. When was the last time God allowed something tough in your life to conform you to the image of Jesus, and you smiled about it?
      • If you keep getting hit with the same stuff, God wants transformation; if you refuse to change, repent, or humble yourself, don’t be surprised when it keeps coming. But here is why Josh loves James’s ministry: you can’t count it joy without some knowledge. Until you know what God’s word says the pain is about and believe his purpose is ultimately for your good, you’ll be miserable and never consider it pure joy.

    5. 28:07 For the joy set before him

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:1–2

      • Josh shows the Hebrews text again, because we aren’t the only ones driven by joy: for the joy set before him, Jesus endured the cross, scorning its shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God. He wants to drill it into their heads: how did Jesus endure the cross? The joy set before him. The joy of what? For starters, returning to heaven, and one day returning to call all God’s adopted children home and reclaim all things for God’s glory forever.

    6. 29:12 Trials reveal what we value

      Scripture: Job 23:10

      • Trials reveal our busted-up values. If you want comfort more than strong, godly character, trials and temptations will always frustrate you; if you care more about being like Christ than being comfortable, you’ll flourish. If you care more about what others think than what God thinks, you’ll be embarrassed and angry in your trials; if God’s view of you is what you value, you will consider it pure joy. Attitude is everything.
      • We should respond, “Wait, God cares enough to throw me this temptation to sharpen my discernment and self-control so I’m stronger for the next one? He loves me enough to allow this to build my patience and affection toward my wife, kids, and neighbor? That’s amazing.”
      • Look what Job says: “He knows the way that I take; when he has tested me, I will come forth as gold.” Job lost everything yet found value in the pruning. It reminds Josh of a favorite song from his boyhood, “Refiner’s Fire,” which he sings with the church: my heart’s one desire is to be holy, set apart for you, my Master, ready to do your will. Just ask Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego: you might be in a furnace, but that doesn’t mean you’re toast. Give thanks and dance in the furnace. A joyful outcome starts with a joyful attitude.

  8. 31:39 Key two: knowledge of the outcome

    Scripture: James 1:3

    • Verse 3: “because you know that the testing of your faith produces perseverance.” If it’s painful, how can you consider it pure joy? Because of its purpose. Perseverance means persisting in spite of opposition; similar words are determination and practice. Josh loves the word practice: everyone wants to be great, but few will do the work. By Gladwell’s 10,000-hour rule, expertise comes from proper, consistent practice.
    • You don’t see the chance to be a better businessman, parent, or husband as a horrible disturbance or unbearable task. So why frown at becoming a better Christian?

    1. 32:55 Faith proved genuine

      Scripture: 1 Peter 1:7 2 Corinthians 4:17

      • Peter says these trials have come so that your faith, of greater worth than gold, which perishes even though refined by fire, may be proved genuine and result in praise, glory, and honor when Jesus Christ is revealed. The pruning in you brings glory to him.
      • To test a diamond, jewelers place it in a clear glass of water: a real one sparkles with brilliance; a fake gives nada. Josh adds a disclaimer: men, if your wife finds out you bought a fake, don’t email him. An authentic diamond with a certificate is valuable enough to insure, and so is an authentic believer with the Master Jeweler’s stamp of approval. Faith is awesome, but faith God has tested and proven is what old-timers called blessed assurance.
      • Trials build up the believer brick by brick. Paul says our light and momentary troubles are achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all: light, momentary troubles with a heavy, lasting reward. Trials offer a certificate of authenticity and help us grow up.

  9. 34:51 Key three: surrender to the process

    Scripture: James 1:4

    • This doesn’t just happen; growth happens when you surrender to the process. In verse 4 James says to let perseverance finish its work so you may be mature and complete, not lacking anything. This might be the hardest thing for a believer, and it is for Josh: to be not lacking in God’s eyes, I may have to lack some stuff in my eyes. Now there is conflict between self and Savior. God often uses trials to remove things you were convinced didn’t need removing, and you may come out with less yet lacking nothing. To a skeptic that sounds strange; to the believer it makes complete sense.

    1. 36:09 God saves us, changes us, sends us

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:21 Ephesians 2:10

      • God doesn’t do anything halfway; he wants a finished product, mature and complete. He works in three ways. First, for us: salvation, what we sing and boast about. Jesus completed the work on the cross; 2 Corinthians says God made him who had no sin to be sin for us so that in him we might become the righteousness of God. That glorious exchange changes everything; by his wounds we are healed, and if we trust him he will save us.
      • Second, in us: Paul calls us God’s workmanship. We use big church words like sanctification; Josh prefers workmanship. God partners with us to eliminate harmful things and build our character to look more like Christ. Third, through us: our service, often our period of greatest growth, because we were created in Christ Jesus to do good works. God saves us, changes us, then sends us.
      • Salvation is God’s work for you; you contributed nothing and simply accept the gift. But you partner with God in the rest of the work. James says let the work happen. It may feel strange, but it is normal if you surrender your life and want to follow Christ.

    2. 38:47 Some of us take longer

      • So don’t be shocked by older believers who are immature: they refused to surrender to the process. If you won’t open your life and God’s word and let him command you like a platoon leader, don’t be surprised when he never sends you on a special mission.
      • God spent 25 years working in Abraham before blessing him with Isaac, 13 years sending trials to Joseph before he went from prison to second in command in Egypt, and 80 years preparing Moses, a slow learner; God can use slow learners too. Jesus was with the disciples three short years before trusting them with the message that changed the world. Some take longer, and Josh is a slow learner; some serve longer or in different areas; some sit on thrones and some get hit with stones. Whoever you are, you must surrender your will and embrace the process, or gripe and complain like a child. After today you can’t say no one told you.
      • God wants your heart, your obedience, and your transformation. He wants you to look like Jesus in the Garden of Gethsemane, resting beneath his will: “God, I don’t think I want this, but I want what you want more.” Mature believers rest in his will. They aren’t driven by emotions or swayed by fancy new thinking; they patiently await Christ’s arrival and consider everything joy in the meantime, because they know how it ends.

    3. 41:33 Jonah: half in, half out

      Scripture: Jonah 4

      • For how not to surrender to the process, Josh thinks of Jonah. God says go preach to Nineveh; Jonah says, “No, God, I’m good.” God keeps pushing, and Jonah finally moves forward, half in and half out, so he never matures through the process. Go read the last chapter of Jonah; some will say it sounds like them. He is angry at the sun, the wind, the poor worm (what did the worm do?), and most importantly at God.
      • Josh says it out of love: some of y’all are just crusty and bitter, not in age but in attitude. Maybe you’ve gotten a rough shake, and he is sorry, but praise God for the opportunity to grow. Have you fully surrendered to the process?

    4. 43:03 Clear eyes, full hearts: reclaim some ground

      • Josh loves Coach Taylor’s game-day line in Friday Night Lights: “Clear eyes, full hearts, can’t lose.” Believer, you can’t lose unless you give up. He challenges the church to reclaim some ground for the kingdom today and let God get back to work on them. He doesn’t care where you’re coming from or what you came in with; he cares what you go out with. Invite God to make a mess of what you’ve built, tear it down, and build something new.
      • Immediately the devil starts: it’s too late, you’re too far gone, why fix this, you’ve got a good life, you earned it. Josh gives permission to defy his lies, be a rebel, be a maverick. There’s a reason you’re here, and it isn’t because you have it all figured out. Get up, get to work, don’t look back, and let him finish his work in you.
      • This is the Christian grind: faithfulness day after day, surrenders, bumps, and bruises, with unrelenting hope in the one who saves us, changes us, and sends us. Every day is a series of choices between the right way and the easy way, and we keep our eye on the narrow road. Jesus didn’t do easy or comfortable, so neither do we. When you let the King rule your life, it isn’t the end but the beginning, and it only gets harder. Satan gets more satisfaction the farther you’ve come when you fall on your face, but he has no answer to grace. So stay vigilant, be patient with the process, and do not lose heart.

  10. 45:52 Key four: trust the one who began the work

    • The fourth and final key is to trust the one who began the work in you. Don’t let doubt creep in or fear invade. Jesus took on hell itself and bore God’s wrath because he loves you; God poured out all his wrath on Jesus, and Jesus just took it. Every morning he says, “Look how I love you. There’s no condemnation, because I endured the cross on your behalf. It is finished.” Do you really think he’ll give up on you or throw you out now?
    • Jesus persevered, and his perseverance is our joy. Having seen his suffering, love, and forgiveness, he wants to teach you to suffer well, to love like him, and, hard as it is, to forgive like him. Perseverance through trials isn’t about becoming a better person; it’s about becoming a new person, a new creation.
    • The suffering, trials, and temptations you endure here are a tiny pixel next to eternity. The reward, inheritance, and precious crown awaiting those who hold tight to Jesus will be worth every tear, pain, loss, and heartache. Hold on, believer, because the Lord is coming to make everything right.

  11. 48:10 Closing prayer

    Scripture: 1 Peter 2:11

    • Josh confesses frustration before God and the body. He is learning every day to walk with God better and thanks him for grace and mercy, and for every trial as an opportunity to reveal and wipe away what needs to go so he can see and think clearly and walk with him.
    • In a world that seems to care less and less about God, he understands what Scripture means in calling us foreigners and exiles; he doesn’t feel he belongs. But he knows God is with him, trusts and loves him, and asks for faith and strength to endure. He hands God whatever he needs to let go of, prays the church will do the same, and trusts that no matter what they go through God will come and make it all right. He longs for that day.
