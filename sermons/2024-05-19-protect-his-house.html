Overview

  1. 01:40 Love needs a framework

    Scripture: 2 John

    • The church continues its study of 2 John, the tiny letter just left of Revelation; if you are using a digital Bible and can’t find it, “we got some real problems.”
    • Last week’s big idea: love and truth can’t be separated. Sparing someone the truth isn’t proof of love; Josh would call it a hate crime, watching someone head for destruction and withholding the way through because it inconveniences you. Love needs a framework to be godly love, and that framework is truth, a boundary or funnel that ensures love flows properly.

  2. 02:54 How do I demonstrate God’s love?

    Scripture: Romans 5:8 Micah 6:8 Matthew 25:40 Matthew 6:20 2 Corinthians 9:6 Galatians 6:2 Philippians 2:3

    • If there is one verse to memorize, it is Romans 5:8: God demonstrates his own love for us in this, while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Those who love God immediately ask how they can demonstrate that love, and having been comforted, saved, and redeemed by it, the redeemed soul starts singing praise in how it lives.
    • We show God’s love by being open-handed: showing kindness, strengthening the weak, sticking by brothers and sisters through adversity, sharing food with the poor, seeking justice, defending the oppressed, fathering the fatherless, and coming alongside the widow. Micah: act justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with your God.
    • Jesus didn’t abolish these but said whatever you did for the least of these, you did for me. If you have two shirts and a guy is shirtless, give him one. Store up treasures in heaven; whoever sows sparingly reaps sparingly; carry one another’s burdens; value others above yourselves; don’t grow weary of doing good. Our call to love others is radical.

    1. 04:57 Love your enemies

      Scripture: Luke 6:35–36

      • The most radical command of all: love your enemies. In Luke 6 Jesus says to do good to them and lend without expecting anything back, because the Most High is kind to the ungrateful and wicked; be merciful as your Father is merciful.
      • Not everyone will be grateful. Some will hate your love and hate it when you share his love, but you aren’t called to a lower human standard; you are called to a divine standard. As members of his church, we have been baptized into this lifestyle: pouring out his love in truth by the power of the Holy Spirit.

  3. 06:03 Realign with the Lord’s Prayer

    Scripture: Matthew 6:9–13

    • When we get out of whack, a short look at the Lord’s Prayer can realign us. Not always, but usually what is out of balance connects to a shortness of breath in your prayer life. Pray as he taught, not word for word like a robot, but as a framework to connect with your heavenly Father.
    • Josh walks through it: hallowed be your name, the holy Lord God Almighty, the Alpha and Omega. Believers live in a tension: we long for a kingdom not of this world, yet hunger to see his kingdom come and his will be done here, a small taste of the fullness to come. We long for the day of the Lord: Maranatha, come, Lord Jesus.

    1. 07:48 We are in the end times; get back to work

      • That is a good longing, but many believers live only in that mindset, focusing on the Lord’s return and forgetting their responsibility while they wait. Believers aren’t just called to await his coming kingdom but to help advance his current one.
      • Is this a sign of the end times? Josh clears it up: we are in the end times, and whether this or that is a sign hardly matters. He will come like a thief in the night; in the meantime, get back to work.

  4. 08:36 Knockoff kingdoms

    Scripture: 2 John 7

    • In his letter John shows what the believer’s work looks like: marked by love for God and others and love for his truth. In verse 7 John makes clear the reason for his instruction in the first six verses: some have abandoned the truth. As we advance God’s kingdom on earth through love and truth, little knockoff kingdoms start growing up around us.

    1. 09:13 Creme Betweens, Dr. Thunder, and Thick Mints

      • Josh asks the bargain shoppers and couponers to raise their hands. He is all for saving money, but never at the expense of quality. Who grabs Creme Betweens over Oreos to save two bucks? “Shame on you. I love you.”
      • If you host a block party this summer to meet your neighbors, spring for Dr Pepper, who has a PhD, not Dr. Thunder, who is running around with a GED. And for Thin Mint lovers, Costco’s Thin Mint bites were bad enough for his waistline, but behold the abomination of “Thick Mints”: a terrible knockoff that also rips off girls raising money for a good cause.

    2. 11:30 The truth is worth protecting

      • We can laugh about silly knockoffs, but when people try to alter, add to, deny, or misrepresent what the Bible says about who Jesus Christ is and what he teaches, we must not welcome them in. It isn’t a laughing matter. The truth is worth protecting, because when we stray from the truth our love suffers, and the more our love suffers, the further we are from the truth.

  5. 12:01 Why John wrote: wolves in sheep’s clothing

    Scripture: Matthew 7:15

    • Before reading the text, Josh shows what Jesus warns against as we wait for his kingdom. In Matthew 7: watch out for false prophets in sheep’s clothing who are ferocious wolves inside; they look and sound like Jesus, but he isn’t there. On the Mount of Olives Jesus says many will come to deceive.
    • John wrote because false teachers went on missionary journeys just as Paul did and found their way into believers’ homes. Early Christians were encouraged to show hospitality to brothers and sisters in the mission field, and deceivers caught on: they claimed Christ to get a warm bed and a free meal, then hit the streets the next day publicly denying what Scripture teaches about Jesus. Maybe believers lacked discernment, maybe the teachers were convincing, but knockoff truths started popping up in the church, and John equips believers to manage the crisis.

    1. 13:43 It still happens today

      • John’s warnings aren’t only for one group at one time; they are a Spirit-inspired warning about wolves in sheep’s clothing to first-century and 2024 Christians alike. It looks different now, but not really.
      • Josh has watched people join a church because it benefits their small business; join to build a following and launch their own home church, starting a life group and pulling people away from the church’s leadership and oversight; and join hoping to gain influence and authority, sometimes pastors and elders. He has personal experiences with each. Outsiders still come to take advantage of the most loving and generous organization in the world.

    2. 14:50 Why we set things up the way we do

      • This is why the church is set up as it is. Without time to flesh it out, Josh says that when Paul says there are no female overseers, they take him at his word, believing men are specifically gifted by God for that protection of the flock.
      • There is one place to give, with multiple layers of accountability and controls and sufficient oversight to make sure gifts actually advance his kingdom. No elder or staff member will ever ask for money by email or in any unofficial capacity, or ask for gift cards or personal financial help; if anyone does, come to Josh immediately. Requests are made officially when the church gathers, through the app, website, or envelopes.
      • The one time Josh asked someone for money, the man had told him to let him know if things got tight. In a stewardship meeting where they were simply trusting God, the text came in: “Do you need anything?” Josh replied that he needed about $50,000 and was praying God would show up, and the man answered, “Done.” Josh praises God for his faith and generosity.
      • These controls don’t come from a desire for control, authority, or power, but from understanding that Satan has a strategy to infiltrate the church. The church is on the offensive but also takes defensive measures, and the only way to expose his deception is by knowing the truth.

  6. 17:48 Reading 2 John 7–13

    Scripture: 2 John 7–13 1 John 2:18–20

    • Josh reads the passage through: many deceivers who do not acknowledge Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh have gone out into the world; such a person is the deceiver and the antichrist. Watch out that you do not lose what we have worked for. Anyone who runs ahead and does not continue in the teaching of Christ does not have God. If anyone comes without this teaching, do not take them into your house or welcome them. John hopes to visit face to face so their joy may be complete.
    • Don’t fall asleep thinking this is a first-century problem; deception has only gotten worse and more complicated. In his first letter John writes that this is the last hour, many antichrists have come, and they went out from us because they did not really belong to us; but you have an anointing from the Holy One and know the truth.
    • Jesus, Peter, Paul, John, and Jude all warn us of deceivers, so we might want to pay attention.

  7. 19:35 Who are these deceivers?

    • Our call to love is not a call to be naive or undiscerning, or to blindly accept anyone who claims to represent Jesus. Love for others must never override love for the truth about Jesus Christ.
    • “Deceivers” translates planos, one who leads astray, or wanderers. They rejected the incarnation, teaching in homes and churches that Jesus’ body only appeared human: docetism. It was a small sect, but it doesn’t have to be a big lie to be a harmful one. Josh hopes most of the church could rip that argument to shreds and would defend the truth if they heard it.

    1. 20:53 Fully God and fully man

      Scripture: Colossians 2:9 Galatians 4:4 Hebrews 1:3 Genesis 3:15

      • The incarnation teaches that Jesus is fully God and fully human. Colossians 2:9: in Christ all the fullness of the deity lives in bodily form. When the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, the very substance of God’s glory wrapped in flesh; Hebrews 1 calls him the radiance of God’s glory and the exact representation of his being.
      • True God in the flesh, Jesus took on human nature from the virgin Mary, who was found pregnant through the Holy Spirit: Immanuel, God with us. In Genesis 3 God told the serpent that the woman’s offspring would crush his head. Jesus is the promised Messiah, true God and true man, two natures in one person, to accomplish salvation for all who believe.

    2. 22:05 A message for everyone

      • Christ pretenders can be deadly. Everywhere the gospel goes, deceivers follow, and they are especially dangerous when allowed into the church, so John says watch out.
      • This isn’t only for elders and pastors. If anyone witnesses deception and lies in the church, it must be brought into the light and to the leaders’ attention immediately.

  8. 22:45 Don’t lose what we have worked for

    Scripture: 2 John 8

    • Verse 8 isn’t salvation language; it is about shepherding, and everyone engaged in ministry is a shepherd of something, with different levels of responsibility.
    • If we let false teachers take the pulpit, lead life groups, or influence children and youth, we risk losing everything we have worked so hard to build and every life God has let us speak into. If we relax our standards, change our definitions, or present only portions of the gospel, the fallout can be devastating.

    1. 23:53 Test the spirits

      Scripture: 1 John 4:1–3

      • John gives the defense in his first letter: do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God. Every spirit that acknowledges Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God; every spirit that doesn’t is the spirit of the antichrist.
      • So, “Welcome to Crossroads Church: we take God at his word, but please be patient with us, because we do not take you at your word.” The marketing team wasn’t excited about that welcome sign, but there is wisdom in it. That is why prospective members sign a written agreement at Starting Point after reviewing the statement of faith; if someone disagrees, the church will try to convince them, and if it can’t, it will help them find a church, no hard feelings.
      • Testing doesn’t make the church unkind or unloving or mean it doesn’t practice hospitality; it means caring about the truth and defending those in its care. You should find comfort when your new home church tests you; you found a good one.

    2. 25:26 Dinner with the pastor

      • That is why Josh takes new people out to dinner; it is in the budget. He knows nobody wants to be grilled, but he wants to get to know them and see what God is doing in their lives, and also make sure they aren’t “some whack heretic who thinks that they no longer sin.” It isn’t personal; he loves God, this church, and every soul in his care, and he has a responsibility to defend the truth. So does everyone else.

  9. 26:10 What to watch out for

    • Denying salvation by faith is antichrist, as is denying the incarnation, the Trinity, or that Jesus is God. Grace plus anything, “grace plus all you can do,” is just another way to abandon Christ’s teaching.
    • People can believe what they want, but they don’t get to deny the deity of Christ and call themselves Christians. If you don’t believe God exists in three persons, you don’t worship the one true God; if you don’t believe Jesus is God, you don’t believe in the one true Messiah; if you don’t believe salvation is by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone, you aren’t a brother or a coheir with Christ. Somebody has to love you enough to tell you.

    1. 27:17 Grace “after all you can do”

      • It gets worse day by day. A Mormon will say they believe in salvation by grace, and Josh replies that he has read their false prophet’s book, which adds “after all you can do.” Ask a Mormon how to get to heaven: be baptized, but differently; join the Mormon church, which answers a salvation question with church membership; and once you show a pattern of righteous conduct, you can be saved. That is salvation by works, not grace.
      • They will say God is gracious enough to let you work your way to heaven. Close, but no: God is gracious enough to send his Son Jesus to make a way, and he is the only way. That gospel doesn’t save, and Christians trust in Jesus for salvation, not their own works.

    2. 28:39 Christology

      • If your view of God is wrong, nothing else lines up underneath it. Josh teaches a word to sound smart at life group: christology, the study of who Jesus is and what he is doing. If your christology is jacked up, your gospel will be too, and if the gospel you cling to is false, you aren’t saved.
      • If you don’t understand why his atoning sacrifice shook the planet, when the sun went out and rocks split, then you do not have God or eternal life. But don’t be discouraged: God is in the business of rescuing sinners, and if he could soften Josh’s hard heart, he can do it for you. Josh wants you to see Jesus today; it was John’s life’s work, and it is his too.

  10. 29:52 That you may know

    Scripture: 1 John 5:13 1 John 5:20 John 14:9

    • John writes to believers so that they may know they have eternal life, and further down: the Son of God has come and given us understanding so that we may know him who is true; he is the true God and eternal life.
    • Josh admits he is assuming, but he thinks John was recalling what Jesus told Philip the night before he was betrayed, in John 14:9: “Anyone who has seen me has seen the Father.” That phrase seems to inform so much of John’s writing. How is it possible? Because Jehovah, in the person of Jesus, became incarnate in human flesh as the promised Messiah, and denying it is antichrist.

    1. 31:05 Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God

      Scripture: Isaiah 9:6

      • Isaiah gives the child unbelievable names: for to us a child is born, to us a son is given, and he will be called Wonderful Counselor (Josh’s son’s name is Nolan), Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. These are names for God; anyone who says Jesus is not God in the flesh is a deceiver.
      • Jesus had to be a man to die in our place and had to be God to conquer sin and death. Take away his humanity and you destroy the atonement; take away his divinity and you destroy the resurrection and our salvation.

  11. 31:57 Rewarded fully: mourn the regression

    Scripture: 2 John 8

    • John keeps going: watch out, because he wants you to be rewarded fully. The verse is easy to misconstrue, so Josh explains it this way.
    • As a youth pastor he told students after camp that Satan knew they had taken big steps with Jesus and would try to make them slip, so watch out. And if they slipped, five steps toward God and one step back is still progress; God wants progress, not perfection, and we walk by grace upon grace. He wasn’t permitting sin in advance.
    • What he wishes he had told them, and now tells the former students listening: celebrate the progress, but never forget that one step back was a bit of theft by the evil one. Mourn the regression, but don’t be overcome by it.

  12. 33:28 Change ourselves, not Scripture

    Scripture: 2 John 9

    • Anyone who runs ahead and doesn’t continue in the teaching of Christ does not have God; whoever continues has both the Father and the Son. Those loyal to Scripture want to protect and defend it.
    • Josh says it twice so no one misses it: the attitude of those who have God toward Scripture is not to change it when it offends us, but to change ourselves when Scripture reveals we have offended God.

    1. 34:08 “Jesus never addresses homosexuality”

      Scripture: Matthew 5 Matthew 19 Leviticus 18 Leviticus 18:13 Leviticus 18:18 Leviticus 18:23 Leviticus 18:22 Matthew 5:19

      • There are countless examples, and Josh dips a toe in. The most polarizing issue in the culture is homosexuality. Instead of recognizing their offense against God, many stand tall in pride, claiming God has offended them. Some walk away from the lifestyle and seek God, and the church praises God for those stories; others have made it their mission, even going to seminary, to prove it isn’t sin.
      • To defend continuing in it they use a one-liner that has deceived many: “Jesus never addresses homosexuality.” But he does, in Matthew 5 and Matthew 19. Asked about divorce, he refers back to creation and uses porneia, illicit sexual activity, usually translated sexual immorality, a word used 25 times in the New Testament. To learn which acts are immoral, refer back to the porneia code in Leviticus 18.
      • Few will fight Leviticus 18: don’t have relations with your mother’s sister (“seems like a good idea”); don’t take your wife’s sister as a rival wife (“some of you can’t handle one wife”); don’t have relations with an animal. Everyone agrees these are good for health, joy, homes, and society, until verse 22, and then there is a debate.
      • Jesus simply says porneia because he knows we can go back to the written law; it is like Josh saying he is pro Second Amendment without explaining it. Paul and John do the same. Those who love God protect and defend his word instead of doing gymnastics and back tucks over what they don’t like, even when it means killing sin in their own lives.

    2. 37:43 A pastoral response to same-sex attraction

      • Josh’s pastoral response is simple. He is crazy about his wife and a one-woman man to death. But he isn’t blind: God made many beautiful women, and when he notices one, he says, “Good job, God,” and runs.
      • That is how to handle same-sex attraction: you don’t act on it. We all have attractions we shouldn’t, and we learn to say no.

  13. 38:46 Don’t run ahead of the teaching of Christ

    Scripture: 2 John 9

    • Don’t run ahead of the teaching of Christ. It is for your good, because he loves you, not because he despises you; he despises what seeks to imprison you.
    • Josh pleads with the church: no matter how much the culture creeps in and pressures you, don’t run ahead without Jesus’ teachings, and don’t embrace teachers who do. Stay rooted in the word; having a lot of followers doesn’t make someone worth following.

    1. 39:29 Do not welcome them

      Scripture: 2 John 10–11 Psalm 119:104

      • If anyone comes without this teaching, do not welcome them; whoever welcomes them shares in their wicked work. True believers love and defend the truth.
      • Josh loves Psalm 119: I gain understanding from your precepts; therefore I hate every wrong path. Do you hate wrong paths? He hates when people are led away from God, and when we make a way for them to continue in sin when God has something better. If you love the truth, you will hate deception too. We are called to stand firm with God’s word in hand, unwilling to compromise where he speaks.

    2. 40:28 Loyal defenders of God’s word

      • Talking with Eli while preparing for Crossroads Speaks, Josh said he would rather hang out with an atheist than a so-called Christian willing to part from Christ’s teaching.
      • We walk by, love, and defend the truth and hold it as the only thing that defends us, never sacrificing it to please people, even at personal cost. There has never been a more important time for loyal defenders of God’s word to step into the light, because many, even in church leadership, will compromise the truth to gain a following.

  14. 41:28 A church that rejoices in the truth

    Scripture: John 3:16–18

    • A church full of people who love the truth rejoices when it hears it. Josh’s hands don’t go up in worship because Eli picks the strings so nicely; they fly up when he hears timeless truth sung by God’s most precious creation and sees God working around him.
    • He gets hyped when lives are surrendered to the truth: God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, not to condemn the world but to save it, and whoever believes is not condemned. God took on flesh to save your soul. If you are ready, the waters are ready; be baptized, because through faith in Christ you can approach God with freedom and confidence.

  15. 42:49 Closing prayer: Paul’s prayer for the Ephesians

    Scripture: Ephesians 3:14–21

    • Josh closes by praying Paul’s prayer for the Ephesians over the church: that out of his glorious riches the Father would strengthen them with power through his Spirit, that Christ would dwell in their hearts through faith, and that, rooted and established in love, they would grasp how wide, long, high, and deep is the love of Christ and be filled to the measure of all the fullness of God. To him who is able to do immeasurably more than all we ask or imagine be glory in the church and in Christ Jesus forever.
