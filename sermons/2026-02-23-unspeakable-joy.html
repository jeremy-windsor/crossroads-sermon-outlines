Overview

  1. 00:33 Forging Faith: steel is not forged in comfort

    • Today they start a two-week miniseries called Forging Faith. The hardest part will be not making it longer. They will probably come back to 1 Peter. For now they dip a toe into chapter 1: faith can be purified through trials. Christians do not just survive. They thrive. Faith can grow stronger than imagined. The visual is the forge: roar, smoke, heat, sparks, metal struck again and again. Fire reveals impurities. The hammer shapes. Repeated blows strengthen. Steel is not forged in comfort, and neither is faith.
    • He will read the first half of chapter 1, then give eight situations where faith is forged.

    1. 02:35 Peter reads the elect exiles into living hope

      Scripture: 1 Peter 1:1-12

      • Peter, an apostle, to God’s elect exiles scattered through Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia, chosen according to the Father’s foreknowledge, through the Spirit’s sanctifying work, to be obedient to Jesus and sprinkled with his blood. Grace and peace.
      • Praise to the Father: new birth into a living hope through the resurrection, an inheritance that cannot perish, spoil, or fade, kept in heaven, shielded by God’s power. They rejoice though they suffer grief in trials so that faith, worth more than gold refined by fire, may prove genuine. Though they have not seen him they love him, believe, and are filled with inexpressible glorious joy, receiving the salvation of their souls.
      • Prophets searched the sufferings of Christ and the glories that follow. They served you. Even angels long to look into these things.

  2. 04:28 An ordinary loudmouth who had been with Jesus

    Scripture: Acts 4:13 1 Corinthians 1:26-31

    • Josh loves Peter: a normal guy with a loud mouth. Paul was a Pharisee of Pharisees; Peter is a fisherman with dirty nails who never went to college. Favorite biblical authors: Peter and John.
    • Acts 4: when they saw the courage of Peter and John and realized they were unschooled ordinary men, they were astonished and took note they had been with Jesus. Unschooled is not unintelligent. Jobs, Gates, Zuckerberg, Jim Carrey. Education matters, but it is one metric. If Josh went to seminary now he would probably be more frustrated than blessed; God let him learn on the job. Peter illustrates 1 Corinthians 1: not many wise, influential, or noble; God chose the foolish to shame the wise so no one boasts. Boast in the Lord.

    1. 07:36 Close enough to ask Jesus about Joshua

      Scripture: Isaiah 40:3 Isaiah 53:4-5 Matthew 8:16-17 Matthew 17:1-8 Exodus 34:29-35 Mark 16:7 Acts 2:41

      • Peter was married; children unknown. Owner-operator of a fishing business with Andrew, who worked with John the Baptist, Jesus’ cousin, preparing the way. Handpicked as apostle. Three years of meals, travel. If he had questions in Joshua he could just ask Jesus. Hard to need seminary after the Master’s feet.
      • Eyewitness to the healing ministry, and he could ask the meaning later. Imagine Jesus explaining Isaiah 53, then displaying it. Matthew: evening, demon-possessed brought, he drove out spirits and healed all the sick to fulfill Isaiah: he took up our infirmities. He heals because he is the suffering servant. Shriveled hand healed because a nail-pierced hand leads to healing. Healing ministry points to Calvary.
      • Inner circle: Peter, James, John. Transfiguration: greater Moses, new covenant, Moses and Elijah from heaven. Peter met Moses and Elijah while hanging out with Jesus. He was there for betrayal, arrest, trial, crucifixion, resurrection, ascension. The angel tells Mary: go tell Peter. Pentecost sermon, reversal of Babel, 3,000 saved. He gave his life as a pastor; his words still preach.

    2. 11:41 Babylon means Rome, and the smell of burning bodies

      Scripture: 1 Peter 5:13 Revelation 17:1-18

      • Chapter 5: writing from Babylon. They just finished Ezekiel; Josh did not see Peter in ancient Babylon. A town named Babylon in Egypt has no evidence Peter went. Timing: early to mid-60s, before Nero’s persecution. Early Christians spoke in code. Rome is Babylon in Revelation 17 and 18: temple-destroying kingdoms. Paul the missionary, Peter the pastor after him. First letter about a year before Paul’s martyrdom; Peter soon laid down his life.
      • Fiery trials are something most of us know nothing about. Some emperors dipped political prisoners in oil, impaled them, set them on fire. Genocide was part of the Roman system. Peter pastors a church that probably smelled burning bodies and wondered who was next. Think twice about raising complaints in church. He mentions suffering at least fifteen times with eight Greek words. They suffered for wanting to live godly lives. Keep being good witnesses, especially to persecutors. Suffering leads to glory.

  3. 15:33 Do not dodge the question of suffering

    Scripture: 1 Peter 3:9 Job 1:1-22 Psalm 13:1 Matthew 27:46 Genesis 3:1-19

    • 1 Peter 3: do not repay evil with evil; repay with blessing so you may inherit a blessing. Suffering is the path to glory. Why do bad things happen to good people? Christians who fire back no one is good make Josh cringe. True, only God is good, but that does not declare the gospel. Stop invalidating a question Scripture validates. Read Job. Read the Psalms: how long, Lord? Hear Jesus on Golgotha: my God, my God, why have you forsaken me?
    • Suffering does not prove who God is. It proves what the world is. Ripple of Genesis 3. We live in the rundown neighborhood outside Eden. The gospel’s radical claim: God entered the suffering. He sent his only begotten Son, the only truly innocent one, who suffered in our place to save sinners. Substitution. Josh cannot explain why some things happen. He knows God is with us, justice will be done, death will be destroyed. The story will have pain. It will not end in pain.

  4. 18:01 Eight places faith is forged

    Scripture: Hebrews 12:2 1 Peter 1:1-2

    • One: when we remember who we are. Elect means chosen; exiles means not home. Aliens. Names once given to the Jews, now to the church. Earth is temporary. That is why people think Christians are crazy. We smile, forgive, sing, give, obey even if, for the joy set before us, as our Lord did. Chosen by the Father, purchased by the Son, set apart by the Spirit. All three for a true experience of salvation. The atonement, the sprinkling, is essential.
    • Foreknowledge trips people up. Arminianism: God looked down time, saw you would love him, chose you. Calvinism: love before time, he knew you would sin and sent Jesus. Two camps barking is not how we handle God’s wisdom. Find Jesus together. Stop trying to be right. The question is who chose whom. Either way, Peter says it started before the foundation of the world.

    1. 22:31 Who loves you, then meditate on the future

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 2:3 James 1:12 1 Peter 1:3-5 Romans 9:13-16

      • Two: when you know who loves you. Before the world, God knew we would rebel and prepared a plan to redeem us. He is not the author of evil. He made us knowing we would, then took on flesh as the suffering servant, died, and could not be contained in a grave. The invitation: join me in suffering like a good soldier. God does not know how to fail. Keep your eyes on Jesus. The crown of life awaits those who endure.
      • Three: when we meditate on our future. Three of the most amazing verses: new birth, living hope, imperishable inheritance, shielded until the last time. Peter erupts in praise at mercy. Religion says look how good we are; the gospel says look how evil I am, but God has been merciful. You cannot understand the gospel until you realize the greatest injustice is your sin against a holy God. Romans 9: Jacob I loved, Esau I hated. Mercy on whom I have mercy. It does not depend on human desire or effort. Mercy piled on mercy. After Easter they will discuss regeneration.

    2. 26:53 Hope is all that remains; he guards us

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:13-14 2 Corinthians 4:7-18

      • Four: when hope is all that remains. Hope says it will be worth it; present affliction is weightless next to eternal glory. Viktor Frankl in Auschwitz: fight and become the monster; give up and die with hope; cling to this-world restoration and collapse when family and career are gone. Those who endured anchored hope beyond this world. Trials do not just hurt us. They reveal us. Many put hope in better instead of heaven. Better waits and strives. Heaven is already chosen, already with the Lord, treasure not here, already united to Christ. The world says live your best life now. The gospel says you have not seen anything yet.
      • Five: when we know he guards us. The power of God guards us, not our strength. Independence Day: the little ones got shields too. Until we see Jesus face to face. Ephesians: sealed with the Spirit, deposit guaranteeing inheritance. Hard to focus on bumps when you are headed home. 2 Corinthians 4: treasure in jars of clay, hard pressed but not crushed, light momentary troubles achieving eternal glory, eyes on what is unseen.

    3. 34:49 Prepared in the fire, then present enjoyment

      Scripture: 1 Peter 1:6-7 Job 23:10 1 Peter 1:8-9 John 20:28 Romans 5:5 John 20:29 Genesis 22:1-14 Daniel 3:19-27 2 Corinthians 12:7-9

      • Six: as he prepares us for glory. Rejoice though you suffer grief so faith, worth more than gold, may prove genuine. Welcome to the Jesus way, where joy and grief unite as the song of the redeemed. After his sister died at 39, church was the last place he wanted to be. Family at the altar. Weeping and smiling, singing, a mess, confident and crushed. An eastern goldsmith keeps metal in the furnace until he sees his face in it. The Lord keeps us until we reflect Jesus. Stop staring at the trial. Look at the promises.
      • Seven: in the fire. Job, with God’s approval: he knows the way that I take; when he has tested me I will come forth as gold. Oldest book in Scripture talking Peter’s language. Born for glory, inheritance locked, guarded, purified. We will be ready. Do not settle into wait and see.
      • Eight: present enjoyment of glory. Though you have not seen him you love him. Three ways first-century believers enjoyed glory now: love Jesus, trust Jesus, rejoice in Jesus. Unlike Thomas we cannot see the wounds and say my Lord and my God. Since the ascension the Spirit has poured God’s love into our hearts. If you want to suffer well, learn to sing. If you love yourself more than Jesus, the fire will burn you alive. If you love him more, it purifies. Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed. Joy is rooted in Christ, not in smiling at circumstances. Abraham learned on the mountain. The three Hebrews learned in the furnace. Paul learned sufficient grace with a thorn. Inexpressible joy: words fail.

  5. 43:45 Forged for re-entry, not escape

    Scripture: 1 Peter 1:12

    • You are being forged for more than escape. Forged for re-entry. Peter does not tell them how to get out. He reminds them who they are: chosen, born again, kept, guarded, heirs, refined. Unspeakable joy now, not someday. The fire is not transporting you to heaven. It is transforming you with heaven. Coming out shining. Black velvet for the jewel of grace.
    • When you forgive in suffering, that is a glimpse of heaven. When you rejoice despite loss, heaven. When you love an unseen Christ with unashamed obedience, heaven coming down. The world expects bitterness, fear, collapse. Trials producing worship, peace, kindness do not belong to this world. Inheritance imperishable. Angels long to look into what you have. Stop living like you are barely hanging on.
    • Spurgeon: little faith will take your soul to heaven, but great faith will bring heaven to your soul. Not just rescued, refined. One day Christ will be revealed. Meanwhile he intends to reveal himself through ambassadors, children, co-heirs. If called to suffer, suffer like Jesus. If called to die, die like Jesus. If called to live, live like Jesus. Amen.
    • Prayer: hard to comprehend that angels long to look into what I see. Thank you for taking on flesh, living the life I could not, dying the death I deserve, walking out of the grave. I cannot see you, but I love you, trust you, and wait for you.
