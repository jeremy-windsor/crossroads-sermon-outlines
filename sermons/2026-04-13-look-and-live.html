Overview

  1. 00:36 Are you strapped in

    • Josh greets the church and states the series goal: opportunities to evaluate whether you are truly born again.
    • He tells of meeting Kristen’s dad at Six Flags. They ride a warm-up coaster, then X2: seats that roll you face-down at the top before a drop around sixty miles an hour. Nolan’s trust, Madison loving it, Kristen not so much, father-in-law done for the day.
    • The rest of the day he was ultra focused on seat belts. He now understands why they check harnesses. He feels like a glorified roller-coaster monitor, concerned some think they are strapped in when they are not.

    1. 03:25 Examine whether you are born again

      Scripture: John 15:4-5 John 3:3

      • Be diligent that you are actually connected to the vine and not something else. Many who think they are born again are not. We must examine ourselves. Josh sees it as his job to give those opportunities.
      • Whatever the background, Jesus says no one can see the kingdom of God without being born again. How do you see the kingdom? You must be born again—born from above, born of the Spirit, born of God.

    2. 04:15 The language of new birth

      Scripture: Ezekiel 36:26 2 Corinthians 5:17 Deuteronomy 30:6 Romans 8:15

      • The theological term is regeneration: made alive, new creation, new self, new heart of flesh, circumcision of the heart, renewed mind, adopted, child of God—from orphan to family.
      • If you have been born again there is visible change: repentance, turning from sin to God. Other words: washed, cleansed, purified. Legal language: justification, a divine pardon better than a presidential one—just as if I’d never sinned.
      • Marital language: Jesus the bridegroom, the church the bride, union with Christ, indwelt by the Spirit. Freedom language: liberated from sin, delivered from darkness, slaves now free, citizens of heaven, in covenant, set apart toward holiness.
      • That is why three weeks for two words. To be born again is a God thing: recreating, adopting, cleansing, awakening, from death to life, darkness into light.

    3. 06:56 Not water, not self-improvement

      Scripture: John 3:5-8

      • Regeneration does not come from water. You can sprinkle a baby and check a box; that does not explain the man twenty years later drunk, violent, beating his wife, blaspheming God. If that is new birth, the gospel means nothing and holiness does not matter.
      • It is not something done to you only externally, and you cannot do it within yourself. It is the result of the initiating love of God—next week’s whole sermon.
      • You can clean up, quit habits, look moral and religious. You cannot make yourself alive. Dead people do not resurrect themselves. Jesus said you must be born of the Spirit—not improved, managed, or rebranded. The Spirit takes a dead heart and makes it live, a hard heart sensitive, a stubborn will bent to his. The one who ignored God runs to him; the one who loved sin hates it; the one who resisted Christ clings to him.
      • Not behavior modification: heart transformation. Not external religion: internal resurrection. You do not upgrade the old life. God gives a new one. Unless a man is born again, he will never see the kingdom of God.

  2. 08:47 John’s two vantage points

    Scripture: John 2:1-11

    • John is Josh’s favorite human biblical author: Gospel, 1–3 John, Revelation. The Spirit worked in John to show the Christ who comes, changes, and conquers.
    • Josh’s view, which some Christians disagree with: the Gospel writes from earth’s perspective, Revelation from heaven’s, the epistles combine both.
    • Earth misses the Lamb; heaven worships him. Earth: water to wine at a wedding (last week); heaven: marriage supper of the Lamb. Earth: temple cleansed; heaven: God is the temple. Same events first as shadow, then in the full light of eternity. John did not change the message; he offered a new perspective. Earth misses her King; heaven cannot take her eyes off him.

    1. 10:59 Nicodemus at night

      Scripture: John 3:1-2

      • John 3:1: a Pharisee named Nicodemus, member of the Jewish ruling council, comes at night. Rabbi, we know you are a teacher come from God; no one could perform these signs if God were not with him.
      • Pharisees are the religious Navy Seals—best of the best in law, memorization, feasts, Sabbaths, tithing, plus add-ons. Nicodemus is a teacher of teachers, Seal Team Six, Israel’s Ivy League. Jesus is not a Pharisee and had no formal education. He is the Son of God.

    2. 12:22 Nicodemus and the Nicolaitans

      Scripture: Revelation 2:15-16

      • One of a dozen parallels Josh sees between John and Revelation: those who hold the teaching of the Nicolaitans—repent, or I will fight them with the sword of my mouth.
      • Nicodemus and Nicolaitan are virtually identical in Greek: the people’s victory. If Nicodemus represents the Pharisees, note the Lord’s hatred of that teaching.
      • The born-again Christian’s anthem is victory to God even at my expense. Pharisees and Nicolaitans are about the victory of the people. Two extremes: Pharisees legalism, adding rules, making grace too expensive; Nicolaitans license, ignoring rules, making grace too cheap. One says do more so God will accept you; the other says do whatever you want because he already has. Both wrong. The gospel: you are accepted in Christ; now walk by the Spirit.
      • Pharisees stand outside the temple and say clean yourself before you come in. Nicolaitans stand inside and say it does not matter how you live once you are in. One locks the door from outside; the other defiles the house from within. Christ, the true temple, does neither: he cleanses you to bring you in, then dwells in you to make you holy.

    3. 14:44 Why night, and accessible shepherds

      Scripture: John 19:38-42 John 2:23

      • The ruling council is seventy of the most devoted men in Israel. Nicodemus has status, wealth, education, community involvement. When it was time to bury Jesus he arrived with Joseph of Arimathea with wrappings and spices.
      • Josh does not know why he came at night. Many say he did not want to be seen; Josh is not sure people of that stature think that way. Maybe it was the only time to get Jesus alone. John 2: crowds after the signs were relentless.
      • Reminder for pastors and elders: some of the best ministry happens outside the walls in the evening, with home and marriage first. Isolated pastors are a disaster. Crossroads is messy and imperfect; what you cannot say is that they do not care. Cards are on the white table.

  3. 17:47 You must be born again

    Scripture: John 3:3-4

    • Nicodemus is ready for class: lay it on me, teacher. Jesus: no one can see the kingdom without being born again. Nicodemus: how can anyone be born when they are old? Enter a second time into the mother’s womb?
    • Josh compares it to walking into a class and getting a haymaker. His comms 101 professor wrote Jesus is a pacifist; Josh wanted to fight. The prof was illustrating overreaction.

    1. 19:04 Born of water and the Spirit

      Scripture: John 3:5-6 John 3:3

      • No one can enter the kingdom without being born of water and the Spirit. Flesh gives birth to flesh; the Spirit gives birth to spirit.
      • The word translated again also means from above—and it is the series title. Every human is born naturally from a woman. To go to heaven you must experience a supernatural birth from above. You were born physically alive and spiritually dead.

    2. 20:40 Dead in sin, not all are children

      Scripture: Psalm 51:5 Ephesians 2:1

      • Psalm 51: sinful from the womb. You did not merely become a sinner; you came that way. Cute little sinners, alive in body, dead to God. Ephesians: dead in trespasses and sins—not sick, dead.
      • Hard to hear. It confronts the idea that we are basically good, and it explains why we drift, resist, and find sin natural. Being born into this world does not mean you belong to the kingdom.
      • Wednesday night with the junior-high boys: are all people children of God? They said yes. Everyone is an image bearer with dignity because God made them and sent his Son—but not all are adopted children. You must be born again.
      • Second birth is not self-improvement, a new leaf, obedience, or enlightenment. It is a miracle. If you are a Christian, you did not tweak your life; you were resurrected. Dead to God, alive to sin; now alive to God, learning to die to sin. Enemy to child. That is not religion. God came after you, opened eyes and ears, softened the heart, showed you Christ, gave you faith, moved in. New creation. New name, new heart, new Father, new family. You are no longer defined by the first birth.

    3. 24:15 Not your parents’ faith

      Scripture: Acts 9:1-9 Matthew 3:7-9

      • Jesus is telling Nicodemus that a nice Jewish family does not make you part of the kingdom. Paul’s parents were Pharisees; Damascus made him a child of God. Josh’s given name is Joshua—salvation—but a Christian home is a testimony to the parents’ faith, not yours.
      • John the Baptist to Pharisees and Sadducees: brood of vipers, produce fruit in keeping with repentance, do not say we have Abraham as our father. God can raise children for Abraham from these stones. Are you going to say that because you were born to a certain family you do not need to repent?

    4. 26:41 See and enter—heaven is a Person

      Scripture: John 3:3 Matthew 18:20 John 14:3

      • Verse 3 and 5 side by side: you cannot see the kingdom and you cannot enter unless born of the Spirit. Not only future; you will not see it now. You need birth from above even to want heaven.
      • Heaven is not streets of gold as the point. Heaven is where God is loved, Christ treasured, worship never stops. If your heart has not been changed you would not enjoy it. You would walk around like Judas calling the gold wasteful. If worship feels long now, what would you do with eternity? Where two or three are gathered, he is here. If his presence feels insufficient now, how would you handle it forever?
      • An unrenewed heart is out of place there. Sin does not get smuggled into eternity. It may get through church doors now; it will not get through the gates. Unless you are born again you cannot see the kingdom—not might not: cannot. Without it you are incompatible with heaven.
      • A dying Christian told a skeptic: if I wake up in hell, the Lord promised that where he is I will be. I will throw my arms around him, and that will be heaven. Josh believes heaven and hell are literal; what makes heaven is a Person, and hell the absence of that Person.

    5. 31:25 Not water baptism

      Scripture: Romans 6:3-4 Luke 23:42-43 John 14:6

      • Jesus is not teaching in John 3 that new birth comes through water baptism. In the New Testament baptism is connected with death, not birth. No amount of physical water effects spiritual change. Dunking someone until they are sure about Jesus is not the test.
      • The evidence of salvation is the witness of the Spirit within, and the Spirit enters when you believe. Baptism is an outward sign and seal of inward grace. Josh recommends the Westminster Confession. Water baptism is obedience, witness, union with Christ and the church—not essential for salvation, or no Old Testament saint is saved, nor the thief on the cross. Make baptism necessary and you call Jesus a liar.
      • The church’s personality is maverick: edgy, truth in love, not afraid to say it. There is only one way: trust Jesus. No one comes to the Father except through him. Grace is offered only through faith in Christ.
      • As a child inherits the parents’ nature, a child of God becomes like the Father. Participating in the divine nature means going from Pornhub to ancient scrolls, addiction to adoration, substances to surrender—not I will work on that in a few weeks, but growing not even to want it.

  4. 35:08 The wind blows where it pleases

    Scripture: John 3:7-8

    • You should not be surprised I say you must be born again. The wind blows wherever it pleases. You hear it but cannot tell where it comes from or where it is going. So it is with everyone born of the Spirit.
    • Why say Nicodemus should not be surprised unless Jesus saw a bewildered face? Nicodemus was born a Jew, covenant people, better than a Roman birth in his own mind.
    • You cannot see wind, but you know when it shows up. Gentle on a pool or a force that moves houses. You do not control it, schedule it, or manufacture it. That is how new birth works. You do not clean yourself and then ask God in. The Spirit shows up and things start moving out.

    1. 37:31 Let the holy wind blow

      • Josh calls for thanks: that wind blew serious sin out of his life and put energy in his sails to be the man of God he never thought he could be.
      • Some try to control this wind and never will. The Spirit does not ask permission any more than a monsoon. When he moves, dead things come to life. People get saved when this wind blows.

    2. 38:53 How can this be

      Scripture: John 3:9-12 John 3:13 Daniel 7:13-14

      • Nicodemus: how can this be? You are Israel’s teacher and you do not understand? We speak of what we know. Josh assumes the Godhead and John the Baptist. Earthly things you do not believe; how then heavenly things?
      • Nicodemus lived where you obey, synagogue, tuck your shirt in, give, memorize, then God loves you—you do yours and God makes up the difference. Shout-out to the Mormons. That is where many trip. You mean God saves, heals, teaches, transforms—what do I do? God is worthy of all the glory, not me.
      • Jesus: don’t you get paid to teach? What good is all your Old Testament knowledge—law, history, wisdom, prophets—if you reject the testimony of the one those books prophesy about?
      • No one has gone into heaven except the one who came from heaven, the Son of Man—a title from Daniel, Messiah and God. I came from heaven. I am that I am. I and the Father are one. You have heard it said; I tell you the truth. You will never see it unless you are born again. Trust my testimony.

    3. 42:44 Are we that different

      Scripture: Matthew 28:20

      • Modern church people judge Nicodemus. Take a humble pill. Do you really believe the testimony of the one who came from heaven?
      • Nicodemus starts confused, then confronts a truth that feels too good to be true. We are not that different. He says you are forgiven; we rehearse the past. Loved; we live like we have to earn it. Clean; we carry shame. Born again; we keep the old name. Children; we act like orphans. Free; we wear chains. Do not be anxious; we never rest. I am with you always; we act alone. Do not be afraid; we walk in fear. My grace is sufficient; we keep proving we are enough.
      • It is not just understanding Jesus. It is trusting him enough to let his words define our worlds. Faith is not only believing he is telling the truth; it is living like he is.

  5. 44:58 Look and live

    Scripture: John 3:14-15 Ezekiel 37:1-14 Numbers 21:4-9

    • The gospel according to Jesus, verse 14: as Moses lifted up the snake in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up, that everyone who believes may have eternal life in him.
    • Nicodemus would have thought of Ezekiel 37 when Jesus talked about the wind—the recent Ezekiel series. This story is Numbers 21: rebellion, fiery serpents, many died. Some complaining Christians should thank God they did not grow up in the fiery-serpent generation.
    • Moses interceded; God provided a brass serpent on a pole; anyone bitten who looked was healed. A story of faith. They had to trust what God said. The verb lifted up means crucified and exalted. The cross was not the end of his glory; it was the path into it.

    1. 47:38 The whole world has been bitten

      Scripture: Romans 6:23 Isaiah 45:22

      • The solution in the camp was not killing the serpents or mixing antivenom. It was looking by faith at the uplifted serpent.
      • The whole world has been bitten by sin, and the wages of sin is death. God sent his Son to die not only for Israel but for the world. How are you born from above? Look to Jesus and trust him.
      • Isaiah 45:22: turn to me and be saved, all you ends of the earth, for I am God and there is no other. A child can do it. Where has the serpent bitten you? Stop staring at the wound; look at the source of healing. Fix your eyes on Jesus.
      • Healing takes time, but it can begin now. Eternal life is a current reality, not only a future hope. No one comes to Jesus and then waits sixty years to live. God made a way because sin kills; he heals because bitterness and anger enslave. You have everything you need in the resurrected Christ. Look to Christ. Trust him again. Step into eternal life now.

    2. 51:19 Closing prayer

      Scripture: Galatians 6:2

      • Josh thanks the Father for blowing wind into his life, for brothers and sisters who celebrate the same wind, living Monday through Saturday with joyous salvation.
      • As they come to the table, evaluate yourselves. If we have the Spirit there will be convictions to work through. God drives different people toward holiness in unique ways.
      • Give patience to bear one another’s burdens, lock arms, and love as he loved us. We give our lives and this place to you. Amen.
