Overview

  1. 00:34 Without a King, and a content warning

    • Last week the church launched its current series, Without a King. Josh warns that today’s text feels much like Grand Theft Auto without the language: adult content. His 11-year-old son is in the room, so parents should use their discretion; the kids ministry is ready to tell children about Jesus.

  2. 01:14 Why the Lord is angry: Baal and Ashtoreth

    Scripture: Judges 2:11–13 Genesis 1–2

    • Things were not going well in Israel at the end of last week’s passage: the Israelites forsook the Lord, served the Baals, and aroused his anger.
    • Canaanite theology differs sharply from Christian theology. A Christian reads Genesis 1 and 2 and understands sex is God’s gift to be shared between a man and a woman in marriage, not a divine act we mimic. God sits on his throne acting in history; he isn’t in some cosmic bedroom with a goddess bride. We are made in his image, but we are not like him and he is not like us.
    • Baal was the god of storm and fertility, of crops, livestock, and family, with a female counterpart, Ashtoreth. Canaanites believed the land’s fertility depended on the sexual relationship between Baal and Ashtoreth, and they didn’t leave it to Baal: they tried to spur him on with prostitution as worship at the Baal shrines, the very places Yahweh wanted Israel to break down. In paganism the gods must be spurred forward; in biblical faith we are taught to trust God.

  3. 04:06 Israel fell for it

    Scripture: Psalm 106:38–39

    • It isn’t hard to see how Israel was pulled away: “Come to our shrine this weekend; your wife should stay home.” Worst of all, they knew Yahweh was the one true God who provides and sends blessing, yet they didn’t drive out the pagans; they married them. They didn’t destroy the altars; they participated. They prostituted themselves, defiled themselves, and shed innocent blood by offering their sons and daughters to the idols of Canaan.
    • That is why the Lord is angry. He told Moses what to avoid, and they not only failed to avoid it but subjected their children to it.

  4. 05:15 Healthy churches push back on godless culture

    Scripture: Romans 12:2

    • God hasn’t tasked us with driving out a wicked nation, but the theme still applies: we can live at peace with the world while maintaining a healthy opposition to the culture, pushing back against godlessness and replacing it with holiness wherever possible.
    • Healthy churches aren’t measured by members, campuses, or services, but by Christians who live godly lives, not just say godly things or look Christian, and who are bold enough to speak up when godless culture creeps into the body. They can identify unhealthy, ungodly trends on their own and course correct before our children and grandchildren pay the price.
    • Josh prays for believers who take Romans 12 to heart, who don’t just memorize or quote it but look inside their lives for possible conformities and rebuke them in the name of Jesus before they become a problem.

  5. 06:44 Judges 2:16–23: notice his love

    Scripture: Judges 2:16–23

    • The Lord’s anger is obvious, but Josh wants the church to notice his love. He reads a large chunk he considers one of the most important for understanding the time of the judges: the Lord raised up judges who saved them, yet when each judge died the people returned to ways even more corrupt, so the Lord left the nations to test Israel.

    1. 08:22 The Lord’s patience and discipline

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:6 Lamentations 3:22

      • Josh can’t help but see the Lord’s patience. They kind of failed the test, yet God keeps testing; three times it says Yahweh tested them, to see whether his ways still mattered to them.
      • Watching the Lord’s anger carried out in controlled, measured discipline time and again is the most comforting thing to a believer who knows how messy they are. We can only join Jeremiah: because of the Lord’s great love we are not consumed. God has great compassion for his people, but he is angry at their sins.
      • Israel’s cycle is disobedience, discipline, despair, and deliverance, and God’s people can still find themselves in it today when they turn from his word and go their own way.

    2. 09:51 Our view of sin matters

      Scripture: Romans 3:9 1 John 3:8

      • Before the first three judges, one more item that may come up five or six more times in the series: why, when a judge died, did they act even worse than their fathers? Why couldn’t they break the pattern?
      • Sin is not just something you do or fail to do. Sin is a power pinning you to the ground. Paul says Jews and Gentiles alike, whatever your background, are all under the power of sin. That’s why they keep going back: it has a grip on them.
      • So Christianity isn’t behavior modification, life hacking, or self-help. It is faith in a divine sacrifice, a glorious exchange where Christ places himself under the power of sin where you were, lifts you out, takes your place, and delivers you from Satan’s grip. The Son of God appeared to destroy the devil’s work, and the judges were raised up for the same reason, bringing peace, though not the lasting peace we know this side of the empty tomb.

  6. 11:58 Othniel: Israel cries out

    Scripture: Judges 3:7–8 Judges 3:9

    • Chapter 3 begins as chapter 2 ends: Israel did evil, served the Baals and the Asherahs, and the Lord sold them into the hands of Cushan-Rishathaim, king of Aram Naharaim, for eight years. Josh is pleased he got the name out. But when they cried out, the Lord raised up a deliverer, Othniel, Caleb’s younger brother.
    • They did evil, but when they cried out he heard their cry, which reminds Josh of a favorite worship song: I sought the Lord, and he heard and he answered; that’s why I trust him.

    1. 12:56 Anger is better than silence

      • Yes, the Lord is angry, but this is good. Josh will take the Lord’s anger over his silence all day; it means there is still hope for Israel. He is angry because of his jealous love.
      • If Josh’s wife cheated on him and divorced him, and he showed up the next week with arm candy, you should have concerns about what that says about his love for her. God refuses to let go of those he loves or let them stay snuggled up to their sin. They didn’t want to serve Yahweh? Fine: here is the pagan king of Mesopotamia. You reap what you sow.

    2. 13:57 Cushan of double wickedness: a mini exodus

      Scripture: Exodus 2:24

      • The king’s name literally reads “Cushan of double wickedness,” surely a title earned by a reputation of oppression, not self-assigned like Cyrus the Great. It was eight years before Israel cried out.
      • Think of it as a mini exodus: God heard their groaning and remembered his covenant. He didn’t have amnesia; he was concerned for his people, the discipline had reached its fullness, and it was time to deliver.

    3. 14:35 The Spirit of the Lord came on him

      Scripture: Judges 3:10

      • The deliverer God raised up was Othniel, who became Israel’s judge and overpowered the king of Aram.
      • We don’t know much about Othniel, but he and his wife came from a family of courageous faith and strong leadership, and he took on a formidable enemy depending on God for victory. In both Testaments God loves to use not just individuals but households of faith.

    4. 15:27 Spirit-filled households, not seminary degrees

      • There’s so much more to leading in the faith than a seminary degree. Josh doesn’t have one; he went to business school and didn’t plan on ministry, but God didn’t care. He isn’t knocking smart people, but the church doesn’t need intelligent leaders or strong communicators so much as Spirit-filled leaders and godly households to look up to.
      • Paul doesn’t close his ministry telling Timothy to find smart men who know business, land purchases, and LLCs. He says put men in place who are serious about Jesus not just in the streets but at the dinner table. We have enough Sabbath warriors; God’s kingdom needs everyday warriors.

    5. 16:37 Never underestimate one Spirit-filled person

      Scripture: Judges 3:11

      • Some don’t like how little detail there is about the first judge and try to jazz it up. Josh sees no need; the simplicity makes it easy to see what God is doing when he gives a man his Spirit and a man runs after God’s will with God’s power.
      • A strong, compelling character can infatuate us so that we miss what God is doing. Othniel reminds us never to underestimate the impact of one person walking by the Spirit. God doesn’t need an army, just one Spirit-filled man or woman.

  7. 17:46 Ehud: the cycle continues

    Scripture: Judges 3:12–15

    • Israel did evil again, and the Lord gave Eglon, king of Moab, power over them; with the Ammonites and Amalekites he took the City of Palms, Jericho, and Israel served him 18 years. Again they cried out, and the Lord gave them Ehud, a left-handed Benjamite, sent with the tribute to Eglon.

    1. 18:19 A word for the lefties

      Scripture: Judges 20

      • Josh grew up with a lefty dad who made it sound awesome, but he knew better: it’s a right-handed world. The graphite smear of shame, the scissors of doom, three-ring binders, microwave doors, the special seat at Thanksgiving, right-handed manual can openers.
      • There are advantages: one study says left-handers are more likely to be geniuses with an IQ over 140, evidently see underwater better, and are rare enough to have an edge in tennis, boxing, fencing, and baseball.
      • The ancient world forced left-handed people to switch. Today lefties are about 10% of the population; back then perhaps 2%. Yet Judges 20 tells of 700 left-handed Benjamite soldiers, expert marksmen with a sling. Only God can do that.

    2. 21:17 A secret message for the king

      Scripture: Judges 3:16–19 Judges 3:20–21

      • Ehud strapped a double-edged sword, 15 to 16 inches long, to his right thigh and presented the tribute to Eglon, “a very fat man,” a detail that matters. Then he returned alone: “Your majesty, I have a secret message for you.”
      • The Star Wars fan in Josh pictures Jabba the Hutt and Han Solo at Docking Bay 94, a scene not in the original 1977 release of A New Hope. Eglon is the Jabba of Jericho.
      • Ehud said, “I have a message from God for you,” reached with his left hand, and plunged the sword into the king’s belly. Eglon never saw it coming and would never have taken Ehud seriously; even if the guards patted him down, they would have checked his left thigh, because everyone was right-handed by birth or by force.

    3. 23:06 Sometimes the message is death

      Scripture: Judges 3:22

      • Only God can do this. God’s messages come from unexpected places at unpredictable times, and sometimes the message is death. The death of an oppressor means freedom for the oppressed: you got fat on the backs of my people, and I’ve heard their cry.
      • It’s nasty and gross, Josh admits, but don’t act like you haven’t streamed worse into your living room on Netflix.

    4. 24:00 Waiting to the point of embarrassment

      Scripture: Judges 3:23–25

      • Ehud locked the doors and left. The servants assumed the king was relieving himself and waited to the point of embarrassment. Josh asks the church not to read this like an American in 2025 but like an Israelite who paid tribute to this gluttonous king for 18 years. They got a kick out of it: the guards pacing, thinking he’s on the throne, “not that throne.” How long is too long? One YouTube video, two? This had to be a favorite story to tell the grandchildren.
      • Finally they unlocked the doors and found their lord fallen to the floor dead, while Ehud got away.

  8. 25:11 Does God have a sense of humor?

    Scripture: Psalm 2:4

    • Yes. God’s humor is reflected in Scripture, creation, and human nature. In Psalm 2 the one enthroned in heaven laughs: a divine mockery of human arrogance, pride, and rebellion.
    • Jesus uses exaggeration about rightly judging others. The church screams “judge not,” but Jesus says judge rightly: why look at the speck in someone else’s eye and ignore the plank in your own? It’s holy humor to picture a guy with a 2x4 sticking out of his eye worried about your speck.
    • Elijah the prophet, not the worship pastor, taunted Baal’s worshipers that maybe Baal couldn’t burn the altar because he was using the bathroom or taking a nap. In creation, look at an axolotl. And there’s the gift of contagious human laughter.
    • God’s humor isn’t crude; it has a purpose. It humbles the proud and lifts the lowly, and it shows in his reversals: the last become first, the weak become strong, the blind see, the crucified Christ becomes the risen Lord. It is holy, redemptive, and filled with joy.

    1. 27:37 God doesn’t need a defense attorney

      • The Israelites weren’t embarrassed to tell this story, yet many Christians are ashamed of the Old Testament’s R-rated stories, and videos try to explain what God is doing. God doesn’t need a defense attorney. The message is clear: don’t mess with God’s people. Kings, take note of big man Eglon, or you might become the butt of one of God’s jokes.
      • After 18 years of hurting, Israel finally got a laugh and a story of how God delivered them again, even after they had done evil. Sharing and laughing about this story is a step above what they were doing when they worshiped with the Canaanites.

    2. 29:00 The Christian life isn’t boring

      Scripture: Job 1:21 Philippians 1:21

      • People think the Christian life is boring. Josh is having a fantastic time following Jesus. He has been praying that the Lord would restore the joy of his salvation and of his calling, and God is helping him despite some of the hardest years of his life. Since he took the lead pastor role in 2021, it has been one blow after another; maybe one day he can tell the story.
      • Walking it out, he is relearning that the Lord gives and the Lord takes; he came in with nothing and will leave the same. He has a divine optimism from the Holy Spirit that no circumstance could shake his faith to live as Christ and die as gain. He can’t lose; God has made it impossible for him to walk in defeat.
      • If you think the Christian life is boring, your eyes aren’t set on Jesus; you’ve gotten distracted. The Father loved Josh while he was still a sinner, then gave him power to beat the sin he was under: “Here’s my Spirit. Go bury that addiction.” When he failed again, the gospel hadn’t changed: “I love you still. Go bury it.” One day it was gone, not just the act but the desire, and he was like a child thrown in the air by his daddy: can we do that again? The rest of the world lives in chains, but the Lord is teaching his children to slip chains, all because of Jesus.
      • We all deserve Ehud’s left-handed secret message, yet because of God’s great love we got the gospel message from Jesus.

  9. 31:58 Salvation from unexpected sources

    Scripture: Judges 3:26–30 Isaiah 53:2

    • No Israelite would have looked at Ehud and said he would deliver them. Yet he blew a trumpet in the hill country of Ephraim, said, “Follow me, for the Lord has given Moab, your enemy, into your hands,” took the fords of the Jordan, struck down about 10,000 Moabites, and the land had peace for 80 years.
    • Ehud was an unlikely hero but not the last. No one thought God could use little shepherd boy David against Goliath; let’s look for his bigger brothers. No one thought anything good could come from Nazareth, and you wouldn’t have looked at Jesus and thought he was the Savior of the world.

    1. 33:26 Jesus handed himself over and killed death

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:55–57 1 Corinthians 1:23–24

      • Ehud says, “Follow me to Moab,” and Josh hears Jesus saying, “Come follow me, and I’ll make you fishers of men.” Except Jesus didn’t hand the enemy over to them; he handed himself over to the enemy. Moab looks more like a victory than Golgotha.
      • The Romans and the religious establishment thought he was dead and it was over. But when they rolled the stone in place, Jesus reached for a weapon no one thought he had and shoved that dagger into the heart of death. He killed death, walked out, and led his people to victory: where, O death, is your victory? Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.
      • The period of the judges shows God loves sending salvation from unexpected sources. Jesus’ victory was so surprising that Roman soldiers were in awe: surely this was the Son of God. So Paul preaches Christ crucified, a stumbling block and foolishness to some, but to those God has called, the power and wisdom of God.

    2. 35:12 Ehud couldn’t change their hearts

      Scripture: Revelation 1:5–6

      • Ehud’s story ends on a sad note; get used to that in this series. His encounter with the oversized king may have encouraged people for a while, but it couldn’t change their hearts. Still slaves to sin, they once again did evil.
      • That’s why, whether for rebellious Israelites 1,200 years before Jesus or carefully curated Christians 2,000 years after he was raised, it never gets old hearing about the one who purchased our freedom. No lefty with a dagger can free us from what held us captive, but one with nail marks has secured the victory. Yahweh has sent his Redeemer, Jesus, who freed us from our sins by his blood.

  10. 36:31 Don’t go back to business as usual

    Scripture: Mark 1:15 Romans 10:9

    • The only way this story ends badly for you is if you go on after this message with business as usual and back to your old ways. Your ways will never be enough; they are filthy rags.
    • The empty tomb proves there is life after death. Humanity has had plenty of time to drop Jesus’ body in the street to prove him a liar and never did. No body means eternity. It is either foolishness to you or the power and wisdom of God that saves.
    • He was patient then and is patient now, but a day is coming when the Lord will close all of time, and you don’t want to see him riding on the clouds unless your name is in the book of life. Repent and believe the good news. If you believe God raised Jesus from the dead and are ready to say out loud that you want him as Lord, the baptism waters are ready. What are you waiting for?

  11. 37:49 Shamgar: God uses unknowns

    Scripture: Judges 3:31

    • Some love Jesus but feel God can’t use them; the last verse is for them.
    • Scholars can string out one verse: was Shamgar named after the Canaanite goddess Anath, or just raised in Galilee? It doesn’t matter whether he was an Israelite; ask Cyrus if God can use anyone. Focus on what he did: he killed 600 bad guys with an eight-foot pole. He too saved Israel.
    • We know nothing about Shamgar, yet he saved God’s people. That is why Josh sings: his God uses unknowns with ox goads, and if he can use Shamgar, he can use Josh.

    1. 39:22 Use what you already have

      • Are you willing to be used by God and content with the gifts he has given you? If you don’t get a sword but an eight-foot ox goad, are you wielding it with courage and joy like it’s the best sword ever?
      • Many Christians are waiting for God to give them something when God is waiting for them to use what they already have. If nothing else, he has given you a story, a testimony. Are you using it, or afraid to go to battle with it?
      • Ask him for help and step out in faith: love your neighbor, serve in a ministry, meet a need through Crossroads Cares, volunteer at the care center, share Christ with a coworker or family member, start a Bible study with your kids. God doesn’t need you to be extraordinary, just willing. He uses lefties, oddballs, and anyone. If Judges 3 shows anything, it’s that all you need is his Spirit.

  12. 40:54 Closing prayer

    • Josh recalls the church singing, with the instruments turned down, “Come fill me again with your Spirit.” People aren’t killing people with eight-foot poles anymore, but temptations to look like, love, and befriend the world are alive, so he asks for the Spirit’s guidance, conviction, and discernment to know God’s voice from the deceiver’s.
    • He prays for just one person to say, “If God can use an unknown dude with an eight-foot pole, he can use me.” Not everyone is gifted to sing like Elijah and Michaela, but God has given different gifts to use together for his glory. He asks for one who has been masking a gift or holding back a story out of shame. We aren’t ashamed of where we came from: when Josh says he is an addict turned preacher, it is to God’s glory. Raise up more.
