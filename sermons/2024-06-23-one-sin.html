Overview

  1. 01:56 After Jericho: when overcoming makes you vulnerable

    Scripture: Joshua 7

    • Josh asks the church to turn to Joshua 7. God has given Joshua and Israel their first military victory at Jericho; morale is high, the reward is great, and Israel’s confidence is at an all-time high.
    • Resting in victory, we often become more confident in our own abilities. That isn’t always bad, but the stain of overconfidence can set in and marinate. When you are overcoming, you become more vulnerable to being overcome, even if you were handpicked by Jesus.

    1. 02:40 Peter on the hillside and in the upper room

      Scripture: Matthew 16:15–16 Matthew 26:35

      • Peter went on special missions with Jesus, saw Jairus’ daughter raised, and witnessed the transfiguration with Moses and Elijah. At Caesarea Philippi, when Jesus asked, “Who do you say I am?” God revealed to Peter, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God,” a confession every believer has echoed since.
      • But in the upper room Josh argues Peter fell into overconfidence. Instead of seeking God’s will, he boasted in his own: “I will never betray you.” Loyalty is a great trait, but when God hands his Son over to take on the sins of the world, your will doesn’t matter, and when God raises a three-day-old corpse, you can’t cover that up.
      • Peter is so relatable. Have you ever walked so closely with Jesus that people think you’re weird, talking to Jesus in your car or singing about him while walking the dog to the mailbox, so content that nothing could shake you, and then out of nowhere you crash and burn and never saw it coming?

    2. 04:51 The purple Honda Elite

      • When Josh was 14, his dad bought a motorcycle (picture his old man on a Harley with his NIV strapped to the back) and approved Josh’s first big purchase, a brand-new purple Honda Elite S. He picked up girls for dates and hit the McDonald’s dollar menu. As the baby of the family, his siblings said everything was handed to him; really he just watched them mess up and didn’t follow their example.
      • He rode it home, spit-shining it at every stop, and his two older sisters were waiting in the driveway, drowning in discontentment. Andrea, the oldest, asked to drive it and wanted none of his instructions: “I can drive a car. How hard can this be?” She headed down the hill, blew through the stop sign, hit the curb across the intersection, and was thrown into the neighbor’s yard; she couldn’t find the brake. She fell into overconfidence and crashed hard, and the moped never steered the same.
      • That is the lay of the land in the Christian life: sometimes we sit comfortably on top of the hill on a new purple moped, and sometimes we fly face-first into the blacktop, and it can happen fast.

  2. 07:28 A land of mountains and valleys

    Scripture: Deuteronomy 11:11

    • Moses describes the promised land in Deuteronomy 11 as a land of mountains and valleys that drinks rain from heaven. He is describing geography, but kings, prophets, apostles, theologians, and pastors have used that language for the life of faith: as we claim our inheritance in Christ, we experience mountains of encouragement and valleys of discouragement.
    • You aren’t on the mountain because of your great faith; God has you there and can move you down a peg anytime. You aren’t in the valley because you lack faith; you may be there because of sin, but you can be on a mountain with sin in your life too, which may be the worst place to be. Your faith is best described by how you respond to the mountains and valleys.
    • In Joshua 7 things are going to change: Joshua goes from the mountaintop at Jericho to the valley of defeat at Ai. Josh asks the church to watch him closely, learning where he comes up short and where he hits the mark, so they are prepared for discouragement or encouragement.

  3. 09:21 Verse 1: Achan takes the devoted things

    Scripture: Joshua 7:1 Joshua 6:17–18

    • Verse 1: the Israelites were unfaithful regarding the devoted things; Achan of the tribe of Judah took some, so the Lord’s anger burned against Israel. If you missed last week, you might call the Lord short-tempered, but he is patient: before the walls fell, in chapter 6, he told them the city was devoted to the Lord and warned them in the very next verse to keep away from the devoted things or bring destruction and trouble on the camp.
    • One disobedient soldier did not keep away. His name, Achan or Achar, literally means trouble, and he would forever be remembered as the man who brought trouble on Israel. Don’t underestimate how much damage one person committing one sin can do; that is all it takes.

    1. 10:50 The church is drifting on sin

      • Church culture is drifting further from a godly view of sin. Every other week we hear about another so-called pastor living in sin for decades, then the parade of press drops: “I’m sorry for what I did.” No, you’re sorry you got caught. The church knows the difference between remorse and self-pity. An apology means nothing unless you show it by killing that sin; you had decades to repent and waited until you got caught. Get out of the pulpit. Insincerity reveals unbelief.
      • Some will call this over the top, but we view sin one way and God views it completely differently. It has gotten so bad that church leaders are rebuked for calling out sin. Josh could share hundreds of emails tearing apart his character, heart, and salvation just for calling certain lifestyles sinful. He has come to terms with the fact that if he calls out someone actively living in sin, not lying to grandma but knowingly refusing to turn, they will find another church, pull their tithe if they give at all, and leave.

    2. 12:54 What true repentance looks like

      • Look at how God handles sin in the camp after Jericho and compare it with how most churches, pastors, and elders deal with it. Josh isn’t suggesting we start stoning people, but how can you be lazy with sin and serious about pursuing holiness? You can’t. True belief is connected to true repentance.
      • Here is how repentance goes: by God’s grace the believer recognizes their sin, sees themselves as an accomplice in Christ’s crucifixion, no different from the Roman soldier holding the hammer and nails, and feels deep sorrow. Hearing the gospel, they confess, and everyone can witness them break free. It may not be immediate or permanent, and they may struggle on and off, but it is a break from the desire for that sin, and over time a deep hatred for all sin, because it robs us of our intended relationship with our Creator.

    3. 14:38 Paul and the man in Corinth

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 5:1–2

      • That is why Paul told the Corinthians to discipline the disobedient man in their fellowship: one man’s sin was defiling the church. There is sexual immorality among you of a kind even pagans don’t tolerate: a man has his father’s wife, and you are proud. Shouldn’t you rather have mourned and put him out of your fellowship?
      • If that is how Paul dealt with incest in AD 50, how would he deal with LGBTQ+-affirming churches or abusive pastors in the pulpit today? Many of those pastors should be in prison, not on a restoration plan, yet they are let off and go plant a church somewhere else. Church leaders are failing because they are afraid to call out sin, but you can’t fear calling out sin and fear God at the same time.

    4. 16:04 God determines what sin is

      Scripture: John 5:22 Ephesians 2:1–2

      • You don’t determine what is sin; Almighty God does. You have zero input; your opinion and feelings don’t matter. “Well, Josh, I don’t think that’s a sin.” It isn’t up to you. Sin means to miss the mark.
      • Josh and his son play darts in the garage, and when his wife steps up, they get out the drywall patch. That is the picture of sin: God gives a beautiful playing surface and says enjoy creation, enjoy me, walk with me, just don’t toss darts on my drywall. Christians have thrown darts all over the walls and ceiling, missing entirely, while shouting “Bullseye!” You can have preferences, but a holy God reigns over creation and determines right and wrong, and judgment has been entrusted to the Son.
      • Ephesians 2 says you were dead in your transgressions and sins, following the ways of this world and the ruler of the kingdom of the air.

  4. 18:05 Achan coveted the old things

    • God is doing something amazing for his people in Canaan, not unlike what he does for his people now through the gospel. They have front-row IMAX seats on opening night to witness his wonders, and one guy decides he wants to keep some old relics. God is about to do a new thing, and Achan can’t stop coveting the old things. He saw it, liked it, and wanted it; he didn’t want to wait for the robe the Lord had for him, so he took the robe he wanted.

  5. 18:52 Defeat at Ai

    Scripture: Joshua 7:2–5

    • Joshua sends men to spy out Ai. They report that only two or three thousand men are needed, because only a few people live there. About 3,000 go up and are routed; the men of Ai kill about 36 and chase them down the slopes, and the hearts of the people melt in fear and become like water.
    • Joshua lost 36 men; 36 families didn’t welcome home a loved one. We can’t know the number, but think how many people have fled the church because of unchecked sin within it. The Lord knows exactly, he grieves it, and so should we.

    1. 20:10 Your sin impacts others

      • It blows Josh’s mind how patient God is as we dabble carelessly in sin. It is one thing to play fast and loose with your own heart, but don’t be a fool: your sin impacts others. Who have you hurt or driven out, running from the church to escape the thick cloud of your pride and hypocrisy? We are so careless with one another. Josh pleads with the church to stop making decisions before considering who they might drown in their wake.

  6. 20:57 Verse 6: take it up with God

    Scripture: Joshua 7:6–7

    • Joshua shows something important in verse 6. Many of us run to people before we stop to pray: “You won’t believe what happened; what should I do?” Many complain about God to others. Joshua sets the example: if you have a beef with God, take it up with him, even if it takes all evening, then move on. Complaining to God in prayer is very different from complaining about God to others, as though he doesn’t know.
    • Joshua tears his clothes and falls facedown before the ark until evening, with the elders putting dust on their heads: “Alas, Sovereign Lord, why did you ever bring this people across the Jordan to deliver us into the hands of the Amorites? If only we had been content to stay on the other side.”

    1. 22:16 The tables have turned

      • In week two of the series, Rahab told the spies Jericho was melting in fear. Now the tables have turned and Israel is melting in fear. Joshua had only known victory, from an upset at Jericho to a devastating loss at Ai, which should have been an easy win. On the heels of Jericho, he could have sent one platoon had they not sinned; instead they lost 36 men.

    2. 22:56 Faith looks the same on the mountain and in the valley

      Scripture: Joshua 7:8–9

      • Joshua blames God for the humiliating defeat. Josh doesn’t see this as a chance to shoot flaming arrows at Joshua or highlight the kink in his armor; he is human like us. He credits both the victory and the defeat to God, and faith looks the same on the mountains as in the valleys: “Thank you for everything you’ve given me, more than I deserve,” and “God, why did you take this from me?”
      • And he keeps his manners: “Pardon your servant, Lord.” The Canaanites will hear, surround us, and wipe out our name. “What then will you do for your own great name?” Josh loves this.

    3. 24:35 This chapter is about God’s view of sin

      Scripture: Joshua 7:8–9

      • Many commentators use chapter 7 to poke at Joshua, saying the defeat came from a lack of prayer, but verse 1 says it happened because Israel was unfaithful regarding the devoted things. Others applaud him for moving quickly. Everyone is all over the place because the chapter isn’t about Joshua’s leadership; it is about one man’s sin and, more importantly, God’s perspective on sin.
      • In verses 8 and 9 Joshua’s chief concern isn’t his record as a military leader or his next job application; it is God’s great name. That is true biblical leadership: godly men more concerned with God’s reputation and his church’s than their own or their church’s.

    4. 26:04 Who speaks into your life?

      • That isn’t what we see in the news. Men in church leadership are ditching foundational doctrines, affirming sin until they convince themselves they aren’t living in it, and handing their God-given role as leaders of the home and church to women because of social pressure.
      • If you get nothing else, write this down; Josh says it is more important for believers now than ever: don’t let anyone speak into your life unless the life-giving words of God are speaking into theirs. Be careful who you listen to.

  7. 27:11 “Israel has sinned”

    Scripture: Joshua 7:10–11 Joshua 6:19

    • Josh loves the Lord’s response: stand up; what are you doing on your face? Israel has sinned; they have violated my covenant, taken devoted things, stolen, lied, and put them with their own possessions. In other words, “Joshua, lay off. You’re talking like I sinned against you. You sinned against me.”
    • This is history Joshua is living: we read of the sin in verse 1, but Joshua didn’t know until God told him. Before the walls fell, God said the silver, gold, bronze, and iron were sacred and must go into his treasury. Sometimes soldiers could keep the spoils, but not this time; this was flat-out disobedience.

    1. 28:30 “I will not be with you anymore”

      Scripture: Joshua 7:12

      • That is why Israel can’t stand against its enemies: “I will not be with you anymore unless you destroy whatever among you is devoted to destruction.” No phrase should bring more fear into God’s people. Forget the embarrassment at Ai; can you imagine life without God’s goodness and presence? That should have us melting in fear.

    2. 29:16 One sinner destroys much good

      Scripture: Joshua 7:11 1 Corinthians 12:26–27 Galatians 6:9 Ecclesiastes 9:18

      • It was one guy, but they all suffered. God doesn’t call out Achan in verse 11; he says Israel has sinned, reminding us the sin of one affects many. In 1 Corinthians 12 Paul compares the church to a body: if one part suffers, every part suffers with it; you are the body of Christ. If you suffer, I suffer.
      • The opposite is true too: tolerated sin spreads and many suffer. The more we affirm sin when we should lovingly rebuke it, the worse things get; if we know about a couple living together unmarried, not calling it out invites more. Don’t grow weary of doing good. Ecclesiastes 9 says one sinner destroys much good; it isn’t an even trade. Sin kills the church’s effectiveness, and we must keep pushing back against it whatever the social pressure.

  8. 30:52 The search, tribe by tribe

    Scripture: Joshua 7:13–15

    • The Lord tells Joshua to consecrate the people, because there are devoted things among them and they can’t stand until they are removed. In the morning they will come forward tribe by tribe, clan by clan, family by family, man by man; “an intense church service.” Whoever is caught with the devoted things will be destroyed by fire with all that belongs to him.

    1. 31:44 There is nowhere to hide

      Scripture: Jeremiah 17:9–10 Joshua 7:16–21

      • When God conducts an investigation, there is nowhere to hide. Jeremiah says the heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure; who can understand it? Then he answers: “I the Lord search the heart and examine the mind, to reward each person according to their conduct.” You think you are burying your sin, but you are digging your own grave, and Achan is about to find out.
      • God singles out Judah, and Josh imagines Achan sweating as God zeros in on the Zerahites, then Zimri’s household. Joshua says, “My son, give glory to the Lord; tell me what you have done; don’t hide it.” Achan confesses: he saw a beautiful robe from Babylonia, 200 shekels of silver, and a 50-shekel bar of gold, coveted them, took them, and hid them in the ground inside his tent.

    2. 33:21 The sinner is responsible, not God

      Scripture: Genesis 50:20

      • Imagine the families of the 36 soldiers. No one would blame them for being angry when they learned Achan caused their loved ones’ deaths. Part of the problem today is that everyone blames God for the consequences of sin when the sinner is responsible.
      • Josh struggled with this for years; he was angry at God. It took a godly father willing to beat him over the head with the word of God for him to see that people of God living in sin were to blame for his pain, not God, and that while others intended to harm him, God intended it for good. People gazing at power and influence in his young life didn’t care who they hurt to get it.

    3. 34:33 Coveted, took, hid

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 1:7

      • This is an issue of self-control. Achan says, “I coveted, I took, I hid.” At any of those three stages he could have confessed and turned away, and he could have spoken up as the tribes, clans, and families were called out. But he didn’t care to confess until he had to.
      • If you see that you are coveting something you know you shouldn’t, don’t take it: turn your back, look away, walk away. If you took it and you are heading back to the tent, please don’t hide it; don’t pick up the shovel. Drop it where you are. You can still walk away.
      • People, even Christians, act like there’s no way out, but there is. In 2 Timothy Paul tells the young pastor that the Spirit God gave us doesn’t make us timid but gives us power, love, and self-discipline. If God’s Spirit lives in you, you have self-control. Use it. Don’t wait for God’s investigation or judgment; deal with it now.
      • This part is hard, but if people won’t recognize where they’ve missed the mark and won’t repent, as much as we love them, we must be willing to part ways. There comes a point when enough is enough.

  9. 36:54 The Valley of Achor

    Scripture: Joshua 7:22–25

    • Joshua’s messengers run to the tent and find it all hidden there, the silver underneath, and spread it before the Lord. Joshua and all Israel take Achan, the silver, robe, and gold, his sons and daughters, his animals, his tent, and all he had to the Valley of Achor. “Why have you brought this trouble on us? The Lord will bring trouble on you today.” All Israel stones him, and they burn them.
    • When you first hear this, it is disturbing, and Josh doesn’t blame anyone for struggling. Most of us have done far worse than Achan, yet for one sin he and his family are stoned and burned. “So, Josh, one guy takes a robe and some metal, and the result is 36 dead soldiers and a whole family stoned and burned?”

    1. 38:29 Achan’s family

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 24:16

      • First, the family. Deuteronomy 24:16 says parents are not to be put to death for their children nor children for their parents; each will die for their own sin. So Josh concludes Achan’s family must have been guilty of assisting him in his sin instead of helping him out of it.

    2. 38:46 Our view of sin is not God’s

      Scripture: Matthew 5:30

      • We struggle with this as we struggled last week with Jericho, and it is fair to ask whether it is just. But our view of sin isn’t God’s. Being sinners, we don’t think sinning against God is a big deal, so we don’t understand his wrath until we see people killed in Joshua 6 and 7. We get squeamish when Jesus tells us in Matthew 5 to cut off our hand and throw it away to avoid sin.
      • If Josh were your doctor and told you that you had cancer but not to worry, just take vitamins, you would think he was crazy; cancer requires hardcore drugs and often surgery. The message of Joshua 7 is to stop treating our sin problem with vitamin D. You need a scalpel. Cut it out and kill it.

    3. 40:00 “Shoot your laptop”

      Scripture: Matthew 16:24–25

      • A man recently asked Josh for help with his porn addiction, which Josh is thrilled about; working together to eliminate sin is God-honoring. Josh asked if he owned a gun (“It’s Arizona, everybody owns a gun”) and told him to shoot his laptop. “Right now you’re pointing a gun at your marriage. You’re going to kill one. Which will it be?”
      • When the man said he would get fired without a laptop, Josh said, “So what? You’ll break your covenant with God if you lose your marriage.” Don’t you think God can provide a new job when you kill sin in obedience? It might even be better. Find a job in an office surrounded by people, because working from home behind that door isn’t working.
      • Those who actually love Jesus don’t see, take, and hide. Jesus says whoever wants to be his disciple must deny themselves, take up their cross, and follow him; whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for him will find it. Stop spending so much time in the tent with hidden sin and a shovel, and start living in the open with our loving Savior.

    4. 42:05 God’s wrath is just

      Scripture: Genesis 2:17 Romans 5:18

      • If you are struggling with the severity of God’s wrath, Josh has been there. But did the families of those 36 men struggle with it that day? Not for a second: “You’re the reason my son didn’t come home.” Yahweh loves justice; he is just, right, and holy.
      • It is the same story as Eden: if you eat, you will die. Here is the line; cross it and there are consequences. We do this with our children and call it good parenting; God does it and we attack his character. “But it was just one sin.” That is all it takes. Romans 5 says one trespass resulted in condemnation for all people. How many sins have you committed? Josh can’t even list his; the pile is overwhelming. One sin warrants the swift and just wrath of God, which is right, good, and eternal.

  10. 43:29 From the valley of trouble to a door of hope

    Scripture: Joshua 7:26 Hosea 2:15 John 10:9

    • The best part: if no one has told you before, praise God you get to hear it today. God’s wrath is escapable; it is avoidable. In verse 26 they heap a large pile of rocks over Achan, which remains to this day, and the Lord turns from his fierce anger. The place is called the Valley of Achor, the valley of trouble, except now the trouble has been dealt with and they can march forward in victory.
    • In our sin we are in a valley of trouble, but because of the cross we can march onward to victory. In Hosea 2 God says he will make the Valley of Achor a door of hope. Our Redeemer has made a way from the valley of trouble to a door of hope, and in John 10 Jesus says, “I am the door; if anyone enters by me, he will be saved.” At any point in your journey through the valley, you can stop and enter through him.

    1. 44:54 Enter and don’t look back

      • Once you enter, don’t look back. There is nothing back there for you. No robe you can get for yourself comes close to the robe he has for you in eternity. Turn from your sin to Jesus; don’t pack a bag or hold a ceremony to reunite your new life with your old. Die to self.
      • The beauty of the gospel: the second you see your sin as he sees it, see his blood dripping into the dirt for your sins, see his resurrection as the first of many including yours, and name him Lord and Savior, you are immediately delivered from under the wrath of God. No stone pile for you, because those who believe Christ rose will be raised; he moved the stone so you don’t have to be buried under it. Repent and believe. Josh pleads with the church to trust Jesus and be saved; to God be the glory forever.

  11. 46:22 Closing prayer

    • Josh is shaking as he considers there may be souls in the room who haven’t turned to God, whose sin remains buried in a tent somewhere in Canaan. Beyond the odd arguments people find in Joshua 7, how can anyone read it faithfully and not ask, “What do I have buried that no one knows about, that I think I’m getting away with?” God knows the heart.
    • God sent his Son because nothing could be done about our hearts, but the blood of Jesus washes us clean, so despite our fallen flesh we can know and walk with him. Josh prays that those who haven’t heard the gospel, watching the baptisms and the Lord’s Supper in the next few minutes, would give their lives to God.
