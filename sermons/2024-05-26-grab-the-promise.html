Overview

  1. 00:02 Kicking off Courage Over Fear

    Scripture: Joshua 1

    • Josh kicks off a new seven-week series, Courage Over Fear, which will end in mid-July, and asks the church to open to Joshua 1, the sixth book in the Bible.
    • The series matters to him because he grew up in a country that didn’t require much courage to obey God. In his small town in Southern Indiana, being the son of a preacher didn’t take courage; being a Christian was arguably an advantage. There were still temptations, the parties and the girls, but the cultural pressures were different.

    1. 01:02 Peers up, mentors down

      • AOL Messenger came out when Josh was a freshman in high school, and the world has never been the same. School friendships followed him home, and he could stay connected day and night. The influence of peers skyrocketed, and the influence of mentors dropped, because mentors didn’t follow his generation online right away.
      • Social media has balanced out some; you can find strong Christian content on almost every platform, and his Instagram feed is sermon clips mixed with the occasional protein ice cream recipe. But the tribes remain: Facebook is the watering hole for Boomers and early Millennials, Instagram for late Millennials and Gen Z, and TikTok is mostly Gen Z.
      • Many dislike social media for taking away face-to-face conversation, and Josh agrees, but adds that it has kept us from engaging face to face with other generations. Most Americans lack the social skills to have a simple conversation with someone 30 years younger or older.

    2. 02:49 When the flow between generations breaks down

      • In the past we shared common values, goals, and interests and could move together. Now everyone perches in their own social nest and minds their own business, and Josh thinks it has been a massive progress killer in the country and in the local church.
      • The flow from generation to generation isn’t as smooth as it once was. Smartphones are one reason; he traces the most massive cultural change of all time back to Steve Jobs announcing the iPhone. Social media is another. And when there are no clear goals to address major problems, why would you need strong leaders? We elect people with the mindset, “Just don’t go screw it up.”
      • This country is amazing, unlike anywhere else in the world, and Josh argues that much of its progress came because we used to value strong leadership rooted in the healthy fear of God.

  2. 04:20 Great leaders are rare

    • More often than not, leaders are either far too passive or abusive, loving their ego more than their neighbor. And even a great leader can’t lead people who don’t want to be led.
    • Whatever your view of the country, Josh offers hope: at any moment everything can change with just one willing leader who seeks to walk in obedience to God’s word. However it goes, his hope is in Jesus, not a man.

    1. 05:13 What gives you hope?

      • That has become a difficult question to answer, even for the pope. If someone who claims to love Jesus and the church goes on 60 Minutes, is asked what gives him hope, and doesn’t take the opportunity to share the gospel, Josh says to run from every church under his authority.
      • Our hope has a name, and his name is Jesus. That shouldn’t be a burden to share; you should hesitate not to share it. We have no shortage of wimpy, puny, weak-sauce leaders.

  3. 06:02 A strong leader who adored God

    • In this series we get to see a strong leader who adored God. Joshua is a beautiful picture of what happens when a leader obeys God and the people he leads offer their loyalty in response to his obedience.
    • Josh wants the church to see a flow of encouragement: God encourages the leader, the leader encourages the people, and the people encourage the leader.

  4. 06:31 “Moses my servant is dead. Now then”

    Scripture: Joshua 1:1–2

    • Josh reads verse 1: after the death of Moses, the Lord tells Joshua to get ready, with all the people, to cross the Jordan into the land he is about to give them. He pauses on “Moses my servant is dead. Now then.”
    • If we’re honest, many of us would be offended. Someone as valuable as Moses, and God doesn’t command a beautiful arrangement or a well-orchestrated procession? There has never been anyone like Moses; surely they should stop and celebrate him.
    • Most people would respond that way, but great leaders don’t. They take a successful transition as the ultimate compliment, because everything they do is for the master, so they are uncomfortable when it is about them even for a moment. Leadership transitions are nearly impossible when the focus has been on the servant and not the master, and there is no wisdom in transitioning leadership overnight.

  5. 08:06 Joshua was led before he led

    Scripture: Exodus 17 Exodus 24:13 Exodus 32:17–19

    • Joshua excelled at being led before he became a leader. He served as Moses’ right-hand man, as a military and a spiritual leader, and he is one of only two adults who experienced Egyptian slavery and lived to enter the promised land.
    • In Exodus 17 Moses sent him into battle against the Amalekites. In Exodus 24 he went with Moses up the mountain of God, the first time he is called Moses’ assistant. He was there for the golden calf in Exodus 32, when he heard the noise in the camp and Moses said it was the sound of singing, then broke the tablets at the foot of the mountain. Joshua sat at the foot of strong leadership through tough times.

    1. 09:17 He loved God’s presence

      Scripture: Exodus 33:11

      • Josh’s favorite thing about Joshua is that he loved to be in the presence of Yahweh; he was in awe and adored God. In Exodus 33 the Lord would speak to Moses face to face as one speaks to a friend; Moses would return to camp, but young Joshua did not leave the tent.
      • He would spend time meditating on Yahweh and waiting on the Lord. The lesson for everyone, Christian leader or not: schedule meetings with the Lord.

    2. 10:04 The spies and the commission

      Scripture: Numbers 13 Numbers 14:30 Numbers 27:22–23

      • In Numbers 13 Joshua, from the tribe of Ephraim, is one of the twelve spies. All twelve said the land was good, but ten doubted Israel could take it. Only Joshua and Caleb urged the people to move forward and grab the land Yahweh promised. For everyone’s lack of faith, that generation would die in the wilderness, wandering forty years, one for each day spent spying.
      • God answered Joshua and Caleb’s faithfulness in Numbers 14: none would enter the land except Caleb and Joshua. Thirteen chapters later, in Numbers 27, Moses publicly commissions Joshua before Eleazar the priest and the whole assembly.

  6. 11:26 Faithfulness on display

    Scripture: Philippians 3:14

    • How you endure trials determines how others experience your faith; your faithfulness is a display of your faith. Joshua was not an overnight decision or the next hot up-and-coming leader. He had been faithful all along: he watched Moses face Pharaoh, drive his staff into the Red Sea, and crush idols.
    • Josh imagines Moses telling him about doubting his abilities at the burning bush, sitting him down in the tent where he spoke face to face with God and saying, “It’s not about you or what you can do. It’s about what you will allow God to do through you. Obey him, trust him, submit to him. I will die someday, but God’s promise lives on.”
    • Moses did die, but Yahweh didn’t go anywhere. They still had God’s promise, presence, word, and people, and that was enough to press on to the prize. The church still has all of these: the promise of eternal life, the presence of his Spirit, his word, and his people. Stop weeping and whining about what you don’t have; God is faithful and enough. “I press on toward the goal to win the prize.”

    1. 13:21 Will we stay the path?

      • Joshua faces the question every generation faces: will we stay the path and press on toward the goal? The church faces the same question as generations get further removed from the eyewitnesses of the resurrected Lord. Will we cling to the promises they told us about, or take our eyes off the prize?

  7. 13:59 Verse 3: every place you set your foot

    Scripture: Joshua 1:3 Genesis 12 Genesis 50:24 Deuteronomy 29:29 Matthew 6:10

    • God chose Joshua, and everyone knew he was the new leader. Saying goodbye to faithful kingdom workers doesn’t mean kingdom work stops. This generational promise traces back to Abraham, and the connections between Genesis 12 and Joshua 1 can’t be ignored. Josh loves how Joseph put it at the end of his life in Genesis 50: God will surely come to your aid and take you to the land he promised Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
    • This is who we are: lovers of God cling to his promises. Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, and Moses didn’t know how God would do it. God’s people don’t wait for explanations; they move forward on his promises.
    • Big leaders are dying, big rivers are raging, and armies are developing, but we trust God will do it because he said he would. “Not this church.” The secret things belong to God, the revealed things belong to us and our children; his will be done on earth as it is in heaven, and we will keep our eyes fixed on heavenly things.

    1. 15:59 Not name it and claim it

      • God is telling Joshua, “If you’re willing to go there, I’ll give it to you.” That isn’t the name-it-and-claim-it teaching, as though your desires had power over God’s sovereignty. That false teaching elevates man and his faith above God.
      • God will only give it if you are willing to go. You can stay on this side of the Jordan, hold worship services, and break into life groups to talk about how awesome God is, or you can cross the river and take the land he promised. What is it going to be?

  8. 16:57 Three tasks, three promises

    Scripture: Joshua 1:4–5

    • Joshua is given three tasks that the series will uncover: cross the river, defeat the enemy, divide the land. God encourages his new leader with a promise for each. The first is that everywhere his feet go is his.
    • The second: the territory will stretch from the desert to Lebanon and from the Euphrates to the Mediterranean, and no one will be able to stand against him all the days of his life. Joshua had been at Kadesh Barnea and saw what happened when the people hesitated at the first sign of resistance. He knew God had already given them the land; it was their responsibility to go and grab it.
    • It must have been tempting to look across the Jordan and think, “This is the land of the Canaanites and the Hittites and all the other ‘ites.’” But God says he has been setting it up for them the whole time: the fields are prepped, the wells are dug, and their enemies are working the ground for them. If they stay in Egypt or hesitate in the desert, they will never see what he has for them. So cross.

    1. 18:52 The third promise: “I will be with you”

      Scripture: Joshua 1:5 Matthew 28:20

      • As God was with Moses, so he will be with Joshua; he will never leave him nor forsake him. That is the third promise, and it is the same promise Jesus gave when he commissioned the church to go make disciples: “I will be with you always.”

  9. 19:18 Be strong and courageous

    Scripture: Joshua 1:6–7

    • Don’t be scared. Josh reads God’s charge to be strong and courageous, to lead the people to inherit the land, and to be careful to obey all the law without turning right or left. Three times in this chapter God says it.
    • God acknowledges that the enemies are big and scary with home-field advantage, and not to underestimate them, but not to underestimate him either: “I am that I am.” When he makes promises, even what others intend for evil he will use for good; he will weave his will through any circumstance. Do you trust me?

    1. 20:21 Keep the book of the law on your lips

      Scripture: Joshua 1:8

      • Keep this book of the law always on your lips and meditate on it day and night. God says, in effect, stay in your lane: let me worry about how this goes; you focus on staying close. You were Moses’ right-hand man; now you are mine. Don’t veer left or right, don’t add or take away, just do as it says.
      • Don’t give God all the reasons it can’t work; meditate on his words, his promises, and what he has said is true. There will be challenges, but “I’m Yahweh, I’m simply too much for them.” You might not have the numbers, the talent, or the strength, and it may look like you are about to fail, but lead not according to what you see but according to what God has told you is true.

  10. 21:42 What success means in God’s kingdom

    Scripture: Joshua 23:6

    • Josh stops to define prosperity and success in the Christian life. He fast-forwards to Joshua’s final address before he dies at 110: be very strong, be careful to obey all that is written in the book of the law of Moses, without turning to the right or the left. It should sound familiar; it is what God told Joshua before the conquest began.
    • That is success in God’s kingdom: from the start of the conquest to 110, keep the faith and keep his words on your lips. Joshua not only kept the faith but built the faith of those around him; you couldn’t be around him without feeling part of something big.
    • Build your 401k if you want; saving and caring for your family matters. But if that is your treasure, it is worldly success, not kingdom success. A lot of unhappy people are rich, living in massive homes with toys and worried about losing it. You would be surprised how little you can have and still build up the faith of those around you. Are you chasing kingdom success?

  11. 23:35 Don’t camp on the wrong side of the river

    Scripture: Joshua 1:9 Ephesians 1:3 Romans 6:23 John 3:16

    • Yahweh says, “Joshua, why are you still here? Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous.” Josh sees too many Christians making camp on the wrong side of the river. Don’t get comfortable on the wrong side of the promise.
    • Paul tells the Christians in Ephesus that we have every spiritual blessing in Christ; the gift is right there, so step out in faith and grab it. The wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord. Talk about every place you set your foot: if you trust in God’s only begotten Son, you will not perish but have eternal life. Joshua got across the Jordan; you get across the grave. Why are you discouraged or afraid, believer?

    1. 24:44 An open door no one can shut

      Scripture: Revelation 3:8 Romans 8:31

      • Jesus says, “I have placed before you an open door that no one can shut. I know that you have little strength, yet you have kept my word and have not denied my name.” Jesus confirms that kingdom success is keeping his word and upholding his name.
      • Who can shut the door Jesus opened? No one; no man can come between you and Yahweh’s promise. Only your excuses can keep you from walking through it. Walk through in faith and trust God with your life; that is the gospel, and there is no greater message, hope, purpose, or success. Believers stand with Joshua on the riverbank and reach the same conclusion: if God is for us, who can be against us?

  12. 25:58 Promises as an alarm clock, not a blanket

    Scripture: Joshua 1:10–11

    • What set Joshua apart is that he didn’t use God’s promises as a warm, snuggly blanket to grow comfy by the campfire. He used them like an alarm clock forcing him out of bed to do the Lord’s work. He orders the officers to tell the people to get their provisions ready, because in three days they will cross the Jordan. “Alexa, remind me in three days to cross the Jordan.”
    • There are times a leader needs to listen to the people and times the people need to listen to the leader, and it isn’t always clear which. Here it was clear: God spoke to his new leader and the people were ready to obey.
    • Never forget Kadesh Barnea. Four decades earlier they believed the ten spies instead of obeying in faith. They crossed the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month, the same day of the year as the Red Sea crossing, exactly forty years after God split it in half.

  13. 27:46 Great leaders repeat God’s promises and commands

    Scripture: Joshua 1:13 Numbers 32:1 Joshua 22

    • After the first hectic days of packing up camp, there would be free time between destinations, and in verse 13 Joshua gives the tribes living on the other side of the Jordan important instructions: remember the command Moses gave you. Great leaders repeat the promises and the commands of God.
    • It is also wise leadership: wise leaders don’t tear down the progress of others but build on it. Moses had allowed these tribes to live outside the promised land. In Numbers 32 the Reubenites and Gadites, with their large herds, saw that Jazer and Gilead suited livestock. Josh is harsh on them: they seemed to care more about the cattle than the congregation, worldly success outranking kingdom success. In chapter 22 they are so far from the house of worship that they build a monument to remind their children they are citizens of Israel.

    1. 29:30 A modern cattle-herder problem

      Scripture: 1 Peter 2:24

      • Josh thinks the church has a modern cattle-herder problem: people willing to serve the Lord when it’s convenient or there is something to gain, who then disappear to their faraway lands and don’t remember the word of the Lord.
      • How can we endure when we don’t recall his promises? The first Israelite to step into the Jordan did so because of the promise on the other side. Josh can’t go a day without recalling that Jesus bore his sins in his own body on the tree, that he is accepted in him and has peace with God, that Jesus opened heaven’s doors. Jesus is his whole life, strength, power, and salvation; he isn’t worried about tomorrow because Jesus slayed death. How can he hear God’s word and not hunger to hear it again and again, or see how God loves his people and not love them the same way?

  14. 30:51 Unity is the goal

    Scripture: Joshua 1:16

    • Despite how Josh feels about the two tribes choosing land for a growing business over their brothers and sisters, he admires how Joshua brought everyone together to obey the Lord, and he recognizes God can use anyone. Unity clearly mattered to Joshua and to Yahweh.
    • As a church we might not always agree, and with 5,000 words a week Josh is bound to say something you don’t like. But we must never be careless with or discourage one another. Learn to recognize the gifting God has placed in someone and build them up: “You have a beautiful gift. How can I pray for you?” A culture of encouragement is modeled in Joshua 1.
    • Whatever side of the river they were on, Joshua encouraged them, and they answered, “Whatever you have commanded us we will do, and wherever you send us we will go.” They could have said, “Nah, we’re good; good luck, Joshua,” but they heard the call and got on board.
    • Outside the essentials there is a lot of room for unity, and we spend too much time picking fights. Josh’s exercise for the week: look for someone you can build up. They might not need it, but you probably need to grow your gift of building others up.

  15. 33:37 Devotion to the master, not the servant

    Scripture: Joshua 1:17

    • In verse 17 the people pledge to obey Joshua as they fully obeyed Moses, and pray the Lord will be with him as he was with Moses. They knew God appointed both; there was no competition, no comparing who did what better. Their devotion was never to the servant but always to the master.
    • That isn’t always the case, and the church has seen wacky stuff when servants of God are elevated higher than they belong. If your loyalty leads to disunity in the church, it is ungodly loyalty. You end up with spiritually soft cheerleaders rooting for their favorite servant when we need solid, obedient Christians eager to follow the master. Shame on followers for pouring out that kind of love, and shame on leaders for soaking it up.

    1. 34:53 Pray for your leaders

      • The best way to encourage spiritual leaders isn’t to build a fan club around them. The officers pray for Joshua: we know this conquest won’t be easy; we’re praying the Lord is with you. Loyalty matters, but your prayers matter far more. The best thing you can do for those leading the church is to pray daily that the Lord would be with them.
      • Joshua was incredibly gifted, yet he failed on several occasions when he got too far ahead of God. No matter how gifted a leader is or how many leadership conferences he attends, a plan without serious prayer will fail miserably.

  16. 35:59 Taking obedience seriously

    Scripture: Joshua 1:18 Joshua 7

    • The people take it to the next level: whoever rebels against Joshua’s word is to be put to death. They had learned the value of obedience the hard way, unlike Achan in chapter 7 (spoiler alert: they stone him), which the series will reach in week four or five.
    • It made Josh wonder whether the church would see more impact if it were more serious about obedience. Not stoning people, but obeying the Lord even when we don’t feel like it, letting his word override us, obeying when it is inconvenient or there is nothing to gain. If the Israelite soldiers had looked like American Christians, the promised land might have been delayed another forty years.

  17. 37:24 Only be strong and courageous

    Scripture: Joshua 1:18 Ephesians 6:10–18

    • Chapter 1 closes with the best encouragement of all, the word of God: only be strong and courageous. If you don’t know how to encourage someone, find a verse that encourages you and share it; sometimes God’s words will do just fine.
    • It takes more courage today to be a Christian than at any other point in Josh’s life. Either way, be strong and courageous. He reads Paul’s call to be strong in the Lord and put on the full armor of God, because our struggle is not against flesh and blood: the belt of truth, the breastplate of righteousness, feet fitted with the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, the sword of the Spirit, and prayer on all occasions for all the Lord’s people.

    1. 39:20 Rise up and take it

      Scripture: Philippians 2:10–11 Joshua 1:9

      • Now more than ever we need soldiers of Christ to stand tall. There may have been a time in this country when you could go outside without your armor, but you would be foolish to do it today. Be prepared and don’t be afraid; the prize is already yours. Rise up and take it: walk by faith, keep the faith, and build the faith of those around you.
      • Josh doesn’t know what lies Satan or his minions are feeding you, but if you are in Christ, they have to come face to face with God to get to you. He isn’t afraid of the devil, and not because he works out; the devil could kick his butt. The problem is the devil can’t handle the one who lives in him. At the name of Jesus every knee will bow, and one day the devil will worship God before he is thrown into the lake of fire.
      • The devil could tear him to shreds, but Jesus’ body was torn too, and Christ rose three days later still wearing the holes of his rejection. Josh is weak, but Jesus is strong; he doesn’t know what tomorrow holds, but he knows what eternity has for him. So be strong and courageous; do not be afraid or discouraged, for the Lord your God will be with you wherever you go.

  18. 41:19 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks God for his written word and for showing that good leadership is always rooted in obedience to him. His heart is burdened for Christians walking around without their armor, grabbing on to lies instead of recognizing them, because they aren’t walking with God.
    • He prays the church would abide in God, stay rooted in his word, and keep it on their lips to build others up, not for the name of the church but for the name of Jesus and the salvation of souls, and that like Joshua they would hear God’s promises and let them echo through their lives.
