Overview

  1. 00:09 Doctrine is not a substitute for inspiration

    Scripture: John 1:1-18

    • Backhanded compliments—from “smarter than you look” to “your sermon didn’t feel long”—establish that words carry weight. Josh says no words matter more than John 1:1-18.
    • He answers the objection that doctrine is less useful than inspiration: the incarnation is the church’s hope and carries eternal consequences. John wrote so people would know whom to trust.
    • The pulpit must offer Christ rather than warm uplift. Listeners should not grade the sermon but examine themselves: the soul does not need soup; it needs a Savior.

  2. 05:08 The Word became flesh: the Bible’s center

    Scripture: John 1:1; 1:14 Romans 1:20; Hebrews 1:1-3 John 1:14-18 John 1:14 John 1:14 Acts 14:11-15 John 1:14 John 1:14

    • John 1:1 and 1:14 hold together the incarnation: the Word was God, and the Word became flesh. The infinite becomes finite, eternity enters time, and the Creator enters creation.
    • Josh reads John 1:14-18: witnesses see the Son’s glory; he is full of grace and truth; grace comes through him; and he makes the unseen God known.
    • A Star Wars viewing-order illustration calls incarnation the Bible’s “missing movie.” Greeks held too low a view of deity; Jews thought God too exalted to take flesh. John answers both: God truly came near so his glory could be seen.

  3. 10:02 God tabernacled among his people

    Scripture: John 1:14 Genesis 3:8 Exodus 25:8-9; Numbers 2:17 Exodus 25-26; 36-40 Exodus 40:34-38; Numbers 9:15-23 Psalm 121:5-6 Leviticus 26:11-12 Isaiah 54:5 Romans 3:29-30 John 4:19-26 Hebrews 8-9 Hebrews 8:1-2 Hebrews 9:11-12 John 1:14, 16-17 Hebrews 10:19-22 John 1:14 Hebrews 10:25

    • Josh reads John through the Bible’s dwelling theme. Eden held fellowship with God, sin destroyed peace, and God chose to close the distance himself.
    • The wilderness tabernacle stood among Israel’s tents, its ordinary exterior concealing glory. Cloud and fire sheltered, illuminated, and guided the camp as God walked among his people.
    • God is not confined to one nation or mountain. Romans includes Jew and Gentile; Jesus tells the Samaritan woman that true worshipers worship in Spirit and truth.
    • Hebrews identifies the earthly sanctuary as a shadow of the heavenly reality fulfilled in Christ. Jesus is high priest, mediator, and the true tabernacle who enters once for all by his own blood.
    • The Word camped among his people. The incarnation is why the church sings, gathers, and proclaims the gospel; Wesley’s hymn answers with incarnate deity and Emmanuel.

  4. 19:19 Holy glory is dangerous apart from Christ

    Scripture: John 1:14 Exodus 19:10-25 Exodus 19:18 Exodus 33:18, 20-23 Exodus 32:1-6; Romans 1:23 John 1:14

    • Ancient reluctance arose from a high view of holiness, while modern presumption often comes from a high view of self. God’s presence is not a warm accessory for unrepentant sinners.
    • Sinai burns, smokes, and trembles. Like a father carefully holding a newborn, God sets boundaries because people cannot bear his unveiled power.
    • Moses asks to see God’s glory but cannot see his face and live. The proper response is humility, confession, and flight to Jesus—not treating God as subordinate.
    • John’s claim is astonishing precisely because glory fatal to mere humans has now been seen in the incarnate Son.

  5. 24:08 Believers receive more access than Israel had

    Scripture: Exodus 40:34-35 John 1:14 Acts 7:54-8:3; 9:1-19 Matthew 1:23 Colossians 1:20 John 1:14 Leviticus 16; Hebrews 9:7 1 Peter 2:9 1 Peter 2:11 1 Peter 1:15-16; Matthew 5:48 Matthew 5:8 John 16:8 John 1:18

    • Exodus says even Moses could not enter the glory-filled tent, yet John says believers have seen God’s glory. Attempts to silence that witness only spread it: Stephen dies and a Pharisee becomes a missionary.
    • Israel viewed the sanctuary from outside; believers have Emmanuel with them, the Spirit within them, and peace through Jesus’ blood. All believers are a royal priesthood beholding glory in Christ.
    • Josh sees present glory in Christians pursuing holiness as foreigners and strangers. The Son makes God known through transformed hearts, endurance, truth, holiness, and his enduring word.

  6. 29:08 Fire illustrates glory and the cross

    Scripture: Exodus 24:17 Exodus 3:5; 19:16-19 Malachi 3:2-3 John 1:14 Exodus 3:2; 13:21-22 John 1:14

    • A family fire recalls Exodus: divine glory is bright, captivating, and dangerous. Fire teaches reverence, exposes flaws, and refines before it comforts.
    • Malachi’s refiner and John’s grace and truth meet in images that burn without consuming God’s people—the bush and guiding pillar.
    • At Calvary, consuming judgment falls on Jesus so the benefits of glory fall on God’s children. Josh prays that sons and daughters will be drawn to God like his son is drawn to flame.

  7. 32:06 Jesus is full of grace and truth

    Scripture: John 1:14 Luke 19:1-10

    • Grace and truth can be distinguished but not separated in Christ. Human beings alternate between affectionate compromise and harsh honesty; Jesus holds both in perfect fullness.
    • He never softens truth to appear gracious and never withholds grace to state truth. His welcome of Zacchaeus and the hostility of religious leaders prepare the extended example in John 8.

  8. 34:11 The adulterous woman: grace satisfies truth

    Scripture: John 8:2-5 Deuteronomy 19:15 John 8:10-11 Matthew 5:17 1 Kings 3:16-28 1 Corinthians 1:24 John 8:6 Leviticus 20:10; Deuteronomy 22:22 John 8:7 John 8:10-11 2 Corinthians 5:21 John 8:11 John 8:7-11 John 8:11 Matthew 11:28-29 John 8:12

    • Religious leaders publicly present a guilty woman and invoke Moses to trap Jesus. The absent man exposes selective use of the law and contempt for both justice and the woman.
    • Josh compares the scene with Solomon’s public judgment, then identifies Jesus as the wisdom of God. His call for the sinless person to cast first exposes the accusers; his refusal to condemn anticipates bearing the woman’s debt.
    • “Neither do I condemn you” is grace; “leave your life of sin” is truth. Jesus neither minimizes sin nor requires self-cleansing before approach.
    • Christians must not cast themselves as Jesus in the story. They are the adulterer in the dirt or the hypocrite holding a stone; either way, they need him.

  9. 40:30 Go tell inexhaustible grace

    Scripture: John 1:14 Romans 5:1

    • The church must tell people that the Lord came not in wrath or judgment but full of grace and truth. Because God came down, believers now invite others to come to him.
    • Raised hands display how much grace Christ has lavished, yet he remains full and can never run out. Stop running away; run to him and receive peace with God.
    • Josh closes by marveling that sinners see God’s glory in Jesus and prays for eternal peace through Christ’s blood, singing souls, and victory in his name.
