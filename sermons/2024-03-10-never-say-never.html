Overview

  1. 00:16 The Road to Redemption

    Scripture: Romans 5:8

    • Today begins a three-week series called The Road to Redemption. As the church prepares to celebrate the Lord’s resurrection, it is a reminder that Christ’s death and resurrection have undeniable power. Gazing into Peter’s life during the Lord’s final hours will transform how you read Romans 5:8: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. There is tremendous encouragement in seeing the fisherman become an apostle, and in Peter’s denial and restoration.
    • Peter’s fall is recorded in all four gospels, “which I’m sure Peter’s thrilled” about. Hardly a day goes by that Josh doesn’t consider what Jesus endured, beaten, mocked, crowned with thorns, spit upon, crucified, yet he can’t imagine any of it hurting as much as hearing Peter say, “I do not know the man.” Jesus predicted it, but that may have increased the pain.

  2. 01:49 Self-confidence: trusting yourself is a mistake

    • Why do four authors record it? Of the many lessons, one stands out: this moment exposes a weakness in all of us, self-confidence. Trusting yourself is a mistake. Leaning on your own experience, strength, and armor, the very thought that you can hold yourself up, initiates the fall.

  3. 02:33 Salvation is a gift, not a reward

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:8–9

    • First, remember how the Lord works salvation in us. Ephesians 2 says it is by grace you have been saved through faith, not by works. Faith itself results from God’s grace; belief isn’t possible apart from the Spirit at work in you. We are at war with God “by default and by fault.”
    • Christians should be the most self-aware people: we know what we are capable of. Evil around us is only half the story; there is evil within us, seen both in how others behave and in how we respond to them.

    1. 03:45 Born again and led by a divine guide

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:17 John 3:3–6 Ephesians 2:10

      • Regeneration is God’s divine action renewing the fallen creation to reflect his character. If anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come. Jesus told Nicodemus no one can see the kingdom of God without being born again. We are born again not to walk in old ways but in newness of life. Baptism is meaningless without a confession of faith, but if you take God at his word, baptism leads heaven to rejoice.
      • The believer’s journey isn’t self-guided. Ephesians 2 continues: we are God’s handiwork, created for good works God prepared in advance. The good in you wasn’t even your idea, and apart from God’s work all your goodness would be filthy rags.
      • Scripture marks true believers by childlike dependence. The Father planned our reunion with God, the Son executed it, and the Spirit works it out in us: one God in three persons working in perfect unity for our adoption.

  4. 05:39 A new creation stuck in a fallen body

    Scripture: Romans 7:14–15

    • Our souls are redeemed, but our bodies are not. We hunger for righteousness while our flesh fights every step. Paul says in Romans 7, “I do not understand what I do. For what I want to do I do not do, but what I hate I do.” This is the Christian grind: assured of rescue, yet battling sin and corruption. “I’m a new creation stuck in a fallen world.”
    • Many Christians love to talk about the evil around us, but more dangerous than the world is the body we are imprisoned in. Our flesh is dangerous too; we are born again into a container of corruption, like your husband’s ugly shirt that keeps reappearing after you throw it away.

    1. 07:56 Country club, not halfway house

      • The church should be honest about this battle, personally and as a family of believers. Instead, over the past 50 years it has been presented as a polished group with their stuff together, more country club than halfway house: entertainment as a priority, top-notch facilities, and pastors crafting rhyming one-liners more than studying the word.
      • Somewhere along the line we began to trust ourselves. We stopped asking what Jesus would do and started telling people what we would do.

  5. 09:07 Healthy skepticism of yourself

    Scripture: Revelation 2:4 Genesis 15:1

    • Jesus’ words to the church in Ephesus show it isn’t uncommon for believers to lose their first love: “You have forsaken the love you had at first.” Before anyone sends emails, Josh isn’t talking about salvation; he is talking about purpose.
    • Believers should remain skeptical of themselves. We start out trusting God for life and drift into believing in God and in ourselves, sometimes after a small victory over an addiction or habit. We go from “Jesus, take the wheel” to “You ride shotgun, I got it from here.” Day 30 of sobriety, the Lord is my shield and very great reward; day 100, me and the Lord; day 300, “I don’t even think about it anymore.” That is dangerous.
    • We have no trouble being skeptical of others. But the wise believer knows the greatest potential for harm comes not from around them but from within, which is why Peter’s story is in all four gospels: Peter loved Jesus and would fight for him, yet self-confidence was his downfall.

  6. 11:25 Peter’s denial

    Scripture: Matthew 26:69–75

    • Josh reads the account: a servant girl in the courtyard says, “You also were with Jesus the Galilean,” and Peter denies it; another says, “This man was with Jesus of Nazareth,” and he denies it with an oath, “I do not know the man”; bystanders say his accent betrays him, and he invokes a curse and swears. The rooster crows, Peter remembers Jesus’ words, and he goes out and weeps bitterly.

    1. 12:09 The higher the position, the greater the fall

      Scripture: Matthew 16:15–17 Matthew 6:13

      • Judas betraying Jesus with a kiss was sad, but Judas loved money; we saw it coming. Peter loved the Lord. He was in the inner circle with James and John, who saw Jairus’s daughter healed, the transfiguration, and Jesus praying in Gethsemane.
      • It deepens when we recall Peter answering, “Who do you say I am?” He said, “You are the Christ, the Son of the living God,” the same confession many make in the waters of baptism, and Jesus said flesh and blood hadn’t revealed it, but the Father in heaven. The higher the position, the greater the fall.
      • Let this warn anyone seeking to lead in his church: it is a noble pursuit, but the greater the responsibility, the more devastating the sin. That is why the Lord teaches us to pray, “Lead us not into temptation,” not because God tempts us, but asking him to guard us from the devil’s schemes, the world, and our own flesh.

  7. 14:39 Self-confidence is a weakness, self-denial a virtue

    Scripture: Luke 14:8–11

    • Our culture says self-confidence is a virtue: the more you believe you are great, no matter how terrible you are, the happier you will be. But self-confidence is a weakness. In God’s kingdom, self-confidence is weakness and self-denial is virtue.
    • The world says scratch and claw your way to significance. Jesus says when invited to a wedding feast, don’t take the place of honor; take the lowest place so the host says, “Friend, move up.” Those who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted.

  8. 15:51 “I never will”

    Scripture: Matthew 26:31–35 Zechariah 13:7

    • It gets worse: Peter and the eleven were warned. At the Last Supper Jesus predicted the denial, fulfilling prophecies from both Zechariah and Christ: “You will all fall away on account of me, for it is written, I will strike the shepherd.” Peter replied, “Even if all fall away, I never will,” and “Even if I have to die with you, I will never disown you,” and all the others said the same.
    • To quote the great philosopher Justin Bieber: never say never. Have you said it? I will never get married, have kids, be like my parents, drive a minivan, buy a hybrid, or decorate for Christmas before Thanksgiving.

    1. 17:40 “I will” statements

      Scripture: Matthew 5:37

      • The positive form is “I will always.” Wedding vows contain five “I will” statements: love her, comfort her, honor and keep her, forsaking all others, as long as you both live. These are big words. Jesus says let your yes be yes and your no be no; Christians are called to be reliable people.

  9. 18:29 Promises define who we are

    • After the rooster crowed, Peter wept bitterly, because promises define who we are. You can know God by reviewing his promises, from Genesis, where he promises a Messiah who will crush Satan’s head, to Jesus promising to prepare a place for us. We can also know you by your promises: they show your intentions, but your integrity shows in keeping or breaking them.
    • The bigger the promise, the more it defines you. Without commitment, denying ourselves and going all in no matter where it leads, there is no intimacy, no community, no identity.

    1. 20:01 Josh’s commitments

      Scripture: Ephesians 5:21

      • Josh gave his life to Christ at nine and lived in rebellion from about 12 to 22, so he can’t be hard on Peter for one bad night. But he surrendered to God’s will: he came into the world with nothing and now has a church, wife, kids, job, and food; if God takes it all, so be it. With Peter he says boldly, “I will never stop praising him, never stop preaching the gospel, never turn my back on him.”
      • Next is his wife: he would rather someone remove his eyes with a spoon than be unfaithful to her. His devotion is to God first and his wife second, and God set it up beautifully: he honors God by submitting to his wife out of reverence for Christ, so long as she doesn’t become an idol. “He almost idol-proofed your wife.”
      • Then his children (“as much as they look like me, which is not a good thing”): child of God, husband, father, then pastor. He loves this body deeply, but all those commitments come first; we have seen the devastation when men of God place the church above their families. “You are your commitments.”

    2. 23:06 Who is Peter?

      Scripture: Matthew 26:35 Matthew 26:74

      • That is why Peter is weeping bitterly and falling apart: he doesn’t know who he is anymore. Is he the man in verse 35, “I will never disown you,” or the man in verse 74, “I told you, I do not know him”?

  10. 23:35 The god of this age

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 4:4 Ephesians 6:12

    • It is Josh’s job to make the church aware of what is going on around them. Paul says in his second letter to Corinth that the god of this age, lowercase g, Satan, has blinded the minds of unbelievers so they cannot see the light of the gospel. God shines his light through the gospel while Satan pulls eyelids closed.
    • We see it in our culture, and no election will fix it, because the problem isn’t flesh and blood but spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms. Satan isn’t a guy in red pajamas; he is smart, cunning, and good at what he does. We err when we underestimate him and err worse when we overestimate ourselves.

    1. 25:00 Watch and pray

      Scripture: Matthew 26:40–41

      • Jesus warned Peter in Gethsemane: “Couldn’t you men keep watch with me for one hour? Watch and pray so that you will not fall into temptation. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak.” We have two tools against temptation: watch and pray.
      • Living in fear differs from noticing subtleties. Are you aware of what the evil one is up to? It is one thing to see it coming and another to call on the Lord for help.

    2. 25:56 A culture building identity on feelings

      Scripture: Jeremiah 17:9

      • Josh saw an Instagram video of a woman in her mid-20s who insisted she was supposed to be blind from birth and so blinded herself. That is how successful Satan’s recent campaign has been: people build their identity on how they feel, though you may feel differently tomorrow.
      • The heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure. Culture tells young people they need affirmation when they need a new heart. Satan says, “You’re perfect just the way you are,” and we buy it hook, line, and sinker. But if everyone is perfect, why is everything so messed up?

    3. 27:17 Self-confidence is the devil’s Trojan horse

      • Remember the Trojan horse (and Brad Pitt): after a ten-year war, the Greeks hid elite soldiers in a wooden horse, the Trojans rolled it inside as a parting gift and threw a party, and the Greeks came out at night and defeated them from within. The walls were too strong for force; it had to be deception.
      • People believe two lies about Satan: that he isn’t real (he is very real) and that he rivals God’s power (he doesn’t hold a candle to it). He schemes because his power is nothing compared to Yahweh’s. So prayer is essential: we call on the Lord’s strength, our shields go up, and we overwhelm the evil one with one word. We don’t have to be losing this battle.

  11. 29:04 Peter doubles and triples down

    Scripture: Matthew 26:71–72

    • The first denial seems casual and evasive, almost a communication issue, a dodge with no oath or cursing, hoping no one recognizes him. Then Peter pulls toward the gateway, close enough to watch, and a second girl says, “This man was with Jesus of Nazareth.” He denies it with an oath: “I do not know the man.” Weeks earlier he called Jesus the Son of God, the Messiah; now he is just “the man.”
    • Josh imagines Peter staying far from the fire, cold at one in the morning, hiding under cover of dark. As he grows comfortable talking, they notice his Galilean accent. After three years with Jesus, the Sermon on the Mount, and the first communion, Peter knew that to prove he wasn’t a Christian, he had to act like he wasn’t one: “If I curse and swear, they’ll know.” So he did. “I will never.”

    1. 31:48 If you think you’re standing firm

      Scripture: Proverbs 16:18 1 Corinthians 10:12

      • Pride goes before destruction, a haughty spirit before a fall. It isn’t a stretch to think Paul had this proverb in mind writing to Corinth: if you think you are standing firm, be careful that you don’t fall.
      • The temptation to trade confidence in the Lord for confidence in ourselves is real. We take credit for his work, defend our freedom in Christ before his honor, and treat grace like a hall pass to sin. When we pray, “Your will be done,” we are really praying that it be done in and through us, even at personal expense, because we were bought at a price. When you see his fruit in your life, let it build your confidence in him, not in you.

  12. 33:05 Why Peter fell so hard

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 12:10

    • What happened to Peter, from cutting off ears in Gethsemane to running from servant girls, from the transfiguration to denying the Master? It hit Josh like a ton of bricks: Peter fell so hard because his identity was built on his abilities as a disciple, not on Christ’s abilities as Messiah.
    • The world says strength means doing it yourself. Paul says, “I delight in weaknesses, insults, hardships, persecutions, difficulties; for when I am weak, then I am strong.” Peter relied on his own strength, so he was weak.
    • What we celebrate most in Peter, his boldness, led to his fall. The boldness we celebrate in leaders can lead them astray or be what God uses to drive souls to himself. Keep watch and pray for one another, your pastors, elders, and their families; equip the sword of truth and help keep watch at the gate.

  13. 35:03 The Lord turned and looked at Peter

    Scripture: Matthew 26:61–67 Luke 22:60–61

    • The account appears in all four gospels with minor differences. Homework: read all four this week; there will be a test next week. Josh points out one significant difference in Luke’s account.
    • Picture the setting: a palace courtyard around midnight or 1 a.m., cold, ringed by connected rooms. Jesus is likely in the courtyard being questioned by Caiaphas and the chief priests. Witnesses say he claimed he would destroy the temple and rebuild it in three days. The high priest charges him under oath to say if he is the Messiah; Jesus says they will see the Son of Man at the right hand of the Mighty One, coming on the clouds. The high priest tears his clothes and cries blasphemy, and they spit in his face and strike him.
    • Peter moves around the courtyard through each denial, always keeping Jesus in sight as he is beaten. Then: “Man, I don’t know what you’re talking about,” the rooster crows, and the Lord turns and looks straight at Peter. Imagine the memories: “We walked on water together, but I couldn’t walk with him through this.” But as the coming weeks will show, Jesus wasn’t done stretching out his hand to save Peter, and Josh believes he is stretching it out to many today.

  14. 38:28 “Simon, Simon, I have prayed for you”

    Scripture: Luke 22:31–32 Luke 22:26

    • One more detail can’t be missed. Before predicting the denial, Jesus says, “Simon, Simon, Satan has asked to sift all of you as wheat. But I have prayed for you, Simon, that your faith may not fail. And when you have turned back, strengthen your brothers.”
    • At the table the disciples argued about who was greatest, and Jesus said the greatest must be like the youngest and the ruler like the one who serves. Peter was proving his greatness with dominant leadership; Jesus was showing him servant leadership.
    • “Simon, Simon,” in other words, “dear Simon”: Satan demands to have you as he demanded Job. “Church, help me preach: but what? I have prayed for you.” The outcome will be seen in the third week, in Peter’s restoration. Jesus prayed his faith wouldn’t fail, then gave instructions for when he turned back: “You will turn away, then you will turn back. ‘No, Lord, I never will.’ Yes, Peter, you will, and when you do, they’re going to need you.”

  15. 40:19 The key to the Christian life

    Scripture: Luke 22:62

    • He went outside and wept bitterly. Many wonder about the key to the Christian life: Peter is stronger outside weeping than he ever was holding a sword, more a man of God in his tears than when proclaiming his loyalty. This lesson turned Peter from a fisherman into an apostle.
    • My salvation isn’t tied to my ability to keep promises; it rests completely on his ability to keep promises, and he hasn’t broken a single one. Try to muscle through like Peter, the most committed disciple, and you will snap, because none of us can bear that load.

    1. 41:23 Jesus takes the pressure off

      • Something was missing that next week will pick up: Jesus is about to usher in forgiveness for our denial and make it possible for promise breakers like us and Peter to be redeemed. Grace lets us run to Jesus with our failures and pursue him again as if we never failed.
      • Jesus didn’t come to condemn us but to save us. He takes the pressure off: we no longer say “I will never”; we say, “Praise God that Jesus did.”

  16. 42:13 A staredown on that day

    Scripture: Matthew 7:22–23

    • Many of us will have a similar staredown with Jesus one day, not with him as a captive in a courtyard but as King on a throne, and it won’t go as many have planned. Perhaps the scariest verse in Scripture: “Many will say to me on that day, ‘Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name?’ Then I will tell them plainly, ‘I never knew you. Away from me, you evildoers.’”
    • Josh wants to know Jesus, and praises him that he never said this to Peter and never will to us if we confess our sins and trust him alone to save us. Run to Jesus, the promise keeper, the committed one. Put your confidence in him; he is the only one worthy of it.

  17. 43:36 Closing prayer

    • Josh comes to the Father with a heavy heart, burdened by sin and stuck in a body of sin, yet knowing God came to bring not captivity but freedom, “not freedom to sin, but freedom from sin,” to pursue and know him and be at peace with God, “because all I have ever done is declare war.”
    • He asks forgiveness for pursuing self-interest instead of God’s word and will, thanks God for the gospel and for doing what he couldn’t do, and lays down any confidence in self to put it all in him: “Thank you for saving me.”
