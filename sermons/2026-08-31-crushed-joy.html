Detailed chronological outline

The sermon begins at 00:37 after an uncaptioned opening. Josh frames relational difficulty as a problem of the inward spirit, directs listeners away from diagnosing other people, and returns to David's first-person prayer for renewal. The chronological outline excludes non-material opening media and preserves the closing invitation and prayer.

  1. 00:37 The spirit beneath the action

    • Josh opens with a relational problem: faithful Christians can love difficult people and still find those relationships hard, because love does not erase difficulty.
    • Jesus is the pattern—he loved enemies and prayed for those crucifying him—and his command is to love, do good, lend without repayment, and imitate the Father's mercy.
    • The day's focus is not the Holy Spirit but the lowercase-s spirit: the inward attitude that gives the same words or actions a completely different moral character.

    1. 01:17 Enemy-love reveals the heart

      Scripture: Luke 23:34 Luke 6:32-36

      • Josh reads Jesus' contrast between ordinary reciprocity and kingdom mercy: loving only those who love us earns no special credit; disciples love enemies because the Most High is kind to the ungrateful and wicked.
      • Jesus' prayer from the cross embodies the command he had already given his disciples.

    2. 02:20 Identical behavior, different spirits

      Scripture: Proverbs 12:1

      • A room can feel heavy before anyone speaks, and polite words can carry a wrong spirit underneath them.
      • Two people may apologize, serve, worship, or receive correction in outwardly identical ways while one acts from repentance, love, worship, or teachability and the other from escape, importance, performance, or pride.
      • Josh's sharp Proverbs example underscores that refusing correction while claiming 'church hurt' can mask a resistant spirit.

    3. 04:07 Renewal must start within

      Scripture: Psalm 51:10

      • Returning to the prior week's prayer, Josh says the need is not merely better circumstances, boundaries, accountability, wisdom, or discernment, but God's inward renewal of a steadfast spirit.
      • External guardrails can block a familiar road to sin, but they cannot substitute for God changing the heart.

    4. 05:09 Self-diagnosis before diagnosis of others

      Scripture: Psalm 51:10

      • Josh warns listeners not to spend the sermon diagnosing a spouse, boss, child, former partner, in-law, or coworker.
      • The humble order is to ask God to work in oneself first; David's prayer is 'renew ... in me,' not a request that God repair everyone around him.

  2. 06:11 The text: a sacrifice God will not despise

    Scripture: Psalm 51:15-17

    • Josh reads David's prayer that God open his lips for praise, his recognition that ritual sacrifice cannot solve the problem, and his offering of a broken spirit and contrite heart.
    • He defines contrite as crushed and contrasts the kingdoms: in human terms crushing means defeat, but in God's kingdom it can become the beginning of freedom.

    1. 07:11 The governing question

      • When God confronts, corrects, disciplines, or crushes a person, in which direction does the spirit break?
      • A wrong spirit breaks away into defensiveness, blame, bitterness, anger, performance, hiding, or despair; a right spirit breaks toward God in confession, humility, dependence, surrender, praise, and hope.
      • A right spirit is not one that never breaks—everyone's spirit will break—but one that breaks in the right direction.

  3. 07:58 1. A right spirit wants to be useful for God's glory

    Scripture: Psalm 51:15

    • David asks for opened lips not to recover status but to declare God's praise.
    • Josh invites honest inspection of whether usefulness for God's kingdom or prominence in one's own kingdom is the real desire.

    1. 08:41 Sin silenced a gifted mouth

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 11:14-17

      • David was a poet, musician, songwriter, commander, politician, and king for whom words came easily, yet sin and shame sealed his lips.
      • The words used to cover Uriah's death could not become an instrument of praise unless God redeemed them; sin promised pleasure but stole David's song.
      • Sin is an evil salesman: it advertises the party, pleasure, and freedom while hiding the hangover, pain, and chains.

    2. 09:24 God works from the inward being outward

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6 Psalm 51:10 Psalm 51:12 Matthew 12:34

      • David knows God desires inward truth and hidden wisdom, so he asks for a clean heart, steadfast spirit, willing spirit, restored joy, and finally opened lips.
      • Jesus' words about the mouth speaking from the heart's overflow frame Josh's Hoover Dam picture: whatever opens the heart's gate determines what flows through the mouth.
      • Vanity releases self-promotion; anger releases cruelty; bitterness releases accusation; lust releases sexual sin; pride releases self-defense; the Holy Spirit releases grace, mercy, peace, humility, love, and praise.

    3. 11:14 Let the mouth diagnose the spirit

      • Josh asks what comes out under correction, exhaustion, disagreement, another person's promotion, or the mention of someone who caused pain.
      • The mouth can preach back to its owner, exposing whether the operating spirit is right or wrong.
      • Shame says a sinner has forfeited the right to sing; grace says Christ, not sin, has the final word, so an opened mouth should praise.

    4. 12:14 Wrong directions: performance, bitterness, and ambition

      • The performative spirit knows the songs, vocabulary, gestures, and timing while keeping the heart closed to surrender.
      • The bitter spirit makes every conversation return to disappointment and debt; untreated pain becomes poison that pours out and wounds others.
      • Toxic ambition wants God to use it so people will notice it. Josh confesses his own struggle with pastoral comparison and says he wants to be content serving whomever God sends, with or without a platform.
      • The diagnostic question is whether being used powerfully but anonymously would be enough.

    5. 15:05 Restored joy turns outward toward sinners

      Scripture: Psalm 51:12-13 2 Peter 3:9

      • David asks for restored joy and a willing spirit so he can teach transgressors and see sinners return.
      • Josh connects that desire with Peter's declaration that the Lord patiently desires repentance rather than anyone's destruction.
      • David seeks more than relief from guilt: he wants inward transformation, better living, and renewed usefulness.

    6. 16:31 Usefulness, not restored reputation

      Scripture: Acts 5:41

      • David never asks God to restore the reputation of his throne; a wrong spirit wants life and image back to normal, while a right spirit asks how even the ashes can glorify God.
      • Josh pictures David wanting to praise as openly as the apostles left the Sanhedrin rejoicing that they had suffered for Jesus' name.

    7. 17:34 Peter's redeemed mouth

      Scripture: Matthew 26:34, 69-75 John 21:15-19 Acts 2:14-41

      • Peter used his lips to deny Jesus exactly as Jesus foretold, but the risen Jesus publicly restored him.
      • The mouth that denied Christ became a preaching mouth through which the church was launched: shame sent Peter fishing, while grace sent him preaching.
      • God can similarly turn lying into truth, cursing into blessing, tearing down into building up, complaint into praise, wounding into healing, and shameful silence into proclamation of Christ's mercy.

  4. 18:57 2. A right spirit stops bargaining and starts surrendering

    Scripture: Psalm 51:16

    • Josh follows David from praise God produces to performance God rejects: no sacrifice David can supply will purchase the cleansing he needs.
    • The issue is not that God never established sacrifice, but that the sign was never meant to replace the surrendered heart or the Son to whom it pointed.

    1. 19:18 Ritual signifies an inward reality

      • Sacrifice, like baptism, is an outward act that pleases God only when offered in faith, repentance, gratitude, dependence, humility, and obedience.
      • Sinful people instead perform ritual while withholding the heart, using sacrifice as a substitute for surrender.

    2. 20:21 The prophets reject worship without obedience

      Scripture: 1 Samuel 15:22 Exodus 1:22 Isaiah 1:11-15 Amos 5:23-24 Micah 6:7-8 Hosea 6:6 Matthew 12:7

      • Samuel tells Saul that obedience is better than sacrifice; this response helps distinguish Saul's resistant spirit from David's contrition.
      • Josh compares Saul's willingness to sacrifice anything to Pharaoh's ruthless command, while David recognizes that no offering he controls can make him clean.
      • Isaiah condemns multiplied offerings from bloodstained hands, Amos rejects songs detached from justice, Micah answers extravagant bargaining with justice, mercy, and humble walking, and Hosea declares God's desire for mercy rather than sacrifice.
      • Jesus later quotes Hosea against those who condemn the innocent, confirming that ritual never was the final solution.

    3. 22:28 Royal resources cannot buy purity

      Scripture: Micah 6:7

      • David is not too poor to sacrifice; as king he could fill the altar repeatedly, yet no bulls or rivers of oil could wash Uriah's blood from his hands.
      • He confronts what every sinner saved by grace must know: 'I cannot pay for this.'
      • Surrender is therefore not an easy escape. People prefer a price, schedule, quota, or period of self-punishment because a transaction leaves them in control, but grace refuses to become a transaction.

    4. 24:56 Wrong directions: transaction, compensation, comparison, control

      • The transactional spirit keeps an invoice and turns obedience into currency with which to negotiate with God.
      • The compensating spirit gets visibly busy instead of confessing, reconciling, and confronting the heart; church activity can become avoidance of God.
      • The comparative spirit seeks confidence by locating someone worse rather than resting in Christ's righteousness, poisoning both person and church.
      • The controlling spirit would rather check a box than be crushed: religion asks for a task, while repentance gives God the whole life.

    5. 26:39 Many lanes of a wrong spirit

      Scripture: Matthew 7:13-14

      • Josh applies Jesus' wide-road and narrow-road image not only to salvation but also to sanctification: there are many wrong directions but only one right spirit.

    6. 27:22 Yesterday's obedience cannot cancel today's sin

      Scripture: 1 Samuel 17:45-51 1 Samuel 24:1-22

      • An entitled spirit could invoke David's victory over Goliath, his mercy toward Saul in the cave, leadership, generosity, victories, or songs as credit against present failure.
      • Yet yesterday's faithfulness cannot answer today's unfaithfulness, and Sunday's obedience cannot excuse Saturday's rebellion.
      • Attendance, giving, service, Bible knowledge, and leadership are good, but become barriers when they are used to keep God distant: hands can rise while pride remains, a Bible can be carried alongside bitterness, and grace can be applauded without being personally needed.

    7. 29:09 The sacrifice David could never bring

      Scripture: 1 Peter 3:18 Genesis 22:14

      • Israel's altar pointed beyond itself: an animal could picture substitution but could not complete or sustain it.
      • Jesus did not bring another animal; the righteous one offered himself for the unrighteous to bring sinners to God, bearing the judgment of rebels.
      • The blood David could not remove is cleansed through Christ's willing blood. David could not provide the sacrifice, but the God who provides did.

    8. 29:55 The offering left to bring

      Scripture: Romans 12:1

      • With purchasing and self-rescue ruled out, David brings what he had protected: himself.
      • Josh connects this surrender with Paul's appeal to offer the body as a living, holy, pleasing sacrifice in view of God's mercy.

  5. 30:16 3. A right spirit breaks toward God

    Scripture: Psalm 51:17

    • David's sacrifice is a broken spirit and crushed heart that God will not despise.
    • Josh develops brokenness not as personality weakness or self-hatred but as surrendered resistance.

    1. 30:16 God receives the broken person

      Scripture: Leviticus 22:20-25 Malachi 1:8 Psalm 34:18

      • Israel could not offer a blind, lame, diseased, mutilated, or defective animal. Malachi rebuked people for giving God what they would not offer a governor.
      • The contrast reveals divine compassion toward image-bearers: God rejected a broken sacrificial animal but receives a broken person, and he is close to the brokenhearted.

    2. 32:01 Brokenness is the collapse of resistance

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 12:1-13

      • A broken spirit is not chronic insecurity, emotional instability, public crying, permanent misery, or learning to hate oneself correctly.
      • It is the end of arguing with God. David stops concealing, defending, minimizing, blaming, controlling the narrative, protecting his image, and rushing back to the throne.
      • When Nathan exposes him, David agrees: he has sinned against the Lord. Contrition is measured not by visible tears but by the end of the fight against God.

    3. 33:04 Nolan's wrestling match

      • Josh's son Nolan cycles through playing dead, kicking, and trying to bite before finally saying, 'I'm done, Daddy.'
      • The playful wrestling illustration pictures contrition: after every strategy of resistance is exhausted, the fight ends and the heart stops wrestling with God.

    4. 34:50 Merciful destruction

      Scripture: Psalm 51:8 2 Samuel 11:1-27

      • David had asked that the bones God crushed rejoice. God broke the wrong spirit so he could build the right one.
      • The humbled David now knows a palace cannot exclude temptation, authority cannot manage sin, secrecy eventually devours, and self-reliance can turn a person into what he never intended to become.
      • God did not destroy David; he destroyed the pride threatening to destroy David. Sometimes God breaks in order to rebuild.

    5. 35:52 Wrong directions: defense, blame, and minimization

      Scripture: Genesis 3:12-13 1 Samuel 15:15, 21

      • The defensive spirit instinctively builds a case. Context can matter, but a defensive spirit uses context to avoid confession; a broken spirit cares more about making things right than proving it was right.
      • The blaming spirit passes responsibility like a hot potato: Adam blamed Eve, Eve blamed the serpent, and Saul blamed the people. David could have blamed Bathsheba, leadership loneliness, throne pressure, or supposedly unmet needs, but instead says, 'I have sinned.'
      • The minimizing spirit reduces guilt through 'everyone makes mistakes,' comparison, or 'only once.' Responsibility cannot be accepted while simultaneously being reduced.

    6. 38:00 Receive correction instead of resenting it

      Scripture: Psalm 51:8 2 Samuel 12:1-13

      • David can imagine crushed bones rejoicing, while an entitled spirit resents correction and asks how anyone could confront it after all it has done.
      • God sometimes sends a Nathan. Maturity should increase teachability and confidence in Christ, not make correction feel like disrespect.
      • If correction always causes withdrawal and revoked access, the operating spirit is guarded and prideful rather than steadfast.

    7. 39:24 Do not turn the wound into a weapon

      • Josh has watched bitterness destroy homes and pride destroy marriages; a person can build an identity around another's wrong.
      • Real evil should not be renamed good, but untreated wounds can become weapons. A Christian cannot change what happened, yet must refuse to let another person's sin reproduce sin within.

    8. 40:30 Despair also argues with grace

      • The despairing spirit looks humble while insisting that God can forgive others but not this sinner, and it continues self-punishment.
      • A crushed heart stops arguing both about sin's seriousness and grace's sufficiency: sin is worse than we admit, and mercy is greater than we comprehend.

    9. 41:12 Trust God with the crushed heart

      Scripture: Psalm 51:17 Luke 18:13-14

      • David brings a broken heart while trusting that God will not despise it, even though human beings often reduce a person to the worst moment and replay it indefinitely.
      • Jesus' tax collector will not look toward heaven, beats his chest, and asks for mercy; Jesus says the broken petitioner rather than the impressive religious man goes home justified.
      • God may be producing the very crushed heart a person fears. Josh urges listeners not to waste it but to run to him.

  6. 42:36 The sacrifice God supplied

    Scripture: Revelation 5:5

    • Josh imagines David reading John's vision: the Lion from Judah and Root of David has triumphed, so weeping can stop.
    • The sermon moves from David's broken heart in Jerusalem to Jesus' broken body at Golgotha.

    1. 42:58 David's Lord and David's son

      Scripture: Isaiah 11:1 Matthew 22:41-46

      • The promised shoot from Jesse bears fruit in the victorious Root of David.
      • Josh asks who can be both David's Lord and David's son and answers that there is only one.

    2. 43:20 Crushed for sins he never committed

      Scripture: Isaiah 53:5

      • David deserved to be broken for his sin; Jesus stepped in and was crushed for sins he never committed.
      • Isaiah's words identify the exchange: the servant is pierced for transgressions, crushed for iniquities, punished to bring peace, and wounded so others are healed.

    3. 44:01 The beloved Son who pleases God

      Scripture: Matthew 3:17

      • At Jesus' baptism the Father names Jesus as his beloved Son in whom he is pleased.
      • Human beings could not climb high enough, pay enough, cleanse themselves, remove the blood from their hands, or create their own righteousness, so Christ came down, paid all, shed his blood, and became their righteousness.
      • The sacrifice God required is the sacrifice God supplied.

  7. 44:44 Crushed joy

    Scripture: Psalm 51:12 Psalm 51:15

    • Josh rejects the caricature that Christians must be miserable: David asks for restored joy and opened lips of praise.
    • He names the gift 'crushed joy'—the absence of pride and the presence of praise.

    1. 45:29 The marks of mature joy

      • Maturing Christians become more honest about sin and confident in Christ, less impressed with themselves and more amazed by mercy, quicker to repent and faster to rejoice.
      • God does not restore David by making him proud again; he gives humble joy that remembers the rescue and turns testimony into usefulness.

    2. 45:50 Break toward God and rebuild

      Scripture: Galatians 5:16 Psalm 51:17

      • A wrong spirit wants forgiveness without responsibility; a right spirit receives forgiveness and looks for ways to rebuild those around it.
      • Josh again asks whether crushing makes the listener break toward God or away, urging believers to stay soft, humble, tender, joyful, and walking by the Spirit.
      • Those stuck in performance, bitterness, pride, control, shame, or another wrong spirit should lay down the heart they have defended. They do not need to repair themselves before coming; they come so God can make them new.

    3. 47:13 Come empty-handed because the sacrifice is finished

      Scripture: Psalm 51:15 Isaiah 53:3 Psalm 51:17

      • Mercy can reopen the song sin silenced. Those whose lips God opens should praise.
      • Josh calls listeners to come honestly, humbly, empty-handed, and broken: the sacrifice has been offered, blood shed, and debt paid.
      • Jesus was despised so sinners could be welcomed; because of him, God will not despise a broken and contrite heart.

    4. 47:54 Prayer for renewed steadfastness and usefulness

      Scripture: Psalm 51:10 Psalm 51:12-13

      • Josh confesses doubt, unbelief, anxiety, worry, and recurring little faith, laying them at the Father's feet while affirming that God has never failed him.
      • He asks God to renew a steadfast, right spirit; restore the joy of first hearing God speak; and help him teach transgressors and point people to Jesus.
      • He closes by thanking God for Jesus and God's constant presence.
