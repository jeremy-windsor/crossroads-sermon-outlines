Overview

  1. 00:41 A load-bearing wall

    • Josh greets the church and tells how, on closing day in October 2021, he and Nolan celebrated a new home and then took sledgehammers to a wall.
    • Before swinging, one question has to be asked: is this wall load-bearing? Some walls do more than separate rooms; they carry weight.
    • Remove the wrong wall and cracks appear elsewhere, doors fail, floors sag. One careless decision in one room can threaten the whole house.

    1. 02:39 Sin works the same way

      Scripture: Psalm 51:18-19

      • David had swung a hammer in the privacy of his life. As Psalm 51 closes, he is no longer thinking only about his heart, joy, or restoration.
      • He is thinking about the walls of Jerusalem, because when a Christian falls the damage rarely stays in the room where the sin happened.

  2. 03:04 The series ends with two verses

    Scripture: Psalm 51:18-19

    • Josh says their series through Psalm 51 ends with two verses: David prays that God would prosper Zion and build up Jerusalem’s walls, then delight in right sacrifices.
    • David was not only a musician; he was a builder who captured Jerusalem, strengthened its defenses, organized its worship, and prepared for the temple Solomon would build.
    • David loved Zion because the city represented God’s presence and glory among his people, and he was supposed to protect it.

    1. 04:17 Building with his hands, tearing down with his character

      Scripture: Joshua 6:1-20

      • While David built Jerusalem with his hands, he began tearing it down with his character: one rooftop night, one refused denial, one summoned married woman, one pregnancy, one cover-up, one abandoned soldier, one stacked lie.
      • Those transgressions were orchestrated from the palace of a man called to do what was best for God’s people, not merely himself.
      • He was not marching around the walls like Jericho, but he weakened from within the city God called him to strengthen.

    2. 05:17 Load-bearing lives

      • Josh pauses at the end of the series to name load-bearing lives and institutions: marriage, homes, educators, leaders, pastors and elders, and the church.
      • People pretend sin stays in the room where it was committed. Trust, marriage, credibility, and unity take years to build and a moment to break.

    3. 06:20 From restore me to restore us

      Scripture: Psalm 51:1-15

      • For most of Psalm 51 David has prayed about David’s needs: mercy, washing, cleansing, a pure heart, restored joy, opened lips.
      • Josh pictures him lifting his head from the same window where the sin began and finally looking beyond himself toward Zion’s needs.
      • True repentance does not stop at God, restore me. It pursues God, restore us—restore what my sin has damaged. Grace does not merely forgive sinners; it rebuilds them and sends them to take responsibility.

  3. 07:53 What is David asking God to do?

    Scripture: Psalm 51:18-19

    • Josh returns to Psalm 51:18–19 and asks what David is actually requesting: that God prosper Zion and build Jerusalem’s walls, then delight in whole burnt offerings.
    • May it please you carries God’s favor and gracious acceptance. David is not arguing that Zion has earned prosperity.

    1. 08:28 Have mercy on us

      Scripture: Psalm 51:1

      • David’s hope for Zion is the same hope named in verse 1 on the first week of the series: have mercy on me, according to your unfailing love—now widened to have mercy on us.
      • Since God is good and they are not, David asks God to do good to his people according to God’s goodness, not theirs.

    2. 09:08 The prophets promise Zion rebuilt

      Scripture: Isaiah 60:10 Isaiah 62:11-12 Zechariah 2:5 Zechariah 8:3

      • Isaiah looked toward a day when God would again show compassion to Zion; though he had struck the city in anger, he would rebuild its walls in favor.
      • Josh then quotes the prophets’ proclamation to daughter Zion: her Savior comes, and she will be called sought after, the city no longer deserted.
      • In Zechariah 2 God promises to be a wall of fire around Jerusalem and its glory within. In Zechariah 8:3 the Lord returns to Zion; Jerusalem is called the city of truth, and Josh assigns the whole chapter as homework—elderly in the streets, children playing without fear, joyful worship.

    3. 10:31 More than masonry

      • Bigger walls and towers are not the point. This is about people enjoying peace with God.
      • David offers a national prayer for his time, but Josh believes he is asking for more than he knows: stability, protection, worship, restoration, and peace.

    4. 11:09 Right sacrifices: King James and ESV

      Scripture: Psalm 51:19 Psalm 51:19 Psalm 51:19

      • Josh turns to verse 19 and substitutes phrasing from King James: Then shalt thou be pleased with the sacrifices of righteousness.
      • He then names the ESV: then you will delight in right sacrifices.
      • David is not doubling back to claim animal offerings can erase confessed guilt. When God restores his people, their worship will no longer be empty performance.

    5. 12:06 Worship in spirit and truth

      Scripture: John 4:23-24

      • Josh ties that restoration to Jesus’ conversation with the woman at the well in John 4: a time is coming and has now come when true worshipers worship the Father in spirit and in truth.
      • Do not skim the two-verse ending. Now that David has experienced restoration, he wants it for all God’s people; now that his lips are singing, he wonders what Zion’s song would sound like together.

    6. 13:08 Zion’s song becomes the church’s song

      Scripture: Revelation 5:12 Isaiah 2:2-3 John 4:28-30 Romans 11:17-21

      • The church already knows the chorus: Worthy is the Lamb who was slain.
      • Isaiah describes the nations streaming to Zion. Jesus tells the Samaritan woman that true worship is no longer tied to one mountain; she sings a song of salvation in her hometown and others join.
      • Romans 11 will not allow Christian arrogance or careless claims here, but at the end of Psalm 51 God is gathering and renewing a people for his praise.

  4. 16:51 Sin damages more than the sinner

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 7:10

    • Josh loves Psalm 51 because it forces a right view of sin. Sin gives tunnel vision: all you can see is what you want, then how to protect yourself, reputation, marriage, and position.
    • Godly sorrow that leads to repentance gives the wide lens back. True repentance asks not only what sin did to me, but what it has done to Zion, whom I hurt, and what I broke.

    1. 17:36 The debris field around David

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 11:1-27 1 Peter 3:7 2 Samuel 12:14

      • David was beginning to grieve what his sin cost others. Bathsheba was dragged into a powerful man’s gravitational pull and taken advantage of; Josh refuses the claim that she pursued David or that beauty is a sin, and tells men to stop blaming women and to control themselves as the stronger vessel.
      • Uriah lost his life protecting Zion. Joab was dragged into a cover-up. Servants were forced to betray men in the field. David’s family felt the aftershock, and the nation was shaken by a compromised king.
      • Nathan told David his behavior had given God’s enemies ammunition. David’s sin weakened God’s people and strengthened God’s enemies: faithful people exhausted, cynics louder, skeptics convinced it is all a front.

    2. 20:06 Against you, you only—and not only against God

      Scripture: Psalm 51:4

      • Every sin is ultimately against God, but no sin is only against God. Psalm 51:4 is not David denying he sinned against Bathsheba or Uriah; it recognizes God as the supreme judge whose glory he treated as worthless.
      • Sin is a storm surge that begins when truth is suppressed and desires are crowned, then blows through homes, churches, and communities until the world says they preach one thing and live another.

    3. 21:12 The heavy burden of leadership

      • Josh does not think Christian leaders are more sinful than everyone else; their sin makes headlines because of reach. If a pastor falls, the flock suffers. If a father abandons responsibility, the family suffers.
      • When a leader fails, the first question is not how quickly he can return to the pulpit. The church must ask who has been harmed, who needs protection, what truth must be told, and what faithfulness requires now.
      • The goal of restoration is not getting a gifted person back on stage—God can gift someone else—but getting a wounded sinner healthy in the family of God. Usefulness matters, but the soul matters more. True repentance stops asking, Can I keep what I had?

  5. 23:40 Grace rebuilds what repentance cannot undo

    Scripture: Psalm 51:18

    • The second reminder is that grace rebuilds. David prays, Build up the walls of Jerusalem, because some things only God can fix.
    • David can confess and step out of the shadows, but he cannot resurrect Uriah, remove Bathsheba’s grief, erase years of family consequence, or preserve Zion’s spiritual welfare by himself.

    1. 26:16 Repentance is not a time-travel eraser

      • Far too many Christians treat repentance like an eraser with time-travel capabilities. You cannot unsay words, unbreak a promise, un-betray confidence, or force a wounded person to trust you on your schedule.
      • Forgiven people still may need time. Neither is anyone condemned to stare at ground zero forever. Tell the truth, stop defending what God is confronting, accept consequences, make restitution where possible, and demonstrate faithfulness without demanding anything from anyone else.
      • Build the walls, Lord, is a big prayer. Biblical grace is more than feeling better: it forgives, cleanses, disciplines, changes, and leads toward truth, responsibility, repair, reconciliation, and worship.

    2. 26:53 When salvation walked through Zacchaeus’s door

      Scripture: Luke 19:1-10

      • Zacchaeus did not repay the people he cheated so that Jesus would save him. When salvation walked through the door, his relationship with money and with other people changed.
      • Grace makes you want to repair what greed damaged and take responsibility for what selfishness caused.

    3. 28:28 The miracle of one stone

      • Josh says we miss little miracles by waiting for finished walls. The miracle is a willingness to bring a single stone: one honest conversation, one kept promise, one humble confession, one honored boundary, one day of sobriety, one step from darkness into light.

    4. 29:25 Restore means repair

      Scripture: Galatians 6:1 Matthew 18:15-17

      • Galatians 6 is commonly used and commonly misunderstood: if someone is caught in a sin, you who live by the Spirit should restore that person gently, watching yourselves.
      • Restore leans toward repair. Read with Matthew 18, the goal is not merely putting a person back into a role but setting what went wrong back into place—like a dislocated shoulder that must be popped in before it can heal.
      • Christians skip the popping: speaking truthfully and carefully, protecting the harmed, and nursing the sinner back to spiritual health, remembering it is only by grace that you stand.

    5. 31:58 Fellowship and office are not the same restoration

      • Josh speaks from church hurt and from recently calling a church he had held bitterness against. Restoration to fellowship and restoration to office are not the same thing.
      • Forgiveness rests on Christ’s finished work; leadership is still governed by Christ’s qualifications. Trust is demonstrated character over time. A fallen leader’s desire to return does not make returning wise or biblically permissible.
      • Do not let anyone shepherd you if no one inside the church shepherds them. Elders must worship with the people; the shepherd smells like the sheep. A church without in-house elders, in Josh’s view, is asking for disaster.

    6. 34:18 Grace says brother; wisdom says not currently shepherd

      • Grace preaches to a fallen pastor, You’re my brother. Wisdom adds, You’re not currently my shepherd; you might need me to shepherd you.
      • Sometimes removing someone from leadership is part of popping the joint back in. Avoidance is not restoration. Leaving one church to escape a hard conversation and seeking the same influence elsewhere relocates the wound and disrupts the unity of Christ’s whole body.
      • Restoration is not always possible, but as far as it depends on us, where it is safe and appropriate, renewed hearts pursue peace. David remained king; that does not mean every restored leader should keep the role. You do not need the same role to give God glory.

    7. 36:14 Failure is not the end of usefulness

      Scripture: Psalm 51:13 Psalm 51:1

      • Failure will always be part of the story, but it does not have to be the end of usefulness. David could not give Uriah his life back, but he could spend the rest of his life building what sin attempted to destroy—preparing the temple, strengthening Jerusalem, teaching transgressors, and giving future generations Psalm 51.
      • Satan meant to destroy David’s witness. God did not call the evil good; he exposed, judged, and did not rescue David from consequences. Yet grace was so sufficient that David’s confession became Zion’s song. Sinners still pray, Have mercy on me, God, according to your unfailing love.

  6. 37:41 Renewed hearts protect unity

    • A heart renewed by grace cannot remain indifferent to the church’s unity and welfare. It is interested in the church being healthy.
    • Christians must stop speaking about the church as they speak about Starbucks or Adidas. The church is not a business, hearers are not consumers, and Josh is not a CEO—even if stewardship uses organizational tools.

    1. 38:52 Consumer Christianity and disposable people

      • Josh names the consumer pattern: stay if you enjoy it, leave if disappointed, recruit an offense club, gossip in the lobby, and stop caring when you go elsewhere.
      • When God renews a heart, he creates concern for all the churches and all brothers and sisters. If someone belongs to Jesus, they have not ceased to matter because they worship in a different building.
      • We still owe truth rather than slander, blessing rather than bitterness, forgiveness rather than vengeance, and honest attempts at reconciliation. Separation can be necessary; contempt never is.

    2. 41:42 From riding lions to never setting foot here

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 13:1-2

      • Josh tells of an encourager who once described a vision of Josh and Nolan riding lions and fighting the enemy. Years later a matter of preference led the man elsewhere, and the language changed from fellow warriors to you can’t worship with me.
      • He tells it to put everyone on trial: how can we speak with spiritual certainty about one another and then treat one another as disposable?
      • Paul says we can speak with the tongues of men and angels, understand mysteries, and have mountain-moving faith, but without love we are nothing. The test of maturity is not claimed visions but whether God’s unfailing love pours out of us.

    3. 43:27 Unity is load-bearing

      • If spirituality produces dramatic declarations but cannot produce honest conversation, forgiveness, and enduring concern, something has gone terribly wrong.
      • The greatest evidence someone has heard from God is that they have learned to love the people of God with an enduring love. Children, new believers, and the world are watching.
      • Someone offering a right sacrifice does not leave the altar to gossip, create factions, or hold a sledgehammer against unity.

  7. 44:30 Rebuilt lives offer righteous worship

    Scripture: Psalm 51:19

    • Number four: rebuilt lives offer righteous worship. Verse 19 again: then you will delight in the sacrifices of the righteous, burnt offerings offered whole, then bulls on your altar.
    • Then is doing heavy work: after confession, cleansing, brokenness, restoration, after God rebuilds his people—then.

    1. 45:28 Does God delight in sacrifices or not?

      Scripture: Psalm 51:16 Psalm 51:17

      • People stumble over verse 16, You do not delight in sacrifice, three verses before verse 19. The problem was never the bull; it was the heart bringing the bull.
      • Last week’s prophets confronted people who used sacrifices as camouflage: bring an offering, then go home a tyrant; sing, but will not help the woman in need; keep the ritual with no intention of being changed.
      • When a broken and contrite heart rises and brings gratitude, obedience, humility, praise, and surrender, God delights in it. The sacrifice does not produce renewal; it flows from renewal.

    2. 47:19 The wrong outrage

      • Josh laughs at boycott Christianity that is more upset by a non-believer’s explicit song than by Christians singing good songs with filthy hearts.
      • You can sing beautifully, but if you are filled with pride, heaven hears an audition gone wrong. Giving, serving, preaching, and communion can be worship—or reputation, recognition, ambition, and empty ritual.

    3. 48:41 Get on the altar

      Scripture: Romans 12:1

      • If he leaves the church with one series line, it is this: God is not impressed with what you bring to the altar unless you are on it too.
      • Burnt offerings mean all of it, nothing held back. Take it. Take it all. I just want you.
      • Paul lands biblical worship in Romans 12: in view of God’s mercy, offer your bodies as a living sacrifice. That is true worship. David goes from pleading for mercy to placing everything on the altar.

  8. 50:45 A greater David is still building

    Scripture: Psalm 51:18-19

    • Jerusalem’s walls would fall, the kingdom divide, the people go into exile, the temple be destroyed, rebuilt, and destroyed again. If the hope of Psalm 51 rested on David, it would not be hopeful.
    • God used David’s life to point to a greater David: a king who would not hurt the people he was called to protect, a priest who would bring lasting relief, a builder whose kingdom sin, death, hell, scandal, and unfaithfulness cannot bring down.

    1. 51:34 Jesus, living stones, and the cornerstone

      Scripture: Matthew 1:21 Matthew 16:18 Psalm 118:22 1 Peter 2:4-5

      • Gabriel told a virgin the boy’s name would be Jesus, because he came to save his people from their sins. David could not resurrect Uriah; Jesus walked out of his own grave.
      • Jesus promised Peter, On this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell will not overcome it. He bore the guilt of adulterers, murderers, liars, gossip, prideful leaders, wounded followers, broken families, and fractured churches.
      • The stone the builders rejected became the cornerstone. Peter, an eyewitness, says believers come to the living stone and are built as living stones into a spiritual house, a holy priesthood offering spiritual sacrifices through Jesus.

    2. 53:33 Stop hiding; bring the ruins

      Scripture: Revelation 5:9 Ephesians 2:1-5 Matthew 16:18

      • God is still building—not merely repairing one ancient wall, but gathering living stones from every tribe, language, people, and nation, making the dead alive and enemies family.
      • Renewal was never supposed to end with David. Jesus died so people could come into the light without fear of rejection. Stop hiding. Bring the ruins and the ashes to the builder, tell the truth, receive mercy, and keep building one stone at a time.
      • The God who restored David builds his church, and the gates of hell will not prevail against it.

  9. 54:33 Where does he need to build?

    • Josh shepherds the end of the series: God is still building. Are you bold enough to ask him to build a masterpiece in you?
    • Elders join him for ministry. The Lord’s Supper is in the chairs. He asks hearers not to rush: where does the Lord need to be welcomed in to do some building? Some long-buried things need to come out; some stalled work needs to resume.
    • Be faithful. Lean in. Ask, God, where do you need to build?
