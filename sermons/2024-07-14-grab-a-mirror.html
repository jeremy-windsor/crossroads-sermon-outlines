Overview

  1. 00:08 Nothing superior about me

    Scripture: Galatians 2:20–21

    • One of the hardest parts of Josh’s job is knowing that the stage, lights, cameras, and boldness can make it seem he is coming from a place of superiority. There is nothing superior about him; the only thing worth boasting in is that Christ saved him and lives in him.
    • He would be a bum of a husband without God’s word and Spirit, a trash father without his heavenly Father showing him how to love, protect, discipline, and care, and still an addict without God’s grace patching him up time and again. Christ makes better husbands and better daddies. He isn’t a seminary saint with fancy language; he is a guy who loves Jesus, called to boast in the cross until he dies.
    • He stands next to legends like Paul, who gave their lives to preserve the gospel, and joins him in declaring, “I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me… if righteousness could be gained through the law, Christ died for nothing.” He clings to grace like the last parachute on a diving plane.
    • Every week he comes up humbled and amazed that God let him lead his church. He has wrestled with the word all week and been pruned first, and he prays the church carries that pruning to spouses, families, neighbors, and co-workers, and is faithful to speak when opportunities come.

  2. 03:09 A new series: Misquoted

    • Today launches a new series, Misquoted. Over five weeks the church will look at some of the Bible’s most recognizable verses and how they have been twisted and taken out of context inside and outside the church, plus a couple of phrases people treat as Scripture that aren’t.

  3. 03:33 Test your foundation: Scripture is not about you

    • Before getting into it, Josh wants to test the foundation. Brace yourself: the holy Scriptures are not about you. In our culture it is hard to believe anything isn’t about you, but trust him, it’s better this way.
    • He could argue much of the church growth movement of the ’80s, ’90s, and early 2000s came from preaching built around you, tailored to make you feel like God’s special treasure child: his plans are only missing you; he wants to lavish you with every earthly blessing; live your best life now; everything happens for a reason, even your choice of toilet paper. It felt like Uncle Sam’s “We want you.” He gets why people flock to it; it’s nice to feel important, accepted, and needed. But just because it feels good doesn’t make it right.
    • God’s word speaks to you and sometimes addresses you directly, but it isn’t about you. You aren’t the hero God has been missing; his hero put in work 2,000 years ago on Calvary. The Bible is about Jesus: how he lived, died, and was raised for you, good news of grace for sinners.

    1. 06:05 One faith, in Jesus Christ

      Scripture: Philippians 2:5–7 Ephesians 4:4–6

      • In Philippians Paul says to have the same attitude as Jesus, who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his advantage but made himself nothing, taking the nature of a servant. Jesus humbled himself for you, not because God’s kingdom couldn’t carry on without you, but because it doesn’t want to carry on without you.
      • Paul tells the Ephesians there is one body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all. There aren’t different versions of the gospel or different ways to salvation, not your truth, my truth, or your uncle’s truth: just one faith that saves, and it is in Jesus Christ.

  4. 07:06 How Scripture gets misquoted

    Scripture: Matthew

    • Misquoting Scripture doesn’t always come from a bad place; sometimes it does, and the series will probably come back to that. Some comes from an innocent place: a well-meaning life group leader reads the text and asks, “What does that mean to you?” Now everyone is empowered to interpret Scripture by how it makes them feel. Boom: misquoted.
    • In a good life group no one cares what you think it means; they care what it actually means. They ask who wrote it, why, when, to whom, and what the cultural setting was. To take it further, ask the genre. Josh polls the hip-hop fans and declares country the best genre, then asks: is it narrative, like Matthew, where today’s first misquoted text is; poetry, like Psalms or Proverbs; or history, like Joshua, which the church just spent seven weeks on?
    • His Bible school theology professor barked before every three-hour lecture: “Context, context, context.” A verse is surrounded by verses, in a chapter, in a book, that help you understand it. Chapters and verses didn’t always exist; they help with quoting and sharing, but that is also the problem: you can grab one and throw it out.

    1. 10:06 Just read it naturally

      • Many Christians think the Bible is a mystery you must Sherlock Holmes with a magnifying glass. The Bible uses symbolic and figurative language, but for the overwhelming majority, the meaning is right there on the surface. Just read it naturally.

    2. 10:33 Quoting the Bible doesn’t make it biblical

      Scripture: 1 Kings 11:3 Luke 10:37

      • Just because you quote something from the Bible doesn’t mean it’s biblical. First Kings 11:3 says Solomon had 700 wives and 300 concubines; in Luke 10 Jesus says “Go and do likewise,” and the polygamist rejoices. People actually do this: “Well, it’s in the Bible.” So is my bookmark. Where in the Bible? Who wrote it, to whom, for what purpose? Most of the time the context is in the very next verse; just keep reading.
      • Anyone who removes a text from its context is trying to make the verse reinforce what they want to be true. That is dangerous: instead of conforming to his words, we seek to conform his words to us.
      • “But Josh, Solomon was the wisest man who ever lived and had many wives.” That’s your argument? His life was a mess; his wives led him astray, and with each one he was less devoted to the Lord, because the model from creation was one woman and one man becoming one flesh for one life.

    3. 12:31 Praise God for faithful singles

      • Josh doesn’t think the church celebrates this enough, and that is on him: if God has you living the single life faithfully, not sleeping around, praise God for celibate, godly singles. Being single allows you to be more devoted to the Lord. Don’t feel pressure to get hitched; hitch yourself to the Lord, use your free time to serve, and enjoy picking what to watch on Netflix. In Josh’s house it’s a vote that always ends in a tie.

    4. 13:35 Let the clear interpret the unclear

      • There are confusing parts of the Bible with different interpretations, and the series won’t deal with those. Multiple interpretations are unavoidable, and that’s fine as long as they don’t violate what Paul would consider the doctrines of first importance. Let the clear passages determine how we interpret the unclear; let the Bible read the Bible, so Scripture never fights against itself.
      • After that Bible 101, Josh says if you can read and have a heart to walk with Jesus, you have everything you need: pray, ask him to help you, and get started, and the church will walk with you.

  5. 14:47 The purpose of it all: life in Jesus’ name

    Scripture: John 20:31 2 Corinthians 1:20 Acts 4:12 John 5:24

    • Never forget the purpose. At the end of his gospel John says these are written that you may believe Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name. John isn’t writing so you can have a good life now; he wants you to receive eternal life.
    • So read your Bible front to back and mark every one of God’s promises. When you get to the Gospels and Paul’s letters, they all say the same thing: every one of God’s promises is yes in Jesus.
    • Peter, the first disciple to toss a net for human souls, preached to the Jews in Jerusalem that salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven by which we must be saved. Jesus claimed this about himself in John 5: whoever hears his word and believes him who sent him has eternal life and has crossed over from death to life. Do you believe Jehovah took on flesh to execute God’s plan to save your soul?

    1. 16:30 Luke 4: a mic-drop moment

      Scripture: Luke 4:16–21 1 Peter 1:3–4

      • Jesus claims he is the fulfillment of all Scripture. In Luke 4 he goes to the synagogue in Nazareth, is handed the scroll of Isaiah, and reads, “The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor… to proclaim the year of the Lord’s favor.” He rolls up the scroll, sits down with every eye on him, and says, “Today this scripture is fulfilled in your hearing.” I am the one Isaiah wrote about eight centuries earlier.
      • Don’t get it twisted: the Bible isn’t a self-help book to make your life better. It is about Jesus, the only one who can make your life better. And here is the comfort: his mission is to redeem us. Scripture points to Jesus, and Jesus brings us back to God. I was lost but now I’m found; his blood washed away my sins; his resurrection guarantees my future; by grace through faith in Christ alone we are his adopted sons and daughters, given new birth into a living hope and an inheritance that can never perish, spoil, or fade.
      • That is why this series matters: Josh wants to know the one who saved him, walk with him, and be like him, as he wanted to be like his dad. He is drawn to the Scriptures not because they offer eternal life but because they tell him about the one who did. He can sit under Jesus’ teaching, become his disciple, drop his nets, and fish for men like Peter.

  6. 19:01 The first misquoted verse: “Do not judge”

    Scripture: Matthew 7:1

    • The first misquoted verse is Matthew 7: “Do not judge, or you too will be judged.” It is misquoted inside and outside the church. Unbelievers use it as a Christian chokehold: try to talk to someone about their sin and they say, “Doesn’t your God teach you not to judge?” If you aren’t equipped you might say, “I guess you’re right.”
    • Our culture loves it because it doesn’t want to be told what to believe or how to act. You’d think the church would stand strong and influence the culture, but that mindset has leaked in and infected the church.

    1. 20:00 The curtain of church hurt

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:16–17

      • In the church people hide behind a curtain of church hurt. Josh has church hurt too, knows there is legitimate church hurt in the room, and wants to be sensitive, but many claim theirs came from judgmental Christians. In 15 years of ministry, no one has ever told him they left a church because they were living in sin, a loving brother pointed it out lovingly, and they didn’t like it. Not once.
      • That tells him confrontation in the church is tricky work, but it is necessary and biblical. In 2 Timothy 3:16 Paul tells the young pastor Timothy that all Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training in righteousness. That shatters the idea of a private interaction with the truth; Christians have a duty to spur one another on to live according to the truth.

    2. 21:23 Reading Matthew 7:1–6

      Scripture: Matthew 7:1–6

      • In Matthew 7 Jesus is finishing the Sermon on the Mount, and verses 1–6 are all about relationships, so the rest of the time will be relational. Josh reads the whole section: do not judge; the speck and the plank; do not give dogs what is sacred or throw your pearls to pigs. So the work is cut out for us: don’t judge, and oh, watch out, some of your friends are pigs.

  7. 22:30 What does “judge” mean?

    • First, define the word. Josh uses Logos, not the Bible app, so nobody should think Pastor Josh hasn’t read his Bible in five months on YouVersion. One word can have a range of meanings; the Greek word Jesus uses, krino, brought up about seven.

    1. 23:19 To evaluate or discern

      Scripture: Matthew 22:29 John 7:7

      • On one end, judge means to evaluate, discern, or observe: this is good, this is bad, this tastes good, this sounds horrible. Is Jesus forbidding evaluations, criticism, or ever telling someone they are wrong? Your Christian friend who is sleeping around certainly wants that to be true: live and let live; it’s not your job to tell me, it’s your job to accept and applaud me. The culture has gone so far that anything short of affirmation is called judgment.
      • Jesus can’t mean that, because most of his ministry was telling people they were wrong. In Matthew 22 the Sadducees, who don’t believe in the resurrection, which is why they’re sad, you see, press him, and he says, “You are in error because you do not know the Scriptures or the power of God.” In John 7 he tells his brothers the world hates him because he testifies that its works are evil.
      • The culture’s view of judgment fails a logic test: aren’t you telling me I’m in error for pointing out errors? You’re doing the thing you tell me not to do.

    2. 25:20 To condemn or punish

      Scripture: John 3:17

      • Another meaning is to condemn or punish: the picture of Jesus coming with a sword at the end of the age to separate and destroy evil, which many struggle to see. When Jesus came by birth he came to save; God did not send his Son to condemn the world but to save it. When he comes back he comes to separate, condemn, and judge.
      • So Christians can judge by evaluating the beliefs and actions of others, even critically, but cannot judge by condemning them. It isn’t that you shouldn’t say hard things; it’s how you view the person when you say them.

    3. 26:14 Luke’s version: what is your motive?

      Scripture: Luke 6:37

      • Luke records the same teaching with slight variations. People who poke holes point to differences among Luke, Mark, and Matthew, but different authors with different personalities and styles are a strength. Luke says: do not judge and you will not be judged; do not condemn and you will not be condemned; forgive and you will be forgiven. That confirms the reading.
      • What is your purpose behind the judgment: to knock them down a peg, or to restore them, lift them up, strengthen your relationship and, more importantly, their faith? Are you going over your words so they represent your love and Jesus’ love, or hurling carefully selected nasties hoping they leave a mark? It’s the approach.

  8. 27:22 Don’t assume you know their heart

    • Josh thinks this is the key: are you assuming you know the heart behind their actions? Being wrong doesn’t mean they intended it or even know it. Maybe no one ever cared enough to talk to them, or they didn’t grow up with godly parents like you. For all you know they came to Christ last week; heaven is still throwing a party over that soul, and you’re worried about what they did Friday night. It’s a long game. God gave you grace; shower some of that surplus on them.
    • Do you remember how hard it was to remove things from your life after Christ? Some are so far removed they’ve forgotten how nasty and dirty they were when he picked them up and dusted them off. Going from pornography to patience and purity, from lust to self-control and faithfulness, from cussing to goodness, from hatred to love, from selfishness to generosity didn’t happen overnight.
    • Some have been swimming in God’s grace so long they think it’s a private pool party: doing cannonballs while a neighbor shows up bloodied and beaten, looking to wash off, and they worry it will get on them. That is the mindset Jesus rebukes. How you look at others reveals how you think Jesus looks at you; those who find it hard to give grace are the least educated on God’s grace.
    • Matthew 7 isn’t telling us never to judge anything; it is telling us how. It’s an instruction manual.

  9. 29:55 The speck and the plank

    Scripture: Matthew 7:3–5

    • Josh rereads the famous metaphor. The surgery is necessary, but there is a qualification: wrongful judgment grows from self-righteousness. Jesus is describing a hypocrite, someone who accuses everyone else of what they are guilty of themselves. Those in glass houses shouldn’t throw stones.
    • It’s like the man 150 pounds overweight rebuking you for a glass of wine with your wife, the pastor preaching generosity while embezzling from the homeless ministry, or the self-appointed modesty police who gossips and talks down on women behind their backs; we do need more women talking to women about modesty. Believer, sell your glass house and buy a house of mirrors.
    • Take the log out of your own eye first; go to the mirror before you go outside chucking rocks. Jesus says because you have a log, not if. If you think you’re logless, go get coffee with Josh; something nasty is certain to be up there. Get tweezers, find a mirror, work on it, then go to the person with humility, and your rebuke can be effective.

    1. 32:20 We need people to hold up the mirror

      • The Christian journey isn’t private, though everyone wants it to be. We need believers to help with our specks and planks, tell us when we’ve missed something in the mirror, and hold up the mirror when we refuse to look.
      • “Hey man, I noticed your double take at Miss Tight Dress. Obviously I noticed too. I’ve struggled with my eyes and with lust for years. I’m praying for you; pray for me too. Let’s lock arms, walk face down to the worship center, and sit in the front row so we see nothing but Jesus.” Satan doesn’t know what to do with that. That is love: the error is exposed, and I’m reminded I’m not the only one with a struggle.
      • It’s all about attitude and approach; that is the difference between biblical and sinful judgment. If their sin stands out more than yours, you’re not ready for surgery; put down the scalpel.

    2. 33:58 Thought bubbles

      • You know far more about your own heart and head than theirs. Josh sometimes asks his wife what she’s thinking, carefully. Wouldn’t it be great if everyone had thought bubbles for a day? We’d know who to trust and who to invite to dinner, and he’d know what people think of this sermon. We know we’re all hypocrites: you are aware of every selfish, twisted thought you’ve had, and for all you know you might be the worst one in the room. No one knows your flaws like you, but you aren’t fooling God.
      • There is something wrong with all of us: we so easily see their flaws because we don’t want to face our own. Watch what happens if everyone recognizes they’re the worst of the bunch. The biggest project in this church isn’t Susan or Dave; it’s you. Lead with that humility and your judgments will be drenched in the gospel.

  10. 35:54 Verse 2: the measure you use

    Scripture: Matthew 7:2 1 John 1:9 Galatians 6:1 1 Corinthians 10:12

    • That’s good, because in verse 2 Jesus says that in the same way you judge others you will be judged. You know you’re a hypocrite when you’re more bothered by someone else’s sin than your own.
    • When we sin we should confess, and he will forgive and purify us from all unrighteousness: 1 John 1:9, an ongoing life of confession. But we should want that forgiveness for others too. Paul says if someone is caught in a sin, you who live by the Spirit should restore that person gently, but watch yourselves or you may also be tempted. Paul doesn’t mean don’t step in their stuff; he means don’t think yourself elevated because it didn’t get on you. When people fall around us we don’t kick them; we restore them, because next time it might be you. Paul also says if you think you’re standing firm, be careful that you don’t fall.

    1. 37:14 Struggling to forgive

      • A more significant sign you’re a hypocrite: you struggle with forgiveness. Christian, there isn’t much room for that. Many say they forgive but keep it tucked away for a rainy day. Refusing to forgive means you’ve forgotten all God has forgiven you. “I can forgive Josh, but I’ll never forget”: forgiving doesn’t erase memory, but you choose not to keep bringing it up because you rest in his grace. Forgiveness is allowing someone else to rest in his grace too.

    2. 38:09 Condemnation is separation

      • Recall the two definitions: evaluate and condemn. Condemnation is separation, so you judge improperly when your desire is separation, cutting ties over disagreements. You can’t control how you’re received, only your attitude and approach, but if you go in wanting to separate, your heart isn’t right.
      • Ask what is more important to God: the person in front of you or the issue you hold dear. How can you cut off a person when God didn’t cut you off, or call someone too far gone when God never said that of you?

  11. 39:11 Verse 6: pearls to pigs

    Scripture: Matthew 7:6 Proverbs 9:8

    • But there is a point where it’s too much. Josh reads the tiny parable in verse 6. Dogs and pigs were despised then and rarely kept as pets; Josh’s wife keeps asking for a pig, and he jokes they’ve already raised two. Wild animals care only about their next meal; toss a hungry pig a pearl and it can’t eat it, but you look like you’ve got some meat on you. They won’t appreciate your precious pearls.
    • Jesus’ point is that some people don’t care what God wants for them or says to them and have no appreciation for what is right, good, and holy. Josh is reminded of Proverbs 9:8, which draws a line down the middle: do not rebuke mockers or they will hate you; rebuke the wise and they will love you.

    1. 40:45 Before you go in

      • As you handle these relationships, stay humble and check your heart. Cover your approach in prayer: go to God before you go to them. Pray for them, and your heart will soften; ask God for the words and to direct the conversation. Most of all, seek the forgiveness you need before pointing the finger and using a scalpel.

    2. 41:15 When to shake the dust off

      Scripture: Matthew 10:14

      • There will be times when you share the gospel or speak wisdom into someone’s life and they reject everything and ridicule you, no matter how prepared you are, and you’ll have to judge when to stop speaking and turn away. In Matthew 10 Jesus tells the disciples that if anyone won’t welcome them or listen, shake the dust off your feet when you leave that home or town.
      • Don’t let it discourage you from speaking the truth in love in the future. Sometimes it just means moving on to the next village.

  12. 42:19 “What about him?” Stay in your lane

    • Jesus tells Peter he will die a martyr’s death. Peter sits with that, sees John walk by, and points: “What about him?” Jesus essentially says that’s none of your business; stay in your lane; you have enough on your plate. Follow me.
    • How can you possibly know what the Lord is doing in another person’s life? If you really want to know, ask them: “What’s the Lord been convicting you of lately?” Then listen before assembling your sermon. Maybe they’ll share their struggle and save you the trouble of pointing it out.

    1. 43:33 Truth with contagious gentleness

      • The church needs truth tellers, but with a contagious gentleness. Some avoid conflict at all costs, and they know who they are; others wear conflict goggles that spot pre-conflict so they can arrive with a pitchfork. Some, no matter how gentle they think they are, come off like a rhino, and that’s who they are.
      • But sometimes you go in with the right heart, attitude, and game plan, all prayed up, and they hate everything you say and hate you for saying it.

  13. 44:37 You’re in good company

    Scripture: John 1:11 Isaiah 53:3–5

    • Jesus knew when he came that we would reject him. John 1 says he came to that which was his own, but his own did not receive him. When you proclaim the truth about God, share the gospel, and instruct and rebuke brothers and sisters for the sake of holiness, and you get smashed for it, remember you are in good company.
    • He was despised and rejected, a man of suffering familiar with pain; he took up our pain, was pierced for our transgressions and crushed for our iniquities, and by his wounds we are healed. What can we say but, Lord, if you did that for me, I will live for you whether received or rejected. When you abide in Jesus you will be humbled, full of grace and mercy, seeking to lift people up rather than tear them down, and you will bear good fruit.

  14. 46:07 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays, humbled by so much instruction: it is a balance to strike. We can’t be afraid to say what is right, true, and good, but we can’t be a jerk about it. He asks God to help them walk this faithfully, to take his orders to heart, to love holiness and seek to be perfect as he is perfect though they are so flawed, and not to be discouraged but picked up time and again by his grace and mercy.
    • He thanks God for the honor of the journey, for being a co-heir with Christ though undeserving, and for loving him and saving his soul.
