Overview

  1. 00:33 Are the wicked getting away with it?

    Scripture: Psalm 94:3–6

    • Josh is half wet from a baptism before the service. Last week the church launched Reclaim, a series on what God did for the Jews exiled to Babylon: in exile or in return, God remains faithful to his word, so when we fail, forget who we are, or sin, we can find comfort that through Jesus God has reclaimed us and nothing can change that.
    • We can also find comfort that the wicked will be dealt with. Josh wrestles with whether the wicked are getting away with it and when injustice will be dealt with. He knows the answers, but if you struggle there too, you aren’t alone: the psalmist asked, “How long, Lord, will the wicked be jubilant?”
    • Those who love God have long cried out, “How long?” We long to be in the garden with him again. In Egypt Israel was oppressed, but Babylon came in response to their disobedience. Whether we did the evil or it was done to us, we cry out to God to rise up and judge the earth.

  2. 02:50 God will repay Babylon

    Scripture: Jeremiah 25:12–14

    • We find hope looking over history, because God is faithful to deal with the wicked. Jeremiah 25: when the 70 years are fulfilled, I will punish the king of Babylon and his nation, and “I will repay them according to their deeds and the work of their hands.” In 539 BC Cyrus the Great conquered Babylon; God did exactly what he said.
    • It might seem God is letting evil go unpunished, but consequences for wrongdoing are inevitable. God will punish evil.

  3. 03:54 Fulfilled promises point to unfulfilled ones

    Scripture: Revelation 22:11–21

    • When Josh sees fulfilled promises, he looks for unfulfilled ones: if God said it then and did it then, anything he has said that isn’t done yet will be done. If he says he is coming to judge the earth, take him at his word.
    • He went right to Revelation 22 and reads it: let those who do wrong continue to do wrong, and those who are holy continue to be holy; “Look, I am coming soon; my reward is with me”; blessed are those who wash their robes; outside are the dogs; the Spirit and the bride say, “Come”; and “Yes, I am coming soon.”

    1. 05:46 Continue to do right

      • Josh loves John’s words: continue to do right, continue to be holy. “But Josh, I’ve relapsed, failed my family, ignored my marriage, sinned against God.” You can continue to do wrong or continue to do right; in Jesus you have grace.
      • Repent: it doesn’t mean say you’re sorry; it means stop. Turn from your sin, turn off influences, turn off social media if you must, because it is so toxic, and start socializing with Jesus. If you want to keep doing good and bear fruit, dig deep into his word, plant roots there, and never let go.

  4. 06:40 “Blessed are those who wash their robes”

    Scripture: Matthew 5 Revelation 22:14

    • Everyone knows the Beatitudes in Matthew 5, but Josh just read the last of the seven beatitudes in Revelation: “Blessed are those who wash their robes.”
    • In college Josh went a whole semester using the same shower towel. He was broke, and he was clean every time he used it, so it isn’t a big deal, but when word got out it became one, and a roommate training to be a pastor stapled this verse to his towel. Josh hopes he understands it better by now, because Jesus isn’t talking about laundry day.

  5. 07:48 Robes washed in the blood of the Lamb

    Scripture: Galatians 2:20 Revelation 7:14 Ephesians 1:7–8 Titus 3:4–5 Revelation 2:7

    • The phrase is a picture of our participation in Christ’s death, like Paul told the Galatians: I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. In Revelation 7 an elder tells John that those who came out of the great tribulation have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.
    • So are your robes washed or soiled? Josh isn’t asking whether you sin; we all sin. He is asking whether you have gone to Jesus and asked him to wash your sins away. In him we have redemption through his blood. You can’t wash your robes; only Jesus can. He saved us not because of righteous things we had done but because of his mercy, through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.
    • Those washed clean by the blood of Jesus will forever have the right to the tree of life in the New Jerusalem, fulfilling Jesus’ promise in Revelation 2: to the one who is victorious I will give the right to eat from the tree of life in the paradise of God.

  6. 09:50 Heaven is not inclusive

    Scripture: Revelation 21:8 1 Corinthians 6:11

    • Heaven isn’t inclusive. It is exclusively for those cleansed from their sins by faith in the blood of Christ, whose names are written in the Lamb’s Book of Life. For soiled robes there is an outcome rarely spoken of even in church: the cowardly, the unbelieving, the vile, the murderers, the sexually immoral, those who practice magic arts, the idolaters, and all liars will be consigned to the fiery lake of burning sulfur, the second death.
    • Josh pleads: nothing is more important than this. It isn’t everyone who has ever committed these sins who is excluded; Josh is going to heaven, and he was on that list. That is what some of you were, but you were washed, sanctified, and justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ.
    • It isn’t those who commit these sins but those who commit to them: who love them, stubbornly cling to their PornHub, refuse to drop the bottle, lie, cheat, steal, kill, and destroy to get what they want, don’t believe justice is coming, and refuse Christ’s gift of salvation.

  7. 11:40 Why Revelation in a series on Ezra?

    • “Josh, you do know we’re in a series in Ezra, right? What’s this got to do with that?” Everything. If we look at promises God made and kept 25 centuries ago, we had better look at the promises Jesus made 20 centuries ago and assume he intends to keep those too. And if God used the unlikeliest people then, assume he still uses unlikely people today.
    • Josh points out two things from Ezra: the unlikely treasure and the unlikely sources. Quick recap: last week showed the unlikely person God used, Cyrus the Great, king of the world at the end of the 70-year exile, who surprisingly gave the exiles a chance to return home and rebuild. God is reclaiming his people.

  8. 13:01 The unlikely treasure: God’s people give to God’s projects

    Scripture: Ezra 1:6 2 Corinthians 9:7 Philippians 4:19

    • Verse 6: all their neighbors assisted them with silver and gold, goods, livestock, and valuable gifts, besides the freewill offerings. Only about 50,000 returned; with the Jewish people having grown in exile, hundreds of thousands if not millions stayed in Babylon. Josh doesn’t assume those who stayed opposed the project; those moved to rebuild had tremendous support.
    • The timeless truth: God’s people give to God’s projects. Some give gold, some silver; some were led to rebuild and some to support financially. Whether money, time, or skill, Paul adds the way: not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.
    • Better still, Babylon kept changing hands, so some neighbors were probably Assyrian, Babylonian, or Persian. God was using false worshipers’ money to rebuild his temple. My God will meet all your needs according to the riches of his glory; he can use unexpected sources to provide for his church and mission.
    • All our resources belong to God, so when you give you are returning what he gave you. The sharp flip side: if you don’t give, you are keeping what isn’t yours, and there is a word for that: stealing from God. The neighbors gave because they believed God was doing what he said. We can see their faith in their giving; can we see yours? Crossroads wants to be a church that gives freely and joyfully, and invites people to join the work.

  9. 16:14 Verse 7: humility in a pagan king

    Scripture: Ezra 1:7 Philippians 2:3–4 Isaiah 44

    • Verse 7: Cyrus brought out the temple articles Nebuchadnezzar had carried off. It makes no sense unless you have seen God work in your own life. God moved Cyrus’s heart, and when God softens a heart you always see fruit.
    • Kings loved adding “the Great” to their names. But when God moves a heart, one of the first fruits is humility, which seeks to bring glory to God: God is great. Ambition says keep the gold and build your kingdom, with more buildings, statues, and treasures. Humility says, “I might be a great king, but I’m not the King of kings,” and values others above yourself.
    • What blows Josh away: as Isaiah 44 showed last week, Cyrus did not acknowledge God, yet a non-believer acts in obedience to God. There is more outpouring in a rebel king with a new heart than in most professing Christians today. That is proof of God at work, and you can’t fake it; you might fool the masses, but you cannot mock God.

    1. 18:25 Pride versus humility

      Scripture: 1 Peter 5:6

      • Anyone can call themselves a Christian, but only true believers submit to God. Pride says my way is best; humility says, “God, your way, not mine.” Pride says, “I got this”; humility says, “God, I can’t do this, and even if I could, I don’t want to without you.”
      • Pride says, “Listen to me; I’ve read the Bible cover to cover 18 times; I’m an expert.” Humility says, “The more I learn, the more I see how much I have to learn,” always at the feet of Jesus. Pride says, “This isn’t fair; I need more”; humility says, “Thank you; this is more than I deserve,” even in the red. Pride wants vengeance; humility says, “I might not trust you, but I forgive you.”
      • God humbles us not to break us but to bless us; he resists the proud and gives grace to the humble. Josh offers a dangerous prayer: ask God to reveal areas of pride, then walk away from them and walk in humility, and come tell him what happens. As Peter says, humble yourselves under God’s mighty hand: either you humble yourself and he lifts you up, or you lift yourself up and he humbles you.

  10. 21:02 Verse 8: the great reversal of riches

    Scripture: Ezra 1:8

    • Cyrus returns everything Nebuchadnezzar took. It is the great reversal of riches, and it is voluntary. Whenever God moves a heart, watch that life to recognize what it looks like: Cyrus had the articles brought by Mithredath the treasurer, who counted them out to Sheshbazzar the prince of Judah.

    1. 21:31 Housekeeping: accountability in the church

      • Because the church keeps growing, Josh reviews some housekeeping. He doesn’t like pastor-led models where one guy does whatever he wants. Crossroads is elder-led: a board of elders holds him accountable to the mission and vision and to his fiduciary responsibility. He thinks accountability is a must, and even if someone leaves for another church, he would never let anyone shepherd his heart or his resources without in-house accountability.
      • A stewardship team of pastors, elders, and members, an elder team that approves the budget and reviews finances monthly, and an external audit every three years mean at least 14 people who aren’t his employees see the dollars every month, so givers can be confident their gifts go to God’s glory.
      • Money isn’t coming out of their ears: the $1.5 million budget ending April 1 is about $7,000 short, but expenses were managed well. The church was faithful to give and the leaders faithful to use only what was given. Some churches in the Valley run multimillion-dollar operations with zero elders, which is how pastors end up in orange jumpsuits on CNN. No one is above accountability.

  11. 24:16 Verses 9–11: the inventory

    Scripture: Ezra 1:9–11 Daniel 1 Ezra 1:11

    • It isn’t a random tangent: when God moves hearts, there is stewardship and accountability. The inventory lists gold and silver dishes, pans, and bowls, 5,400 articles in all, which Sheshbazzar brought with the exiles from Babylon to Jerusalem.
    • Ezra mentions Sheshbazzar four times and then never again. Most scholars agree it was another name for Zerubbabel, governor of Judah and grandson of King Jehoiachin. Exiles were often given Babylonian names, as Daniel was given Belteshazzar in Daniel 1.
    • For the numbers people with calculators out: the listed items add up to 2,499, yet verse 11 says 5,400 in total. Some cry contradiction, but slow down: Cyrus gave back what was stolen, and the neighbors gave as well, for a grand total of 5,400 articles to carry across the wilderness.

  12. 26:14 Ezra 2: ancient Social Security cards

    Scripture: Ezra 2 Ezra 2:62

    • Ezra 2 is a long list of family units and their numbers. Josh jokes he will read it, then doesn’t, but explains why it matters. Today we care about family history only if there is a trust fund; to Old Testament Jews it was very important to prove ancestry.
    • How would you prove you are an American citizen? Josh wouldn’t accept a driver’s license from a sketchy DMV employee, but a birth certificate or Social Security number would do (keep that to yourself). Should someone here illegally have the same rights and privileges? Josh’s opinion is no: they have human rights that should be upheld, but citizens are afforded rights noncitizens are not.
    • These genealogies were like ancient Social Security cards. Without proven ancestry you couldn’t receive the rights of the covenant people, who were set apart with a God-given task and couldn’t let outsiders lead them into idolatry. Returning Jews also couldn’t reclaim family land without proving lineage.
    • Josh’s favorite as a pastor: the lists kept non-Levites from serving in the temple and collecting a free pastoral check. Verse 62 shows some who searched for their family records and couldn’t find them, and they weren’t given a chance to be nice; they were excluded from the priesthood as unclean. Don’t leave Babylon without your ID.

  13. 30:01 200 singers: a singing faith

    Scripture: Ezra 2:65 Psalm 150:6 Zephaniah 3:17 Revelation 5:7–9 Job 38:6–7

    • Josh’s favorite verse in chapter 2: they also had 200 male and female singers. God is moving hearts, and they aren’t going on this journey without a decent playlist. Since the first Exodus the Jews composed songs to honor God; in captivity there weren’t many days for singing in the streets, but now they were heading home with a new song.
    • God loves music and loves your praise. We have a singing faith, and God loves to see his people’s joy in his faithfulness pour out in music. Psalm 150 says let everything that has breath praise the Lord, and over a dozen instruments are named in Scripture. Elijah preaches on worship next week and may be mad Josh let this play out.
    • Not only does God delight in the music; he joins in. Zephaniah says the Lord your God will rejoice over you with singing. With so much complaining about worship, Josh wonders whether there will be complaining in heaven, because heaven is filled with worship: in Revelation 5 the living creatures and elders fall before the Lamb with harps and golden bowls of incense and sing a new song. The elders have music and haze.
    • There was singing at creation: God asks Job who laid its cornerstone while the morning stars sang together and the angels shouted for joy. Heaven worshiped before man knew a single note. David plays and Saul is less of a jerk; Paul sings hymns and God shakes the prison doors open. How can we be lazy in worship when God delights in it, joins it, and responds with wonders? Worship isn’t just music; it is a divine expression of a changed heart. It is a miracle.

  14. 33:07 A 900-mile walk home

    • Good thing they had music: the walk home was 900 miles, like walking from here to Redwood National Park in NorCal. Josh doesn’t know why Ezra leaves out the details of the journey, which would be preaching gold; he will have to ask him one day.
    • But very little about your journey matters if you don’t have Christ. Why would it matter where you go or what you experience while alive if you don’t know where you will go when you die?

  15. 33:53 They gave even more, and the leaders went first

    Scripture: Ezra 3

    • The first thing they do on arriving in Jerusalem is give even more, willingly and according to their ability, no different from how God’s people should give today.
    • Expect things of those who lead your church: be supportive and encouraging, but notice how the tribal leaders and Zerubbabel were faithful first and the people followed. Crossroads uses the same model; Josh challenged every elder and staff member to go first in So Boldly this year, and now it is the church’s turn to sign up. Elijah picks up in chapter 3 next week with the altar.

  16. 34:40 The unlikely sources: the big three

    • For the rest of the message Josh turns from the unlikely treasure to the unlikely sources, the big three: Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes, the most powerful men in the world during the homecoming, ruling a Persian Empire that stretched from Africa and southern Europe to China.
    • God used three pagan kings to help a small tribe of exiles go home and rebuild. Unlike the first Exodus, when Pharaoh let them go with little and they complained that at least they were well-fed slaves, these kings let God’s people go, packed their bags with gold and silver, and signed executive orders securing their freedom. God uses who he chooses, and if he can use Cyrus, Darius, and Artaxerxes, he can use you.

    1. 36:11 Cyrus: God works through the secular to build the spiritual

      • God used Cyrus to liberate his people, yet many Christians pretend they can tell God whom he can and can’t use. If God needed a gifted speaker, Moses is out; a clean record, Paul is out; a backbone, Peter is out. We see problems; God sees potential.
      • God can work through the secular to build the spiritual. Someone actually left the church because Crossroads gave Nikes to kids with holes in their shoes; the brand was so bad in their eyes they couldn’t see the person in need. Josh doesn’t care what a brand is doing; if they have a shoe, he will put it on a foot in need.
      • God loves reclaiming and repurposing; he is into thrifting, but with people. You don’t need a seminary degree to build God’s kingdom; you need a new heart.

    2. 37:49 Darius: God handles opposition

      Scripture: Ezra 6:7 Ezra 6:7

      • The series will cover it in a couple of weeks, but God has no problem handling opposition. Darius says, “Do not interfere with the work on this temple of God.” In our country one president signs executive orders and the next comes in and undoes them all with angry signing. Darius instead upholds the direction and vision: let the governor and elders of the Jews rebuild this house of God on its site. Only God can do that.
      • Darius even gives them an expense account from the royal treasury: “The rebuild’s on us. Check, please.” Some Christians object that Darius might be doing it for personal gain. That is the point: pagans will be pagan, but God can use even false motives to serve his will. Trust him.

    3. 39:01 Artaxerxes and Ezra: doers of the word

      Scripture: Ezra 7:10 Psalm 119 Psalm 119:97 Psalm 119:98 James 1:25

      • Artaxerxes, a pagan king, granted Ezra authority to teach the law, which sparked revival. When God wants to punish a nation he sends a Nebuchadnezzar; when he wants to bless a nation he sends an Ezra.
      • Ezra was born in Babylon yet cherished the Jewish Scriptures. With no temple, the priests and Levites were on something like a 70-year vacation, yet Ezra studied up. Verse 10 says he devoted himself to the study and observance of the law and to teaching it, and even the king noticed.
      • Maybe the author of Psalm 119 isn’t the mystery we think; some say David, Josh says why not Ezra: “Oh, how I love your law! I meditate on it all day long.” Ezra didn’t just study it; he obeyed it, like the next line: “Your commands are always with me and make me wiser than my enemies.”
      • God doesn’t want big brains; he wants humble hearts. James says those who look intently into the perfect law and do it will be blessed. If knowing the truth doesn’t result in obedience, the truth becomes a toy for entertainment instead of a tool for growth. Ezra fought that by teaching it to others.

  17. 41:03 What does this have to do with us?

    • For some, studying God’s word hardly ever makes it onto the calendar, and you may need to clear things out to make space. Pray a dangerous prayer: ask God to reveal what in your life is less important than him, and fill it with him instead.
    • For others, study is no problem; you meet the Lord morning, afternoon, and evening. Your problem is a big head and no fire: you know stuff from your study, but you never kill stuff after it. You read about not forsaking the gathering but won’t come; about not sinning in anger but take no steps; you are impatient with your wife, hold grudges, watch porn constantly, know the Ten Commandments and the Beatitudes, and your theology is rock solid, but you don’t look anything like Jesus. You need a new heart, not a different study Bible.
    • Josh believes most of the church lands here: making time for the Lord, renewing their minds, seeking his ways, trying to act like Jesus, falling and getting back up to grab grace. The problem is you don’t have a single person you are willing to share it with. You are afraid they won’t like it, will ask a question you can’t answer, or will find out you don’t have all 800 proverbs memorized if you serve, launch a life group, or join a team.

    1. 43:27 No one is qualified for the job

      • No one is qualified for the job. Josh is wrestling with that personally. When he was offered the lead pastor role, he thought, “Me? Why? I can’t do that.” If they had waited for him to learn it first, they would still be waiting. We don’t learn the job; we learn on the job.
      • Every Christian has a role in God’s kingdom buildout. Some lead and some teach, but everyone serves, gives, encourages, and builds up. Are you willing or afraid to be used by God? Are you open to being his next unlikely candidate to bless and serve his church? God doesn’t need you, but he would love to have you join the work. Start praying, “God, how do you want to use me?”

  18. 45:04 Closing prayer before communion

    Scripture: John 19:30 2 Corinthians 5:21

    • Josh prays that God would have his way in his life, heart, and mind. He believes Jesus’ words on the cross, “It is finished”: the war is done and the debt paid. He accepts that finished work and invites God’s continued work in him.
    • He prays that the church would not be great in number or flashy, only faithful, and that God would build the body from top to bottom, preacher to nursery worker. Coming to the table, he reflects that Jesus took our place, knowing all we had to offer; he had no sin but became sin so we could become the righteousness of God, an exchange we didn’t deserve, done because he loves us.
