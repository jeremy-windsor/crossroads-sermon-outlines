Overview

  1. 01:50 Closing out a series

    • Chris loves closing out a series because he gets to look back over it, pull it together, and talk about anything he wants; during a series you can’t encroach on the passages and themes of the pastors coming up. Nothing was missed, but recapping leads into the message God has given him: the present is already wrapped, and he gets to stick the bow on top.

  2. 02:44 Before the wilderness: the baptism

    Scripture: Matthew 3:13–17

    • Steve reminded the church a few weeks ago that before Jesus went out to live one of Chris’s worst nightmares, not eating for 40 days (“I like to eat”), he stopped to be baptized by John. Chris reads Matthew 3 from verse 13: John tried to deter him, Jesus said it was proper to fulfill all righteousness, heaven opened, the Spirit descended like a dove, and a voice said, “This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.”
    • Chris is with John: “Excuse me, you want me to baptize you? Maybe it’s opposite day.” John knows who he is looking at; he has been preparing the way for him. Jesus says, in effect, “You heard right.” Then he is led by the Spirit into the wilderness, a place so terrible even the animals didn’t hang out there, where he fasts and is tempted by the devil.

  3. 04:55 Called, prepared, then sent

    • These 40 days are how Jesus prepared for his ministry. For 30 years he lived a quiet life as a carpenter; then his first stop was baptism and his second the wilderness. Chris believes the wilderness prepared him not because he needed it but because we did: everything Jesus did was for us. He was in heaven “living the dream,” but he came to die for us, live for us, and be an example for us. He was called, he prepared, then he began his ministry.
    • Paul did the same: called on the road to Damascus by a literal blinding light, he went to Tarsus and Arabia for perhaps 5 to 10 years, and when he came back he spread the gospel throughout the world.
    • All of us are called to live out and share the gospel every day, but some are called to do it as a career: missionary, lead pastor, executive pastor, worship, kids, or youth pastor. Chris knew in high school God was calling him to ministry, but he needed time to mature and study God’s word. If you are called, don’t skip preparing; in ministry you follow Christ and lead others who are following him, and you can’t lead if you haven’t prepared.

  4. 08:15 Forty days of temptation

    Scripture: Luke 4:2 Luke 4:1–13

    • Jesus is in a wilderness where nothing living wants to be, which is probably why Satan was out there; it fits his character. Luke 4:2 says that for 40 days he was tempted by the devil. We see three temptations, but Jesus was tempted the entire time, without food, which makes us irritable and vulnerable, and with no rest from Satan: “an all-out temptation festival,” like Coachella without the music, the people, or the weird statues.
    • Chris reads the whole passage again, Luke 4 from verse 1, so the church remembers what Jesus had already gone through before the third temptation.

    1. 10:41 The first two temptations

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 8:3 Deuteronomy 6:13

      • Jesus has fasted 40 days and is hungry, and the devil tempts him with food; “Satan didn’t see that one coming.” In the second, the devil offers all the kingdoms of the world, their splendor and authority. Both times Jesus rejects it with Scripture: Deuteronomy 8:3 and Deuteronomy 6:13.

  5. 11:15 The highest point of the temple

    • The devil takes Jesus to the very tippy top of the temple. Most estimates put it 10 to 20 stories high, 100 to 200 feet; one wall overlooked the Kidron Valley, 45 stories, about 450 feet to the bottom. Either way it’s a long way down.
    • Chris gives some morbid context to move us from reading Scripture as a story to understanding that these things really happened. One study of falls: under 30 feet, a 76.5% chance of living; 40 feet or higher, a coin flip; 60 feet or higher, survival doesn’t even rate. Without intervention Jesus has a 0% chance of surviving.

  6. 13:26 Satan flips the script with Psalm 91

    Scripture: Psalm 91:11–12 Psalm 91:13

    • Satan (after Chris accidentally says “Jason,” who isn’t Satan) is getting smarter. Jesus has fended him off with Scripture, so he flips the script and quotes Psalm 91:11–12: he will command his angels concerning you, and they will lift you up so you will not strike your foot against a stone. He quotes it correctly, because Jesus would have corrected him.
    • But the devil conveniently stops at verse 12, because he wouldn’t like verse 13: you will tread on the lion and the cobra; you will trample the great lion and the serpent. Not what the old serpent wants to hear.

  7. 14:45 Satan knows Jesus

    Scripture: John 1:1–3 Colossians 1:15–20 Colossians 1:16

    • These 40 days are one battle in the long war Satan has waged against God and his creation, and Satan has known Jesus since his creation. John 1: in the beginning was the Word, the Word was with God and was God, and through him all things were made. All, not some.
    • If someone says that means only the universe and not heavenly beings, Colossians 1 from verse 15 says the Son is the image of the invisible God, and in him all things were created, things in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, thrones, powers, rulers, and authorities, and through him God reconciled all things by making peace through his blood shed on the cross. Chris rereads verse 16: there is nothing Jesus didn’t create.
    • That hits Chris at the core, because we want to put God in a box and say Jesus was just a man. Wrong. The only thing he didn’t create was God himself. Jesus knows Satan as one of his creations, and Satan knows Jesus as his creator.

    1. 17:19 He wants to be like the Most High

      Scripture: Isaiah 14:13–14

      • Satan wants to be like Jesus, and Isaiah 14 tells us he wants to be in the place of God: “I will ascend to the heavens; I will raise my throne above the stars of God… I will make myself like the Most High.” Not the servant Jesus who would give his life, but the God role with ultimate power. Filled with pride, arrogance, jealousy, and hatred, he wants to destroy what God holds dear, his creation.

    2. 18:29 Satan before the throne in Job

      Scripture: Job 1:6–12 Job 2

      • Chris reads Job 1 from verse 6: the angels came before the Lord and Satan came with them, from roaming the earth. The Lord pointed to his blameless servant Job; Satan said Job feared God only because of God’s hedge and blessing, and the Lord gave everything Job had into Satan’s power, but not the man himself.
      • Satan just moseys right into the presence of the Lord with the angels. If Chris were one of the other angels, he would have wanted to take a swing (“a big old uppercut”). Worse, Satan comes back again in chapter 2.
      • Satan was created by Jesus, and Job makes clear he has spent time before God’s throne. In the wilderness he isn’t a stranger; he knows Jesus’ power, which is why he tempts him to do miracles. Humans debate and reject that Jesus is God, but Satan knew exactly who Jesus was and wanted to dethrone him.

  8. 20:52 Satan knows Scripture and has fought from the beginning

    Scripture: 1 John 5:19

    • Satan knows Scripture; he just quoted it. He can read, and he knows the prophecies about the coming Messiah without needing us to interpret them. He knew Jesus was coming and was ready to fight.
    • He has fought God and creation since the beginning: he tempted Adam and Eve in the garden; humanity became so evil and corrupt that God sent the flood, and so prideful that God scattered us at Babel; parents sacrificed their children to Molech, which is still being done today; evil men have slaughtered millions. 1 John 5:19: the whole world is under the control of the evil one.

    1. 22:32 Herod and the prophecies fulfilled

      Scripture: Matthew 2:15 Matthew 2:17–18

      • Chris believes Satan used Herod to try to kill Jesus as a toddler. When the Magi were warned in a dream not to report back, Herod ordered every child two and under in Bethlehem killed, but God warned Joseph and they fled to Egypt, fulfilling two prophecies: Matthew 2:15, “Out of Egypt I called my son,” and Matthew 2:17–18, Rachel weeping for her children.
      • You’d think Satan would realize God already knows and the prophecies will be fulfilled; ultimately he is just a pawn in the story God has already written. Yet he is so prideful he still thinks he has a chance: “So you’re saying there’s a chance.”

  9. 24:07 Jesus answers: do not put the Lord your God to the test

    Scripture: Luke 4:9–13 Deuteronomy 6:16

    • Back to the passage: the devil set Jesus on the highest point of the temple, quoted the psalm, and Jesus answered, “It is said: Do not put the Lord your God to the test,” and the devil left him until an opportune time.
    • Satan, who has stood before God’s throne, fought God’s plans since creation, and knows Scripture, challenges Jesus with the Psalms, thinking he’s clever. But Jesus, the author of Scripture and creator of all things, hits back with Deuteronomy 6:16: do not put the Lord your God to the test as you did at Massah.

    1. 25:11 Massah and Meribah

      Scripture: Exodus 17:1–7

      • Jesus quoted a verse referencing when Israel did put God to the test. Chris reads Exodus 17: camped at Rephidim with no water, the people quarreled with Moses and grumbled, “Why did you bring us out of Egypt to make us die of thirst?” Moses cried out that they were almost ready to stone him; God told him to strike the rock at Horeb, water came out, and Moses called the place Massah and Meribah because they tested the Lord, saying, “Is the Lord among us or not?”
      • Satan would have known that, and was probably there whispering in their ears to get them to complain. Jesus hits back harder than we realize because we don’t usually understand the depth of the Scripture he uses.
      • Jesus stops at “Do not put the Lord your God to the test.” Moses warned Israel not to test God again as at Massah, but Jesus never tested God, so there was no “again” for him; we are simply not to put God to the test, period.

    2. 27:23 Questions aren’t testing

      Scripture: Luke 16:29–31

      • Scripture doesn’t say we can’t ask God questions, struggle, and need him to help us find answers or have faith in what we can’t understand. Chris teaches his children to question, seek, and ask God so their faith will be their own. God can handle it; if you don’t believe he can handle your questions and doubts, the god you think you know isn’t the God of the Bible.
      • Testing is “God, if you give me the job, heal my child, save me from this situation, then I’ll believe and follow you.” That kind of testing won’t change our hearts: in Luke 16, Abraham tells the rich man that if his brothers won’t listen to Moses and the prophets, they won’t be convinced even if someone rises from the dead.
      • In our desperation we want to test God not so that we believe in him but so that we get what we want, and at that point God truly becomes a genie in a bottle.

    3. 29:24 The sign of Jonah

      Scripture: Matthew 12:38–40

      • The Pharisees tested Jesus by asking for a sign, and in Matthew 12 he answers that a wicked and adulterous generation gets only the sign of Jonah: three days and three nights in the heart of the earth. Jesus says, “You want a sign? Here you go”: dead and buried, then out of the grave on the third day, conquering death so that this wicked generation and everyone else might have life.
      • Are some of you testing and asking for a sign? There’s your sign: the cross, the grave, the resurrected Jesus. If that’s not enough, nothing ever will be.

  10. 30:35 Until an opportune time

    Scripture: Luke 4:13 1 Peter 5:8

    • Satan tried to twist Jesus’ own words, the words he gave the psalmist, and Jesus answered with the verse that trumped it all, a mic-drop moment. Satan put his tail between his legs and ran off.
    • But don’t miss the end of verse 13: he left him until an opportune time. Satan wasn’t done; he went back to the war room to devise a new attack. Until he is thrown into the lake of fire, he is out to destroy God’s people and creation.
    • Some want to convince you the devil isn’t real, but then Jesus was hallucinating in the desert, talking to cacti and rocks. 1 Peter 5:8: be alert and of sober mind; your enemy the devil prowls around like a roaring lion looking for someone to devour. We need to stand firm.

  11. 32:23 Three observations

    • As the series closes, Chris makes three observations from Jesus’ temptation in the wilderness.

    1. 32:29 1. Scripture can be twisted to fit what someone wants

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 11:12–15

      • Satan twisted Scripture to deceive Jesus, and he does it to us, and we are more vulnerable than Jesus was. Chris sometimes watches a Mormon apologist to understand how they interpret Scripture, and notices that no matter what the text says, he makes it fit, sometimes pulling out an obscure expert to define a Greek word in a way no one else does.
      • Satan is brilliant at taking truth and creating a lie close to it that sounds amazing and even seems to make sense, but if it’s not in God’s word, it’s false doctrine. Paul warned the Corinthians in 2 Corinthians 11 about false apostles masquerading as apostles of Christ: Satan himself masquerades as an angel of light, so his servants masquerade as servants of righteousness, set on deceiving the lost and the church.
      • But a brother or sister who brings an incorrect interpretation isn’t necessarily masquerading; they may just need a mature believer to help them. Provide love and correction, and don’t confuse them with someone trying to steer you wrong.

    2. 35:11 2. You can’t spot twisted Scripture if you don’t know it

      Scripture: Psalm 119:11 Deuteronomy 6:6–9 Matthew 4:4

      • Psalm 119: I have hidden your word in my heart that I might not sin against you. Deuteronomy 6, which Chris would love to dive into: impress these commandments on your children, talk about them at home and on the road, tie them on your hands and write them on your doorframes. And Jesus in Matthew 4: man shall not live on bread alone but on every word that comes from the mouth of God.
      • Are you talking about them, writing them? They couldn’t take scrolls home, so they wrote them on their doorposts. Most of us carry the whole Bible on our phones and have five or six Bibles at home. Are they getting used? We need to be in the word consistently, studying and memorizing it, so we know when it is being twisted.
      • The Secret Service never gives trainees a fake bill; they study the real thing so well they know a fake instantly. Do you know God’s word so well that you recognize a false gospel instantly? If not, you’d better get started.

    3. 37:31 3. Scripture is your best defense and offense

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:16–17 Ephesians 6:10–17 James 4:7 1 John 4:4

      • Scripture is the words of God himself, and if Jesus used it to fend off Satan, it’s good enough for us. 2 Timothy 3:16–17: all Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training, so the servant of God may be thoroughly equipped. Ephesians 6:10–17: put on the full armor of God to stand against the devil’s schemes, for our struggle is not against flesh and blood, and take the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God.
      • Scripture thoroughly equips us, and the word of God is our sword. If you have accepted Christ and are in his word, you have everything you need to stand firm. As James says, resist the devil and he will flee from you. He isn’t gone forever, but you are ready, because the one who is in you is greater than the one who is in the world.

  12. 40:05 Bad news and good news

    • Chris has bad news for those who haven’t made Jesus their Lord and Savior: you have no defense against the devil and his lies, no armor of God. The good news is that Jesus made a way to free you from sin and Satan’s lies and make you a child of God: he suffered and died to pay the penalty of your sin and bring you into right standing with God. Anyone with questions can find Chris, another pastor, or the Engage cart.

  13. 41:02 Jesus understands

    Scripture: Hebrews 4:14–15 Matthew 27:46

    • Chris addresses a claim he has heard: that because Jesus was God in the flesh, he doesn’t really understand what we go through. He reads Hebrews 4: we have a great high priest who is able to empathize with our weaknesses, one who has been tempted in every way, just as we are, yet did not sin.
    • Everything the Bible tells us to do to defeat sin and temptation, flee, resist, take every thought captive, Jesus did himself; we know because he never sinned. Don’t ever tell Chris that Jesus doesn’t understand. You’re not alone; he has been through it.
    • More than that, he suffered more than we will ever know: the physical suffering leading to the cross and the cross itself, then taking on every sin humanity has ever committed or will commit, and experiencing what he had never known, being forsaken by his Father because of our sin. He cried out, “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” We have no way of understanding what he went through: the suffering, the pain, the rejection, the loneliness. “I don’t think I understand.”

  14. 43:57 Prayer: from our heads to our hearts

    • Chris prays that the last piece, which God drove from his head into his heart this week, would move from the church’s heads to their hearts: how horrific it was, how much Jesus suffered, and how could we not give everything to someone who would do that for us. God made a way where there wasn’t one, loved us beyond understanding, lived as our example, and died a brutal death because we were supposed to be there.
    • He asks that they would hide God’s word in their hearts, know it so well that they can stand as Jesus stood and say, “It is written,” tell the devil off and stand firm, and have a desire to know God and his word like never before.
