Overview

  1. 00:04 Beginning Unstoppable

    • The church begins a series in Acts called Unstoppable, and Josh’s blood is pumping on another level, maybe from the 4:00 a.m. thunder. He frames why: looking for a meeting schedule from Dave, the chair of the elders, he ran into a December 2020 email telling the body about a couple who found out they were sick the day after Christmas Eve services. It reminded him of a strange and tragic time in the capital-C church.

    1. 00:57 2020: when the church stopped being the church

      • In Josh’s opinion 2020 was the hardest year to oversee the church ever. A few, like John MacArthur, insisted the world needed the church doors open, but there was tremendous fear inside and outside the church, and fear caused a lot of mistakes.
      • Josh admits he led like a coward during Covid: he went along with everything, preached to empty rooms and said nothing, ran to get tested, reported who he had breathed on, and kept his distance. He isn’t proud of that period. The email reminded him how churches and the culture isolated the sick to preserve everyone else, shamed people for coming to worship with a mild cough, and fought over masks and vaccines. The capital-C church didn’t speak truth or unity into the divide; it made it worse, and he was part of that.
      • The church stopped being the church because the government said it was unsafe. Being the true church is never unsafe. If green stuff is coming out of your nose and you’re running 104, stay home and rest, but we shouldn’t avoid, shame, or isolate ourselves from you; we should bring you soup. He dredges up these sour memories because when we make mistakes we have a duty to own them and learn from them.

    2. 03:41 How Acts helped Josh reset

      • A deep dive into Acts helped Josh recover and reset from 2020. He was about to walk away from ministry altogether: “This is not what I signed up for.” A few moments in the book reminded him what the Lord intended his church to be.
      • He saw the birth of a community that stands apart, a divinely established institution that doesn’t think or act like the world, thinking more about the life to come than the current one, marked by a love that didn’t change in the face of suffering, persecution, fear, sickness, or death.
      • The most amazing part: it wasn’t the people doing it. They were discovering something in themselves that changed because of their faith, and the spread of faith resulted in a spread of love. They were still themselves, sometimes with new names, but something had been added and given; something in them was moving that was not from themselves.

  2. 05:13 God reveals himself, then entrusts his plan to those who love him

    Scripture: John 1:14

    • Zoom out: God revealed himself first through the prophets of old, then by taking on flesh, the logos (John 1, the Word became flesh), and in Acts we read of the work of the Holy Spirit in and through his church. The Creator declares his plan of salvation, comes to accomplish it, and then entrusts the work of sharing it with all nations to those who love him.
    • They haven’t even been called Christians yet, but they are Josh’s brothers and sisters. Something unites us that isn’t a title, a denomination, culture, or even religion: something in us pulling us toward what we used to fight against.

    1. 06:14 Any good in me doesn’t originate with me

      • The more Josh follows this pull, the more he realizes any good in his life doesn’t originate with him. He finds “great message” comments in the lobby encouraging, because he labors hard for the church’s benefit and God’s glory, but he would have nothing of worth to say had Christ not said it first and had God not declared his word through the prophets and the Acts of the Apostles.
      • Even his desire to be like Christ comes from God inside him. If anyone sees anything good in him, he hopes they notice Christ standing next to him, dragging him all the way. This is the prayer of all who truly love God: “Lord, shape me so that when they interact with me, they’re forced to witness you.”

    2. 07:33 A man came along claiming to be God

      • There has always been a general, natural view of God: creation looked around and wondered about a Creator. Then a man came claiming to be God. He wasn’t the first or the last, and you might call him crazy, but his claims were backed with divine demonstrations. Even so, he convinced only a few good men, who dined with the Divine and then saw him killed.
      • As Acts shows, a community of faith developed and recognized that the same God who walked with them in Galilee was now inside them. Guided by that presence, they did things they couldn’t do before; as they surrendered to the Spirit they came to be known as Christians, and the church began an unstoppable movement.
      • Many came to describe this God in three persons as the Trinity. Nobody is a bigger fan of the doctrine than Josh, but any effort to describe the indescribable comes up short.

  3. 09:06 Luke leaves us hanging

    Scripture: Luke 24 Luke 24:44–52

    • Dr. Luke wrote Acts, the same Luke who wrote the Gospel. Most of us finish his gospel and turn the page to John, forgetting how Luke ended by the time we reach Acts. But Luke leaves us hanging; on Netflix you would be frustrated waiting for the next season.
    • Jesus is crucified and raised, and in chapter 24 he eats broiled fish with his disciples and says everything written about him in the Law of Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms must be fulfilled. He opens their minds to understand the Scriptures: the Messiah will suffer and rise on the third day, and repentance for the forgiveness of sins will be preached in his name to all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. “You are witnesses of these things.” Stay in the city until you have been clothed with power from on high. Then near Bethany he blesses them and is taken up into heaven, and they return to Jerusalem with great joy.

    1. 10:31 Acts is the sequel

      • That is where Acts comes in: the sequel, the rest of the story. Luke is part one, Acts part two, and Luke wants to show the origin story of the Christian faith.
      • Luke was a Gentile and a physician who later became friends with Paul, did ministry with him, and traveled with him. Who better to tell Paul’s origin story and his involvement in the early church? Luke wrote Acts to explain how the gospel went from a hope of the Messiah among the Jews to the talk of the town in the heart of Rome, and eventually the entire world.

    2. 11:14 Sunday can’t be your only meal

      • This is a six-week series through a 28-chapter book, so they will skip some things. Josh challenges everyone to read and study on their own and fill in the gaps with a good commentary; ask him, the teaching team, or any staff member for recommendations. What happens on Sunday must not be your full diet. If it is, you’re starving yourself. Please stop.

  4. 11:51 How to approach Acts: prescriptive or descriptive?

    Scripture: Acts 1

    • Each new series starts with genre: Genesis is history, Leviticus law, Proverbs wisdom, Psalms poetry, Isaiah and Jeremiah prophecy, Matthew and Luke gospel narratives, Romans an epistle, and Acts history. The bigger argument is whether Acts is prescriptive, telling us what to do, or descriptive, telling us what God did in a specific place and time. Josh says yes: a little of both.
    • If you want to argue it is 100 percent prescriptive, keep reading: at the end of chapter 1, after Judas’s betrayal, the apostles choose between Joseph and Matthias by praying and casting lots, and the lot falls to Matthias. How would you feel if that was how the church chose elders or children’s workers? “You guys look suitable; let’s grab some dice.”
    • That part is descriptive; we have other ways to determine whether someone is qualified. Prayer, careful study, and discernment uncover whether something is prescriptive or descriptive. Some parts are obviously meant to be applied, which becomes clear as we compare them with other Scripture; other parts simply tell you what happened.

    1. 14:15 Wrestle with the text, not with each other

      • We can wrestle with the text without wrestling with each other, because the Lord prayed we would have unity. Josh tells any Christian who asks: don’t love an idea more than you love your neighbor. If our preferences are dividing us, they are extra-biblical. Let us be one as he and the Father are one.

    2. 14:41 Signs and wonders are not the point

      • Acts is full of signs and wonders, tongues, prophetic words, healing the sick, and raising the dead, but that is not the point of the book. You might want it to be the main thing, but it isn’t. God did amazing things to launch his church; some he still does today, and some he only did then.

  5. 15:05 Jesus’ work continues

    Scripture: Acts 1:1–5

    • Josh reads the opening: in my former book, Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus began to do and to teach until the day he was taken up. He gave many convincing proofs he was alive over 40 days, spoke about the kingdom of God, and commanded them not to leave Jerusalem but to wait for the gift the Father promised: John baptized with water, but in a few days you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit.
    • These first verses are huge: Jesus didn’t stop his work when he was taken up; he continued it through willing, surrendered apostles filled with the Holy Spirit. Acts has 28 chapters, but Jesus is still doing that work today through willing and surrendered servants, and he will keep doing it until he returns to bring us home.

    1. 16:10 We are a continuation of Acts

      • An American studying European history is an outsider looking in; an American studying American history is a continuation of what they study, and their actions will eventually be history themselves. The same is true for Acts: we are part of it, a continuation of it.
      • The Holy Spirit is very much alive; Josh has seen it in the church’s testimonies, including Adam’s that day, and sees Jesus growing in their hearts. There is completed work, Christ’s finished work on the cross and his defeat of sin and death by walking out of the tomb, and there is unfinished work: his church waiting for him to return.

  6. 17:07 Acts 1:8: power, purpose, and plan

    Scripture: Luke 24 Acts 1:6–8 Acts 1:8

    • Luke starts Acts by recapping Luke 24, the second time we hear about Jesus ascending; a good meeting starts with a review. Then come Jesus’ final words: the apostles ask, “Lord, are you at this time going to restore the kingdom to Israel?” He answers that it is not for them to know the times or dates the Father has set, but they will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on them and will be his witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.
    • If one verse summarizes Acts, it is verse 8, the Lord’s mission statement for his church: “I will give you power so that you can fulfill your purpose, so that we can accomplish my plan.” Many believers get excited and want to talk about the power right away, but Josh wants to look first at Jesus’ first response.

  7. 18:22 Some things are above your pay grade

    Scripture: Luke 24 Deuteronomy 29:29

    • Believer, some things are above your pay grade. When Jesus will come to reign and rule on earth was a closed-door meeting, and you weren’t invited. The disciples assumed it could happen immediately. But in Luke 24 Jesus had opened their minds to understand the Scriptures, and they still had no indication of the timing. Scripture gives us no indication of the timing of his second coming.
    • Remember Deuteronomy 29:29: the secret things belong to the Lord our God, but the things revealed belong to us. The timing of Christ’s second coming falls under the secret things.

    1. 19:25 The date setters

      • Despite this, many have claimed to pinpoint the time: “Hey guys, I got it, come look at my calendar.” Some do it to make a quick buck, and we should pray for them, because the Lord will judge them more harshly than we could. Josh may be naive, but he thinks most simply want to see Jesus face to face and worship him in fullness and took it too far. They are sincere, but learn from their mistakes.
      • It’s not for you to know. If your study takes you to a date and time for his return, you are making yourself the master of your study, the belle of the ball, and you’re not. Acts 1 reminds us we are not sent into the world to be prophets but to be his witnesses. We look for his coming, but we don’t assign a time.

    2. 20:44 “He’s coming soon”

      Scripture: Revelation 12:9

      • Some Christians sidestep by being vague, which annoys Josh: “He is coming soon.” What do you mean by soon? Before you die? Before your generation dies? None of us really knows what the Bible means by soon, and other generations have read their times as signs of the end too.
      • The last couple of weeks he kept hearing, “They’re mocking Jesus at the Olympics; he’s coming soon.” Dude, they mocked him while he hung on the cross; they always have and always will. Jesus calls Satan the prince of this world, and Revelation says he leads the whole world astray.
      • They don’t mock Allah, Vishnu, Brahma, or Buddha, because Satan isn’t worried about man’s creations or wasting his time; he is crafty and has a plan. Why is Christianity always in the crosshairs? Satan knows Roman officials couldn’t produce Jesus’ body because he is risen, the promised one who ascended to sit next to God and promised to return and set everything right. Satan won’t spend time mocking dead men; he is coming after the man who killed death and promises to dethrone him.

    3. 22:49 Prepare your heart and the people in front of you

      Scripture: 1 John 1:9 Matthew 6:10

      • Josh doesn’t know when Jesus is coming, but he knows with everything in him that you can’t afford to sit around waiting. You need to prepare, starting in your heart, then your home and your marriage. Repent, be baptized, ask for forgiveness, and he is faithful and just to forgive you and you will find rest. Let Jesus reign in your life now, so when he comes you aren’t standing in front of him but beside him.
      • “Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.” The believer’s prayer is for God’s kingdom in God’s timing, not what you want when you want it. Leave the secret things to God.
      • One more thing: the person in front of you matters more than the future ahead of you. The church should spend far less time on how it will all go down. No one cares about your eschatology; the only question that matters is whether they are ready.

  8. 24:21 You will receive power

    Scripture: Luke 24:49

    • Shift the focus from what we don’t have, a date and time, to what Jesus says we do have: the 11 will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on them. Describing the Holy Spirit is an honorable and very difficult task, because the truth is he is indescribable; every effort comes up short, and fewer words seem better. Jesus puts it this way in Luke 24: clothed with power from on high.

    1. 25:09 What the power can look like

      • To some he gives miraculous power for signs and wonders, from the staff Moses carried to the shadow Peter cast. Some are given mental power: the Spirit leads us to all truth, teaches us, and illuminates the Scriptures, which was vital as these men took on the task of assembling the word of God. He takes otherwise unimpressive individuals and gives them great wisdom about heavenly things.
      • There is moral power: qualities that set us apart, gentleness with courage and conviction, intense passion with deep patience, honesty, goodness, kindness, and charity. And there is spiritual power, the fruit of the Spirit described as self-control, which makes the believer a strong influence: confident, ready to speak at a moment’s notice, bold when what they say isn’t what people want to hear, humble and transparent, disliking sin in themselves more than in others.
      • Meek is the word that keeps coming to mind: not weakness but confident humility. It is attractive; it makes people pause and ask what is different about this person, which would come in handy for a community spreading the gospel to the ends of the earth.

    2. 27:31 Joy, like a son who knows his dad has his back

      • When this power lands on a person it bears fruit, and it isn’t a difficult labor but one filled with joy and praise. The key to happiness isn’t what you acquire but who acquires you; the Holy Spirit is the source of Christian joy.
      • Josh thinks of his son Nolan, one of his best friends at 10. They do everything together; Nolan feels safe and Josh feels useful. Discipline stinks, but afterward Josh can hold him and tell him how much he loves him; after a bad day or a bad grade he can reassure and encourage him. Nolan will always be his son and Josh always his daddy. The confidence Nolan has that Josh has his back is the confidence Josh has that the Lord has his.
      • He walks down the street singing, “Oh, how he loves us.” “But Josh, have you even seen him? Did you see him on the cross? Did you see him walk out of the tomb?” No. “Then how do you know he loves you?” “I just do.” His heart is so full of God’s love that he has extra for people he doesn’t even know. That is what it is like to be clothed with power from on high.

    3. 30:12 Wait for the power

      • We need this power before we can do anything useful for Christ. A Navy SEAL doesn’t go to battle without his M4, and Christ followers don’t spread the gospel without his power. So they were told to wait. Josh pictures Peter pacing: Jesus opened our minds, we’re ready, let’s go preach. No, Peter. None of them were ready until God poured out his Spirit on them.
      • It would be good for many Christians to lock themselves up in the city for a few days. How many minutes of your day are actually quiet? Not your prayer time or study, but shutting your mouth and sitting in his presence. Add that to your routine and come tell Josh what happens.
      • He isn’t advancing passivity or laziness; he condemns those. But he would rather wait and do nothing than do anything without him. He just wants to walk with God, and that is what his power helps him do.

  9. 32:09 To be his witnesses

    • Instead of speculating over dates and times, the apostles were told to focus on the task at hand: to be witnesses. Witnesses see something and tell others. On the witness stand, the judge doesn’t care about your ideas or opinions; give me the facts. (“You can’t handle the truth.”)
    • A common misconception is that witnessing is reserved for those with the gift of evangelism, so we push it onto someone else’s plate or the pastoral staff. Bringing people in to hear the truth is fine, but you have the relationship and are the discipleship component. Some have a greater measure of that gift, but every Christian is called to bear faithful witness. The question isn’t whether you are a witness; it is whether you are an effective one.

    1. 33:38 Peter and John before the Sanhedrin

      Scripture: Acts 4:19–20 1 Peter 3:15

      • Josh takes a page from Peter and John’s playbook before the Sanhedrin. The authorities say, in effect, your services are getting loud, the gospel is a little intrusive, could you back off? Peter and John answer: which is right in God’s eyes, to listen to you or to him? You be the judges; as for us, we cannot help speaking about what we have seen and heard. Will you be quiet, please? No.
      • If you have seen and believe in Christ, you must speak of Christ, and if anyone, even the government, asks you not to, you must not listen. As Peter later writes in 1 Peter 3: in your hearts revere Christ as Lord, always be prepared to give an answer for the hope you have, with gentleness and respect.
      • Effective witnesses always begin as his students: they read, study, memorize, and learn the doctrine taught by the apostles, then present the facts with confidence from the power they’ve been clothed with, and with respect and love.

    2. 35:10 How you live affects whether you are believable

      Scripture: Titus 2 Titus 2:10 Titus 2:7–8

      • We can always grow as witnesses, but there are also ways to become less effective, and this might sting: how Christians live affects the believability of their witness. A drug addict or convicted felon isn’t your best witness in a murder trial; haven’t you watched Law & Order? A lawyer in the church told Josh that if a jury thinks a witness lied about anything, they wonder if the witness is lying about everything.
      • Homework: read Titus 2. In verse 10, in the NIV, our goal is to make the teaching about God our Savior attractive. We are salesmen; our product is the truth and the gospel, and our direct report is God. Paul tells Titus to set an example by doing what is good, with integrity, seriousness, and soundness of speech, so that those who oppose you may be ashamed because they have nothing bad to say about us.
      • The opposite is true too: if people can say all kinds of bad about you while you’re outspoken as a Christian, you dishonor the word of God, and the Christian is put to shame instead of the opponent, which hurts the momentum, growth, and health of the church. To help God save a lost world, we must be sanctified and work to rid our lives of sin. Nothing is worse than a salesman uninterested in his product, pitching a Mercedes while driving a Camry. Witnesses can’t run around looking like they did before they knew him.

  10. 37:47 Josh’s testimony

    Scripture: Matthew 11:28–30

    • The calling is big, and it feels like big shoes to fill. Wherever you are coming in today, Josh closes by telling about his Savior, because he knows Jesus can do for you what he is doing for him.
    • Josh was full of darkness and pride, drowning in sin, selfish; he caused many people pain, took what wasn’t his, and gave nothing away. He was a fool, and sometimes that guy still rears his ugly head. But he heard the words of Jesus, and keeps coming back to them: come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest; take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart; my yoke is easy and my burden is light. It’s not complicated: it’s Jesus.

    1. 38:59 The freedom of power from on high

      • He remembers the freedom when he received this power from on high and the weight of grace lifted. He couldn’t take back what he had done or the pain he had caused; he was still the same person, but in a single moment he was aware of his brokenness and of God’s forgiveness, and that is all God needs to change everything.
      • He asked God to give him rest, forgive him, teach him, and walk with him, and he is a witness to the whole world that God did it. “I’m not crazy, I’m not a nutcase, I’m a Christian.” He is a living testimony to the grace and mercy of God.

    2. 39:48 The next testimony could be in this room

      • Josh doesn’t know what people are carrying, whether a struggling marriage, kids acting crazy, or a career on the fringes, but he has prayed all week that the next testimony of God’s grace and mercy is in the room. No one can heal the wounds of a sinner like Jesus, and he can also heal the wounds Josh caused others. He isn’t a harsh master but a gentle, patient, kind one.
      • Josh was a drunk, swearing, pleasure-chasing addict and idiot, and God melted his heart. He used to love those things and now hates them. He used to mock Christians as dorky, weak nerds; now he looks up to them and is proud of their faith, persistence, hope, joy, and confidence. He wishes people had known him 15 or 20 years ago: he isn’t the man he was. He isn’t perfect, but he has been transformed, and all he did was rest and sit at the Master’s feet.
      • Jesus sustains him every day, pulls him out of bad patterns, reminds him of his word, his goodness, how far he has come and where he is going, leads him away from temptation, and makes clear what he does and doesn’t want for him. This confidence, joy, freedom, hope, passion, and transformation is what the church was launched on. Josh finds no greater joy than walking with Jesus, and hopes everyone has it too.

  11. 42:59 Closing prayer

    Scripture: Matthew 25:21

    • Josh’s heart breaks for anyone who doesn’t know that confidence, assurance, and joy even as the world falls apart, who feels lonely, ashamed, anxious, or hopeless. He prays that whoever feels lost would find God this morning and learn it isn’t a list or a behavioral standard God is after but our hearts, affections, and joy in him.
    • Everything feels empty without God; when he is added, everything gets meaning and changes. That is the church’s message: God is redeeming everything and will one day complete that work for his glory and the joy of his church. He pleads with God to fill them with power so they can be witnesses who bring him joy, and when it is all said and done hear, “Well done, good and faithful servant.”
