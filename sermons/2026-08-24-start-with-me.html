Overview

  1. 00:39 A repair project that really needs replacement

    • Josh opens with the familiar homeowner mistake of calling a repair easy, only to uncover layer after layer of unsafe work and discover that replacement—not repair—is required.
    • He uses the illustration to challenge a reduced view of Christianity: Jesus certainly changes conduct, relationships, habits, and stewardship, but resurrection would be unnecessary if his purpose were only to renovate the old life.

    1. 00:39 The twenty-minute homeowner project

      • A supposedly simple fix becomes repeated trips to the store as each removed layer exposes another underlying problem.
      • The point of the humor is diagnostic: some things cannot be patched indefinitely because the underlying thing itself must be replaced.

    2. 02:04 Jesus does more than improve behavior

      • Josh lists the kinds of repairs people may ask Jesus to make—better self-control, finances, marriage, speech, sobriety, and character—while insisting that Christian transformation is larger than moral improvement.
      • Following Jesus changes how people live, but the gospel announces new life rather than a polished version of the old life.

    3. 02:25 Psalm 51 turns from David’s deeds to David himself

      Scripture: Psalm 51:1-9 Psalm 51:10

      • Josh recaps David’s first nine verses: appeals for mercy, cleansing, washing, and the blotting out of transgression and iniquity.
      • At verse 10 the prayer changes. David no longer asks only for God to deal with what he has done; he asks God to deal with who he is: ‘Create in me a pure heart ... and renew a steadfast spirit within me.’

  2. 03:30 Justification changes one’s standing; sanctification changes the person

    • Josh introduces two theological terms because they clarify the movement in David’s prayer, while warning that knowing religious vocabulary is not the same as knowing Jesus.
    • Justification declares the believer forgiven and right with God through Christ’s cross; sanctification is God’s continuing work of giving the believer the family resemblance of Christlikeness.

    1. 03:50 Two distinct but connected works of God

      • A message about justification says that Christ died the death the guilty deserved and that their sins are forgiven.
      • A message about sanctification says that the Jesus who saved his people now begins changing them toward Christlikeness.
      • David knows that saying goodbye to yesterday will not be enough unless he wakes tomorrow changed.

    2. 04:54 David’s requests: create in me, restore to me, use me

      Scripture: Psalm 51:10-14

      • Josh reads Psalm 51:10-14, moving from a pure heart and steadfast spirit, to God’s presence and restored joy, and finally to teaching transgressors and singing of God’s righteousness.
      • He organizes the rest of the prayer into three movements: create in me, restore to me, and use me.

  3. 05:38 Create in me: only God has creation power

    • David’s first request is not for coaching but for an act only God can perform: the creation of a pure heart.
    • Josh follows the Bible’s creation theme from Genesis through David, the prophets, Jesus, Paul, and Revelation to show that salvation is God’s new-creation work.

    1. 05:38 Bara is creation language

      Scripture: Genesis 1:1-3

      • Josh explains that human beings create by rearranging existing material, while the Hebrew verb bara points to God’s power to bring into existence what was not there.
      • Genesis begins with emptiness, darkness, and chaos, but God speaks light into being. David reaches for that same divine power and asks God to do it again inside his heart.

    2. 07:24 David’s radical diagnosis: start with me

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 11:1-27 Psalm 51:6 Mark 7:20-23

      • Josh says David has stopped treating Bathsheba, Uriah, the throne, or leadership pressure as the fundamental problem; David now recognizes that he himself is the problem.
      • The earlier confession that God desires truth in the inward parts and Jesus’ teaching that evil proceeds from within both force attention toward the heart rather than the blame game.

    3. 09:07 Useful tools cannot create a pure heart

      • Different circumstances may help, and behavior matters, but trying to solve a heart problem without calling on God’s power is foolish.
      • Filters, pouring out alcohol, counseling, budgets, accountability, and new friendships can restrain access or expose patterns; none can remove desire, dethrone an idol, create love for the light, or give a new heart.
      • Josh’s distinction is not tool versus no tool, but a tool versus creation power: dead people do not need coaching; they need to be made new.

    4. 10:52 The Bible’s continuous promise of new creation

      Scripture: Ezekiel 36:26 John 3:1-7 2 Corinthians 5:17-21 Revelation 21:1, 5

      • Ezekiel answers David’s longing with God’s promise to remove a heart of stone, give a heart of flesh, and put a new spirit within his people.
      • Jesus does not congratulate the morally serious and religiously informed Nicodemus and polish him up; he tells him that he must be born again.
      • Paul declares that anyone in Christ is a new creation and grounds that newness in God’s reconciling work through the sinless Christ, who was made sin for the guilty.
      • Josh explains that Scripture interprets Scripture because its many human writers serve one divine Author. Revelation completes the arc: the God who created in Genesis, whom David asked to create within him, announces from the throne that he is making everything new.

    5. 14:55 Christian testimony says, ‘Look what God created in me’

      • David’s closeness to God’s heart appears in his plea, ‘Start with me.’ He asks God to perform the needed work in him rather than merely identifying what is wrong elsewhere.
      • Christianity is not self-congratulation for what a person has made of himself; it is wonder at what only God could create and purchase in that person.

  4. 15:38 Renew in me: restore what has grown weak

    • The second half of Psalm 51:10 complements creation: David asks God both to create what is absent and renew what remains but is no longer what it once was.
    • Josh applies renewal not only to spectacular moral collapse but also to slow, unnoticed spiritual decline.

    1. 15:59 David remembers the young fighter who trusted God

      Scripture: 1 Samuel 17:32-50

      • Josh contrasts the older David’s failure at the palace window with the young David who rejected a king’s armor, carried a few stones, and faced Goliath in the Lord’s name.
      • He voices David’s longing: renew the fighter who trusted God without posturing, and help him face lust as he once faced the giant.
      • Josh presents age seventeen as an illustrative estimate; the biblical narrative itself does not state David’s age.

    2. 17:34 Everything living requires renewal

      • Bodies repeatedly need food, water, and sleep; plants need water, fields rain, and deserts monsoons. Spiritual life is likewise designed as dependent life.
      • Using ‘Jesus Take the Wheel,’ Josh pictures the mistake of inviting Jesus back into the passenger seat after quietly taking control again. The God who gives new life is also the one who sustains it.

    3. 18:59 Renewal is not only for spectacular failures

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 11:1-27

      • People may distance themselves from Psalm 51 because David committed adultery, practiced deception, and arranged a death, but avoiding his scale of catastrophe does not remove anyone’s need for renewal.
      • Spiritual decline does not always arrive as an explosion; it can develop almost invisibly over time.

    4. 19:41 Gray hair on the soul

      Scripture: Hosea 7:9

      • Hosea’s image of gray hair appearing without Israel noticing becomes Josh’s picture of imperceptible spiritual aging.
      • A person may not consciously reject prayer, Scripture, worship, holiness, or the gospel; each may simply become less precious, less practiced, less convicting, and less amazing.
      • The danger is a slow drift—a little less softness, hunger, thirst, prayer, conviction, joy, and interest in God—while no dramatic alarm announces the change.

    5. 23:12 Look in the mirror and ask God to breathe again

      Scripture: Psalm 51:10

      • A heart can drift, a spirit can weaken, affections can cool, and desire can move. David looks honestly at himself but does not answer with a pep talk or behavior-only resolve.
      • He asks God to make Christ precious, Scripture sweet, worship joyful, sin ugly, and heaven glorious again—to renew a steadfast spirit within him.

    6. 24:13 Steadfast in a world designed to move us

      • Josh defines steadfast as determined, prepared, firm, unshakable, and immovable. David has discovered how easily desire, lust, fear, reputation, and pride can move him.
      • Sin does not honestly advertise destruction; it offers pleasure and a shortcut. Modern notifications, scrolling, pornography, outrage, approval, fear, status, and attention multiply those pulls.
      • David therefore asks God to set him near God and make him immovable for God, regardless of what shifts around him.

  5. 26:20 Do not cast me from your presence

    • Josh slows down over Psalm 51:11 because hearing David’s fear without knowing David’s past can cause Christians to misread the verse.
    • David has seen multiple biblical pictures of rebellion, lost strength, exile, and life outside God’s presence; after Nathan’s confrontation, he fears becoming the kind of man he watched Saul become.

    1. 26:20 David’s fear must be heard in its covenant setting

      Scripture: Psalm 51:11

      • David prays, ‘Do not cast me from your presence or take your Holy Spirit from me.’ Josh says this prayer is informed by David’s history as Israel’s anointed king and by what happened to Saul.

    2. 26:41 David watched Saul unravel

      Scripture: 1 Samuel 15:23 1 Samuel 16:12-14 1 Samuel 16:23 1 Samuel 10:10-12 1 Samuel 28:7-25

      • Saul had been chosen and empowered for kingship, but Samuel announced that God had rejected him as king because he rejected the Lord’s word.
      • Samuel then anointed the healthy, handsome David, and soon afterward the Spirit of the Lord departed from Saul. David personally played music to calm Saul’s torment.
      • Josh contrasts Saul’s early experience among the prophets with his eventual resort to the medium at Endor, underscoring the trajectory David witnessed.

    3. 28:05 Other biblical pictures behind David’s dread

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 12:1-7 Genesis 3:22-24 Genesis 4:8-16 Exodus 13:21-22 Judges 16:17-21

      • Nathan’s accusation forces David to see his resemblance to Saul and to recognize himself as the rich oppressor in Nathan’s story.
      • Josh suggests that David may also remember Adam and Eve barred from Eden, Cain leaving the Lord’s presence after bloodshed, God’s cloud leading Israel from Egypt, and Samson losing strength and sight after the Lord departed from him.
      • The cumulative lesson is that David has discovered something more terrifying than consequences: life without God.

    4. 29:29 Godly sorrow wants God, not merely relief from consequences

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 7:10

      • Paul’s contrast clarifies David’s grief: worldly sorrow mourns what sin costs the sinner, while godly sorrow repents over what sin has done in relation to God.
      • David does not chiefly plead to keep his throne, image, or comfort; he pleads not to lose God.

    5. 30:12 What are you most afraid of losing?

      • Josh asks whether trouble makes people fear losing image, home, relationships, reputation, career, or money more than they fear living without God’s presence.
      • God’s presence is the believer’s comfort, joy, strength, transforming power, and what makes heaven heavenly.
      • If every request for health, family, success, money, and security were granted while God withheld himself, David would notice what was missing; Josh asks whether the church would.

    6. 32:18 Grief over distance is an invitation to run home

      • To the person who fears having wandered too far, Josh says that grief over distance and the inward cry to come home are gifts from God, not reasons to hesitate.
      • The response is to run toward God and ask him to breathe life again.

    7. 32:59 The Spirit under the new and better covenant

      Scripture: John 14:16-17 Ephesians 1:13-14 Ephesians 4:30

      • David lived under the old covenant and had watched the Spirit depart from Saul. Josh warns against using David’s royal-covenant fear to teach Christians that the Spirit leaves whenever they commit a serious sin.
      • Jesus promises an Advocate who will be with his disciples forever, and Paul says believers were marked and sealed with the promised Holy Spirit as the deposit guaranteeing their inheritance.
      • Security must not become carelessness: Christians need not live terrified that the Spirit will abandon them when they stumble, but they must not become comfortable grieving the Spirit who dwells in them.

  6. 34:24 There is no Christian life or church without the Holy Spirit

    • Josh rejects treating the Holy Spirit as an awkward subject. The Spirit’s presence and power are indispensable from conversion through perseverance.
    • The same dependence applies corporately: church excellence cannot regenerate a heart or replace the Spirit’s work.

    1. 34:46 The Spirit convicts and enables

      Scripture: John 16:8

      • Jesus says the Spirit convicts the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment; apart from him people have no true compass for those realities.
      • Christians need the Spirit to come alive, know God as Father, understand truth, pray, receive comfort and power, grow in holiness, serve, endure, and finish the race.
      • The Christian life therefore keeps praying, ‘Create in me, renew in me, use me,’ rather than treating salvation as an annual religious visit.

    2. 36:10 A congregation cannot manufacture spiritual life

      Scripture: Ezekiel 37:1-14

      • Leadership, funding, talent, programs, music, preaching, doctrine, crowds, systems, apps, coffee, air conditioning, and hospitality all have limits: none can carry truth into a heart or regenerate it.
      • Preachers cannot make dry bones live. A polished service without the Spirit is an event, not the church; Josh says he would rather be replaced than help Crossroads become so self-sufficient that it no longer needs the Spirit.

  7. 36:55 Restore joy, then use the forgiven sinner

    • David asks for restored joy and a willing, sustaining spirit; renewed love then turns outward in teaching other transgressors and singing God’s righteousness.
    • The movement culminates in blood guilt and communion: David’s need for a Savior points to the innocent King who gives his own blood for the guilty.

    1. 36:55 Restore the joy of your salvation

      Scripture: Psalm 51:12

      • Josh imagines David longing for the aliveness of trusting God in the Valley of Elah, but he distinguishes renewal from nostalgia or a sinful demand to be young again.
      • The prayer is not ‘bring back the good old days’ but ‘make my love alive again.’

    2. 37:36 Ephesus: steadfast doctrine without first love

      Scripture: Revelation 2:1-5

      • Josh imagines the Ephesian church hearing Revelation’s message: Jesus commended hard work and resistance to false teachers but warned that they had left their first love.
      • The needed restoration is childlike dependence and deep, passionate love—singing, life, and joy renewed even in an older and worn vessel.

    3. 38:39 Rescued sinners rescue sinners

      Scripture: Psalm 51:13-14

      • Restored joy leads David to teach transgressors God’s ways and sing of God’s righteousness.
      • Forgiveness does not make David aloof from sinners. The transgressor becomes a teacher, those who receive mercy become merciful, and people who find water tell thirsty people where it is.

    4. 39:45 David finally names his blood guilt

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 11:14-17

      • An innocent Uriah is dead because a guilty king tried to save himself. David now looks to the holy God he calls ‘my Savior.’
      • The unresolved question inside David’s story is how God can erase guilt without erasing justice or pretending Uriah does not matter.

    5. 40:25 The guilty king points to the innocent King

      • David is a guilty king who sacrifices an innocent man to save himself; Jesus is the innocent King who sacrifices himself to save the guilty.
      • At the Lord’s table, the church remembers that Jesus also had blood on his hands—his own. David’s blood guilt brought condemnation, while Christ’s blood brings redemption.

  8. 41:07 Jesus was forsaken to bring the guilty to God

    • The sermon closes by connecting David’s deepest request—not to be cast from God’s presence—to Jesus’ abandonment on the cross and Peter’s explanation that Christ suffered to bring the unrighteous to God.
    • Josh then calls every hearer to ask God for creation, renewal, restored joy, willingness, and steadfastness while waiting for King Jesus to return and make all things new.

    1. 41:07 Christ suffered outside the presence David desired

      Scripture: Psalm 51:11 Matthew 27:46 1 Peter 3:18

      • David prayed not to be cast away; on the cross Jesus cried, ‘My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?’
      • Peter explains the purpose: Christ suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring people to God.
      • Jesus does not merely produce repaired sinners. He gives God himself to new creations who run home to their Father.

    2. 41:48 Before singing, ask the Father to do what you cannot

      Scripture: Psalm 51:10, 12

      • Josh asks those who have never received a pure heart to pray for God to create one now.
      • Longtime believers who have grown spiritually gray should ask for renewal; the joyless should ask for restored joy; the stubborn and critical should ask again for a willing spirit that wants to serve.

    3. 42:52 The God of Genesis, Ezekiel, and John still creates

      Scripture: Genesis 1:3 Ezekiel 36:26 John 3:3-7

      • The God who spoke light into darkness still creates; the God who promised a new heart still softens stone; and Jesus still offers the new birth he required of Nicodemus.
      • The Savior of blood-guilty sinners is alive, so the congregation’s requests rest on God’s continuing power rather than self-repair.

    4. 43:14 Come, Lord Jesus, and make all things new

      Scripture: Romans 8:11 Revelation 21:4-5 Revelation 22:20

      • The Spirit who raised Jesus dwells in God’s people, and King Jesus will return to raise the dead, wipe every tear, destroy death, and bring his kingdom in fullness.
      • Josh turns the sermon into a prayer: ‘Come, Lord Jesus.’ The one who first came humbly will come again as King.

    5. 43:48 Trust his presence and remain steadfast until he comes

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:1 Psalm 51:10

      • Josh looks beyond visible people, enemies, and obstacles to a cloud of witnesses, angels, and the unconquerable spiritual reality of God’s presence among his people.
      • He closes with trust and prayer: the church is in God’s hands; if Christ does not return today, may God give his people a steadfast spirit until he does.
