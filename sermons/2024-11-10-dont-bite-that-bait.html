Overview

  1. 00:06 John the Baptist: a messenger sent from God

    Scripture: Matthew 3:3 Matthew 3:2

    • One of Steve’s all-time favorite Bible heroes is John the Baptist, who was “kind of a freak”: a child of the desert with dark leathery skin, a leather wardrobe, and a diet of locusts and wild honey. RFK Jr. would have gone ballistic: bugs and sweets. No wonder the elite said he was unstable.
    • He stomped around the desert with untamed dreads and bugs in his teeth, always yelling “Repent!” The authorities couldn’t stop him because he was sent from God as his chosen voice calling in the wilderness: prepare the way for the Lord.
    • Preaching his one-word sermon by the Jordan, “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand,” not even John knew how at hand it was.

  2. 01:53 The baptism of Jesus and the Father’s resting pleasure

    Scripture: Matthew 3:16–17

    • John looks up and here comes Jesus to be baptized. When Jesus came up out of the water, heaven opened, the Spirit of God descended like a dove, and the Father said, “This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.”
    • Steve offers what he calls a far better translation: “in whom my pleasure rests.” It speaks of the Father’s permanent, lasting pleasure, not the fleeting pride we feel when our kids perform, but an abiding pleasure, like drinking in the love while your baby naps.

    1. 03:30 Great catch, Josh

      • When Josh was about ten, they played burnout, and Josh fired one so hard Steve’s mitt cracked and his hand stung. Steve looked at his son with resting pleasure, remembering further back to the Velcro glove and Josh’s first real glove. At first Josh couldn’t catch a cold; he burst into tears, hid the mitt in the closet, and didn’t touch it for weeks.
      • Then one evening Steve showed him how to hold it and gently tossed the ball into his open mitt. Josh closed his hand, the glove did the rest, and Steve shouted, “Great catch, Josh!” The next night Josh said, “You throw me the ball and then say ‘great catch.’”
      • Kids need to know their dad is proud of them and will always love them, even when the boy is big enough to eat baled hay. If Jesus needed to hear his Father’s affirmation after setting aside the prerogatives of deity for his mission, of course he was over the moon.

  3. 06:33 Great temptation follows great blessing

    Scripture: 1 Kings 18:39 2 Corinthians 12:2 1 Corinthians 10:12

    • Immediately after that moment, in the very next verse, Jesus is swept into the desert to be tempted. Great temptation typically follows right on the heels of great blessing; in Scripture God’s beloved servants often go from a high and holy moment into a dark valley.
    • Moses spent 40 days alone with God on the mountain and came down with his face glowing, only to see Israel dancing, many naked, around a golden idol shaped like a cow. No wonder he threw down and broke the tablets.
    • Elijah, armed with God’s power, defeated Baal and his prophets as fire fell from heaven and the countryside shouted, “The Lord, he is God!” Then Jezebel threatened his life, and he folded like a little bitty baby, pleading with God to take his life.
    • Paul was caught up into the third heaven and received revelations too wonderful to speak. To keep him from getting puffed up, God gave him a thorn in the flesh; Paul pleaded three times for it to be removed, and three times God said no.
    • That is the human experience: way up, then just as far back down. That is why the Bible says, let anyone who thinks he stands take heed lest he fall.

  4. 10:03 Led by the Spirit into the desert

    Scripture: Luke 4:1

    • That “anyone” includes Jesus. Remember how you felt at your baptism; his excitement was even greater because his true identity had been publicly celebrated by his Father. Yet Luke tells us Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and was led by the Spirit into the desert. Matthew has already told us why: to be tempted.
    • Steve senses that Jesus knew why, perhaps with some anxiety. For 40 days he is tempted by the devil himself, not a JV demon but El Diablo, Beelzebub, who has always aimed to disrupt and, if possible, derail the holy mission of God.

  5. 12:02 A fast, not a cleanse

    Scripture: Luke 4:1–2

    • Steve reads Luke 4:1–2: for 40 days Jesus was tempted by the devil and ate nothing. This was a fast, not a cleanse; its purpose was purely spiritual, saying to his Father and to himself: you are far better than food, all I need, what I desire above all created things. I set aside something as essential as my appetite as a declaration of my honor and loyalty to you alone.

    1. 13:20 “He was hungry”

      Scripture: Luke 4:2

      • If this is too deep for anyone: after 40 days without food, the Bible says he was hungry. But don’t miss Luke’s phrase “at the end of those 40 days.” The fast was over, and that is when the devil showed up.

  6. 13:58 Satan’s predictable playbook

    Scripture: 1 John 2:15–16

    • Before getting to what Satan said, Steve explains how temptation happens, because Satan tempts us essentially the way he tempted Jesus. He always plays dirty, but he always fights predictably; he isn’t very creative and doesn’t have to be.
    • In the first century the apostle John revealed Satan’s playbook, and not one word has changed: do not love the world; everything in the world, the cravings of sinful man, the lust of his eyes, and the boasting of what he has and does, comes not from the Father but from the world.
    • Every day our minds are bombarded with sights and sounds designed to feed these three appetites, and every human has all three in spades. Temptations aimed at them produce desires, feelings, and longings that make us want to act, and that is where the battle is always fought.

    1. 16:37 The bait and the hungry fish

      Scripture: James 1:14

      • James says each one is tempted when by his own evil desire he is dragged away and enticed. The word “enticed” pictures a juicy worm wiggling in front of a hungry and very stupid fish. The bait isn’t the problem; the fish’s own inner cravings make him bite. Satan may use all sorts of bait, but every temptation is one of these three types, and they all come from within you.

    2. 17:36 Three kinds of bait

      • Bait may tempt your cravings. Desires to eat, drink, sleep, and express our sexual drives can be satisfied in good and proper ways, but the drive for food can become gluttony, the drive for sleep laziness, and the sex drive expressed outside marriage between a man and a woman is sin.
      • Bait may tempt the lust of your eyes, which speaks to the mind, imagination, and innermost thoughts: constantly thinking about possessions you wish you had, or looking at porn and pondering being with her instead of her. That is bait you have no right dabbling in.
      • Or it may be boasting of what you have or can do. “I don’t do porn or live in a fantasy world like those losers.” No, but you are guilty of boasting about your bad self, and that bait can just as effectively keep you from following close after Christ.

    3. 19:22 Wanting to bite isn’t sin; biting is

      • The bait that works for you won’t necessarily work for Steve. And wanting to bite the bait isn’t sin; it is sin only when you take it. But if you keep feeding your ego and imagining what it would be like, in time you will bite.

    4. 20:03 Eve took all three on the hook

      Scripture: Genesis 3:6

      • The serpent put all three approaches on the hook for Eve. Genesis 3:6: she saw the fruit was good for food (the craving), pleasing to the eye (the lust), and desirable for gaining wisdom (the boasting). What did she do then, sports fans? She took some and ate it. Play with temptation long enough and that is what always happens.

  7. 20:54 Wave one: “Tell this stone to become bread”

    Scripture: Luke 4:3 Luke 4:4 Matthew 4:4

    • Jesus was tempted in all three realms, and that is what this series is about: examining each of the three waves of temptation based on John’s words. After the baptism, the affirmation, and 40 days of spiritual connection in the desert, wave number one came.
    • The devil said, in effect, “Feeling kind of hungry there, Mr. Son of God? If you really are the Son of God, eat up. Tell this stone to become bread; we both know you can.” But Jesus kept his hunger in check and said no.
    • In every wave he explains why the same way: “It is written,” a quotation of Scripture. People do not live on bread alone, and, as Matthew adds, but on every word that comes from the mouth of God.

    1. 23:10 Israel learned to trust bread

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 8:2–3

      • Moses first used the phrase just before he died, reminding Israel how the Lord led them through the desert, humbled them, caused them to hunger, and fed them with manna, to teach them that man does not live on bread alone but on every word from the mouth of God.
      • Israel’s only job in 400 years of slavery was building huge storehouses for Pharaoh’s grain, and the more they built, the more he wanted. Egypt taught them to do whatever it takes to have enough bread. So God led them into the wilderness to teach the opposite: don’t trust in bread, because like Pharaoh you will never have enough. Trust me, and I will provide for your every need.

  8. 25:07 The world’s bait: you are what you have

    • The world tries hard to convince us we are the sum of what we have, that we really do live by bread alone. In the Bible, bread isn’t toast but a symbol of what sustains life. The world says find your worth in what you accumulate, never let an appetite pass unsatisfied, and hang your identity on the car, the house, the job, the closet. Every one of us has been sucked into that siren call for more and more.

    1. 26:17 “Looks good from back here too”

      • A husband had just paid the bills and told his wife on her way shopping, “It’s okay to look, but don’t buy.” She came home loaded with packages. She explained that when she tried on an outfit, the devil said, “Wow, that looks good on you.” “You should have said, ‘Get thee behind me, Satan.’” “I did, but when he got behind me he said, ‘Looks good from back here too.’”

    2. 27:05 Never enough bread

      • Whatever you feed on, whatever bread alone looks like to you, that is the voice you will hear. Steve agrees with an author who says that when we live by bread alone there is never enough, not even when we make so much some rots away; every bite leaves a bitter aftertaste, and we always want more and better bread.
      • That is the world’s bait: you are what you have, so if you don’t have much, you’re not much, and even what you have is never good enough.

    3. 28:09 The Dodge Coronet and Donna

      • After his sophomore year, Steve worked all summer to pay next year’s tuition and buy his first car, with $1,200 left for a ten-year-old Dodge Coronet, a total beater. Back at school he met Donna, pretty and from “glitzy” Akron, Ohio, and apologized for his crummy wheels.
      • She said she’d never been attracted to guys into their cars or their bodies, and that was why she was drawn to him. “Oh, um, thank you.” That has always been our world: you are what you have, and if that is your focus, it is never enough.

  9. 30:06 “Rock into roll”: answering with Scripture

    • Back to Jesus: 30 years old, 40 days alone without food, his calling not yet begun. He needed food, resources to launch his mission, and a spark to get noticed, the routine desires of any entrepreneur. Satan pressed the food: “Your Father sent you out here to starve? Surely he wouldn’t mind a couple of dinner rolls. Let’s do a little rock into roll.” Steve apologizes for the pun.
    • Jesus was literally starving, and his routine desire could easily have become a runaway desire. Yet he tamped down the deception by quoting Scripture, and he resists every temptation the same way.

    1. 31:55 Not an incantation, but the living word

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:12 Psalm 1:2 Psalm 119:11

      • Steve believes passionately in the power of God’s word, but Jesus wasn’t using verses as a magical incantation to ward off the boogeyman, or repeating a mantra: “Abba is better than bread.” God’s word is truth, and more: it is living and active, able to penetrate and judge every heart.
      • Real change happens when you fill your mind with God’s mind, meditating on it day and night, as the psalmist says, and memorizing it, as David said: I have hidden your word in my heart that I might not sin against you. That is where the true power resides.

  10. 33:17 Steve’s story: a way out

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 10:13 2 Corinthians 10:4–5 Psalm 119:9 Psalm 119:11

    • As a young man Steve was struggling with temptation and losing battle after battle. Then he found 1 Corinthians 10:13, the promise that when you are tempted God will provide a way out so you can stand up under it. He didn’t know how, but he was tired of thinking and doing things he shouldn’t, and it was killing him.
    • His study Bible’s margin pointed him to 2 Corinthians 10:5, and he couldn’t get there fast enough: the weapons we fight with have divine power to demolish strongholds and arguments, and we take captive every thought to make it obedient to Christ. In that moment he decided to spend his life filling his mind with God’s mind by reading, meditating, and memorizing.
    • One of the first verses he found was Psalm 119, where young David asks the question young Steve had been struggling with: how can a young man keep his way pure? By living according to your word; I have hidden your word in my heart that I might not sin against you. He memorized it immediately.
    • Whenever he started thinking about things he shouldn’t, God brought that verse up from his heart into his brain: “Voilà, I do have a way out.” It didn’t happen overnight, and he didn’t listen every time, but every night and morning he reviewed the verse and asked God to bring it to mind when he started “thinking stupid,” and God did. Over time he found a stronger strength than he imagined.

  11. 37:46 Stand firm on the living word, like Jesus

    Scripture: 1 Peter 5:8–9

    • Even at Steve’s age, the devil still prowls like a roaring lion looking for someone to devour, and he prowls after you too. But you have the power to resist him by standing firm in the faith on the living word of God, just like Jesus.
    • Jesus walked in the same sludge and the same static tried to distract him. Life is a life-and-death battle for your soul and for the souls of those we love and long to see follow Jesus. That is the bad news, and it will continue.

  12. 39:05 Good news: Jesus didn’t fail

    Scripture: John 19:30 Colossians 2:15

    • Because Jesus submitted to the Father’s will, freely came to this “desperately evil sludge ball,” resisted the relentless attacks, and stood firm knowing there would be no hope for any of us had he failed, he alone was worthy to stand in the gap for every soul.
    • Jesus didn’t fail. They killed him, but the grave couldn’t hold him; three days later he burst out of the tomb gloriously alive, and because he lives, victory over our sin is already ours. The same Jesus who said no to Satan in the wilderness shouted “It is finished” on Calvary, having disarmed every power, ruler, and authority. Jesus alone rules, now and forevermore.

    1. 40:49 Even when you crumble, he’s got you

      • Satan can’t lay a hand on you if you resist and stand firm, though he will throw up every roadblock to make you throw in the towel. Even if you crumble before his temptations, Jesus has got you. It’s called forgiveness of sins, and if the cross means anything, it means he will restore you even when you totally screw up.
      • But because all this is made possible by his great love, you don’t really want it to come to that. You want to walk as Jesus walked, and Steve knows that is his desire too.

  13. 42:02 Standing to read an affirmation of resolve

    Scripture: Galatians 6:9 1 Corinthians 15:58 Ephesians 6:13

    • Steve asks the church to stand and read the verses on the screen as an affirmation of resolve, not a boring church ritual: let us not become weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up.
    • “I don’t want to let anything move me either”: stand firm, let nothing move you, always give yourselves fully to the work of the Lord, because your labor is not in vain. And with God’s word as our weapon of choice: put on the full armor of God, so that when the day of evil comes you may be able to stand your ground. His last question: “Who’s with me?”

  14. 43:47 Prayer at the Lord’s Table

    • Steve prays as dirty, messed-up sinners who want to walk in Jesus’ footsteps. He asks God to fill their minds with his word, make them students of this life-giving power, and help each one seek out the verses that speak to their greatest temptation, perhaps with help from someone they respect, so they can stand immovable.
    • For the moments they don’t, he thanks God for his grace, forgiveness, and what Jesus did on Calvary. As they take the bread and cup, they remember his body broken and his blood spilled not for his own sins but for theirs, declaring their desire to be like him.
