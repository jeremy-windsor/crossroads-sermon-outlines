Overview

  1. 01:27 One of the hardest chapters in the Bible

    Scripture: Genesis 19 Isaiah 5:20

    • We continue in Genesis 19, one of the most difficult, controversial, and important sections in the entire Bible. Josh jokes that he won’t be shaking many hands in the lobby after this one.
    • His motivation is to explain the text in a way that helps people better understand God and the magnitude of their sin. He isn’t trying to beat anyone over the head or take the moral high ground; he isn’t against them or the people they love. He is against Satan convincing people it is okay to stay in their sin.
    • Plenty of people will affirm you, tell you you’re perfect just the way you are, and say nothing bad will come of a lifestyle of sin, claiming they love you because they accept you no matter what. They don’t love you; they love themselves, which is why they would never sacrifice their reputation to tell you the truth. Josh loves people enough to tell the truth, knowing some may leave and tell their neighbors the pastor is a bigot and a hater.
    • Who loves people more: the one who pleads with them to leave a wicked lifestyle, or the one who says the Bible is just an ancient text that doesn’t mean what it says, so go ahead? Who loves sinners more, the one who preaches acceptance or the one who preaches repentance? Isaiah 5 says woe to those who call evil good.
    • People no longer feel guilty when they turn from what was once considered right; the moral code is being hijacked and is based on how you feel in the moment, not on what God says. We do the same with God: instead of seeing his justice as evidence of his love, many claim it proves he is unloving. We have taken the author of all that is good and labeled him evil because he says no to bad things.

  2. 04:21 What this church is about

    • For those who are new: Josh can’t speak for the church down the street or the one you were raised in, only for the body he is tasked with leading. We don’t look down on others with holy hatred or hide in a holy huddle. This church seeks to love those who disagree with us and serve those who hate us, and it freely gives time and money to complete strangers almost every day, because that is what Jesus would be doing.
    • What we teach will be countercultural, but we aren’t tasked simply with winning a debate. We aren’t against any group or demographic; we are for all people knowing Jesus as Lord and Savior, which means saying some hard stuff. This church tries not to say it and leave you alone with it, but to walk with you in it. Josh will speak boldly where the Bible speaks and won’t be ashamed, but never out of hate; it is always done in love.
    • Don’t take his word for it; put us to the test. If after today you want to leave a life of sin but don’t know how, or your sin is tied to your career and you can’t see how to make it work, Josh doesn’t know how either, but this church body will commit to serve and support you through that transition. Our hearts are filled not with condemnation but with compassion.

  3. 06:34 Not a Mary Poppins Jesus

    Scripture: Mark 10:45 Jude 4 Romans 6:1–2

    • Before the details of Genesis 19, Josh wants people to see where it fits in our understanding of God and his plan to reconcile sinners to himself. There is no separation from Genesis to Jesus. A Mary Poppins view of Jesus, a nice nanny with an umbrella, isn’t accurate. Jesus is King of kings and Lord of lords. He traded equality with God, became a man, was marginalized, mocked, and spit upon, and came not to be served but to serve and give his life as a ransom for many.
    • Christians get excited about that, and the world has no problem with a gentle Jesus, the sacrificial lamb. Things take on another tone when we talk about Jesus returning with a sword, rewarding what you’ve done, judging what is done in secret, separating good and evil, and establishing a perfect kingdom. It is nice to hear Jesus died for our sins, to which our culture says, “Well, I’m worth dying for.”
    • We love hearing about grace, but listen to Jude: certain individuals whose condemnation was written about long ago have secretly slipped in among you, ungodly people who pervert the grace of God into a license for immorality and deny Jesus Christ our only Sovereign and Lord. This is happening. Paul would say, “Shall we go on sinning so that grace may increase? Certainly not.” Christ’s victory over sin and death isn’t a hall pass to sin. Your sin matters, how you deal with it matters, and how you deal with others’ sin matters.

    1. 08:55 Luke 17: as it was in the days of Lot

      Scripture: Luke 17:28–32

      • Jesus connects what happened to Sodom and Gomorrah with what will happen when he returns, in Luke 17: it was the same in the days of Lot; people were eating and drinking, buying and selling, planting and building, but the day Lot left Sodom, fire and sulfur rained down from heaven and destroyed them all. It will be just like this on the day the Son of Man is revealed: no one on the housetop should go down for his possessions, and no one in the field should go back for anything. Remember Lot’s wife.

    2. 09:32 Uncovering the sulfur

      Scripture: Genesis 19 Luke 17

      • There is archaeological evidence for this. Joel Kramer, a Christian archaeologist on YouTube, shows a layer of ash with burnt pottery and heat-stained human remains, bones warped and bent by intense heat, and sulfur balls he pulls from the ground that are still flammable. The excavator who dug down to that burn layer concludes it was very likely caused by the events in Genesis 19. We are uncovering the sulfur Jesus talks about in Luke 17.

    3. 10:30 Jesus was involved in it

      Scripture: John 5:22

      • Josh has heard pastors boast that they don’t preach fire and brimstone. How could you not, if Jesus did? How much do you have to hate someone not to warn them of coming destruction? “We just love Jesus; we aren’t about all that Old Testament stuff.” You can’t ignore what Jesus teaches and claim to love him.
      • Don’t miss this, or Josh has failed you today: Jesus doesn’t only teach about Sodom and Gomorrah and tie it to the day of the Lord; he was involved in it. Jesus says in John 5 that the Father judges no one but has entrusted all judgment to the Son.

  4. 11:27 Abraham pleads, and two angels arrive

    Scripture: Genesis 18:23–32 Genesis 18:20–21 Genesis 19:1 2 Peter 2:7

    • In the previous chapter the Lord listens to Abraham plead for Sodom: will you sweep away the righteous with the wicked? What if there are 50 righteous people, 45, 30, 10? The Lord said the outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah is so great and their sin so grievous that he will go down and see. So he sends two angels to check it out, and in verse 1 they arrive in Sodom in the evening while Lot is sitting in the gateway of the city.
    • Lot is a man of high status, hospitable and generous, a judge in the town. Judges sat at the city gates where business was conducted, so Lot was involved in politics, business, and commerce, and influential. He didn’t approve of his neighbors’ wickedness; he knew truth, justice, righteousness and evil. Peter tells us Lot was a righteous man distressed by the wickedness around him.
    • Catch this: he didn’t agree with their lifestyle, but he also didn’t want to walk away from his good life. Why live in a tent in the boonies when you can have your own crib in town? Lot is trying to influence the city, but as the story unfolds he is the one being influenced: a good man living at the center of a completely wicked culture. Familiar? Christian, are you doing the influencing or being influenced?

  5. 13:38 Lot insists they stay with him

    Scripture: Genesis 19:2 Genesis 19:3

    • When Lot saw them, he got up, bowed down with his face to the ground, and said, “My lords, please turn aside to your servant’s house; you can wash your feet and spend the night.” Lot knows good when he sees it because he is surrounded by evil. Josh doesn’t think he knew they were angels, just that they were good, and he was tired of being the only good one in the city. Finally, someone to have Friday dinner with.
    • “No, we will spend the night in the square.” Lot knows what these good-looking men are walking into: “Dude, you should not go there; trust me, come with me.” It is like a relative visiting Phoenix who booked a hotel down on Van Buren: “Don’t worry, we got a good deal on Groupon.” “No, I insist; you’re staying with us.” The last place they needed to be in the middle of the night was the square.
    • Lot wouldn’t take no for an answer, because they probably wouldn’t have made it through the night. He insisted so strongly that they went with him into his house, and he prepared a meal, baking bread without yeast, and they ate.

    1. 15:28 Reading the room

      • If Josh were one of these men, he would be reading the room. A few weeks back, Bill and Kristen, new members, had Josh’s family over: a smoked pork butt, little apple-caramel dessert cups, a new game, and a long, fun night. It would have gone very differently with a plastic-wrapped Circle K burrito tossed in the oven and an effort to get them out the door by eight. You can tell when someone is hosting you but trying to make quick work of it: “Beautiful home. Lights out, bedtime, get out.”

  6. 16:47 The men of Sodom surround the house

    Scripture: Genesis 19:4–5

    • Before they had gone to bed, all the men from every part of Sodom, young and old, surrounded the house: “Where are the men who came to you tonight? Bring them out so that we can have sex with them.” This is disturbing. Where are the good guys? There are none; they are all bad. Anybody having flashbacks to Abraham’s chat with the Lord, 50, 30, 10? Abraham, Josh admires the optimism, but there aren’t.
    • Josh struggled with how to communicate “young and old.” The word means child, adolescent, youth.

    1. 17:49 Not about hospitality

      • The growing number of people calling themselves gay Christians will say Genesis 19 is about hospitality. If someone came to Josh’s house showing hospitality like this, they picked the wrong house. It is clearly about homosexuality, and also about sexual abuse: these old men clearly had influence over the young boys.
      • How vile must a society be to train young boys to go looking for homosexual orgies after dinner, and if this is what they do in the streets, imagine what these fathers looked like at home. It is disgusting and unbelievable, and it makes Josh uncomfortable to share.

    2. 18:50 To those who have suffered sexual abuse

      • But he needs to, because he knows many in the room have been victims of sexual abuse, and it breaks his heart. He is so sorry it happened to them. If they need someone to talk to or encourage them, the church is here, and if it lacks the tools to help, it will find someone who has them.
      • God designed us as sexual beings, but his intention was that one man and one woman would be one flesh for life. This society got so far from that that children followed perverted men into the streets. This town should make you sick; Josh hopes people are uncomfortable and see how disgusting, hopeless, and wicked it was.

  7. 19:47 Lot confronts it: “My friends”

    Scripture: Genesis 19:6–7 2 Corinthians 6:14

    • There was only one good thing in that city: Lot. And Lot does what good does in the face of evil; he confronts it. He went outside, shut the door, and said, “No, my friends. Don’t do this wicked thing.”
    • Look what he calls them: my friends. Josh isn’t saying you can’t have homosexual friends or family you are ministering to; the key phrase is “that you’re ministering to.” But if you are playing wickedness tug-of-war against the world and losing, pack up your stuff and your family and get out.
    • Sometimes believers are called to minister in messy places, but you need to know when to walk away and give up the fight. What do righteousness and wickedness have in common? What fellowship does light have with darkness? Be careful; we aren’t bulletproof, and we risk developing a distaste for biblical truth if we stay in Sodom too long.

  8. 21:10 Is it wicked?

    • “Don’t do this wicked thing.” It is now heavily debated whether what we are witnessing is wicked, and it didn’t used to be; many grew up never knowing anyone who was gay. Over thousands of years of church history it was never really debated. God-fearing, Bible-believing, Jesus-preaching Christians hold that all sex outside of heterosexual marriage is wicked.
    • Josh can’t settle the debate in this country, but this church can be clear about its stance. Does God view men who have sex with men as wicked? Yes, without a doubt.

    1. 22:06 Leaders who lead others into sin

      Scripture: John 19:11

      • The problem is that not all religious or Christian leaders agree. Brian McLaren and others argue we need to rethink our response to homosexuality: the Bible isn’t clear, the ancient ethic is too offensive to be useful, so leaders should stop talking about it. Offensive, therefore no longer true; that is the argument. Another so-called Christian author wants heterosexual married couples and gay married couples to grow together in faith. How is that supposed to work, if one cares what God’s word says about marriage and the other clearly doesn’t?
      • Many say these ideas aren’t mainstream and these people have no influence. Time magazine named Brian among the top 25 most influential Christians, and Taylor Swift, not a Christian influencer, can tweet once and have 2 million voters at her beck and call. They are grabbing young people and trying to make an obviously wicked thing culturally acceptable. We just read about the men of Sodom; that should sound familiar.
      • Jesus told Pilate, “You would have no power over me if it were not given to you from above; therefore the one who handed me over to you is guilty of a greater sin.” Not all sin is equal; leaders and influencers have the greater sin for leading others into sin. Josh refuses to lead people into sin or tell them it is okay.

    2. 24:32 Scripture speaks to it

      Scripture: Leviticus 18:22 1 Corinthians 5:12–13

      • Scripture clearly speaks to homosexuality. Leviticus 18:22: do not have sexual relations with a man as one does with a woman; that is detestable, some versions say an abomination. It isn’t natural or normal, even if you love one another. There is no circumstance where we can take what God says is wicked and make it good; we are wicked, so how can we make things good?
      • To balance it, Josh loves Paul’s wisdom to the church in Corinth: what business is it of mine to judge those outside the church? Are you not to judge those inside? God will judge those outside; expel the wicked person from among you. He didn’t write this sermon to come after the LGBTQ+ community. His focus is men and women in the church who claim to be Christian but don’t take God at his word: self-proclaimed gay Christians.
      • That title alone says everything. How would people feel if Josh called himself a porn-addict Christian? Putting the identity first shows it matters more than identity in Christ. You can’t be a Christian and not follow Christ; ask the rich young ruler, who walked away sad. Again: who is more loving to those struggling with sexual sin, or any sin, the one preaching acceptance or the one preaching repentance? Turn; run away.

    3. 26:24 The law, Paul, and the creation order

      Scripture: Leviticus 20:13 Romans 1 1 Corinthians 6:9–10 Genesis 1:28 1 Corinthians 7:5

      • Josh has to warn people, because the law makes this serious. Leviticus 20 shows the consequence of ignoring godly sexuality: if a man has sexual relations with a man as one does with a woman, both have done what is detestable; they are to be put to death; their blood will be on their own heads.
      • Paul addresses it in Romans 1: God handed them over, as men and women exchanged natural relations for unnatural ones. And 1 Corinthians 6:9: do you not know that wrongdoers will not inherit the kingdom of God? Neither the sexually immoral nor idolaters nor male prostitutes nor practicing homosexuals nor thieves nor the greedy nor drunkards nor slanderers nor swindlers. Josh isn’t condemning; he used to be on this list, and it is by the grace of God he isn’t.
      • Men having sex with men and women with women would have kept Adam and Eve from obeying God’s first command to humanity: be fruitful and increase in number. In other words, go have sex with your wife and make babies, and praise God.
      • But it isn’t just about babies. God placed sexual desires in us from creation, intended to be satisfied only by a man and a woman in marriage. Why else would 1 Corinthians 7:5 say do not deprive each other except by mutual consent for a time of prayer? Men, feel free to add it to your memory verses. Why? So that Satan won’t tempt you because of your lack of self-control. Satan will use our sexual desires against us, to harm us and others and to disrupt what God intended.

    4. 28:45 An assault on the promised seed

      Scripture: Genesis 3:15

      • Don’t miss this: the men of Sodom’s captivation with each other is an all-out assault by Satan to destroy God’s promise of the Messiah, which the series has been tracing. The first proclamation of the gospel, Genesis 3:15: the seed of the woman will crush the seed of the serpent. So what is the serpent trying to do? Kill the seed: put men with men and women with women and see about the seed of the woman.
      • Josh calls homosexuality a tool used by Satan to stop the Messiah. In 2024, just because culture presses on the church with its agenda, many churches welcome and celebrate it as part of God’s beautiful design. It isn’t his design; it is Satan’s. It is a tragedy.

    5. 29:37 Jesus defers to the creation order

      Scripture: Matthew 19:4–6 Genesis 1–2 Leviticus 18:22

      • When asked about divorce in Matthew 19, Jesus refers back to Genesis 1 and 2, and even to Leviticus: haven’t you read that at the beginning the Creator made them male and female, and said for this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and the two will become one flesh? So they are no longer two but one; what God has joined together, let no one separate.
      • Asked when divorce is permitted, Jesus gives one reason. Many say adultery, but the word is porneia, sexual immorality, which traces back to Leviticus and the porneia code that outlines how to avoid becoming sexually immoral. So while addressing divorce, Jesus addresses our sexuality, home life, and marriage, all of it.
      • If Jesus, asked about divorce, defers to God establishing marriage in Genesis, the creation order, and the law, we should too. The Bible is clear on homosexuality.

  9. 31:08 Lot’s plan: Sodom lives in Lot

    Scripture: Genesis 19:8

    • So Lot is on his front porch doing the Lord’s work, and we are cheering him on: call it what it is, brother. But Lot has a plan: “I have two daughters who have never slept with a man. Let me bring them out to you, and you can do what you like with them, but don’t do anything to these men, for they have come under the protection of my roof.”
    • What about your daughters, fool? Aren’t they under the protection of your roof? Josh would die a thousand deaths before handing his daughter over to be raped by a mob, and he would take some with him.
    • Leave it to the Christian to call out everyone else’s wickedness while being completely ignorant of their own. “Don’t have your way with these men; that’s wicked. Take my daughters instead.” Has he lost his mind? Yes, because this is what happens when you conform to the pattern of this world. Sodom is a problem, which is why Jesus judged it, but the other problem isn’t that Lot lives in Sodom. It is that Sodom lives in Lot.

    1. 32:45 Hypocrisy destroys our witness

      Scripture: Matthew 7:5

      • This is why society doesn’t care when Christians scream about morality: God’s people are great at hypocrisy. We say homosexuality is immoral, and the gay community answers: you value marriage so much, what is the divorce rate in your churches? Didn’t I read another article about a pastor sleeping with his secretary, a pastor who embezzled millions and hid money in his walls like a drug lord, one of your greatest evangelists sexually assaulting women for years? Didn’t Jesus say to pull the plank out of your own eye before worrying about the speck in your neighbor’s? Don’t lecture me on morality.
      • The truth is homosexuality is a sin, and so was Josh’s porn addiction, and so is your drinking problem, and so was your gambling issue. We should be concerned about sin and call out wickedness, but a church that spends all its energy on the wickedness outside and not enough calling out the wickedness inside will be filled with hypocrisy. We destroy our witness when we fail to call out our own wickedness.

  10. 34:29 Lot has zero credibility

    Scripture: Genesis 19:9

    • “Get out of our way. This fellow came here as a foreigner, and now he wants to play the judge! We’ll treat you worse than them.” They pressed forward to break down the door. Lot has zero credibility now: “Pretend to know good versus evil, buddy; you just paraded your two virgin daughters out here after telling us how terrible we are. You’re a joke.”
    • This is why your reputation, and the church’s reputation, matters. In youth ministry they used to tell high schoolers, “If you get in trouble at camp, just tell them you’re from CCV.” How can you preach into others’ lives when there is no sign you have listened to your own sermon?

  11. 35:38 Get them out of here

    Scripture: Genesis 19:10–11 Genesis 19:12–13 Genesis 19:14

    • Lot doesn’t cry out to God for help, doesn’t pray, and doesn’t seek counsel from the wisdom in his own house. He waited to act and is backed into a corner. But the men inside pulled Lot back into the house, shut the door, and struck the men at the door, young and old, with blindness: a riot of biblical proportion, blind punches thrown everywhere.
    • The two men said, “Do you have anyone else here, sons-in-law, sons or daughters, or anyone else in the city who belongs to you? Get them out of here, because we are going to destroy this place.” Grab your kids, your family, your wife; we’ve seen enough.
    • Lot spoke to his sons-in-law, pledged to marry his daughters. Imagine that conversation: “You did what?” This can happen to anyone; a believer can surround himself with the wrong people and become a twisted mess of faith and funk. “Hurry and get out; the Lord is about to destroy the city.” But his sons-in-law thought he was joking.
    • How you lead your family now will most definitely affect how they treat you later. It is an extreme example, but presenting your daughters to be raped by a mob will leave lasting scars. They watched their dad operate for years, so it shouldn’t surprise us when, a few verses later, they get him drunk and have sex with him to preserve the family line.

  12. 37:41 When he hesitated: the Lord’s mercy

    Scripture: Genesis 19:15–16

    • With the coming of dawn the angels urged Lot to hurry and take his wife and two daughters or be swept away. When he hesitated, the men grasped his hand and the hands of his wife and daughters and led them safely out of the city, for the Lord was merciful to them.
    • Why did Lot hesitate? Josh doesn’t think he was submitting a prayer request on the church app. He thinks Lot was dreading life outside the walls.
    • Here we get frustrated with Lot, the one we were cheering: “You coward, you punk.” But are you really that different? God floods your life with mercy and says, “I’ve forgiven you; leave your life of sin,” and you say, “Check in with me in a month. Is it really a sin? It’s not hurting anyone. Can’t I follow Jesus and keep doing this?” None of us is better than Lot. We hesitate too, clinging to our sin while claiming to follow the one who died to overcome it.
    • Yet notice the Lord’s mercy: “Okay, Lot, you don’t want to lead your family right now; you’re sad to leave that life. I’ll make sure they’re with us.” Isn’t it amazing how far you can fall and still be considered worth saving? We want to condemn and disqualify Lot, but the Lord keeps dragging them out.

  13. 40:08 Flee, don’t look back

    Scripture: Genesis 19:17 Luke 9:61–62 Genesis 19:26 Romans 12:2 Micah 6:8

    • As soon as they were out, one of them said, “Flee for your lives! Don’t look back, and don’t stop anywhere in the plain! Flee to the mountains or you will be swept away!” People ask Josh how to leave their sin life; Genesis 19:17 explains how believers deal with sin: flee, don’t look back, don’t stop. Kill it before it kills you.
    • Many of us say, “Is this really a big deal? It’s just a little pot; I can stop looking at porn anytime; I’ll stop drinking in a couple of months, plus the holidays are coming.” No: run, stop thinking about it, and go. Don’t regret dropping that filthy habit for Jesus.
    • Jesus talks about looking back when invited forward. He says, “Follow me,” and someone answers, “I will follow you, but first let me go back and say goodbye to my family.” Jesus replied, “No one who puts a hand to the plow and looks back is fit for service in the kingdom of God.” Followers of Christ have eyes up, moving forward; we operate not on our past but on what he says about our future.
    • But Lot’s wife looked back, and she became a pillar of salt. Once you have Jesus, there is no reason to look back. Believers, flee this corrupt world. Your response to Sodom and Gomorrah should be to run to Jesus: we are wicked and he is good, so grab his hand, run from your sin together, and never look back. Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind.
    • To evangelists: you won’t win anyone for Christ if you look nothing like Christ. He has shown you what is good and what the Lord requires: to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God. You know what is keeping you from walking like that and from Jesus. If you need help, ask God; he is alive, active, and powerful, and he will reach into your life and help you. Call out to him.

  14. 43:05 God sent his Son to rescue you

    Scripture: 1 Peter 3:18

    • God sent two angels to Sodom to rescue Lot before he judged the town, and God sent his Son Jesus to rescue you before he judges the entire world. The judgment to come has already been satisfied on the cross; the blood of Christ was poured out for the forgiveness of sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, the just for the unjust, to bring you to God. Believe and be saved, and never look back.

  15. 43:39 Closing prayer

    • Josh confesses that he finds it hard to approach others’ sin without first approaching his own, and prays that the whole church would be marked by doing the same, for what can we preach to sinners if we don’t run to God for forgiveness ourselves? He asks God to make them aware of their blind spots in a wicked world that looks more and more like the times God warned of, to prepare them for his coming, and to give them hearts for sinners so they speak grace.
    • He also asks for discernment to know when enough is enough in ministry and it is time to walk away, since sometimes they honor God by walking away, and thanks God for the grace and mercy they receive freely as they navigate difficult decisions in a difficult world.
