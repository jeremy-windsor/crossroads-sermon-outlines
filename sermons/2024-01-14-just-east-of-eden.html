Overview

  1. 00:43 The most mocked and most consequential chapter

    Scripture: Genesis 3

    • Few chapters in the Bible are more widely mocked than Genesis 3: a talking snake, an angel wielding a lightsaber, an apple dangling from a tree, and a naked man and woman with her long flowing hair covering all the necessary bits. Steve gets it; the whole scene is viewed as kind of a joke.
    • Yet what gets unpacked is the most consequential event in human history. Never mind 9/11 or October 7; set aside Hitler, Pearl Harbor, even the Holocaust. Genesis 3 surpasses even the invention of electricity and the internet, with apologies to Al Gore.
    • What happened in Genesis 3 changed everything. It is no exaggeration to say that you and I now live in a post Genesis 3 world. Everything you see and experience is not at all the utopia God intended.

  2. 02:07 The world as it once was

    Scripture: Genesis 1:31 Genesis 2

    • The world as it is isn’t the world as it once was in Genesis 1, when God reviewed his handiwork, saw all that he had made, and it was very good. The whole world moved in a perfect, God-paced rhythm. God was so impressed by his handiwork that he tells the creation story again in chapter 2 with more detail.
    • Adam and Eve lived in a perfect world, in a state of innocence, totally free from sin: no anger, no hostility, just two naked people, beautiful and unashamed, living an old-fashioned love song coming down in two-part harmony. That one is for the old people.
    • All that glorious harmony lasted 7.2 seconds. All it took was the turn of a page. In Genesis 3 everything goes bad, fatally, terminally, generationally bad, and every human since, everyone living in this post Genesis 3 world, lives with the dreadful aftereffects.

  3. 03:45 Before the beginning: a war in heaven

    Scripture: Job 38:7

    • To understand how Genesis 3 even happened, we have to go back before the beginning. Before the beginning there was a great conflict in heaven: a deity denier disputed God’s right to sit on the throne, and a battle ensued between God and this adversary. He goes by many names: Beelzebub, devil, dragon, the evil one, the god of this age, Leviathan, roaring lion, Satan, serpent, thief, and more.
    • Where did this angelic jerk come from? When God laid earth’s foundation, his angels shouted for joy, but not all of them offered praise. The most beautiful and powerful of the angels, also named Lucifer, son of the morning, saw the adulation directed toward God and was ticked off, because he thought he deserved some of that glory.
    • Lucifer incited one-third of the angels to join him in all-out civil war. How did that work out? Not so hot. The Bible calls it a war, but it wasn’t a very exciting one. God basically said, “Lucy, you’re done,” threw him and his buddies out of heaven, and one day, when God decides, will throw him into an eternal fire prepared just for him. That is the backstory of Genesis 3.

  4. 06:01 The serpent in Eden

    Scripture: Genesis 3:14

    • Now we come to the lush garden called Eden, where Lucifer, also called Satan, approaches Eve in the form of a serpent.
    • We aren’t given the detail, but most people believe snakes at that time traveled upright, and Steve believes it, because what happens next led God to change the snake’s physiology: “Because you have done this, you will crawl on your belly and eat dust.” Before that, maybe the serpent looked like a cute, cuddly, quirky little bipod gecko.

  5. 06:57 Two special trees

    Scripture: Genesis 2:9 Genesis 2:16–17

    • Genesis 2:9 tells us the Lord made all kinds of trees, pleasing to the eye and good for food, and in the middle of the garden were two special trees: the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.
    • The tree of life was given to sustain us. We were made to live forever; our bodies were designed to reproduce, regenerate, and recreate, so every damaged cell gives way to a newer, healthier one. Cut yourself and white blood cells rush in to heal and drive out infection while red blood cells regenerate new skin. These marvels happen because we were never supposed to die, and the tree of life was God’s gift to ensure living forever would happen.
    • The tree of the knowledge of good and evil was given to test us. Fancy places tend to have a lot of rules, but the lush garden had only one. God told Adam he was free to eat from any tree, a gazillion of every variety, with one exception: “That tree over there in the middle, you must not eat from that one.” Given the abundance, the command was neither restrictive, harsh, nor controlling. The garden was all about freedom: just don’t mess with that one stinking tree.

  6. 09:10 Satan lies about God lying

    • Then the gecko comes along and tells Eve that God had lied to her, because the fruit she was forbidden to eat would make her like God, not to mention it was juicier and tastier than all the other trees combined. Satan lied to her about God lying to her.
    • He uses the same tactic on us: God is trying to keep something from you, rob you, deny you the pleasure you deserve; I will make you happier and give you more delight than you’ve ever known, but mean God won’t let you have it.

    1. 10:03 Wrong place, wrong time, wrong voice

      • Why was Eve hanging around the one place God told her to avoid? God had made it clear the tree was bad news. Eve was in the wrong place at the wrong time because she was listening to the wrong voice. She was sin just waiting for a tempter, and guess who was there to meet her on that spot.

  7. 10:36 A dash of truth, a bucket of deception

    Scripture: Genesis 3:1 Genesis 3:2–3 Genesis 3:4–5

    • Satan was a crafty little viper. Instead of saying, “Here, Eve, have a bite,” he plants doubt by mixing a dash of truth with a whole bucket of deception: “Did God really say, ‘You must not eat from any tree in the garden’?” God never said that.
    • So Eve springs to the Lord’s defense and answers a question she had no business answering: we can eat from the trees in the garden, but God did say you must not eat fruit from the tree in the middle of the garden, and you must not touch it. Say what? We just read exactly what God said; does he say anything about touching? Like the gecko, Eve misquotes God.
    • “You will not die,” the serpent said. “For God knows that when you eat of it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil.”

  8. 12:03 The progression into sin

    Scripture: Genesis 3:6

    • Follow the progression. Eve’s stumble into sin began because she was in a place she wasn’t supposed to be, listening to someone she shouldn’t have been listening to. That resulted first in confusion, and now Eve begins to doubt that God has her best interests at heart: “I guess I’m going to have to start looking out for myself.”
    • When the woman saw that the fruit was good for food and pleasing to the eye, meaning, “God’s been holding out on me,” and desirable for gaining wisdom, she took some and ate it. The gecko was right; this is good stuff.
    • Then, loving wife that she was, she gave some to her husband, who was with her. Wait a minute: Adam was there for the whole spiel. But passive, conflict-avoiding hubby that he was, he ate some too.

  9. 13:17 Eyes opened: cover up and hide

    Scripture: Genesis 3:7 Genesis 3:8

    • She likes it and he likes it until verse 7, when their eyes were opened and they realized they were naked. They had always been naked, but now being naked felt dirty, so they attempt the world’s first cover-up, sewing fig leaves together over their most vulnerable parts.
    • Then, when they heard the sound of the Lord God walking in the garden, they hid from him among the trees. First they cover up; now they flat out hide. Does that sound familiar? It would be a hoot if it weren’t so tragic: “Quick, Eve, finish your apple, we’ve got to climb this tree,” as though the God who made the tree would be stymied by branches.
    • We pull the same lame moves. When we do dumb things, we do dumb things to cover them up, and when we feel guilty, we try to hide. So don’t cluck your tongue at Eve. You too. Me too.

  10. 14:39 The fall

    Scripture: Genesis 3:7

    • Back to verse 7: the eyes of both of them were opened. Satan was partly right; their eyes did open, and they were a little more like God, because they knew good and evil. What Satan didn’t say was that something insidious would happen, not just to them but to the whole human race. Before, everything was perfect and pure; not anymore.
    • Adam and Eve decided to be their own god, and the tragedy that resulted is what we call the fall. Suddenly the human heart wasn’t functioning as designed. Like glass is predisposed to shatter and nitroglycerin to explode, the human heart was now predisposed to do evil. Whenever we think we know what we want, we will do whatever it takes to get it: lie, gossip, cheat, slander, lust, covet, hate.
    • And we can’t fix the thing that got broken, because that thing within us is called depravity.

    1. 16:28 Total depravity and original sin

      Scripture: Psalm 51:5

      • We’ve heard “depravity” a lot since the atrocities of October 7. They were utterly depraved; but then again, whose sins aren’t? Total depravity is the evangelical term for the doctrine of original sin, and both are feeble human attempts to describe what happened in the garden that day.
      • Because of Adam’s sin, the whole human race fell. We were created in God’s image and after his likeness, and we are still image bearers who desire to reverence, trust, obey, and glorify God, but because of Adam’s sin everything changed.
      • David described it: “Surely I was sinful at birth, and in sin did my mother conceive me.” He isn’t saying it was sinful for his mom to get pregnant and give birth to a precious little shepherd boy, or that he did wrong by being born. He is acknowledging the universal human condition post Genesis 3: all of us are shot through with rebellion, pride, and evil desires, which is why we all fall short of God’s glory again and again.
      • We are not sinners because we sin; we sin because we are sinners. “Total” doesn’t mean we are as wicked as we could possibly be; it means the fall was so all-encompassing that this global pandemic called sin has infected every heart, and there is no vaccine. According to the apostle Paul our will is in bondage; apart from Jesus we are slaves to sin, and our bodies, minds, and will have been infected.
      • Is there an antidote? Yes, but slow your roll.

  11. 19:48 God’s four questions

    Scripture: Genesis 3:9 Romans 5:12 Genesis 3:9–10 Genesis 3:11

    • Before we go there, we have to go back to the garden, where Adam and Eve are still hiding in the tree. God plays along with their silly game and asks four questions.
    • Question one: “Where are you?” Whom did God ask? The man, not Eve, even though Eve was the first to eat. Centuries later, when Paul teaches about original sin, he doesn’t even mention Eve; he says sin entered the world through one man. Men, being by God’s perfect design the head of your home is not a privilege; it is your solemn responsibility.
    • God called to the man, “Where are you?” Adam replied, “I heard you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked, so I hid.” Question two: “Who told you that you were naked?” Question three: “Did you eat from the tree I commanded you not to eat from?”

  12. 21:13 The shame and blame game

    Scripture: Genesis 2:23 Genesis 3:12 Genesis 3:13

    • What happens next is part two of what Steve calls the shame and blame game. Part one, the shame, was the leaf thing. Part two, the blame, is the only somewhat humorous scene in this tragic story, because in less than 13 verses humanity plummets from “Whoa, man, this is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh” all the way down to “That woman you put with me, she gave it to me. So it’s your fault, God, or hers; you pick. Somebody owes me an apology, and none of this mess is my fault.”
    • The very first thing the world’s first person did after the first sin was blame God and then his very first wife. Not to be outdone, Eve blames the gecko. After question four, “What is this you have done?”, Eve replies, “It was the serpent. He deceived me.”
    • Under the weight of shame, the whole cast tries to dodge it by spitting out mutual blame. Sound familiar? Adam was right that Eve played a role, and Eve was right that the serpent lied, but neither was completely right. They both spoke truth, just not the whole truth. Sound familiar?

  13. 23:25 The curse

    Scripture: Genesis 3:14

    • All three players were guilty, and that is why the Lord handed down stiff consequences, what we have come to call the curse. To the serpent: cursed are you above all the livestock and wild animals; you will crawl on your belly and eat dust all the days of your life. There is more to the snake’s curse, but Steve skips it for now.

    1. 23:58 To the woman

      Scripture: Genesis 3:16

      • To the woman: “I will greatly increase your pains in childbearing; with pain you will give birth to children.” Every mama says amen. But if agonizing childbirth is a pain, check this out: “Your desire will be for your husband.”
      • Easy, men; this isn’t sexual desire or a desire to please you. It is a sin-corrupted desire. From that moment to this, wives will desire to kick against God’s perfect order for marriage, to rebel, manipulate, and control their man by doing their dead-level best to wrestle away his God-given leadership. Sorry, ladies, Steve is just reporting the news, and if he weren’t straight with them he would be in more serious trouble with someone more powerful than they are.
      • That is why making a marriage great is so hard: the perfect rhythm God planned for the male-female relationship was already broken 7.2 seconds in.
      • Making it worse is how men respond, because men botch it too. Insecure enough, with egos fragile enough, we react to our wives’ desire to usurp our leadership in one of two destructive ways. One, men compensate by being bullies, an aggressiveness that can show up verbally, emotionally, and sometimes, God forbid, physically; that is what God predicts in “he will rule over you,” and God didn’t want that either. Two, most men just go passive, hand off the reins, and go watch football, leaving a void that leads to generational brokenness that seems to have no remedy.

    2. 26:44 To Adam

      Scripture: Genesis 3:17–19 Genesis 1:28

      • Men, it’s your turn. To Adam God said: because you listened to your wife and ate from the tree, cursed is the ground because of you; through painful toil you will eat of it all your life; it will produce thorns and thistles; by the sweat of your brow you will eat your food until you return to the ground, for dust you are and to dust you will return.
      • The basic gist: if Eve will have relationship issues because of her sin, Adam will experience futility in most every career path he pursues. Back in chapter 1 God made Adam his representative ruler on earth, a leadership role that would play out first in the home and then across the planet: be fruitful, increase in number, fill the earth, and subdue it.
      • Instead of that leadership being a blessing, everything the man attempts to build will resist him and stubbornly fight against him. His work will never not be difficult again, not by God’s perfect plan but because of the fall.

  14. 28:28 Why the test: love is proven by choice

    Scripture: Genesis 3:22 Genesis 3:23–24

    • Remember the two special trees. The tree of the knowledge of good and evil was given to test us, a test we clearly failed. Why test us? It is all about free will. Creation happened because God wanted to be in relationship, but if you are the only woman and I’m the only man, there is no choice. Since love can only be proven by choice, God put one measly tree in a garden stocked with trees and told us to choose not to eat from it. Every other tree, have at it, baby.
    • But the gecko lied, saying they would be like God, which was true, just not the whole truth, and both of them ate. Afterward God said the man has now become like one of us, knowing good and evil. Innocence was lost.
    • So God said he must not be allowed to reach out and take from the tree of life and live forever. In effect: “I’ve got to get these two out of my garden, because if they eat from the tree of life they will live forever in a fallen, sinful state. I designed Adam to live forever, but not to live forever in blatant rebellion against me.”
    • So the Lord God banished him from the garden to work the ground and placed something on the east side of the garden; Steve will circle back to that. East was the only way out, bounded on the north and west by the Mediterranean and to the south by the Persian Gulf, so they were banished somewhere east of Eden, and God placed a cherubim and a flaming sword flashing back and forth to guard the way to the tree of life.

  15. 31:36 Chapter 4: Cain and Abel

    Scripture: Genesis 4 Genesis 4:3–4 Hebrews 11:4 Genesis 4:5

    • Turn the page to chapter 4: Adam and Eve are settling into their new digs east of Eden when Eve starts to feel odd, nauseous, out of sorts. Adam, not the brightest crayon, says, “Eve, your garments aren’t fitting like when God gave them to you; are you putting on weight?” Really, dude. Sure enough, she was pregnant and gave birth to Cain, and later to his brother Abel.
    • With nobody else to play with, you might think the boys got along famously. Not so much. They chose different careers: Abel kept the flocks; Cain worked the fields. And if you picture Abel as the good boy who went to church while Cain bet on camels at the racetrack, drank moonshine, and hated organized religion, not at all. Both were hard workers, both believed in God, and both tried to please him. Cain brought fruits of the soil, and Abel brought fat portions from the firstborn of his flock.
    • But something was off in Cain’s heart, and God knew it. Why did the Lord look with favor on Abel’s offering but not Cain’s? Hebrews 11:4 unpacks it: Abel offered his sacrifice by faith, but Cain was motivated differently, and we know it because Cain was very angry and his face was downcast.

    1. 34:09 Anger turned inward

      • “Downcast” is an old Semitic term meaning he was depressed. Cain was angry so long it began to consume him, and much depression is anger turned inward: unresolved sin. You can’t remain bitter against someone without it hardening and poisoning you and distorting your perceptions and responses. No matter how justified you feel, unresolved anger will uncoil on you in ways you can’t imagine.

  16. 35:01 Sin is crouching at your door

    Scripture: Genesis 4:6–7

    • God sees it happening to Cain and says something, which is always a great plan. Unlike with Cain’s parents, sin has now become epidemic, so God gets proactive: “Why are you angry? Why is your face downcast? If you do what is right, will you not be accepted? But if you do not do what is right, sin is crouching at your door; it desires to have you, but you must master it.”
    • “Desires” is the same word God used of Cain’s mom desiring to control his dad. Step one in mastery is recognizing what is happening: “Why are you angry, dude? You think it’s because of Abel, but are you sure he’s the real reason?”
    • Sin crouches so it can’t clearly be seen, or so it looks smaller and not that big a deal: another deception, like in the garden. “I probably shouldn’t nurse that grudge, but it’s not that bad, not as bad as what he did.” That is a crouching sin you either justify or minimize, and it is still sin.
    • In the early stages of self-pity, bitterness, pride, greed, anger, even addiction, you still have some measure of control. But a crouching sin creeping slowly and methodically into your life desires to have you and devour you. That jealousy, desire for control, competitiveness: it will have you if you don’t master it, and you’ve got to master it early.

  17. 38:02 Cain went out from the Lord’s presence

    Scripture: Genesis 4:8 Genesis 4:16

    • Cain doesn’t try to master it; he does some crouching of his own. Out in the field, Cain attacked his brother Abel and killed him.
    • Then verse 16, one of the saddest verses in God’s word: Cain went out from the Lord’s presence. God didn’t leave Cain; Cain left him and lived in the land of Nod. There it is: east of Eden.

    1. 38:34 We live in the same zip code

      • Steve underscores it because we live in the same zip code. Until Jesus comes again or calls us home, we also live east of Eden, a phrase that came to mean a place of brokenness where restless wanderers live. That is our world; we are so messed up.
      • People everywhere ask why things are in such freefall and why evil is wreaking havoc on so many lives and even our culture. Steve wishes he had an answer. All he has is this: we are broken, restless wanderers; like Cain, we are living in the land of Nod, just east of Eden.

  18. 39:34 Garments of skin

    Scripture: Genesis 3:21

    • We don’t have to end on this tragic note. Remember the leaves Adam and Eve used to cover their shame? It wasn’t long before they started crumbling. Did God say, “That’s what you get”? Laugh at their feeble attempts? Decide it would be easier to make two more of them than clean them up? No.
    • Because God’s heart is full of redemption, he made them garments of skin and clothed them. In doing so he painted a picture of his heart of forgiveness and what it would cost: a covering of animal skin required that blood be shed, pointing ahead to a coming Savior who would lay down his life and purchase salvation for a lost, broken world. God painted that picture and promised he would pay the price, right then, right there.

  19. 40:58 He will crush your head

    Scripture: Genesis 3:15 Genesis 3:15

    • Even as he handed down severe punishments for sin, God gave a promise: “I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel.” That is Genesis 3, and amazingly it is a prediction of the crucifixion of Jesus Christ.
    • Satan hoped to destroy Adam and Eve, and us, by deceiving us into sin, but Jesus volunteered and was destroyed in our place. We deserved the death he died; he bore the consequences of our sin. Satan struck Jesus’ heel in that horrific moment on Mount Calvary, but Jesus crushed Satan’s head when he walked out of the tomb, just as Genesis 3:15 said he would.

  20. 42:17 “Take and eat”

    Scripture: Matthew 26:26 Genesis 3:6

    • One more hint of redemption rocked Steve’s world when he first uncovered it. At the Lord’s Supper we drink the cup to remember the blood Jesus shed, and eat the bread representing his body, so utterly decimated by the beatings, the nails, the spear, and that awful crown. When Jesus taught his disciples about this meal, he handed them the bread and said, “Take and eat; this is my body.”
    • Is there another place in Scripture where those two verbs are joined? Right here in Genesis. In chapter 3, verse 6, Adam and Eve took and ate the forbidden fruit, and with that simple act they introduced sin, death, and destruction into our world. Utter disaster.

    1. 44:09 What got wrecked in Genesis is restored in Jesus

      Scripture: Matthew 26:26

      • But help is on the way: one day the promised one of God bursts onto the scene and turns “take and eat” on its head. What got wrecked in Genesis is restored in Jesus. Hallelujah.
      • The first man Adam got kicked out of Eden and forced the rest of us to live in a sorry place of brokenness, east of Eden, that we can’t break out of no matter how we try. But the last Adam, Jesus, was the only one who could do the deed to get us back where we were meant to be. Just before he laid down his life, not for anything he had done but for you and me, Jesus redeemed the taking and eating that got us into this mess when he broke the bread and said, “Take and eat; this is my body, and I’m giving it for you.”

  21. 45:37 Closing prayer

    • Steve thanks the Father for his heart of redemption: even in that moment of such brokenness he brought restoration and found a way to tell us a moment was coming when we could be restored from this life of brokenness east of Eden. “Hallelujah, what a Savior. We are redeemed.”
