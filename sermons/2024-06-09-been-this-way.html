Overview

  1. 00:05 “We’ve never been this way before”

    • Steve asks: if you were flying to Vegas and the pilot came over the PA before takeoff to say he had no clue which way to go because he had never been this way before, would you stay or go? He would ask for the closest exit door, and if there weren’t one, where they would like one.
    • That is the setup for the study: as Israel prepared to enter the promised land for the second time, their leaders passed through camp saying, “We don’t know which way to go, because we’ve never been this way before.”
    • Was it true? For this crowd it was a different way, but not for the nation. Only two of the two and a half million had been on the earlier trip, and the saga of the Red Sea crossing was told and retold every time they circled God’s mountain during those forty years. Before we cluck our tongues at them, remember that the unsettling, frightening path you may be on right now may feel new to you, but it is not at all new to your God.

  2. 02:06 Joshua was born in bondage

    Scripture: Joshua 3

    • By Joshua 3, Israel has returned to the same place they started from. They had been in captivity in Egypt for over 400 years, never able to think their own thoughts, plan their schedules, or make their own decisions, for twenty agonizingly long generations.
    • During that time Joshua son of Nun was born and raised in bondage, as were his daddy, granddaddy, and great-great-great-granddaddy. Bondage was Joshua’s family business; he had no clue how not to be in bondage.

    1. 03:21 Have you ever been in bondage?

      • Maybe you were addicted to alcohol, a drug, or co-addicted to some person. Most of us have been hooked on something, so we understand the dynamics. You try to say no, but no doesn’t work, and it leaves you irritable, jumpy, and afraid, stuck in a never-ending spin cycle until you knuckle under and your captor finds sweet satisfaction.
      • After you feed the addiction beast, the anxiety and dread that drove you turn into disgust. You are depressed and angry because you are no better than last time, and you beat yourself up for being so weak. You hate everyone when you can’t get satisfaction, and after you indulge, you hate yourself most of all.

    2. 05:08 “You mean I don’t have to do what I want to do?”

      • A friend of Steve’s spent years on this treadmill, toggling between parties and partners until she was exhausted by what she called her endless pursuit of pleasure. When he asked why she didn’t just stop, she looked at him like he was Chewbacca: “You mean I don’t have to do what I want to do?” That is bondage, and living in it is hell.

    3. 05:46 Two lies from the pit

      Scripture: Romans 8:37

      • The repeating, defeating cycle leaves you hopeless, if not helpless, trapped by two ugly deceptions straight from the pit of hell: that nothing you do will matter, and that nothing is ever going to change.
      • Even if Jesus is your forever treasure and you have everything you need in him to overcome, if the battle rages long enough, even though the Bible says you are more than a conqueror, what you feel is conquered. You just want to curl up in the fetal position and whimper.

  3. 06:57 Out of Egypt, the long way

    Scripture: Exodus 14:5–7 Exodus 13:17 Exodus 14:11

    • That is exactly what happened to the Israelites. God told Moses to tell Pharaoh to let his people go, and Pharaoh did, but almost immediately he changed his mind, saying they had let their free slave labor go, and saddled up 600 of his best chariots. No wonder the Israelites were terrified.
    • Worse, God didn’t lead them on the road through Philistine country, though it was shorter, barely more than an eleven-day hike along the sea, and they wouldn’t have had to tread water. Why take two and a half million people the long way, not to mention the wet way? The freshly freed people turned on Moses: what have you done to us, bringing us out here? Moses had no clue; he had never been this way either. He said, “Lord, you’ve got to fix this.”

    1. 08:34 God shows you what you already have

      Scripture: Exodus 14:15

      • The Lord, the only one who knew the way, said, “Why are you crying out to me?” because Moses already had everything he needed to lead these former slaves into freedom. When you are breaking out of your own bondage, God will always show you what you have already got.
      • We keep looking for God to give us some bright, shiny, next-day-shipping thing, but more often he wants to show us what we already have and aren’t yet using. God asked Moses what was in his hand: a rod. Raise it and stretch it out. When he did, the waters divided and Israel went through on dry ground. These people had been this way before.

  4. 09:38 Phase two: walking with heads held high

    Scripture: Leviticus 26:13

    • The parting of the waters was only phase one of the rescue. Phase one was God calling them out of bondage; phase two was following God into a whole new dimension of living. Getting to the other side of whatever has had a hold on you doesn’t mean you have stopped thinking, much less behaving, as though you were still in bondage.
    • God wanted more for his people and told them so: “I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of Egypt so that you would no longer be slaves; I broke the bars of your yoke and enabled you to walk with heads held high.” God doesn’t want you walking around with the title former slave, recovering addict, or unfaithful spouse.
    • So God didn’t take them straight from the outhouse to the penthouse. He removed them from their bondage and let them languish a while in the wilderness. From slavery to the desert doesn’t sound like an upgrade, and it wasn’t intended to be. But Steve has learned from his own desert time that when God is your guide, great blessings come from the toughest of times, and when you break through, you will have become relentless.

    1. 12:15 Out of captivity, but captivity not out of them

      • God didn’t want them to stay in the desert, only to go through it; his original plan was a weekend hike. The problem is that just because the Israelites were taken out of captivity doesn’t mean the captivity had been taken out of them. In their minds they were still slaves.

  5. 12:47 The spies at the front door

    • Steve proves it by what they did when they reached the front door of the promised land. It looked amazing, but to make sure everything was copacetic, they sent twelve men, one from each tribe, to check whether what God said it is really is what it is.
    • Joshua and Caleb came back affirming everything God said: this place is so good, one grape could feed the whole cul-de-sac, and they had already put their tents on Craigslist. Yes, there will be obstacles, but if God split that mighty sea and promised to hand them this land, “we got this.”

    1. 14:17 There will always be the ten

      Scripture: Numbers 13:33

      • Then the ten made it back. However wonderful an opportunity is, there will always be the ten, full of negativity: the walls are fortified, the people are like giants, we seem like grasshoppers, and one of those big grapes could crush us.
      • It was ten versus two, and Steve has experienced ten against thousands. Negativity carries enormous power; its voices can drown out the most positive opportunity and spread fear, doubt, and anxiety to everyone within earshot. If ten are willing to say “we can’t,” thousands will be willing to say “we won’t.”
      • Sure enough, the former slaves voted no, even though it meant forty more years in the wilderness, taking laps around Sinai: no longer in Egypt but still in bondage, until that entire generation of “stinking thinkers” died off and a new generation came back to try again.

  6. 16:07 Joshua 3: follow the box

    Scripture: Joshua 3:1–3

    • That brings us to Joshua 3. Early in the morning Joshua and the Israelites set out from Shittim (go ahead and giggle) and camped at the Jordan. After three days the officers went through the camp with orders: when you see the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God and the priests carrying it, move out and follow it.

    1. 16:39 What the ark was

      • Most of what we know about the ark comes from good old Indiana Jones, but it was far more than a good-luck charm. It was the holiest manifestation of God himself on earth: a box about two feet by four holding a golden jar reminding Israel how God fed them forty years, Aaron’s shepherd’s rod that kept budding though long dead, and, holiest of all, the tablets on which God wrote the Ten Commandments.
      • What made the ark so special was God’s promise that as long as the people were faithful and trusted him, he would meet with them from atop the mercy seat. We are talking God himself.

    2. 17:54 “Follow it where?” Just follow me

      • When the presence of God starts heading out of camp, you move too. Steve imagines the first question: “Follow it where?” Don’t ask; just follow it, and that is how you will know which way to go, because you haven’t been this way before. Following the box really means “Follow me.”
      • That is the perfect answer for anyone breaking out of bondage, because the big fear is always “I don’t know what to do or where to go or what life will look like now that I’m set free.” God says, “I get that. Just follow me.” Some think they do know the way. “I love you, but you don’t.” When the box moves, follow it, because he is taking you somewhere amazing you have never been.

    3. 19:34 Keep your distance and face forward

      Scripture: Joshua 3:4

      • As you follow, keep a distance of about a thousand yards between you and the ark, and Steve will explain why. The presence of God is about to move, and when it does, you move too, but you have to be ready.
      • Don’t look around and don’t look back. God will never lead you from behind; he will always be ahead of wherever you are, because he is far less concerned with where you have been than with where you are headed. Always face forward, and when you sense God is moving, move too.

    4. 20:39 Why following Jesus can feel hard

      • Just don’t jump the gun: wait one football field, no, ten. When Steve hits his driver, he has to hit the ball fifteen or sixteen more times to get to a thousand yards, and with his old eyes he would barely see a two-by-four box, much less which way it was heading.
      • That is why following Jesus sometimes feels really hard. You look, pause, and strain, never sure you are seeing. “Do you see me, Steve?” “Oh yes, Lord,” while under his breath he says, “I can’t see him, and I have no clue where he’s going.” But actually it isn’t hard: face forward and press on, because Jesus is always just ahead. When in doubt, keep looking ahead, don’t be distracted, and press on.

  7. 22:33 Consecrate yourselves

    Scripture: Joshua 3:5

    • One more thing: consecrate yourselves, because tomorrow the Lord will do amazing things among you. That is one reason to keep your distance: God is holy, and you are anything but. After 400 years, now we are getting to amazing.
    • Consecration is a high-octane spiritual term for a declaration that you are holy, not perfect, because none of us will get there, but set apart for God’s intended and holy purpose. In the Old Testament a worshiper went to the tabernacle and, after a painstaking process, made consecration. That is what Joshua is telling them to do before tomorrow.

    1. 23:57 Do the holy thing, then do holy things

      Scripture: Leviticus 11:44–45 James 1:22 Ephesians 4:24 Ephesians 4:25–29

      • After you do the holy thing, you also have to do holy things. Do the holy thing at the altar, in the baptistry, or at communion, but don’t neglect doing holy things afterward.
      • God put it this way in Leviticus: consecrate yourselves and be holy, for I am holy; I am the Lord who brought you up out of Egypt, therefore be holy. James says don’t merely listen to the word and deceive yourselves; do what it says, or why bother listening? In Ephesians Paul urges believers, already in Christ and already consecrated, to put on the new self, created to be like God in true holiness.
      • So Paul goes on: put off falsehood and speak truthfully. Everybody blows a cork now and again, but don’t let the sun go down while you are still angry. If you have a history of stealing, cut it out. If you have used your mouth destructively, use it only for what builds others up. That is what Joshua means by consecrate: do what needs doing to be made holy, then by your actions be holy.

  8. 26:31 The next morning at the Jordan

    • The next morning two and a half million people approached the Jordan with no strategy for crossing except that, unlike forty years before, this time they would obey and do what God says.
    • Most months the Jordan is a lazy river, no more than 30 yards at its widest and under six feet deep. But at flood stage during the harvest it could swell to almost a mile wide, with snowmelt from Mount Hermon creating a current strong enough to sweep livestock downstream. How would millions of people, babies and toddlers, the old, the sick, and the disabled, get across? No one was to be left behind.

    1. 27:57 The ark goes ahead; the priests step in

      Scripture: Joshua 3:6 Joshua 3:8 Joshua 3:15–16

      • Joshua told the priests to take up the ark and pass on ahead of the people. There is that word again: ahead, always straight ahead. Then the priests were told that when they reached the edge of the water, they should go and stand in the river. “Seriously, God, have you seen this river?”
      • As soon as the priests set their feet in the Jordan, the waters flowing downstream were cut off and stood up in a heap.

  9. 28:58 God never does it the same way twice

    Scripture: Exodus 14:16–22

    • Steve pauses to marvel at God’s creativity. Forty years before, God told Moses to raise his staff and stretch out his hand, and all night the Lord drove the sea back with a strong east wind, and Israel walked through on dry ground between walls of water. Forty years later it is not a sea but an angry, flooded river tangled with brush, and priests not known for their machismo must step into a raging torrent before the water stops and piles up in a heap. What happened on the other side of that heap is on Steve’s list of things to ask.

    1. 30:26 Three healings of the blind

      Scripture: Matthew 9:27–28 Mark 8 John 9

      • It reminds Steve of Jesus giving sight to the blind three times in the Gospels, never the same way twice. In Matthew a group of blind men cry, “Lord, Son of David, have mercy on us,” Jesus asks if they believe he is able, and he heals them. In Mark 8 friends bring a man who says nothing; Jesus leads him out of town, spits in his eyes, and when he sees people like trees walking around, touches his eyes again. In John 9 Jesus makes mud with spit, puts it on the man’s eyes, and sends him to wash.

    2. 31:50 The former blind man Christian conference

      • Having been around church stuff all his life, Steve imagines a Former Blind Man Christian Conference. The man from John 9 gives the keynote about the mud; the man from Matthew objects that you have to say “Lord, Son of David, have mercy”; the man from Mark 8 insists you can’t be healed unless Jesus spits in your eye. Like most church groups, they would leave with four new denominations: the mudball Christians, the spit-in-the-eye Christians, the word of faith Christians, and the Orthodox “Lord, Son of David” Christians.
      • Steve is glad God’s work isn’t limited to one way of getting it done. Joshua’s way wasn’t better than Moses’ way, just another way, and it still got done, and it truly was amazing.

  10. 33:48 Step after step, and nothing happens

    • Picture the moment: the Jordan at flood stage, the priests hoisting the ark, everyone ready for the amazing thing God promised. The priests have waited too long to blow it now, so they believe the same miracle will happen again. They likely expected the wind to start blowing, maybe through Joshua’s thick wavy hair like a Michael Jackson video, dramatic, powerful, and inspirational, the way it happened last time.
    • Instead they plopped their big hairy toes into the water and nearly drowned. Kudos to them, they took another step and another until they were knee-deep in boiling water, and one priest says to the other, “I think this water is broke.”

    1. 35:56 Have you ever been there?

      • You are tired of bondage and of treading water, and come hell or high water you will follow hard after God. You don’t want to stay in the desert or go back to Egypt; you want to dwell in the land of his promise. “Here I come, Jesus. One, two, three, step.” Nothing. Step again. Nothing. Did I hear wrong? I thought the deal was confess my addiction, get healed, follow you a thousand yards back, and voila, the waters part. Either the water is broken or I am hopelessly broken.
      • That is the priests in this moment. Step one, two, three, four: nothing, except their feet got muddier. At first this amazing thing seemed like no thing at all. “God, we’re doing what you asked, but all we’re getting is more stuck in the mud, and I’m starting to feel like a fool.”

    2. 38:01 God meets you in the mud

      • Steve is convinced that’s how they felt, and he has felt it too. But he has learned over the years that in moments like this, true strength and courage are finally allowed to break through. Those men decided they were not going back to Egypt or the barren desert; they would stand on God’s promise, muddy feet and all.
      • Great stories of faith so often happen exactly like that. Many times, maybe most of the time, the place where you will meet God is in the mud.

  11. 39:00 God works upstream

    • Biblical archaeologists strongly believe that when the priests’ feet touched the water, what seemed like nothing was actually an earthquake about 20 miles upstream at the town mentioned here, Adam. Steve’s God doesn’t need the earthquake, but that doesn’t mean he won’t use one.
    • Earthquakes evidently happen at that spot every 300 to 400 years. In 1927 the cliffs overlooking the Jordan there fell into the river and blocked its flow for about 21 hours, plenty of time for two and a half million people. If God used an earthquake, what a reminder that he really does work upstream, a thousand yards or twenty miles ahead, so he can meet you in the mud and turn your chaotic life into a masterpiece.

    1. 40:48 Don’t stop stepping

      • So don’t stop stepping, and don’t ever turn back. Keep taking steps even when you don’t receive the instant feedback you think you deserve. That is what the priests did, and by the time they got to the middle, everyone was standing on solid ground.

  12. 41:23 Stones from the middle

    Scripture: Joshua 3:17 Joshua 4:10 Joshua 4:2–3

    • In verse 17 the priests stand in the middle of the Jordan as two million people cross by, and turning the page to chapter 4, verse 10, they are still standing in the same place; the story hasn’t advanced. Why? The Lord told Joshua to choose twelve men, one per tribe, to take twelve stones from the middle of the Jordan, where the priests stood, and carry them to where they would stay that night.
    • They built an altar to remind everyone who has ever been stuck in the mud: don’t miss the amazing. Don’t be in such a hurry to get to the other side that you miss what God was doing for you in the muddy middle, because that is where God loves to do amazing things.
    • There is a second reason: one day your kids will look at those stones and ask, “Mama, what are those stones for?” and you can tell them what God did when you were stuck: “I got stuck, baby, but God set me free.”

  13. 43:35 Joshua’s second set of stones

    Scripture: Joshua 4:9

    • Joshua had a second set of stones. The first set was for all the people to see and remember; the second would remain unseen. While the priests stood in the middle, Joshua built his own private altar as the people hurried across, not knowing how long the water would hold. Steve would hurry too and do his remembering once he got to shore.
    • Please don’t be in such a hurry that you neglect remembering time, because what God is doing in your muddy middle you don’t want to miss. If the mud is where you are, God wants to meet you right there.
    • Joshua paused in his muddy middle and carefully set up the stones, knowing the water would soon cover them and no one would ever know but Joshua and his God. Still he took the time in the raging waters to select them and build the altar at the very spot where the priests had stood, and they are there to this day.

  14. 46:01 Steve’s own stones

    • Do you have visual reminders of how faithful God has been? Steve learned long ago the power of a tangible reminder, and he brought a few. His dad gave him a small figurine in 1979 when he started preaching, and writing this message he realized it was time to pass it on to his son.
    • He scavenged a rod from the Sea of Galilee during a hard season and saw the Lion of Judah in it, remembering that the Jesus who walked on those waters could carry him across his. A portrait of the laughing Jesus, his favorite, reminds him of a time stress nearly stole his joy. His kidney pillow, used after his transplant, reminds him God gave him more time. A picture cube Josh made him not long after his voice changed has an audio message Steve listens to more often than Josh knows, thanking God for his part in the man Josh is today.
    • These aren’t valuable to anyone else, but they are so valuable to him. Do you have them?

    1. 48:42 Identify your own stones

      • Don’t ever assume God isn’t doing anything just because you feel stuck in the muddy middle; Steve promises he is, and it is easier to remember that he is when you can remember that he was.
      • Identify some of your own stones, a picture or a voicemail, something that takes you back to the moment he pulled you out or provided when you were about to go under. Then build an altar to the God who specializes in pulling the people he loves out of the muddy middle. Do it even if you feel too stuck to imagine how, because he will meet you there. You may not see him yet, but he is up ahead, and though he is ahead of where you are, he always knows where you are.

  15. 50:12 Prayer before the bread and the cup

    • Steve prays as the church shares another memory moment, the bread and the cup, tangible reminders of what God has done. As they consider their own muddy middle, not to get stuck there but to celebrate how God pulled them out, he asks for hearts of gratitude and joy for what God has done, continues to do, and will do, in the name and memory of Jesus.
