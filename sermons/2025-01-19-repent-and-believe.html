Overview

  1. 00:13 Eight weeks in the gospel of Mark

    • Over the next eight weeks the church will explore key themes in the gospel of Mark. Mark records Peter’s eyewitness testimony and labors to make sure the Romans don’t miss the Son of Man, and we must open our eyes and ears so we don’t miss him.
    • Most scholars agree Mark was the first gospel written; of its 678 verses, all but 31 are quoted in other gospels. Details that fit together across accounts without being forced are called undesigned coincidences. Mark leaves out that the temple cleansing happened during Passover, and John adds it later, which builds the narrative stronger.
    • Each writer had his own style for his own audience, which should encourage modern readers: there were many sources and eyewitnesses, and the writers didn’t collaborate, yet their independent accounts confirm each other as authentic.

    1. 02:00 Four portraits, four A-pluses

      • Think of Mark as an announcement, with the trumpet going. Matthew wrote to prove to Jewish readers that Jesus is the rightful heir to David’s throne. Luke focuses on Christ’s humanity, which his Greek audience would eat up: a perfect baby who grew up to be a perfect man. John wrote to prove to the whole world that Jesus of Nazareth is the Son of God.
      • Matthew says Jesus is King, Luke says Jesus is a man, John says Jesus is God, and Mark’s theme is that Jesus Christ is God’s servant. They all got an A+ on the assignment.

  2. 02:48 Show me: what Jesus does

    Scripture: Mark 10:45

    • Because Mark writes to Romans, he focuses on what Jesus does and how people respond, and he records more miracles than any other gospel writer. Romans were used to powerful men with God complexes, so Mark shows Jesus going from place to place meeting physical and spiritual needs of all kinds of people. Much of this church’s identity develops the same way, by fixing our eyes on the ministry of Christ.
    • Josh believes revival in the church won’t come through more proclamations but through more demonstrations. You can talk about your faith with confidence, but with YouTube anyone can do that: show me you believe. The Romans had heard all the emperors’ claims and were desperate to see something real, and did they ever.
    • The most important verse in Mark, which will be plastered everywhere this series as the church is challenged not just to speak Jesus but to serve like Jesus, is Mark 10: “For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many.” Jesus came full of grace and truth; truth is best communicated with words, but grace is best expressed with our hands.

  3. 04:55 The servant’s identity and his messenger

    Scripture: Mark 1:1–3 Mark 1:4

    • While Mark aims to show a servant, he wastes no time recording who this servant is: “The beginning of the good news about Jesus the Messiah, as it is written in Isaiah the prophet: I will send my messenger ahead of you… a voice of one calling in the wilderness, prepare the way for the Lord.” The prophecy should be fresh after a Christmas season in Isaiah; both Malachi and Isaiah tell of the messenger who will roll out the red carpet for Jehovah God.
    • So John the Baptist appeared in the wilderness preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins. Jesus called him the greatest of the prophets, and his lifestyle and message resembled Elijah’s. His approach was simple: the people were in a spiritual desert, worse than the one their ancestors wandered in for 40 years, and he called them to leave it, trust their Yeshua, Joshua, Jesus, and walk in the promise.

    1. 06:10 Not a flashy preacher

      • Josh loves John because he always made much of the name of Jesus. He wasn’t a dynamic communicator, online influencer, YouTuber, podcaster, or Amazon bestseller, which is what people look for in a pastor today. “Josh, you should have a podcast.” No, Josh should have a healthy marriage and children who love and obey Jesus. He wants to do this, right here: know the church, walk with them, watch them raise their kids, and be their pastor.
      • John “kept it 100.” He was faithful, and that made him great in the Lord’s eyes; he showed up and followed the call even when it cost him.

    2. 07:10 Water and Spirit

      Scripture: Mark 1:7–8

      • John’s message: after me comes one more powerful than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to untie; I baptize you with water, but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit.
      • With many church backgrounds in the room, Josh addresses an issue. John was sent with authority to preach repentance and baptize in water, and Spirit baptism doesn’t make John’s baptism meaningless or mean water baptism would one day be replaced. John’s message and baptism were preparation so people would be ready to trust Jesus, as Andrew did. Water baptism demonstrated a willingness to turn from sin; when Jesus baptizes with the Spirit, the whole person is transformed, so Jesus offers both forgiveness and power to live godly lives.
      • Baptism on its own, apart from the Spirit, is just a bath. Its power isn’t in the ritual but in how it connects us to the redemptive work of Jesus, the work of the Spirit, and God’s promise in his word. Anyone the Lord is prompting about baptism can reach out to Josh for coffee.

  4. 09:00 A servant who gives orders

    Scripture: Mark 1:9–13

    • Mark shows a servant, and uses the prophets to show he is the Son of God. You would expect a servant to take orders, but Jesus doesn’t place himself under the authority of those he serves; God’s servant gives orders, and throughout the series even the demons obey. Most servants roll out the red carpet; they don’t walk on it.
    • Before Jesus calls his first disciples, Mark shows how unusual this servant is: Jesus is baptized by John in the Jordan, heaven is torn open, the Spirit descends like a dove, and a voice says, “You are my Son, whom I love; with you I am well pleased.” Then the Spirit sends him into the wilderness for 40 days, tempted by Satan, with wild animals and angels attending him.
    • Mark calls Jesus the Son of God throughout his gospel. This was no ordinary Jewish servant but the Son of God who came down from heaven to die for the sins of the world. In the kingdom of God the greatest ruler is always the greatest servant; nobody saw it coming, but ready or not, the servant has arrived.

  5. 11:02 The announcement: repent and believe the good news

    Scripture: Mark 1:14–15

    • After John was put in prison, Jesus went into Galilee proclaiming the good news of God: “The time has come. The kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the good news!”
    • Throughout his ministry the crowds were amazed because the teachers of the law said, “This is what this says,” but Jesus spoke with authority: “You’ve heard it said, but I say to you.” He corrected the experts.

    1. 12:02 What “gospel” meant to a Roman

      • “Good news” comes up twice in this first message. The Greek word euangelion is translated gospel or good news: angelos is a messenger, and the prefix means joyful, so it is news that brings joy.
      • For Mark’s Roman audience it didn’t yet carry today’s religious weight; it carried historical weight. There is a difference between the 5:00 news and the news during 9/11. Josh was in ninth grade, in first-period English, when his teacher rolled in the TV cart as the second plane hit the World Trade Center, a life-altering moment for every American.
      • An ancient Roman inscription begins, “The beginning of the gospel of Caesar Augustus,” speaking of his birth and coronation. A gospel was history in the making: a coronation, a new king, or a victory. When the Greeks won at Marathon and Salamis, they sent heralds with the good news: we fought for you, we won, now you are free. A gospel is an event so significant it changes your status in the world.

    2. 14:02 What sets Christianity apart

      • Every other world religion says, “This is what you have to do to be saved.” Christianity declares a gospel: this is the moment everything changed and this is what has been accomplished. Jesus lived the life you couldn’t and died the death you deserve so you might become the righteousness of God. Repent and believe, and you will receive eternal life.
      • It is the gospel of God because it comes from God and brings peace with God; the gospel of the kingdom because faith in Christ brings you into God’s kingdom; the gospel of Jesus Christ because he is its heartbeat. Without his life, death, and resurrection, death would have the final word, but God’s servant killed death once and for all. Paul called it the gospel of the grace of God. The gospel according to Mark is breaking news that God accepts you not on the basis of your performance but on the performance of his holy servant Jesus.

  6. 15:43 “The kingdom of God has come near”

    Scripture: Genesis 1–2 Genesis 3 Romans 7:24

    • To understand that, go back to creation. Mark doesn’t give a traditional creation account, probably because it is assumed at Jesus’ baptism: only one can tear the sky, the one who made it.
    • In Genesis 1–2 we see a perfect world with perfect relationships with each other, God, and creation, because God and we were in our proper positions. In Genesis 3 Eve is deceived, and instead of leading her away from temptation Adam listens, and they break the only law in the garden. When God was King there was one law; when we chose to be kings there were 613. We didn’t do well with one; how did we do with 613?
    • No one could hold up the weight of the law. All we could think about was ourselves, and nothing kills relationships quicker than self-centeredness, so every relationship suffered. As humanity treads water and the law pulls us under, the only question worth asking is, who will rescue us? Paul asks it most authentically: “What a wretched man I am! Who will rescue me from this body of death?”

    1. 17:30 Earth’s hero

      Scripture: Revelation 5:12

      • Josh thinks humanity, believers and unbelievers, deeply understands its need for a savior, and it shows in the greatest stories. Star Wars is the obvious one for this nerd: the lightsaber battles are cool, but the story is about who will rescue us from the evil empire, and George Lucas should pay royalties to God. Every culture’s stories draw on the biblical theme that something has gone terribly wrong and someone must come make it right.
      • The gospel of the kingdom is the good news that Jesus is earth’s hero, the one everyone can’t stop making movies about. When Captain America catches Thor’s hammer, everyone cheers, “He’s worthy.” That is actually going to happen: when Jesus shows up, the whole world will say, “Worthy is the Lamb who was slain.”

    2. 18:57 The kingdom’s two-phase battle plan

      Scripture: Romans 7:25

      • Phase one: Jesus came in weakness to die for us, the suffering servant. Phase two: he will return in strength to end history, the conquering King. Meanwhile he is healing things and preparing us for glory.
      • The key to preparing for the kingdom is surrender: placing ourselves under the servant and following him as King. When he returns there will be no more pain, cancer, sadness, disease, poverty, divorce, tyrants, corruption, fires, tears, or death; he will wipe away our tears, and humanity will be reunited with its maker in the new heavens and new earth. Paul asks who will rescue us, and on that day we will join him: “Thanks be to God, who delivers me through Jesus Christ our Lord.”

  7. 20:13 His method: “Come, follow me”

    Scripture: Mark 1:16–20 John 1

    • Jesus gives his message, and next his method: walking beside the Sea of Galilee, he sees Simon and Andrew casting a net and says, “Come, follow me, and I will send you out to fish for people.” At once they leave their nets. A little farther on he calls James and John, who leave their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men and follow him.
    • Andrew was a follower of John the Baptist and first believed John’s message of repentance before coming to faith in Christ. Reading this in Mark 1, we often assume it is their first encounter with the Messiah, but that happened in John 1; this is much later. Reading the gospels together makes sense of it. This isn’t a call to faith; it is a call to discipleship, sacrifice, and following Christ.

    1. 21:27 Jesus before family and career

      • Zebedee had hired servants, so his business was more of a fishing enterprise. In ancient cultures family was huge; today some people skip family Thanksgiving for “Friendsgiving,” which Josh thinks is stupid. For us, leaving family can feel like a crowning achievement of adulthood, but for these men it was a big deal.
      • Jesus wanting priority over mother and father may not bother many of us, but priority over your career has you sweating. “Come, follow me” is a call to prioritize Jesus over family and career. It doesn’t mean abandoning family: Zebedee had help, James and John talked with him again, and Peter will see his mother-in-law healed later this chapter. It isn’t dishonoring parents; it is a change of priorities: everything else comes second.
      • Your career is a way to serve him by building up the church and supporting his ministry. Your family is a way to serve him by making disciples, starting at home; men, your wife is the greatest earthly gift God has given you, and your opportunity to serve him by serving her.
      • A raise if you work Sundays: career or Christ? He is nobody’s backup plan, not even to family. If Dad wants more time only if you stop talking about Jesus, are you going to hush up? Jesus isn’t calling you to a cozy Christian life but to put him first and live with the results. Some relationships might suffer and some might thrive; your career might suffer, and God might lead you to a more fulfilling one. He doesn’t call you to a nominal, average Christian life.

    2. 25:00 Not just for the pastor: Luke 14

      Scripture: Luke 14:26

      • Some think this radical call is for the pastor, not the pew people; these men were going into full-time ministry. But in Luke 14, Jesus turns to a large crowd and says, “If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters—yes, even their own life—such a person cannot be my disciple.” Nobody prints that on a T-shirt, and the crowd probably got thinner. “If anyone” means all of them, not just elders and life group leaders.
      • We are taught not to hate our enemies, so how can Jesus say hate father and mother? He wants you to follow him so passionately, consistently, wholeheartedly, and faithfully that your family devotion looks like hate by comparison. He won’t be your backup plan or play second fiddle; if you have stipulations, you don’t get him.
      • “I’ll obey if the cancer goes away, my career takes off, my children forgive me, my family reconciles.” If you say, “Jesus, I’ll drop my nets if blank,” your blank is what you really want and follow; that is your god. Jesus is not a means to an end and will not be used. If you want to follow Jesus, he is the ultimate goal, and you learn contentment with whatever he adds or takes away. You want relevance, fulfillment, purpose; Jesus is the only relevance and fulfillment you need, and he is your purpose.

  8. 28:12 Fishers of men

    Scripture: Mark 1:17

    • “Come, follow me, and I will send you out to fish for people,” perhaps more familiar as “I will make you fishers of men.” Josh’s stepbrother is a pro bass fisherman. Josh walks through what he has heard about fishing: look under the log, set the hook, take the selfie, fry it in peanut oil, go find its brothers and sisters, and dynamite works too.
    • Part of the point is that becoming a fisherman takes commitment and planning: skills, equipment, practice casting, trolling, and jigging, knowing where and when fish bite, rods, reels, hooks, bait, a nice bass boat (“you’re welcome, fellas”), knowing how to swim, and patience. It is a process, and Jesus is saying, “Come, I’m on a journey; I want to show you where I’m going.”
    • Jesus didn’t invent the term; it was a common phrase for teachers able to persuade minds, who baited their hooks with teachings and caught disciples. A fisherman’s life translates to ministry: teamwork, patience, courage when things get crazy, understanding how things work. Fishermen weren’t quitters or complainers, because you can’t quit on a boat, and nobody wants quitters and complainers in ministry either.
    • In biblical imagery the sea is a place of chaos and death, cold and dark, representing the kingdom of darkness where we tread water calling for rescue. “I will make you fishers of men” means: I will make you someone who knows how to grab people out of the kingdom of darkness and introduce them to the kingdom of light.

    1. 31:30 They had no idea

      • Peter, Andrew, James, and John had no idea what the journey held; they dropped their nets with no questions asked. They didn’t know Jesus would die, that his kingdom was spiritual rather than political, that he would entrust them with a world-changing message, walk on water, and command the wind and waves. They didn’t know they were walking with God, that the crowds would grow so big and then so small, that he would be found innocent yet crucified, that the tomb would be empty three days later, that they would eat with the risen Jesus, see Pentecost, be persecuted, and be killed for saying Jesus is alive.
      • Nor did they know that 2.4 billion Christians, a third of the world’s living population, would thank them for dropping their nets, not counting billions through the ages. They believed Jesus was the Messiah; that was all they knew, and it was enough.

  9. 33:00 A demon in the synagogue

    Scripture: Mark 1:21–28 Mark 4 James 2:19

    • When they followed Jesus to Capernaum on the Sabbath, they realized something special was about to happen. A man with an evil spirit cried out, “What do you want with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One of God!” “Be quiet!” said Jesus sternly. “Come out of him!” The spirit shook him violently and came out with a shriek, and the people were amazed: a new teaching, with authority; he even gives orders to evil spirits and they obey him. News spread quickly over Galilee. This is one reason reading large chunks of text improves your understanding: keep reading.
    • The disciples have a lot to learn, but the evil spirit knows exactly who Jesus is, his humanity and deity, calling him Jesus of Nazareth and the Holy One of God, and tries to jump ahead of the Lord’s timing. The last thing Jesus wanted was a teaching assistant from Satan’s army. He literally muzzled the demon, the same word he would use to hush the storm in Mark 4.
    • Unfortunately many people, even in our gatherings, can tell exactly who Jesus is and even fear judgment yet remain lost. James mentions them in chapter 2: “You believe that there is one God. Good! Even the demons believe that—and shudder.”

  10. 35:06 Repentance and faith are twins

    Scripture: Mark 1:15 Deuteronomy

    • That is why we must never forget how Jesus announces the good news: “Repent and believe the good news.” The repentance we are commanded is a result of faith, born at the same moment: you were expecting faith, and oops, twins. Don’t ask which twin is older; it is one of those mysteries the Lord reminds us in Deuteronomy not to worry about. Sometimes faith looks first, sometimes repentance; Josh thinks of it like an ice cream swirl: he doesn’t know whether vanilla or chocolate is first, just that it’s good.

    1. 35:55 Repentance that brings salvation

      Scripture: Galatians 2:20 1 Corinthians 6:19–20

      • What Josh knows about repentance is that it makes him weep and hate his past life, because that is why Jesus had to endure the cross. Every time he takes communion and remembers that Christ’s blood washes away his sin, it drives him to repent, knowing he is part of the reason Christ had to die. When a person realizes they wounded Christ and their heart breaks because he was nailed to the cross for their sin, that is the repentance that brings salvation.
      • When he seeks a holy life because Christ loved him and gave himself for him, and because he is not his own but was bought at a price, that isn’t legalism; it is the leading of the Spirit. When he counts the cost and decides to die to self instead of living for self, that is the repentance Jesus is looking for.
      • We don’t want repentance without belief or belief without repentance; Jesus commands both, of new believers and gray-haired believers too. This sinner will spend the rest of his life repenting of a life of sin, and believing that Jesus did exactly what he was sent to do: he saved sinners like Josh.

    2. 38:02 A life marked by repentance

      Scripture: Mark 1:15 John 3:18

      • Maybe Christians think the more they follow Jesus the less they need to repent, or that once they repent of an old sin there is no value in remembering it. Josh has found the opposite: a life marked by repentance is a life marked by Christ. His sin can’t bury or accuse him, but he refuses to forget it.
      • Spurgeon said the more a Christian knows of Christ’s love, the more he will hate himself for sinning against it. How could Josh sin against the greatest love he has known, the God who chose him? He doesn’t want to sin again, but when he does, he will resubmit himself to this divine command in Mark 1 over and over until Jesus comes: repent and believe the good news.
      • Josh pleads with the church to obey their Redeemer: believe, repent, be saved, receive eternal life. Refuse, and you stand condemned already.

  11. 39:46 Closing prayer: we drop our nets

    • Josh comes to the Father torn up over the man he was, the people he hurt, and the sin he committed in the face of God’s love, and asks the Lord to help everyone. Encouraged by his grace and mercy, he asks that they would also learn to be holy: set apart and sanctified by his word and Spirit.
    • “Lord, you say come follow me. We drop our nets. We are here, we are ready, we are yours. We love you.”
