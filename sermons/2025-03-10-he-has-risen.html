Overview

  1. 00:36 Three words that hold up the faith

    Scripture: Mark 16 Mark 16:6

    • The church wraps up its series in Mark with Mark 16. Three words stand out across all time and history, a phrase that forces us to do something with Jesus, to think about life and death, and that holds up the entire Christian faith: “He has risen.” Another quickly follows, one of Josh’s favorites: “He is not here.”
    • There is an empty tomb, and the one who emptied it predicted his death and resurrection months before. The event was planned by someone with the power and authority to pull it off. Who planned it, and why?

  2. 01:30 If Christ has not been raised

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:19 1 Corinthians 15:12–19

    • All of Christianity hinges on whether the resurrection occurred as a historical event. If someone came up and presented enough evidence that it didn’t, Josh and billions of believers would have to say with Paul that we are to be pitied more than everyone. It would be a massive embarrassment.
    • Paul argued the point to the Corinthians, who were dealing with resurrection deniers: if Christ has not been raised, our preaching is useless and so is your faith, we are false witnesses, you are still in your sins and still an enemy of God, and if only for this life we have hope in Christ, we are to be pitied more than all others.

  3. 03:05 The burden of proof is on the skeptic

    • Josh thinks the burden of proof is on the non-believer, not the believer; historically the church has felt it had to prove this. There is so much evidence that the greater leap of faith, and a blind one, is to deny the resurrection.
    • In the first century there are over fifteen historical authors who claim Jesus was raised, more if you include non-Christian sources: Paul, Peter, James, Jude, Matthew, Mark, Luke, John, and others such as Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Justin Martyr, and Tacitus. They either witnessed it or had an immediate connection to an eyewitness.
    • Then the list of authors from that same time who denied the resurrection: there isn’t a slide, because there aren’t any. The critics show up much later, and the most credible objective ones not until the fifteenth century; the earliest were protecting an existing philosophy more than seeking truth. No one in the ancient world stood up with a conflicting eyewitness report. There is literally no contest.

  4. 05:52 What kind of God lets himself be humiliated?

    Scripture: Philippians 2:5–7 Romans 4:25

    • The ancient world’s biggest pushback wasn’t against the reality of the resurrection. It was: why would you worship a God who lets people humiliate him? We see it in the Gospels: if you’re God, save yourself.
    • The modern Christian who was in Sunday school says, “Pick me”: Philippians 2. Christ Jesus, being in very nature God, made himself nothing by taking the very nature of a servant. What kind of God allows himself to be humiliated? A loving God. The one who made everything made himself nothing.
    • Why? The Christian who doesn’t forsake the gathering knows Romans 4: he was delivered over to death for our sins and raised to life for our justification. He humbled himself to bring us peace with God and reconciled us to God forever.

  5. 07:24 A dead savior can’t save anyone

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:1

    • Many others claimed to be the Messiah before, during, and after Jesus, but all those claims died when the claimant died, because a dead savior can’t save anyone. Christianity stands apart because our Savior lives. We sing about it; it is everything to us, and without it we have nothing.
    • Like Lazarus, we were dead in our transgressions and sins, but we are alive in Christ. He called us by name and we walked out. Our flesh might reek, but our hearts have been made new. If you are going to take that from a Christian, you had better bring some Rosetta Stone–type discovery.

  6. 08:40 To the skeptic: where is your museum?

    Scripture: Deuteronomy 27:4–8 Psalm 19:1–4

    • To the skeptic: all they keep uncovering is more evidence that the Bible can be trusted. A recently discovered underground church in Israel has a floor inscription saying, “Here we worship our God Jesus Christ.” First-century Christians always believed Jesus is God, and they believed it because of the resurrection; that is why they were willing to die and worship underground.
    • One of Josh’s favorite discoveries is the Mount Ebal curse tablet (Eli called it the “cinnamon toast tablet”), found in December 2019 in a pile of dirt discarded from a 1980s excavation. Many believe it is the earliest written evidence of the name Yahweh, and it aligns with Deuteronomy 27:4–8, where Moses commands the Israelites to declare curses from Mount Ebal. They found it in a trash pile; God said, “You missed something.”
    • We have so much evidence we have to build museums. So, skeptic, one question: where is your museum disproving the resurrection? Ironically, the believer doesn’t even need the evidence, but it is amazing as it floods in. God reveals himself not only through oceans and mountains but through piles of dirt, history, science, and archaeology. If we treated creation like a crime scene, God wouldn’t get out of this one: he did what he said he would do.
    • Josh reads, “The heavens declare the glory of God… they have no speech, they use no words… yet their voice goes out into all the earth.”

  7. 11:54 The resurrection proves who Jesus is

    Scripture: John

    • The resurrection proves Jesus Christ is who he claimed to be, the very Son of God. Some 99 percent of scholars, believers and non-believers, agree Jesus died on the cross under Pontius Pilate, and over 500 eyewitnesses claimed to see him alive.
    • He made incredible claims in the seven “I am” statements in John: the bread of life, the light of the world, the gate for the sheep, the good shepherd, the resurrection and the life, the way and the truth and the life, and the true vine. He accepted worship, forgave sins, and taught with such authority that it blew everyone away. Jesus claimed to be God.
    • An empty tomb, multiple eyewitness accounts, ancient writings confirming the resurrection and none earlier refuting it, the transformed lives of the disciples, the birth of the church, the rapid spread of Christianity, and changed lives today: compelling isn’t a strong enough word. He has risen.

  8. 13:31 Faith, not evidence, pleases God

    Scripture: Hebrews 11:6 John 20:28–29

    • For believers especially: your understanding of the evidence is not what pleases God. Without faith it is impossible to please God.
    • Josh never met Paul but believes he met Jesus on the way to Damascus; never met Mary but believes she found an empty tomb; never met Peter but believes he preached the first gospel message; never touched Jesus, but he is Josh’s best friend. He was never invited up a mountain by God, but he teaches his children the law as if God handed him the tablets. Mark wasn’t an eyewitness; he heard it from Peter, and Josh believes him.
    • When Thomas came to faith he said, “My Lord and my God,” and Jesus told him, “Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed.” How many actually saw the risen Jesus? Paul says 500. Far more Old Testament believers trusted in the coming Messiah without seeing; some generations saw the waters parted, others saw people dying in the wilderness. The pool of those who have not seen him is so much larger, yet the vast majority who have received salvation have never seen him. That is the faith that pleases God.

  9. 16:02 Mark 16:1–8: the testimony of the resurrection

    Scripture: Mark 16:1–8

    • All through the series Mark has shown us God’s servant, and the series closes with the testimony of his resurrection. Josh reads Mark 16:1–8: the women bring spices, ask who will roll the stone away, find it rolled away, meet a young man in a white robe who says, “He has risen! He is not here,” and are told to go tell his disciples and Peter. Trembling and bewildered, they flee and say nothing because they are afraid.

  10. 17:07 Driven by love, ignoring the obstacles

    • When Josh has a big day ahead, he can’t sleep the night before. The Sabbath ended Saturday evening, it was too dark to travel, and from sundown Friday to sunrise Sunday there were tough hours. Those who followed Jesus probably thought, “Well, it was a good run,” the way Chiefs fans felt after the Super Bowl. They felt defeated.
    • Bringing spices to the tomb was like bringing flowers to a grave. The women were driven by love, reverence, and admiration, and didn’t stop to consider the armed Roman guards or the huge rock. Sometimes faith just doesn’t make sense; sometimes you just go with it.

  11. 18:52 Josh’s sister Jess

    • Josh has been missing his sister. His wife has been using Jess’s quilt because he keeps the thermostat so low. Jess died at 39 and would have been 41 in January.
    • The last time he saw her was a Crossroads Cares need he will never forget: she was going through a divorce, and his family and his mom helped her settle into a new apartment. A member of the church bought security cameras through Crossroads Cares, and Josh installed them so she would feel safe.
    • They both professed faith and were baptized around age 10. High school was hysteria; Josh came back to the Lord, kicking and screaming, after about a decade, and lost connection with Jess. One night she handed him a beer and talked about how graduating from Harvard Law was the crowning achievement of her life. He had the chance to tell her that the crowning achievement of his life was conquering the shame of his sin through the Holy Spirit, but he was a coward and said nothing. He can preach to a stranger but was a coward with his sister, and he beat himself up for it.
    • Weeks later his older sister Andrea told him Jess had said: “When Josh and Kristen heard about my struggles they dropped everything and came running to help me, even though we hadn’t spoken in years. I’ve never experienced that kind of grace and love.” Josh wishes he had more time to show her that Jesus is the one to celebrate and rekindle with.

    1. 21:55 The Pharisee in me

      • After she passed, his mom was convinced Jess was with Jesus. Josh wasn’t sure. The Pharisee in him was focused on everything she did wrong, all the obstacles: the old Christian line, “I don’t see fruit.”
      • It took him a while to remember he hugged her when she was still wet from baptism. She put her faith in Jesus just like him and wandered just like him. “I’m not saved because I came back home. I’m saved because he gave his life as a ransom for me.”

  12. 22:47 The resurrection removes all obstacles

    Scripture: Mark 16:3

    • It is so easy to see all the obstacles between us and Jesus, and between Jesus and everyone else. Even the best of us complicate salvation, but his resurrection uncomplicates it and removes all obstacles.
    • “Who will roll the stone away?” Jesus didn’t need the angel to move the stone so he could get out. The stone was moved so the women, the disciples, the Sanhedrin, the Roman authorities, and the world could see in. The testimony of one woman set the world on fire: in just over 200 years the movement took over the Roman Empire, and today it is the largest religious faith on earth.

  13. 23:41 “It was so long ago”

    • People now use the length of time since then to break down historical credibility. “Josh, it was so long ago.” Yes, but they wrote it down. “The Gospels were written hundreds of years later.” No: Mark is the oldest Gospel, and some scholars date it around AD 55, within 20 years of the eyewitnesses. Peter informs what Mark shares, Peter met with Paul in Jerusalem, and we can probably get within eight months of when the first creeds of the resurrection were circulating.
    • That matters because the eyewitnesses were still alive. We can’t ask Mary Magdalene, but James could: “Tell me again how that went down. Let’s walk to the tomb. Still empty? Has Rome handed over a body? They’re killing disciples in the streets and no one has confessed to stealing the body?” That is compelling.
    • Josh shares a video from Apologetics Canada by Wes Huff, part of the “Can I Trust the Bible?” series.

  14. 28:53 The women named as witnesses

    Scripture: Mark 16:1

    • Back to verse 1. Mark names these women three times in the last two chapters. He isn’t name-dropping; he is challenging readers to take his account as credible history. Richard Bauckham, a British scholar of ancient historiography, argues ancient historians gave more weight to oral accounts from living eyewitnesses than to written documents alone, because you could go ask them. Today we treat writing as ironclad, unless it is the Bible.
    • So the women’s names scream history, not myth. Mark is saying, “Go ask Mary; she’s right down the street.”
    • Celsus, a Greek pagan philosopher about 80 years after Jesus, wrote books to debunk Christianity, and his strongest argument was that it stood on the testimony of women, one “hysterical, half-frantic” woman who saw Jesus alive. Josh feels bad for his wife. The argument gained traction because women were so marginalized. But that means if Christians were making this up, they would never have made women the only eyewitnesses. The transparency is compelling: the women actually saw Jesus. The burden of proof is on the skeptic; show a lot of evidence that what the Bible says isn’t true.

  15. 32:08 Chronological snobbery

    Scripture: Mark 16:5–6

    • C. S. Lewis called it chronological snobbery: looking down on primitive people who came before as if they believed anything and we are too enlightened to fall for it. But are you reading the same text? They had as hard a time believing: alarmed, trembling, bewildered, afraid. Jesus said he would rise on the third day, yet no one else came to the tomb.
    • The young man in white gives the first resurrection message ever spoken, and the women are overwhelmed. It is a lot to take in.

  16. 33:40 Do you remember the first time you heard it?

    • Do you remember the first time you heard the resurrection message? It can take a moment to sink in; people don’t die and show up days later for the family barbecue.
    • In high school Josh wrestled with the swoon theory, the idea that Jesus didn’t actually die. He is grateful for loving, patient men and women who let him wrestle and welcomed him as a skeptic in the church, and he wants to continue that legacy: if you are a skeptic, you are welcome; come with your questions.

    1. 34:41 Roman crucifixion

      • As a junior, his youth leader explained Roman crucifixion, and that helped him overcome his unbelief. It was designed for maximum pain, humiliation, and suffering: scourging with a whip of bone and metal that could expose muscle, bone, even the spine; a procession carrying the crossbeam under lashes, spit, and stones; nails through the wrists and feet; hours or days fighting suffocation; legs broken to hasten death; corpses left to rot in public.
      • It was for the lowest of the low; Roman citizens were banned from it, and some said it shouldn’t even be on a Roman’s lips.
      • There is no chance Jesus survived, no way the Romans in that politically charged moment handed him over alive, and no way he appeared three days later completely fine, telling Thomas to touch his wounds. If you survived a crucifixion, the first thing you would say is, “Do you have a Band-Aid? Take me to the hospital.”

  17. 38:06 “Go, tell his disciples and Peter”

    Scripture: Mark 16:7 Mark 14:31

    • There is no better way to end Mark. Jesus could say a lot to Peter. Anybody grateful that Jesus doesn’t treat us like we deserve? He could have said, “Wait till I see these faithless, backstabbing cowards in Galilee; I’ve got a sermon that’s going to hit hard.” They all fled, and Peter was the worst: “I’ll die with you if I have to; I will never deny you.” They all denied him, never claimed his body, and hid.
    • Then the part everyone hates: Jesus forgives Peter before he repents. Make sure Peter knows. Tell that loudmouth to lace up his sandals and get to Galilee, because God isn’t done with him, and the biggest screw-up gets to give the biggest sermon ever.

    1. 39:45 Jesus is looking for humility

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 12:9 John 3:30

      • We don’t like that, because the world doesn’t work that way. We want the most polished, professional, charismatic speaker, maybe someone from the tribe of Benjamin. There are other qualifications for church leadership, but none matter without humility.
      • When you fall on your face and receive an education in grace, now God can use you and you can be made strong. Religion wants you to come in strong; God says his strength is made perfect in weakness.
      • Peter pounded his chest, “Jesus, I will die with you,” with too high an opinion of himself, wanting to be seen next to Jesus in victory like James and John. John the Baptist had the accurate view: “He must become greater; I must become less.” Not a recommendation: I must. John rejoiced when the crowds left him for Jesus; it was never about him.
      • Peter eventually learned it: sentenced to crucifixion, he told the Romans he preferred not to be crucified like Jesus, so they crucified him upside down.

  18. 42:02 Admitting we’re wrong

    • Josh has never met anyone who enjoys admitting they are wrong, and people do crazy things to avoid it. We even pay shrinks to tell us how bad our childhood was to excuse our actions as adults, and point the finger at everyone without asking whether we are contributing to the problem.
    • It is especially odd from a Christian, because that is how God set up salvation: you admit you need a Savior, you have sinned, you can’t fix it, and you need Jesus. Christians who hide their failures miss the point. The gospel doesn’t erase your flaws; it puts them next to his love, so you see what killed you and what raised you, humble but confident. God loves odd combinations.

  19. 43:15 Justification: peace with God

    Scripture: Romans 4:25 Romans 5:1–2 Romans 8:1

    • The main benefit of the resurrection is justification. Remember Romans 4: he was raised to life for our justification, “just as if I’d never sinned.” Jesus bore our sins and satisfied God’s righteous requirements, and God credited Christ’s righteousness to us.
    • Since we have been justified through faith, we have peace with God and stand in grace. We were once enemies of God but aren’t anymore. This grace isn’t used once and gone; it is grace upon grace, no limit to his mercy and forgiveness. There is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus, so when circumstances are hard we know they are temporary and that God can use even our darkest moments for good. How good is he?

  20. 44:45 The call of Mark’s Gospel

    • The call of Mark’s Gospel is simple, for the believer and for everyone: will you follow the world or follow Jesus? Many have decided to follow Jesus, some as recently as last week. If you have put your faith in Christ, Mark has one more question: are you serving, or expecting others to serve you?

  21. 45:16 Closing prayer

    Scripture: 1 John 1:9

    • Josh prays that as a 10-year-old he put his faith in God without knowing the whole picture; he wandered and refused to come home, and God still loved him, before he knew a shred of evidence. He thanks God for the faith given to him and many in the room and for showing them who they are without him: sinners in need of a Savior.
    • The servant of all came and died so we could come with our sinfulness and confess, and he is faithful and just to forgive and cleanse us. He prays this would be a daily walk of humbling ourselves, being loved and forgiven, and showing that love and forgiveness to others: “Cleanse this church; cleanse every home.”
