Overview

  1. 00:17 Only Luke is with me

    Scripture: 2 Timothy 4:6-8

    • Josh greets the church and introduces Luke not at the beginning of Luke’s story but near the end of Paul’s life: chained in Rome, cold, expecting execution, in the tradition of the underground Mamertine prison.
    • Praise does not always open prison doors; sometimes it fills the cell. Paul writes his final letter to Timothy knowing the time of his departure is near.

    1. 02:00 Troubled times reveal friendship

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 4:9-11

      • Josh names the country-song test: you find out who your friends are when crowds thin and association becomes risky. He jokes that he will not be naming people today.
      • Paul asks Timothy to come quickly: Demas, in love with this world, has deserted him for Thessalonica; Crescens has gone to Galatia and Titus to Dalmatia. Only Luke is with me.
      • Luke is mentioned three times in the New Testament, but no other sentence tells more about him. When association with Paul could put a target on his back, Luke stayed—no platform, no crowd, possibly the last person to see Paul alive.

    2. 03:33 Twelve weeks with steadfast Luke

      Scripture: Colossians 4:14 Philemon 24 Acts 16:10–17; 20:5–21:18; 27:1–28:16

      • The church will hear from steadfast Luke for twelve weeks. Josh prays that by the end of the series some of that Luke character grows up in this place.
      • In Colossians Paul calls him our dear friend Luke the doctor. Educated, intelligent, observant, Luke wrote the Gospel of Luke and Acts—more New Testament words than any other author, even though Paul wrote more books.
      • Before he was a great writer he was a faithful man, a fellow worker. In Acts the narrator shifts from they to we: Luke was on the ship, on the road, in the city, waiting out the storm, not an ivory-tower embellisher of another man’s journeys.

    3. 05:29 Luke opens the book

      Scripture: Luke 1:1-4

      • Luke put safety, reputation, and life at risk to share the most important message hearers will ever hear.
      • Josh reads the preface: many have undertaken to draw up an account of the things fulfilled among us, handed down by eyewitnesses and servants of the word; Luke carefully investigated everything from the beginning and wrote an orderly account for most excellent Theophilus, so that he may know the certainty of the things he has been taught.

  2. 06:00 A new series: King For All

    • Today they begin a new series through Luke’s gospel called King For All.
    • Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John all tell God’s salvation through Jesus, each in their own style: Matthew reveals Israel’s Messiah, Mark displays Jesus in action, John reveals heaven’s perspective on the eternal Son, and Luke gives a careful orderly account so hearers may be certain.

    1. 06:42 No one is beyond Jesus’ reach

      Scripture: Luke 19:10

      • Luke was probably a Gentile writing to a man who appears to have been a Gentile. Throughout the gospel Jesus encounters respected and rejected, wealthy and poor, men and women, insiders and outcasts, the confident and the desperate.
      • That is not universalism. Not everyone automatically belongs to Jesus; no one is too far gone. Heaven’s eternal king has come to save God’s people—those who will repent and believe.
      • After dining with Zacchaeus, Jesus confirms his mission, a text Josh will keep in front of the series: Luke 19, the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost.

    2. 07:42 God acted. Witnesses saw it. Luke investigated.

      Scripture: Luke 1:1-4

      • The natural flow of Luke’s intro: God acted, witnesses saw it, Luke is not a witness, he investigated through the witnesses so Theophilus could know it—and if Theophilus can know it, so can we.
      • The first claim of the day: Christianity is breaking news that God has acted. Many have undertaken to draw up an account of the things that have been fulfilled among us.
      • That phrase will carry the first two weeks: this week the historical block, next week the prophetic block.

  3. 08:49 Not once upon a time

    Scripture: Luke 1:1-4

    • Luke does not begin with once upon a time or a galaxy far, far away. He also does not pull a Joseph Smith: a private spiritual experience no one else can examine, with instructions not to ask questions.
    • He does not aim to give Theophilus fourteen ways to live his best life now. He begins with verifiable historical events: people saw it, talked about it, and handed eyewitness testimony to educated writers.
    • Jerusalem in 30 AD is ground zero for an act of God, and Luke investigates that act so we can have certainty.

    1. 10:02 Certainty is why Christianity is different

      • Christianity is not a search for meaning. It is an announcement that God has acted in human history: real places, rulers, towns, roads, meals, storms, sickness, healing, suffering, blood, forgiveness, death, and bodily resurrection.

    2. 10:31 A historian of the first rank

      Scripture: Luke 1:1-4

      • Luke names governors, emperors, methods of travel, powers, events, and witnesses by name. He writes like a man who knows people will try to prove him wrong.
      • Oxford historian Sherwin-White, examining Acts, said the confirmation of historicity is overwhelming. Archaeologist William Ramsay began skeptical of Luke and of the Christian faith; after years in the geography and history of Asia Minor he called Luke a historian of the first rank and embraced the faith, saying this author should be placed with the very greatest of historians.
      • Evidence never removes the need for faith, but it removes the accusation that Christianity rests on carelessly invented stories. When Luke says he carefully investigated everything from the beginning, there is reason to take him seriously.

    3. 13:08 Fulfilled among us

      Scripture: Philippians 2:8 1 Corinthians 15:20 1 Corinthians 15:17 Luke 23:26

      • Christianity does not begin with something you feel. It begins with something God has done. Fulfilled means accomplished, brought to its intended end.
      • The eternal Son took on flesh, humbled himself, became obedient to death on a cross, lived under real rulers, stood next to a real Pilate, and suffered in a real body. A real man helped him carry his real cross, and real men drove real nails through his hands. On the third day God raised him, and his resurrection is the firstfruits of all who belong to him.
      • If the cross is merely a symbol of sacrificial love, guilt remains unpaid. If the resurrection is only a metaphor, death remains undefeated. Paul says it plainly: if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins.

    4. 14:58 The gospel is not a ladder

      Scripture: 1 Peter 2:24

      • The gospel is not first what happens inside you. It is about what happened for you on a cross outside ancient Jerusalem.
      • Hearts turn religion into a ladder: if I obey, improve, give, and become sincere enough, perhaps I can climb to God. The gospel announces that God came down because sinners could never climb up. Christ obeyed in our place, bore guilt, and walked out of the grave.
      • The gospel is not advice about what sinners must accomplish for God. It is an announcement of what God has accomplished for sinners. That announcement demands a response—repent, believe, follow—but the response is not the saving event. Faith does not finish his work; faith receives his finished work.

  4. 17:13 Eyewitnesses and servants of the word

    Scripture: Luke 1:1-4

    • These things happened among us, publicly, in named places, under identifiable rulers, before people who could be questioned.
    • Luke writes that the accounts were handed down by those who from the first were eyewitnesses and servants of the word. He is not one of the Twelve and never pretends to be. He does not claim he stood beside Galilee when Jesus calmed the storm or entered the room when Jairus’s daughter was raised.
    • That honesty builds reliability. He received testimony, investigated it, and arranged it so Theophilus and later readers would know the faith is not founded on rumor or private religious experience but on the saving acts of God in Jesus Christ.

    1. 18:13 Verse 3: carefully investigated

      Scripture: Luke 1:3

      • If the church embraces Luke as a faithful servant, it must also embrace that Christianity welcomes careful investigation.
      • Josh rereads verse 3. Luke did not take one person’s story as gold. He found another witness and another, cross-examined, and labeled it credible. From the beginning likely means the birth of Christ; Josh even wonders whether Luke talked to the wise men.
      • Luke wants hearers to know he put in the work so they do not have to: compared accounts, asked the whole street, not one neighbor.

    2. 20:03 Historian’s mind, physician’s heart

      • Warren Wiersbe described Luke as writing with the mind of a careful historian and the heart of a loving physician. Doctors ask where it hurts, how long, medications, family history, because a bad diagnosis guarantees bad treatment.
      • Hands go up: people prefer a doctor who investigates carefully. Luke brings that mind to Jesus’ story because spiritual lives are worth more than physical lives.

    3. 21:13 God-breathed, not a trance

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:16 2 Peter 1:21

      • Josh welcomes newcomers, points them to the church’s beliefs and mission to change the way people see the church, and says this is not the place for those content with the last forty years of church-as-usual.
      • They believe the Bible is the word of God: the Spirit carried human authors along so that what they wrote is what God intended. God-breathed does not mean Luke entered a trance while God moved the pen. God used Luke’s mind, education, temperament, medical attention to detail, relationship with Paul, access to witnesses, Roman connections, and writing skill.
      • In one move hearers get Luke’s carefully investigated account and God’s inspired word. You can investigate carefully and depend on the Holy Spirit at the same time.

    4. 23:27 Prayer is not an excuse for laziness

      • Some Christians make carelessness sound spiritual. Josh has been told that writing every word is small faith. He tries to write and pray every word because Jesus and the hearer’s soul are worth that effort, and he will not preach what he will not first submit to himself.
      • The Spirit who inspired Luke also led Luke to investigate. Prayer is not an excuse for laziness. Trusting God is not an excuse for ignoring evidence. Depending on the Spirit does not mean refusing the mind God gave. Luke loved God, so he did the work—a labor of love.

  5. 25:08 Different gifts, same God

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:4-11

    • Luke approached the work differently than Paul, yet they worked in unity. Do you leave room for God to display his creativity in others?
    • 1 Corinthians 12 says there are different kinds of gifts, service, and working, but the same God. The Spirit distributes as he determines. Differences among genuine Christians are not accidents to correct; they are expressions of God’s wisdom.
    • Some run into the burning building; some are measured. Some sense a hurting room; some sit alone and explain the Word. Some lead from the front, some from behind. Pride tempts us to treat our style as the definition of faithfulness: the decisive call the careful afraid, the careful call the decisive reckless, public servants wonder why others will not step up, quiet servants wonder why anyone needs to be seen.
    • God is not reproducing you in the church; he is conforming every believer to Christ. Unity does not require uniformity. Luke did not need to become Peter. Stop envying other gifts and be faithful with what God has given. Never despise faithful work because it does not look like yours.

  6. 28:35 Luke welcomes skeptics

    • If you are not sure what you believe, you are welcome. Ask questions about the Bible. Investigate the resurrection. If Christians have hurt you, have an honest conversation before tossing the whole church out.
    • Christianity does not ask you to check your brain at the door. The faith is not a leap into the dark; it is thoughtful, intentional trust in the person and work of Jesus taught by reliable people. Faith goes beyond what can be seen, but it is never contrary to what is true.

    1. 29:33 You already trust reliable witnesses

      • You were not a reliable witness to your own birth. You accept documentation from people who were there. You believe 9/11, Pearl Harbor, and World War II on the basis of character, knowledge, expertise, and evidence—even as half of Americans were not alive on 9/11.
      • The Christian faith stands historically, but no one comes to Christ unless God opens the heart. Evidence alone will never make a heart of stone love Jesus. The Spirit must work—and the Spirit uses evidence and truth. They are not in competition. Bring questions honestly.

    2. 31:12 Tender skeptics and stubborn skeptics

      • Tender skeptics have questions because they want the truth. Stubborn skeptics will always have questions because they do not want Jesus to have authority.
      • Bad information multiplies confusion: gospels written centuries later, no eyewitnesses, church leaders choosing favorite stories. If you are going to be a skeptic, be an honest one. On the resurrection, do you want the truth or are you desperate to disprove it?
      • If Jesus is only one inspiring teacher, you can borrow what you like. If he rose, he is not asking to be one of many; he wants to be Lord. Many questions delay the only question that matters: will I bow to King Jesus?

    3. 34:15 Christians play the same game

      Scripture: Matthew 18:15-17

      • Christians say amen to an infallible Bible and then treat his commands as if they are not for them. Jesus taught a sexual ethic; they look for ways around it. He commands forgiveness; they search for why it should not apply. He gives conflict resolution in Matthew 18—homework—and they ignore it.
      • Christians can use interpretation the way an unbeliever uses skepticism: not to understand the truth but to avoid living by it. Nobody approaches Jesus from a neutral position. Saying miracles cannot happen is a philosophical claim. Saying we cannot know spiritual truth is itself a spiritual claim. Saying all religions are the same means you have not listened.
      • Welcome, skeptics: examine Christianity and examine your own assumptions with the same honesty. God’s truth can withstand investigation.

  7. 35:20 The gift of certainty

    Scripture: Luke 1:3-4

    • Luke’s investigating comes with a gift: certainty. He wrote an orderly account for most excellent Theophilus so that you may know the certainty of the things you have been taught.
    • Theophilus—friend of God or lover of God—is the only person named in the intro. Some wonder if the name is symbolic for every lover of God, but most excellent points to a real person of significance, possibly a Roman official.
    • How they met is unknown. What is known: Theophilus had already been taught. He is a believer, and Luke aims to build him up. There is no shame in a believer who needs a hand. God gives his word and one another.

    1. 37:49 Certainty is settled confidence in a trustworthy Christ

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:20-23

      • Certainty does not mean you will understand everything God is doing, never feel doubt, become a theologian, or be spared disappointing Christians and hard life.
      • Christian certainty is settled confidence in a trustworthy Christ: I do not know everything, but I know the one I trust is right here. A child does not understand an operating room but knows the parent holding both hands.
      • You may not understand the path, but you can know the one who walks it. Josh cannot tell you what tomorrow holds; he can tell you what the resurrection guarantees: Christ reigns, death will not have the final word, and everyone who belongs to him will rise. Jesus already happened.

    2. 39:33 The aim of the series is that you know him

      Scripture: Matthew 7:23 Luke 19:10

      • Josh’s singular aim for twelve weeks is that people know, love, walk with, hear, and lean on Jesus—not merely on people.
      • You can know the Christian story and never know Christ, defend the Bible and never submit to it, grow up in church and still hear, I never knew you.
      • Over the next twelve weeks, a variety of people will encounter Jesus. Ask one question: what will I do with Jesus? The series title is King For All: not that every religion leads to God or that everyone is automatically saved, but that every kind of person needs a Savior, and Jesus is the only Savior.

    3. 41:33 One category before a holy God

      Scripture: Romans 3:22-24

      • Outcasts, healthy, sick, young, old, first-timers and thousandth-timers all need Jesus. The enemy divides people into worthy and unworthy. Before God’s holiness there is one category: all have sinned, and all must be saved by grace.
      • Josh quotes: this righteousness is given through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe. There is no difference between Jew and Gentile. All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and all are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that came by Christ Jesus.

  8. 42:44 The physician points to the great physician

    Scripture: Luke 19:10 John 10:18 1 Corinthians 15:6 John 21:15-17 Romans 6:23 Romans 10:13 Philippians 2:10-11 Matthew 26:53

    • Dr. Luke knows the deepest problem is sin. People want to sit on the throne; pride and self-crowning corrupt desires, thoughts, and relationships and lead to death.
    • Jesus came to seek and save the lost. He lived the righteous life we could not, went to the cross willingly, and laid down his life of his own accord. He could have called for divine backup, but he did not. He stood in the place of the guilty, and on the third day God raised that pierced body. More than five hundred saw him at one time. The risen Christ restored Peter and commissioned him to feed his sheep.
    • Forgiveness and eternal life are offered to everyone who turns from sin and trusts Jesus. The church is certain God sent his Son, Jesus accomplished salvation, the gift of God is eternal life, death will not have the final word, everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved, and every knee will bow.

    1. 45:10 Luke stayed because Christ had first taken hold of Luke

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 4:9-11 John 19:30

      • Josh returns to the underground prison. From death’s edge Paul writes, Only Luke is with me. Luke stayed.
      • Luke’s faithfulness points to someone infinitely more faithful. Jesus entered death for everyone who abandoned him. He knew Judas would betray, Peter would deny, disciples would scatter, soldiers would crucify; he stayed until the debt was paid and declared, It is finished.
      • Jesus was forsaken so that everyone united to him by faith would never be abandoned. Luke held on because Christ had first taken hold of Luke. Bring questions, uncertainty, and sin. Do not settle for information about Jesus when you can have friendship with him. Repent. Believe. Entrust your life to the King.

    2. 46:52 So that you may know

      Scripture: Luke 1:3-4

      • Luke investigated carefully and wrote so that you may know—not guess, not pretend, not borrow somebody else’s faith—so that you could know the truth and through the truth come to know the King.

  9. 47:10 We do not sit in this earthly prison alone

    • Josh prays: thank you for loving me; forgive the ways we fall short in loving others; help us love and live and look like Jesus.
    • From a place that can feel like a prison and get lonely, he asks God to remind the church, as they hold communion, that they do not sit in this earthly prison alone. You are with us always. We cannot wait to see you. Amen.
