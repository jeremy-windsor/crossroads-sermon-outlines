Overview

  1. 00:06 Backstage: planning, the Spirit, and an election-year series

    Scripture: Matthew 16:18

    • Josh invites the church backstage: he plans a year of teaching at a time and prays over it for weeks, yet is willing to abandon the plan when something needs to be said among the family.
    • Some claim planning cuts out the Holy Spirit, a “bottle-fed diaper Christian view of the Holy Spirit.” If he is surrendered to the Spirit and walking by him, his planning will be Spirit-led too; the Spirit moves on Tuesday every bit as much as on Sunday.
    • Last November he placed this series here knowing it was an election year, because the cave-dwelling Christians would emerge to say the country will collapse unless you vote for their candidate. It is a message of fear from people who aren’t supposed to live in fear.
    • Our Lord didn’t give us a political system to change the world; he gave us the church, and promised the gates of hell will not overcome it. “Your message of fear is rebuked in this place.” If you want a political commentator, find another church; if you want a pastor who preaches Christ crucified, stay as long as you like.

    1. 03:03 When the church must speak

      • There are times the issues can’t be ignored, when political parties become institutions of evil and Christians cannot be silent. Josh isn’t overly political; people have left because he isn’t political enough. His title as a Christ follower matters far more than his title as a Republican. Why tell you about a man when he could tell you about the God-man?
      • He struggles when the church expects political commentary in every sermon: “Go watch Fox News.” Asked why he hasn’t talked about abortion, he says the church talks about it often, maybe if church were more than a once-a-month commitment you’d know. Asked why they don’t encourage voting: “Were you in church last week?”
      • He lets the text lead their time together, and that is a blessing, not a burden. Picking 1 and 2 Thessalonians was an attempt to battle fear during an election. But since many want to know what he thinks, he takes time to honor that.

    2. 05:45 Josh’s opinion on this election

      • Taking into account its values and beliefs, Josh says the Democratic Party has become incompatible with Christianity, a platform built on everything God hates: feminism and the LGBT movement that deny men and women were made distinct yet complementary; marriage hijacked; perversion taught and celebrated instead of abstinence; abortion tents outside the DNC.
      • The true Christian reminds everyone that women do have a choice: in over 90% of abortions she could have exercised self-control. The child isn’t safe inside the womb, or outside it, with the push for the mutilation and sexualization of children. Even long-term Democrats are jumping ship.
      • Gone is the day Christians can stand on either side of the aisle. In a two-party system, one party hates life, the family, children, and the church. Voting as a Christian used to be more complicated; it isn’t anymore, and you have an obligation to vote righteousness. There are no perfect candidates, but one is by far the better option if you care about life, children, and family.

    3. 08:42 You need to vote, but our hope isn’t in a party

      • Perhaps 32 million Christians are choosing not to decide. Some fly “Jesus 2024” flags: is Jesus running? Opting out, American to American, dishonors those who gave their lives for life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. Christian freedom isn’t only the ability to say no but also to say yes without perfect clarity. Of course the candidates come up short; you watch Jesus every day. Don’t worry, he’s coming.
      • Our hope isn’t in a party or in whoever lives at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue; it matters far less than who sits on the throne in heaven. The gospel’s success doesn’t depend on who is in the White House. Christianity has always spread fastest where persecution is highest; if religious freedom disappears, we may see revival unlike anything we’ve seen.

    4. 11:18 Pray for candidates; your opponents aren’t your enemies

      • Both candidates and their running mates deserve and need our prayers. And your political opponents are not your enemies. Be firm on the issues: God weeps when innocent life is eliminated in the womb, when a boy is told God made a mistake, when criminals kill civilians and children, and when children are abused and trafficked. And heaven rejoices when a lost sheep is found.
      • Don’t turn a neighbor who votes differently into an enemy; the damage could have eternal consequences. If you care about the issues more than the souls of those who disagree, do the kingdom a favor and sit this one out. If it is more obvious who you plan to vote for than who saved your soul, you are part of the problem. After that intro, Josh jokes, he has zero friends.

  2. 13:00 2 Thessalonians 1: a faith that is growing

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:3

    • Some Thessalonian believers were still confused after Paul’s first letter; even the great apostle had to repeat himself. With a young church Paul leads with patience and gentleness. Verse 3: we ought always to thank God for you, because your faith is growing and your love for one another is increasing. Clearly an answer to prayer: growth not in numbers but in faith.
    • Faith is like muscle; trials and persecutions are the dumbbells God uses to build it. This election can be a great time of fear or a great builder of faith. “Christian, so far what’s it been for you?”

    1. 14:07 Suffering grows love

      • Suffering can make us selfish: “Why is this happening to me?” But maturing believers find that their suffering helps them notice the suffering of others. That’s why Josh urges every Christian to take an international mission trip at least once, and wishes it were required of high school students before graduating.
      • An easy life can lead to a weak faith. You must either suffer or witness suffering to grow in love. Life wasn’t easy for the Thessalonians, but they trusted God and serious faith muscle was built.

    2. 15:25 A testimony of perseverance

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:4 Job 13:15

      • Verse 4: among God’s churches we boast about your perseverance. Josh wishes Paul would talk about Crossroads this way. It isn’t just personal faith being celebrated but one person’s faith impacting another’s; God can use your trials to build faith in others.
      • The church has many stories of perseverance: marriages that looked headed for divorce, health issues lasting months while people kept singing, financial hardship, addiction clung through withdrawals, and the loss of loved ones. Many have adopted Job’s battle cry: though he slay me, yet will I hope in him.

    3. 16:46 Suffering proves God cares

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:5 Matthew 6:33

      • Verse 5: this is evidence that God’s judgment is right, and you will be counted worthy of the kingdom of God. We think suffering proves God doesn’t care; the opposite is true, because he is preparing us for his kingdom, so we can face trials with joy.
      • Jesus said to seek first his kingdom and his righteousness. The Thessalonians didn’t just hear about the Sermon on the Mount; they took Jesus at his word, though they heard it from Paul. Paul doesn’t commend them for being well-read, for tidy theology or eschatology, impressive services, a social agenda, or political awareness. He commends stronger faith and more love, seeking first the kingdom. Keep it up, and don’t get off mission.

  3. 18:34 When the Lord Jesus is revealed

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:6–10

    • Josh reads verses 6 through 10: God is just; he will pay back trouble to those who trouble you and give relief to you who are troubled, when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven in blazing fire. Those who do not know God will be shut out from his presence, on the day he comes to be glorified in his holy people.
    • Believer, no matter how bad things get, our future is secure. Paul is describing Judgment Day. Jesus is exalted at the right hand of God as our mediator, and one day he will return in full glory.

  4. 19:37 God is just; God will repay

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:6

    • Starting in verse 6: God is just; God will repay. Some say this seems unfair, but Josh doesn’t follow the logic: of course God brings justice to those who violate his law. He isn’t a ticked-off teenager; he has waited patiently and desires everyone to be saved. God isn’t rejecting anyone; he is honoring your decision to reject him.
    • It isn’t possible for God to be unjust, and if justice matters to God, it should matter to you. That is why Josh addresses political issues today: justice is on the ballot.

    1. 20:40 Justice on the ballot

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 24:7 Numbers 32:17

      • Josh holds up the stack of election mail, including his wife’s ballot, which they haven’t filled out yet, and teases the plain black-and-white publicity pamphlet. “These, Christian, are not your voting guide. This is,” he says of the Bible.
      • Statistics say one in four in the room plan to vote yes on Prop 139. The message that sends: God is just but his people don’t care. Shouldn’t my vote save innocent children, protect the physical and mental health of women, declare all life valuable, and give a voice to the voiceless made in God’s image? We can put a man on the moon but can’t care for women and children at the same time; we must do both. No to Prop 139: not a political issue but a biblical and moral one.
      • Prop 313 ensures child traffickers are put away for life; Josh will vote yes. Deuteronomy 24:7 says the kidnapper must die: purge the evil from among you, which isn’t just an Old Testament thing. How many times would someone ignore their conscience to kidnap a child?
      • Prop 314 would let border agents enforce immigration law. There is a right and a wrong way to enter the country. Numbers 32: the Israelites armed for battle while their women and children lived in fortified cities for protection. If their women and children deserved to feel safe, so do ours.
      • Bad presidents don’t last forever, but bad policies always outlast bad politicians. That is why we need good, vocal Christians. God is just; his people must act justly.

  5. 25:45 Keep eternity in view: relief for the troubled

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:6–7 Jeremiah 12:1 2 Corinthians 5:7 Galatians 6:7

    • We can live well now as Christians, but we must keep eternity in view. Josh rereads that God will give relief to you who are troubled and finds great comfort in it. He is troubled: abortion is worse than it appears, with people profiting from killing babies, and we rarely see justice this side of glory.
    • Many can relate to the prophet Jeremiah: why does the way of the wicked prosper? But we walk by faith, not by sight. Nobody is getting away with anything; God will not be mocked. It will all come out and be dealt with in time.

    1. 27:24 God doesn’t always pay his people in September

      Scripture: Matthew 7:1

      • Two farmers, a believer and an atheist. At harvest the atheist had a record season and taunted his neighbor: “I thought it paid to believe in God.” The Christian said, “It does pay, but God doesn’t always pay his people in September.”
      • Believer, you are promised a crown, peace, rest, and God’s presence; they are promised destruction, punishment, and being shut out. Don’t envy them, be angry at them, or fight with them; grieve for them, pray for them, intercede for them.
      • Pharaoh tried to drown the Jewish boys, and his army drowned. Haman plotted genocide, and he and all his sons were wiped out. Daniel was thrown to the lions and was fine; then his accusers were thrown in. The Jewish leaders chose power over the Messiah, and years later their city was destroyed. When we judge sinners we look foolish; when God judges sinners, they look foolish.

    2. 29:26 God isn’t sending anyone to hell

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:8 Revelation 22:13

      • Those who want nothing to do with God will get exactly what they want when Jesus returns. God isn’t sending anyone to hell; you are walking there yourself. He offered salvation through Jesus, you wanted separation, and perfect justice upholds that choice.
      • Verse 8: he will punish those who do not know God and do not obey the gospel. It is often forgotten that before the Lord judges anyone, he first died for them. He isn’t itching to get even; he sent his Son and is patiently pleading for you to return.
      • Either your sin was judged on Calvary or it will be judged when Christ returns. Josh pleads: don’t wait. Call on the name of Jesus today, ask him to forgive your sins, and trade your little gods for almighty God, the Alpha and Omega, who offers a treasure that will never rust or fade.

  6. 31:21 The nature of the punishment: shut out from his presence

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:9

    • Verse 9: everlasting destruction and being shut out from the presence of the Lord. Asked the worst thing that could happen, some would say losing a spouse, a child, a home, or a job. Josh would be devastated to lose his wife or a child, but with the Lord he would be okay; a home he wouldn’t miss (plumbing is the worst), and a job he’d replace.
    • But if he lost Jesus, nothing could soothe that pain or fill that void. Without his presence there is no love, joy, or beauty. This is God’s punishment for those who reject his Son: he simply removes his presence. It is perfectly just.
    • C. S. Lewis says in The Great Divorce there are only two kinds of people in the end: those who say to God “Thy will be done,” and those to whom God says, “Thy will be done.” All in hell choose it; it is for people who prefer some other presence to his. If God is your greatest treasure, you will receive him; if you live as your own god, judge, and authority, you’ll get that and not him.

  7. 34:47 The cross explains Judgment Day

    Scripture: Luke 23:42 Luke 23:43 Matthew 27:46

    • The thief on the cross had a simple request: “Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom.” The other criminals could have asked too, but preferred to mock with the crowd. Jesus’ answer to the one seeking him: “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise.”
    • Only once did God shut out someone who was seeking him and saying “Thy will be done.” On the cross God didn’t throw stones at Jesus or toss him in the fire; he turned his back. About three in the afternoon Jesus cried out, “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?”
    • Some struggle with how one man could pay for so many sins. Jesus was perfectly obedient and humble, and God turned his back on him, which had never happened and never will again. God only turns his back on those who turn their back on him; one person in history sought the Lord and did not find him, by design, so that whoever you are, you can seek him and find him. Jesus was forsaken so that you could be forgiven.

  8. 37:18 Glorified in his holy people

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:10 Matthew 5:16 Romans 8:19

    • Verse 10: he comes to be glorified in his holy people and marveled at among all who have believed. It doesn’t say glorified before his people, as if we were an audience watching: “Wow, Mike, look at that.” He is glorified in us.
    • Think of the light-bulb aisle at Home Depot: you don’t know what you’ve got until you put some voltage in it. Now we let our light shine as a spiritual light of faith; on that day his glory will radiate through us as a true light the world has never seen. Romans 8 says creation waits eagerly for the children of God to be revealed; creation can’t wait for the lights to go on.
    • Josh sings, “When we all see Jesus, we’ll sing and shout the victory.” Living believers will marvel as they are united with resurrected saints, and the funeral home will become ground zero for the most epic celebration ever. One empty tomb is amazing; wait until you see billions. And, as the Thessalonians wondered, this includes you, if you believe.

    1. 39:47 What we will marvel at

      • To marvel is to experience amazement beyond what you thought possible. We will marvel at the person and work of Jesus Christ. It is one thing to believe there is a God, another to believe God took on flesh and died in your place; one thing to say God is love, another to be amazed at God’s love.
      • Do you know what it cost God to love you? Imagine watching your son crowned with thorns and executed like a criminal after being found innocent, able to save him, knowing most would reject him. We will look on Jesus amazed.

  9. 41:02 Keep marveling at Jesus now

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 1:11–12

    • Josh reads Paul’s closing prayer, verses 11 and 12: that God would make you worthy of his calling, so that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you and you in him. Marveling at Christ isn’t only for the final day; it can start now, here, at home, in your neighborhood.
    • When worried, it is easy to take our eyes off Jesus and manage anxiety on our own. The key isn’t managing anxiety but marveling at Jesus all the more. If you doubt, marvel at Jesus in Gethsemane: God doesn’t need you doubt-free, just obedient. If you lack patience or forgiveness, marvel at how patient and forgiving God has been with you.
    • The ability to glorify Christ comes only through an experience of God’s grace. You can try really hard to be like Jesus, or marvel at Jesus and let his power transform you. WWJD is good; it is even better to ask what Jesus has done.

  10. 43:07 Closing prayer

    • Josh comes humbled that God saw him fallen, broken, and captive to sin and death, and carried out a divine plan to save him. The gospel is everything, and it can change this church, city, and country, because no one loves like God does.
    • He asks God to make them vessels of his love so that people see God and come running. He looks forward to the Lord’s coming, not knowing when so that we anticipate it daily, and waits in peace, rest, and joy: whether Jesus comes while he is here or after he is gone, he will be with him.
