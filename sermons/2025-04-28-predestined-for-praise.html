Overview

  1. 00:17 Launching Ephesians, verse by verse

    • The church is launching a series on Paul’s letter to the church in Ephesus, verse by verse. Ephesians has six chapters, each with two sections, so it will be one section a week for 12 weeks: “buckle up.”
    • Paul isn’t writing in response to anything. The church hasn’t let in some heresy or started worshiping one of the many idols in Ephesus. Instead he writes to strengthen the church and encourage believers in their faith.
    • The key theme is simple: that every believer who sets eyes on this letter knows exactly what they have in Christ. We might be broke or dirt poor down here, with serious cash flow problems, but as believers we are rich in Christ, and when Christ is flowing, all your needs will be met.

  2. 01:30 Two halves: riches and responsibilities

    Scripture: Luke 12:48 Ephesians 1–3 Ephesians 4–6

    • Look at Ephesians like a football game. The first half focuses on our riches in Christ: what do I have now that I didn’t have before? The second half is about our responsibilities in Christ.
    • That shift in mindset brings Josh to Jesus’ parable in Luke 12 about faithful and wise servants, which he wants to be: everyone to whom much was given, of him much will be required. When God entrusts us with more blessings, knowledge, resources, leadership, and authority, he expects more from us in return.
    • Make a mental note, or write it down because it’s a long series: chapters 1 through 3 are riches; chapters 4 through 6 are responsibilities. You have something you didn’t have before, and now you are expected to do things you weren’t doing before. If you have Christ’s riches, you’ll have some Christ results.

  3. 03:11 Spenders and savers

    • Josh does a warm-up activity, because this lesson will be harder for some than others; we are all wired differently. After nearly tripping over a phone charger, he mentions the Bind marriage enrichment conference in May, led by his wife and her “less than impressive husband.” Invest in your marriage early and often; put it on autopilot and you’ll crash into a mountain.
    • Bind has a session on finances where everyone identifies as a spender or a saver, so he asks the room. The spenders get tithe envelopes from the ushers; spenders get a bad rap, but their moment is coming. The savers are so frugal their tires are paper thin and their favorite Bible story is God providing manna: free food.
    • His point: spenders have an edge in applying Ephesians. Believer, you have deep pockets because of Christ, and those blessings aren’t poured out so you can lock them up in a savings account. Make it rain.

  4. 05:58 Verse 1: Paul, an apostle

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:1 Acts 20 Acts 19

    • Paul is one of the most well-known names in Christian history. Originally Saul, from the tribe of Benjamin and likely named after Israel’s first king, he was, unlike that king, so obedient and faithful that he persecuted early Christians in Jerusalem in God’s name. Jesus blinded Saul, or gave him sight, depending on how you look at it.
    • Paul wrote 13 of the 27 New Testament books. A missionary at heart, he launched the church in Ephesus on his third missionary journey. For background, read about his ministry in Ephesus in Acts 20, which the series will reference throughout, and for the challenges he faced there, Acts 19 is a good place to start.
    • Nearly a decade later, as a prisoner in Rome, Paul writes to his close friends in Ephesus to share what the Lord had taught him about salvation, about Christ, and about his bride, the church.

    1. 07:26 The true heart of a pastor

      • Josh loves Paul’s testimony: he went from persecuting Christians to being persecuted as one. On trial for his life, he didn’t write about how lonely prison felt or how he thought his career would end with a bang. All he could think about was the spiritual welfare of the churches the Lord had given him to care for.
      • That is the true heart of a pastor and the kind of elders this church needs to pray for God to reveal. Paul knew his job was to lead, teach the word, and build up the church, and not even prison would get in the way.

  5. 08:16 “To the saints who are in Ephesus”

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:1–2

    • The letter is addressed to the saints who are in Ephesus and are faithful in Christ Jesus: grace and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. Josh brings out the ESV here because knowing the audience is as crucial as knowing the author. Some manuscripts don’t include “in Ephesus,” which suggests Paul intended the letter to circulate throughout Asia Minor. His love for the Ephesians led him to write it, but it is for all the saints.

    1. 08:55 Saints are not just old dead people

      • Depending on how you grew up, it may surprise you that Paul calls ordinary believers saints. We think of old dead people who achieved amazing things: St. Thomas Aquinas, St. Mary, St. Peter, or St. Nicholas, better known as Santa Claus.
      • The Roman Catholic Church formalized a process called canonization: investigating whether a life was above reproach, confirming at least two miracles through the person’s intercessory prayers, and sending it to the Pope for final approval. Josh isn’t against man-made processes that edify the body, but nowhere in the Bible is such a procedure authorized or even necessary; it seems like a process made by man to elevate man.
      • Nine times in Ephesians Paul refers to the saints, and he isn’t writing to Santa Claus. He is writing to any and all believers blessed enough to read it.

    2. 10:32 Living saints

      • Saints can be alive. We were dead in trespasses and sins, but we have been made alive in Christ; we walk in resurrection life today. The only miracle you need to be involved in to be a saint is the miracle of salvation. That is the literal definition of a saint: one who has trusted in Jesus Christ as Savior.
      • We go by many names: disciples, people of the Way, the elect, Christians, saints, children of God, the body of Christ, a kingdom of priests, the redeemed, Christ’s ambassadors, brethren, the flock. Whatever the name, it refers to people set apart. When we trust in Jesus we are removed from the world and placed in Christ: physically here, spiritually there.

    3. 11:31 The astronaut’s special equipment

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:19–20

      • Think of an astronaut, a particularly handsome one in this case. Space doesn’t support life, so the astronaut needs specialized equipment. Believers are no more of this world than an astronaut is of space, and our special equipment is the Holy Spirit.
      • 1 Corinthians 6:19: your bodies are temples of the Holy Spirit; you are not your own; you were bought at a price; therefore honor God with your bodies. If you have the Holy Spirit, you are a saint. Every Christian has been set apart and made holy through the perfect righteousness of Jesus Christ credited to us. Josh is honored to be the first to welcome them to sainthood.

  6. 12:32 The longest, most amazing sentence: verses 3–14

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:3–14

    • Considering the riches we have in Christ, a salvation God planned before time began, executed through his Son, and completes through the Spirit, Paul’s heart explodes and he utters the longest, most amazing sentence you’ll ever hear. In the original Greek, verses 3–14 are one sentence.
    • Josh reads it all the way through before coming back to unpack its themes: praise be to the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in the heavenly realms with every spiritual blessing in Christ, through being chosen, predestined for adoption, redeemed, let in on the mystery of his will, and marked with the seal of the promised Holy Spirit, to the praise of his glory.

  7. 14:50 He chose us: the doctrine of election

    Scripture: John 15:16 Romans 3:10–11 Luke 19:10

    • The first spiritual blessing God gave us in Christ: he chose us. This is the doctrine of election, and if you ever have more Christian friends than you know what to do with, just bring it up. Some Christians insist they have unraveled the mystery, but not all mysteries are meant to be unraveled; many are an opportunity to praise God in the unknown. “I don’t know. But man, do I trust him because I know he does.”
    • If Christians agree on anything, it is that salvation begins with God and not man. Jesus tells his disciples in John 15, “You did not choose me, but I chose you and appointed you so that you might go and bear fruit, fruit that will last,” and we are that fruit. Paul makes it clear in Romans 3: there is no one righteous, no one who understands, no one who seeks God. So God seeks out the sinner: Luke 19:10, the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost.

    1. 16:24 Chosen before creation, because he loved us

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:4 Deuteronomy 7:7–8

      • Note the timing. Verse 4 says he chose us in him before the creation of the world. That matters because salvation is by grace, not works: he chose us in Christ, not in us.
      • Why did God choose me? Did he see I would be a good Christian who honors my parents and plays guitar, since the church is always hurting for musicians? “Doug seems like saint material, so let’s grab him.” That is not what the conversation in the Godhead sounded like.
      • In Deuteronomy God explains why he chose Israel: not because they were more numerous, for they were the fewest of all peoples, but because the Lord loved them and kept the oath he swore to their ancestors. God’s election comes from his love and his faithfulness, his oath-keeping and promise-keeping. That is why Josh keeps barking, “Keep reading,” when people stop reading to debate.

    2. 17:52 “But I’m a really good person”

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:5 Deuteronomy 9:6

      • Verse 5: in love he predestined us for adoption to sonship through Jesus Christ. “But Josh, I’m a really good person. Didn’t God see that compared to all these wicked people, I wasn’t that bad?” There is so much wrong with that question it would take two pastoral sessions. God can see that and do as he pleases, but that is not why he chose you, and the fact that you aren’t wicked by your own definition is God’s work, not yours.
      • Later God tells Israel to understand it is not because of their righteousness that he is giving them the land, for they are a stiff-necked people. Considering God’s choosing should highlight his love, which elevates our praise. Be careful not to highlight your obedience, because that elevates your pride. You were a hot mess and he still chose you; you didn’t earn it. It was all God’s grace.

    3. 19:25 Chosen for a purpose

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:4

      • Don’t miss the last part of verse 4: you were chosen for a purpose, to be holy and without blame. As a teaser, the series will go deeper into this when it shifts halfway through. The riches come with great responsibility.

    4. 19:43 “How is it fair?”

      • Another question: how is it fair that God chose us and not others? Ask questions; Josh feels the church has somewhat failed by making people feel they can’t. Skeptics are welcome as long as the relationship is healthy and fair. But approach God with reverence, or you may end up on the wrong end of a lecture from God like Job: where were you when he hung the stars, when the angels sang as he laid the foundations, when he filled storehouses with avalanches just in case? Who are we to give him a lesson on morality?
      • If you want to talk about fair, fair would be God choosing none of us. The human race chose to reject God, and we rightly deserve judgment and death. “I wasn’t in the garden.” That is pride and inexperience talking; live longer and then let’s talk. If we put you in Eden, you would eat the forbidden fruit too. That God chose any of us is unmerited grace.
      • The election debate gets heated because people walk around deceived, thinking they don’t deserve God’s wrath and that God owes them salvation. We are so entitled. God isn’t the one with a debt to pay; we are. What we deserve is a global flood, but God kept his promise, and instead of flooding us with water again, he flooded us with grace. A flood of judgment is coming, and the only way through it is to be washed in the blood of Jesus Christ.

    5. 22:34 “Doesn’t that violate my free will?”

      Scripture: John 6 John 6:37 John 6:44

      • Josh hates this question, not because it is hard but because it makes God out to be the creepy boy trying to date your daughter, the stalker in the parking lot who can’t take a hint. It is sloppy, lazy language. What needs to be said more often is that the mystery of divine sovereignty and human responsibility will never be solved in this life, because there is nothing to solve. They aren’t in conflict; God’s choice and our choice are both true and both essential.
      • Homework: read all of John 6. In verse 37 Jesus says all whom the Father gives him will come to him, and whoever comes he will never drive away. They will come; God isn’t dragging them; they freely choose him. But salvation begins with God: in verse 44 Jesus says no one can come to him unless the Father who sent him draws them.
      • Does the sinner respond to God’s grace against his will? No. He responds because God’s grace makes him willing. The problem with the human race isn’t that we can’t choose God; it is that we don’t want to.

    6. 24:29 The beauty of election and the Trinity

      Scripture: John 19:30

      • Stop seeing the conflict in election and start noticing the beauty and grace of God. Think of the Trinity. As far as God the Father is concerned, you were saved when he chose you before the creation of the world. From the perspective of God the Son, you were saved when he died on the cross and shouted, “It is finished,” paid in full, before you were even born. As for the Spirit of God, you were saved the moment you called to Jesus with your own voice.
      • Your salvation began before time and will exist long after the conclusion of time. Praise be to God Almighty forever and ever.

  8. 25:37 Verse 5: adopted

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:5–6

    • The blessings keep pouring out on the saints: next, he has adopted us. A couple in the church is in the process of adopting, and Josh can’t wait to hear how they feel when they first see the child and bring them home. He would love to hear the story of anyone who has adopted over dinner.
    • Having a child is beautiful, though the delivery room is a nightmare. But there is something extraordinary about bringing someone into your family who wasn’t born into it, who has experienced another home and is now in yours forever. That is what God did for us through Jesus. He came to the orphanage and said, “You’re coming home with me. You can have my name, live in my house. I will clothe you, feed you, love you, listen to you, and share my wisdom and wealth with you. Everything I have is yours.”

    1. 27:31 Adult standing: the family trust

      Scripture: Galatians 4

      • What makes God’s family unique is that you aren’t actually adopted into it; you are born again into it. Our adoption to sonship has more to do with our standing in the family than our entry. Paul talks about this in Galatians 4, but Josh felt led to explain it this way.
      • He and his wife finally created a family trust; they just turned 40, so they joke they could die any moment. Madison is now of age to be the executor, with full benefits as an adult heir: if something happens to Josh and Kristen, she has it, with no one in between. A child can’t use an inheritance, but an adult can. In this adoption you are given adult standing immediately, so you don’t have to wait to receive your riches in Christ. If you are born again today, you receive your inheritance today.

    2. 28:51 A future adoption

      Scripture: Romans 8:22–23

      • That is the present adoption, but the same adoption also plays out in the future. We walk in resurrection life now, but it can’t be fully seen until Jesus returns. In Romans 8 Paul says the whole creation has been groaning, and we who have the firstfruits of the Spirit groan inwardly as we wait eagerly for our adoption, the redemption of our bodies.
      • The paperwork is done and we are active beneficiaries, but this private adoption won’t be publicly seen until we receive glorified bodies on the final day. Our lives will bring him glory now and on that glorious day.

  9. 29:47 Verse 7: redeemed

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:7

    • Verse 7: in him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of God’s grace lavished on us. Up to this point God plans and directs salvation before creation; now notice the Trinity, as Jesus our Redeemer accomplishes the Father’s will.
    • To redeem means to purchase and set free by paying a price. There were about 60 million slaves in the Roman Empire, bought and sold like items at a rummage sale, but a man could purchase a slave and set him free.

    1. 30:33 Genie, Aladdin, and the boycotting Christians

      • Josh thinks of the Genie in Aladdin, the blue Robin Williams, greatest genie of all time, complete with a bad impersonation. For the Genie to be free, Aladdin had to sacrifice his final wish.
      • Pausing for the anti-Disney crowd: Josh loves them, but boycotting Christians drive him nuts, because you can bring anything back to Christ. Maybe the problem isn’t so much the content creators our children watch as parents who don’t know how to use the content to point their children to Christ. When his son, watching a MrBeast video, asked about a man who “was a man and then he wasn’t,” they talked it through: this is how God intended it, this is biblical sexuality, this is why you guard your heart. All of a sudden you are discipling your children instead of putting them in a bubble. Parents, stop being complacent.

    2. 32:28 Slaves set free, with a new master

      • Jesus purchased slaves and set them free, and the price was his blood. We live in a sensitive culture and some may not like thinking of themselves as slaves, but that is exactly what you were before Christ redeemed you, and if you sit here unredeemed, you are a slave.
      • “I’m not a slave. I’m free.” Then don’t sin. How’s it going? You are probably already having sinful thoughts about Josh. If you are free, be perfect. You can’t, because you are a slave to sin, just as you can’t live forever because you are a slave to death.
      • Redemption is Jesus doing what the slave can’t do, setting the slave free and, better, giving him a new master who never sins and who resurrects. In Christ the chains are gone; we are no longer slaves to sin or death; we belong to God forever and live in holiness, freedom, purity, and eternal life.

    3. 34:04 Forgiven: the pardon that carries sin away

      Scripture: Psalm 103:12

      • The blessings keep pouring out: he not only redeemed us, he forgives us too. They are similar but different. Redemption deals with our bondage to sin and is a payment that restores freedom; forgiveness deals with the guilt of sin and is a pardon that restores the relationship.
      • To forgive means to carry away, and Jesus has carried our sins far away: as far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us. Not only will you not be held accountable for your sin, you don’t have to be a slave to it anymore.

    4. 35:00 “How do I know if I have the Holy Spirit?”

      • Someone recently asked Josh how they could know they have the Holy Spirit. After a moment he said: identify sin in your life, call on Jesus to save you from it right now, and ask him to help you kill it. If you find help you didn’t know was there, then you’ll know, and then you can tell the world.

  10. 35:42 Verses 9–10: the mystery of his will

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:8–10

    • With all wisdom and understanding he made known to us the mystery of his will, to bring unity to all things in heaven and on earth under Christ. All of creation is witnessing this divine mystery now, waiting on the day of the Lord.
    • Listen to Joe Rogan go on for 15 minutes about a mysterious force behind all things, connecting everything, and you wonder whether he is describing Avatar or a Star Wars movie. When it is a mystery, all we can do is speculate; that is why we have religion and philosophy. God is revealing himself, but those who haven’t seen the mystery are guessing their way through, and it is painful to watch.
    • We know the mystery of God. He has revealed who he is, who we are, what sin is, and what had to happen to fix what was broken. Sin is tearing everything apart, but in Christ everything will be gathered up and made new. And we not only know about God’s will, we get to participate in it.

  11. 37:47 Verses 11–12: chosen for an inheritance

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:11–12

    • Verse 11: in him we were also chosen, having been predestined according to the plan of him who works out everything in conformity with his will, so that we who first hoped in Christ might be for the praise of his glory.

    1. 38:03 A tip for rookie Bible readers

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:11 1 Peter 1:4

      • Josh is excited there are new believers and rookie Bible readers in the room, and they should have no shame about where they are; some longtime members unload everything they know on him and he thinks, “That’s a lot.” He used to be a rookie, and in many ways still is, but good men taught him tricks.
      • The trick: read multiple versions. Don’t become the guy who decides one version, like the Amplified, is the best version ever. Inserting the ESV in verse 11 gives “in him we have obtained an inheritance.” Peter says this inheritance can never perish, spoil, or fade; it is kept in heaven for you.

    2. 39:30 Christ’s inheritance is his bride

      Scripture: Romans 8:17

      • Do the math on the price God paid to purchase us out of slavery. Jesus is God’s demonstration of love to us, and we are God’s demonstration of love to Jesus: Christ’s future inheritance is his bride, the church. Do you see the beautiful marriage?
      • Romans 8: if we are children, then we are heirs of God and co-heirs with Christ, if indeed we share in his sufferings in order that we may also share in his glory. Another tip for young Christians: don’t fall for a false gospel promising health, wealth, and prosperity. The true gospel causes us to suffer for the name and promises we will share in his glory. Greater suffering, greater glory, and we do it with a smile because God plans it, Jesus accomplishes it, and the Spirit seals it up.

  12. 40:35 Verses 13–14: sealed with the Spirit, a deposit

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:13–14 Matthew 5:6 1 Corinthians 13:12

    • You also were included in Christ when you heard the word of truth, and when you believed you were marked in him with a seal, the promised Holy Spirit, a deposit guaranteeing our inheritance. God’s riches are extraordinary, but this is just the beginning, just a deposit. It drives a preacher crazy: he tells the greatest things within human comprehension and still feels he is coming up short, because this is just the pregame compared to the marriage supper of the Lamb.
    • Jesus says, “Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled,” and Josh gets the feeling we haven’t been fully filled yet. Paul tells the Corinthians that now we see only a reflection as in a mirror, but then face to face. Walking with the Spirit, we see only the down payment, the beginning of blessings.

    1. 42:06 The glorious seal: finished, owned, secure

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:14

      • The Ephesians heard the word and believed, and that faith brought salvation. The same is true for us: when we heard and believed, we were marked with a seal.
      • First, the seal speaks of a finished transaction. Notice the Godhead: God started salvation before creation, Jesus accomplished it at just the right time, and the Spirit sealed it when we believed. What God starts, he will finish.
      • Second, it shows ownership. As the song Eli will lead says, he has given us his name; God has put his seal on us because he purchased us to be his own.
      • Third, it speaks of security and protection. God’s salvation is not temporary or conditional; it is eternal and guaranteed. Hebrews 10:14: by one sacrifice he has made perfect forever those who are being made holy.

    2. 43:45 Blessed assurance

      Scripture: Matthew 7:23 John 6:37–39

      • Josh wondered why the old-timers always sang about blessed assurance; now he knows. When he notices how filthy he still is, with thoughts he shouldn’t have and things he does that he doesn’t want to do, he looks at Jesus, then in the mirror, and wonders how he could possibly be saved. The closer you walk with Jesus, the more you notice how rotten you are, and a fear creeps in: am I the one who will hear, “Away from me, Josh, I never knew you”?
      • That is when the seal comes in. His blessed assurance rescues Josh from the pit of despair every time he tries to throw himself in, and God whispers, “I started it. I’m going to finish it.” Jesus preaches to him: all whom the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes I will never drive away; this is the will of him who sent me, that I shall lose none of all those he has given me, but raise them up at the last day.
      • He will not lose you or Josh; we are going to be okay. He is a good shepherd, a great shepherd, and he will not lose a single one. God is the author, sustainer, and finisher of salvation. If it depended on you, you would certainly lose it, but because it depends on God, it is a guarantee. Trust him.

  13. 46:38 God is drawing you

    • Josh thinks God is seeking some people in the room. Some are starting to feel conviction over sin without knowing where it came from, seeing things in themselves they used to be comfortable with and now don’t like, seeing the beauty in Christ and waking up to the ugliness in themselves. He isn’t trying to shame you; he wants to save you. God is drawing you, so don’t ignore his call. Grab a shirt and get in the water, because you need a Savior and Jesus is the only one who can do it.

  14. 47:27 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks the Father, who is holy, good, and awesome, for saving him when he didn’t deserve it. He will spend his lifetime trying to understand why God loves him, but looking at the cross he knows: because God loves his Son, and Josh loves him too.
    • He confesses being cheap with God’s riches, pocketing them and locking up treasures in savings accounts, and asks forgiveness and help to know when to set them free and make it rain. He prays that people would see in the church something so different, joyful, and Spirit-filled that they would have to ask why, and that the church would serve God and point people to him.
