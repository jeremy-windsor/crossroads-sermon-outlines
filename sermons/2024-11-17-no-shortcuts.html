Overview

  1. 00:33 Everyone loves a good showdown

    • We are attracted to competition, as spectators or participants. We shout louder in a close game, leave early in a blowout to beat traffic, call ourselves fans of the hometown team and wear its brand, and spend money to watch a showdown, from $100 for Tyson versus Paul (“who watched that nonsense?”) to $6,000 for the Super Bowl. Josh teases the football fans who say they would never spend that much: “You just don’t have it.”
    • On Monday his son Nolan had Timmy and Levi over to shoot hoops, and Josh was the fourth man, in his old church basketball jersey, knee sleeve, and LeBron 10s. In the middle of a game their mom Jennifer said the dreaded words, “It’s time to go, boys,” and the supposed grown-up walked over and asked, “Can we please finish this game?”

  2. 03:30 Competition through history

    • Some couldn’t care less, but the history of civilization disagrees. Ancient Egyptians, Greeks, and Romans held organized sporting events; the Olympics began in Greece around 776 BC as a religious festival honoring Zeus, with running, wrestling, and Josh’s favorite, chariot racing, “like pod racing but with horses.”
    • The Middle Ages brought jousting and archery, the Renaissance brought rules, officiating, and protecting the integrity of the game, and the Industrial Revolution brought regional, national, and international competition. Through television, mass media, and social media we have gone from Romans wrestling in the mud to a multi-billion-dollar industry.

  3. 05:31 The weigh-in: eventually the talk stops

    • Picture a boxer at the weigh-in: two men closer than two men should ever be, locking eyes in a way that more closely resembles The Notebook than a boxing match. In one infamous weigh-in Mike Tyson ranted about wanting to eat Lennox Lewis’s children.
    • What makes competition compelling is that eventually the talk stops and it’s time to perform and back it up. A showdown answers who has power over who, who will outlast, and who is greater.
    • The problem is that every year there’s a new champion, and not two months go by before you must prepare to defend your title. Lose next year and the history books might remember you, but the headlines have forgotten you.

  4. 07:18 A 40-day showdown in the Judean desert

    • About 1,991 years ago there was a 40-day showdown in the Judean desert between the kingdoms of this world and the kingdom of God: the world’s prince versus God’s Prince. Newspapers stopped writing about it, some churches stopped talking about it, and Christians rarely live in its power, but Josh’s heart pumps faster because of it, the church is unshakable in light of it, and eternity is visible as a result of it.
    • In the desert Jesus answered who has power over who, who will outlast, and who is the greatest, clinging to a kingdom that lasts forever and is infinitely more valuable than anything we can claim here on earth.
    • We are given three examples of the temptations, which can be read in five minutes and which the church has extended to a three-week series, but for Jesus this was 40 days. Luke and Matthew record them in a different order; Josh’s take is that it doesn’t matter, because the list isn’t exhaustive but a summary of 40 days, and for all we know Jesus was tempted many times.

  5. 09:02 The devil doesn’t quit after one no

    • Isn’t this how the devil works? Someone feels the Lord calling him to stop watching porn, to control his temper, or to stop pursuing material things, and Josh says that sounds like something the Lord would want. Then along comes the first temptation, and fresh from a sermon or a podcast you say, “No, get behind me, Satan.”
    • You honestly think that’s it? That the devil says, “Guess he’s changed, guess she’s turned over a new leaf,” and goes to find somebody else? The devil is like a birthmark: you might wish it wasn’t there, but it’s still there.
    • What Josh has learned clinging to Christ through multiple addictions and endless temptations: you don’t find victory by trying to suppress Satan’s lies; you find victory when you elevate God’s promises.

  6. 10:38 No shortcuts

    • Satan loves to offer shortcuts and is really good at making them look nice, which is why the message is called “No Shortcuts.” There are no shortcuts in the Christian life and no easy way to spiritual victory: the perfect Son of God had to hang on a tree and be buried in a tomb before he could sit on a throne. If Jesus had to suffer, why would you get a shortcut?
    • Last week Pastor Steve showed that Satan loves to take a good thing like bread and make it the main thing, turning God’s gifts into gods. True believers answer, “I’d rather be starving with Jesus than stuffed without him; I’d rather be living on the street and have Jesus than be in a mansion without him.”

  7. 11:47 Temptation two

    Scripture: Luke 4:5–8

    • Josh reads from verse 5: the devil led him up to a high place and showed him in an instant all the kingdoms of the world, offering all their authority and splendor, which “has been given to me, and I can give it to anyone I want to. If you worship me, it will all be yours.” Jesus answered, “It is written: Worship the Lord your God and serve him only.”

  8. 12:15 All the kingdoms of the world in an instant

    • All the kingdoms of the world: Josh assumes all means all, and the view must have been breathtaking, especially for a lonely Jesus. We reserve isolation for our worst criminals; after 40 days alone, Jesus sees all the nations, people, communities, connection, fellowship, power, and influence.
    • Josh remembers watching the sunrise from Haleakalā on his Hawaii honeymoon, freezing but remarkable, standing on solid ground looking down on the clouds. Still, it’s hard to imagine the magnitude of the supernatural display Jesus encountered.
    • The closest modern comparison is Central Park Tower in New York City, the tallest residential building in the world, with a penthouse listed at $250 million. Imagine waking up to that view every morning, and even more, the pride that could build up in someone with the posture of being above it all.

  9. 14:26 The big lie: God’s way isn’t the only way

    Scripture: Luke 4:6 Psalm 2:8 Daniel 7:13–14

    • While they gaze at the horizon, the devil makes a mind-blowing offer: “I will give you all their authority and splendor.” Here is the insidious nature of the offer, the big lie: God’s way isn’t the only way. Satan is trying to get Jesus to doubt God’s plan: “Jesus, did you know there’s a shortcut?”
    • A shortcut to the promise. Psalm 2 says, “Ask me, and I will make the nations your inheritance, the ends of the earth your possession.” Josh reads Daniel’s vision in chapter 7 of one like a son of man coming with the clouds of heaven, given authority, glory, and sovereign power, worshiped by all nations and peoples, with an everlasting dominion and a kingdom that will never be destroyed.

  10. 15:51 The difference between the plans: suffering

    • So here is Jesus in a desert, lonely, hungry, dirty, and tired, preparing for an earthly ministry of poverty: the son of a carpenter, homeless as an adult with no place to rest his head, rejected by his own people, humiliated before family and friends, and finally forsaken as he bore sin on the cross. He is preparing to be the Lamb of God, and Satan offers him a palace.
    • What’s the difference between God’s plan and Satan’s plan? One word: suffering. According to Satan, Jesus can sidestep all the bad stuff and get straight to the good stuff.

  11. 16:54 The suffering servant was always the plan

    Scripture: Luke 24:25–27 Genesis 3 Deuteronomy 18 Numbers 21 Isaiah 53 Daniel 9 Isaiah 9 Psalm 110 Psalm 116 Malachi 4

    • The message of a suffering servant wasn’t new; it had been on the lips of God’s prophets for thousands of years, beginning with Moses. On the road to Emmaus, Jesus got frustrated with two men who saw the glory of the kingdom but didn’t understand the suffering: “How foolish you are… Did not the Messiah have to suffer these things and then enter his glory?”
    • The Scriptures foretell the suffering. Josh puts a list on the screen as homework: Genesis 3, Deuteronomy 18, Numbers 21, Isaiah 53, Daniel 9, Isaiah 9, the Psalms (“probably 110, 116, don’t quote me on that”), and Malachi 4. God has been working on this plan throughout history, and the second temptation is the prince of this world’s attempt to tear it all down: “Jesus, there doesn’t need to be suffering. We can end suffering today, right here, right now.”

    1. 18:27 Made perfect through what he suffered

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:10

      • Hebrews makes clear that suffering is necessary and, especially for the Son of God, produces something extraordinarily important: in bringing many sons and daughters to glory, God made the pioneer of their salvation, Jesus, perfect through what he suffered.
      • The problem with Satan’s lie is that you can’t end suffering unless you end sin. Looking out over kingdoms that all inevitably suffer, Satan says, “You and me, we can do what the kings of the earth can’t. We don’t need God’s plan of reconciliation; we can reconcile this right here, right now.”

  12. 19:41 “God told me”

    • Josh pauses in the tension and asks for hands: has anyone ever said to you, “God told me”? More hands go up than he expected. He believes most who say it have good intentions, but some say it because it sounds more holy, and he would wager it is more reckless than anything. You had better be sure it’s from God.
    • That is why Josh sticks close to the word: what does God’s word say, what does Jesus teach, does this interpretation make sense with what Jesus and God said elsewhere? Where Jesus speaks, he preaches boldly, and where Jesus doesn’t, he trusts. Forgive him if he doesn’t sound Christian enough, but he is incredibly cautious to put words in God’s mouth.

    1. 21:14 Filled with the Spirit, talking to the devil

      Scripture: Luke 4:1

      • Remember how Jesus walked into the showdown: full of the Holy Spirit, he left the Jordan and was led by the Spirit into the wilderness. What is Jesus filled with? What is he led by? Who is talking to him in the wilderness? The devil. Both can happen at the same time; being filled with and walking by the Spirit doesn’t mean Satan can’t shove his lies in your face.

    2. 22:00 Get in his word

      • If your alternative to time in the word is “I feel like God has a word for me,” Josh says, “I got a word for you from God: get in his word.” If you aren’t reminding yourself of his promises by studying diligently, you risk confusing God’s promises for Satan’s perversions.
      • It might sting, but we have a good shepherd who loves us and will nurse us back to health. When we recognize our shortcomings, Jesus, not just a gospel then but a gospel now, still embraces us, draws us in, restores us, and continues to sanctify us.

  13. 23:00 Worldly benefits mistaken for heavenly blessings

    • Some are being blessed by Satan’s perversions yet claiming it’s a result of God’s promises: they jumped on lies that brought blessing and call it favor. Some have taken shortcuts and are confusing worldly benefits with heavenly blessings.

    1. 23:26 The dream home and the giving

      • Some finally landed that dream home, and Josh is happy for them, but maybe they are turning down every prompt of the Spirit toward a godly generosity that sees the church as the hope of the world. Too many Christians give as if the church were a broken-down nonprofit on its way to failure, when they should give as if it were the hub of truth for all of humanity.
      • Was that home God’s plan for you, or your plan, and you just took a shortcut, even if it meant tossing your spiritual growth on the back burner? “If we just decrease our giving this much, we can do this; if we’re not obedient here, think of what we could do.”

    2. 24:33 Josh puts himself in check

      • Josh puts himself in check too: is the church really growing, or is it growing because he is willing to take shortcuts and soften his stance on certain issues? Are people in his church killing their sin, or is he making way for them to keep it?

    3. 25:01 Good stuff doesn’t equal God stuff

      • Please don’t think that because it’s good, it’s from God. “My career’s taken off, God’s blessing me”: or you’re gone before the sun comes up and home with no energy left for your wife, and maybe that’s why you’re successful. To men and women wrapped up in careers: what would you take first, a demotion or a divorce?
      • “My kids are doing really well, God has blessed me”: or you have made your kids an idol, your identity is wrapped up in them, and when they move out you will fall apart. Super moms, dads, grandpas, and grandmas: do you find more comfort in what your kids and grandkids say about you or in what your heavenly Father says?
      • This good-stuff-equals-God-stuff mindset is an equation that kills. What if some things you claim as blessings from God are actually benefits from Satan?

  14. 26:17 “It has been given to me”

    Scripture: Luke 4:6

    • Josh asks the church to consider it, because the devil says, “It has been given to me, and I can give it to anyone I want to.” That phrase stirs two things in him. First: has he given me anything? If so, Josh doesn’t want his stuff, gifts, shortcuts, or perversions. He wants God’s plan even if it rips him to shreds and takes everything he owns.
    • Second: who gave Satan the thing he says he can give to others?

  15. 27:01 How do I know who gave it to me?

    • First things first: look at the fruit. God’s blessings bring joy, not sorrow. Another test is your attitude when and if it is taken away: it’s yours now, but how is it going when it’s not?
    • Josh shares the words of the song “If” by Beautiful Eulogy: if in one unfortunate moment you took everything I own, my ministry, influence, reputation, health, friends, even my kids and my wife, it would crush me, but you would still preserve my faith. “What’s concealed in the heart of having is revealed in the losing of things.” “I came into this world with nothing and when I die it’ll be the same. I will praise your name in the giving and taking away. If I have you, I can lose everything and still consider it gain.”

  16. 28:35 Everything loss for the surpassing worth of Christ

    Scripture: Philippians 3:7–11

    • This is the attitude Paul describes in Philippians 3: whatever were gains to me I now consider loss for the sake of Christ, I consider everything a loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord, I consider them garbage that I may gain Christ, I want to know the power of his resurrection and participate in his sufferings, becoming like him in his death. Who talks like this?
    • Is this your attitude toward stuff? Do you consider it garbage? That word is a nasty one; Paul used a four-letter word, “dung,” and Josh won’t use the modern equivalents. Your dream home, career, health, influence: so many of us are pursuing garbage. Stop filling your life with junk and calling it blessing.
    • You can have stuff, but some have way too much junk: hoarders who say, “I might need this in 16 years.” Let it go. “I’m not saying you can’t have crap, just don’t make the crap a crown.”

  17. 30:47 Who gave Satan the thing he offers?

    Scripture: Luke 4:6

    • Back to the devil’s phrase, “It has been given to me.” Josh lost sleep over this verse this week. There are a couple of ways to understand it, and he gives both and lets the church decide.

    1. 31:13 Adam forfeited authority

      Scripture: Genesis 1:26 Luke 4 Ephesians 2:1–2

      • Some say Adam gave the world over to the devil. In Genesis 1 God said, “Let us make human beings in our image, in our likeness, so that they may rule.” That authority was given to Adam; he was passive, which led to disobedience, and he handed it over to the devil.
      • This is a limited authority, both in Luke 4 and in Genesis. Satan offers Jesus all their authority, not all authority, and Josh is certain Jesus caught that. Paul describes how this authority affected us in Ephesians: you were dead in your transgressions and sins when you followed the ways of this world and of the ruler of the kingdom of the air.

    2. 32:37 Or the ultimate bluff

      Scripture: Luke 4:7 Isaiah 14:14

      • The other view is that this is the ultimate bluff. Satan is a liar, pretending to offer Jesus something that isn’t his to offer, and even if he had it, he wouldn’t keep his word.
      • Bottom line, Satan knows Jesus’ earthly ministry will exalt Yahweh, reconcile the people to God forever, and lead the nations to worship God only, which is why he says, “If you worship me, it will all be yours.” This is not a new play: Satan does in the desert what Isaiah says he did in heaven. He wanted to be worshiped, and if he could get the Son of God to worship him, “I will be like the Most High.”
      • Satan failed. Jesus was hungry, but he had everything he needed.

  18. 33:52 Jesus’ weapons are still available to you

    • Every weapon Jesus used against Satan is still available to you and me. Are you using them? Hungry, lonely, and tired, Jesus immediately pulls out the sword of truth. Is that your first response, or do you prefer other weapons first?
    • You don’t have to be a theologian or have the New Testament memorized, but you need to know who God is, what he says about you, who he says you are, and what he has planned for you.

    1. 34:41 Bible races

      • Josh explains Bible races, where kids on a stage race to turn to a verse the children’s pastor calls out. He was never fast and still isn’t; now he just types it in and it autofills. One punk kid would always beat him and turn around and say, “Thought you were a pastor’s kid.” Josh jokes that they could meet at the baptistry in five; these days he doesn’t fight by the baptistry, he fights out back. He’s kidding.

  19. 36:02 Knowing it isn’t the same as believing it

    Scripture: Proverbs 10:22

    • Many Christians seem to have mastered the intellectual exercise of Christianity but struggle with the identity part. You can memorize Scripture, toss it out in life group, and have your theology organized, but do you believe it?
    • Satan says to Jesus, “If you are the Son of God.” Satan knows he is, Jesus knows he is, everyone there knows, but it doesn’t matter what you know: if Satan can stir up even a little doubt, he will, and he will use everything you know against you. If you’re a child of God, how come you lost your job, your home? How come your marriage is so toxic? Doesn’t he love you?
    • That’s Satan’s game. If you’re doing well, God must approve of you, and in the goodness Satan builds your pride. If you’re doing poorly, God must not approve, so you try to change your behavior. But behavior change comes from your abilities; heart change comes from the Lord. You need a new heart.
    • Proverbs 10 says the blessing of the Lord makes rich and adds no sorrow with it. God’s blessings come with joy, not sorrow, so if you claim to be surrounded by blessings and feel miserable all the time, you might need to double-check the source. God’s blessings don’t bear bad fruit.

  20. 38:32 Worship the Lord your God and serve him only

    Scripture: Luke 4:8

    • Jesus is fasting, filled with the Spirit, and in complete submission to God’s will, and he answers, “It is written: Worship the Lord your God and serve him only.”
    • A tragedy in the American church is that worship and service have become separate: many worship, few serve; many sing, few offer their gifts in service of others. But whatever we worship we will serve. Satan wasn’t asking Jesus to sing him a Chris Tomlin song; “worship me” meant “serve me”: make me the object of your greatest affection, the thing you give your time, heart, and money to, the thing you obey and teach your kids to obey.

    1. 39:40 Suffering first, then glory

      Scripture: 1 Peter 5:10

      • Remember the shortcut. 1 Peter 5:10 says the God of all grace who called you to his eternal glory in Christ, after you have suffered a little while, will himself restore you and make you strong, firm, and steadfast. God’s plan begins with suffering and ends with glory; Satan devours people by starting with glory and ending with suffering.
      • Satan would love for us to forfeit the eternal for the temporary, so we must pray against temptation, take up our guard, grip the sword of truth like it’s our only hope, and wait for, pray for, and notice God’s will, because God’s way is always the best way. Satan will offer instant gratification and next-day delivery, but Jesus refused it, and so should you: no glory before suffering. “I see your plan, God, and I’m all in, whatever it costs.”

  21. 41:13 They refused to compromise worship

    Scripture: Joshua 24:15

    • Josh’s favorite moment of worship in Scripture is Daniel in the lions’ den. Like Jesus, Daniel refused to compromise the worship of God even facing death: King Darius forbade praying to anyone but him, Daniel kept praying to Yahweh, and God shut the lions’ mouths.
    • Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego refused to bow to Nebuchadnezzar’s golden image and were thrown into the furnace, but there was a fourth man in the fire, a protector and savior. Josh believes Jesus was with them; the crowd didn’t see the fourth man until the door closed, but those young men of faith saw him every day.
    • Moses destroyed the golden calf and led Israel to repent. Elijah confronted Ahab and the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel, “talk about a showdown,” and God answered with fire from heaven. Noah kept building, Mordecai refused to bow, Nehemiah kept working on the wall, Peter kept preaching, and Paul kept traveling.
    • Josh’s favorite is Joshua, who, surrounded by nations serving other gods, made his allegiance clear: if serving the Lord seems undesirable to you, choose for yourselves whom you will serve, “but as for me and my household, we will serve the Lord.” Nothing about serving the Lord seems undesirable to Josh. He has made his choice; whether God tears down or builds up, he will praise him, and no matter how attractive the world pretends to be, he will worship almighty God and serve him only.

  22. 43:44 Why expressive worship matters

    • This is why expressive worship matters and why Josh raises his hands in praise. There are other things in this world people will stand and do this for, but there is no one else he will stand and do this for. He gives him everything and leaves his heart on the line, only for him.

  23. 44:16 Two kingdoms, two spirits

    Scripture: 1 John 4:5–6 1 John 4:4

    • If the showdown in the desert teaches anything, it’s that every day you will face temptation to worship other things: career, success, family, social approval, material wealth. But the true kingdom we are called to seek is not of this world.
    • We can tell the difference between these kingdoms. John explains in 1 John 4: they are from the world and speak from its viewpoint, and the world listens to them; we are from God, and whoever knows God listens to us. This is how we recognize the spirit of truth and the spirit of falsehood.
    • Are you discerning those every day and standing firm in God’s promises like Jesus did? If so, you, dear children, are from God and have overcome them, because the one who is in you is greater than the one who is in the world.

  24. 45:40 Stay frosty

    Scripture: 1 Peter 5:8 Romans 8:31 Philippians 2:10–11

    • Josh doesn’t want to spoil it, but Jesus would walk out of the wilderness victorious. Satan didn’t give up, stop, or rest; he kept looking for opportunities to keep Jesus from God’s will, and he didn’t always use prideful dictators: sometimes he dressed up like a dear friend.
    • Stay frosty, watchful, on your toes. The winner of this showdown has been decided, but Satan still prowls around like a lion looking for someone to devour, so we cannot let our guard down.
    • When Satan starts talking and you recognize it, you have Josh’s permission to talk back with all the power of the Spirit of God in you: “Get back, Satan, you have no power over me, because the one who is in me is greater than you.” Whatever power Satan has, we have more. We are co-heirs with Christ, and if God is for us, who can be against us? Josh is an adopted child of almighty God; he might suffer, but one day the Lord will pry open the heavens, every knee will bow, and every tongue will confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.

  25. 47:39 Stand, join hands, and pray

    • Josh asks those who are with him to stand, because if you can’t stand tall in this place you’ll never stand tall out there, and to grab the hand of the person next to them (“if they want their thumb on top, let their thumb be on top; you’re a Christian, be nice”).
    • He prays: this whole thing is built on the foundation of Jesus Christ. The church is God’s; he rescued us, reserves us for his purposes, and bought us with the precious blood of his perfect Son, the sacrificial lamb. That victory didn’t look like a victory that day; it wasn’t until three days later, when faithful followers found an empty tomb, that our eyes were opened to a future with him forever and peace with God through Jesus Christ. “Thank you. We love you.”
