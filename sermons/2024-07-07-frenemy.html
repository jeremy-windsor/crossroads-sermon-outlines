Overview

  1. 01:04 Final week of Courage Over Fear: “Do not be afraid”

    • It is the final week of the series Courage Over Fear. Joshua is a difficult book that many avoid putting on the teaching calendar, and those who hung in through the whole series know why. Josh has learned a lot, and one application keeps hitting over and over: the words Yahweh uses to encourage his young leader Joshua, “Do not be afraid.”
    • Growing up we feared monsters under the bed, being dumped, not having a prom date, or a bad report card. We don’t grow out of fear; we develop advanced fears. A 2023 survey found 60% of adults feared corruption among government officials, 54% economic collapse, and 52% another world war. We go from monsters under the bed to monsters in government.
    • God’s word is chock-full of corrupted leaders, governments, and establishments, yet Yahweh’s message to those who love him stays consistent: fear not, take courage, do not lose heart. The command implies a choice: operate in fear or choose to live with courage.

    1. 02:57 The fear of our own mistakes

      • After all those external fears, the next thing to torment us is ourselves, especially a fear of our mistakes. Believers grow less fearful of the world and more skeptical of themselves. Living a holy life matters more to us, but that desire can become sinful when we start to fear our own failure and grow apart from grace. Our growing knowledge of righteousness makes our mistakes even more devastating to us.
      • Ultimately we fear failure because we weren’t created to fail. We were created to thrive in close proximity to our Creator, but sin entered through Adam and now there is separation where there was supposed to be intimacy and enjoyment.

    2. 04:12 Avoiding mistakes by never trying

      • A popular way to deal with it is to avoid mistakes by being inactive: “I won’t fail because I’ll never try.” The pessimist says the surgeon, lawyer, or pilot who tries could make a deadly mistake. The optimist answers that if no one tries, the surgeon never saves that life, the lawyer never defends the innocent, and the pilot never delivers emergency supplies.
      • If you do nothing because you fear doing something wrong, you aren’t walking by faith. Effective Christians have learned that when you walk with the Lord, mistakes can be turned into victory.

  2. 05:25 Joshua’s second failure, in three parts

    Scripture: Joshua 9

    • We see it again in the conquest of Canaan. Joshua’s first failure came before the first attack at Ai; his second comes as he handles the Gibeonites in chapter 9. Josh breaks it into three parts: Joshua was fooled by the enemy, Joshua became friends with the enemy, and Joshua wound up fighting for the enemy.
    • The Gibeonite deception comes on the heels of Israel’s covenant renewal at Mount Ebal, where they read the blessings and curses and celebrated before the Lord. But the remaining cities had heard about Jericho’s walls and the ambush at Ai, and they were determined to go out swinging.
    • Imam Ali defined a friend three ways: your friend, your friend’s friend, and the enemy of your enemy. That is Canaan: the kings aren’t buddies, but everyone hates Israel, so they mount a joint counterattack.

  3. 06:50 Part one: fooled by the enemy

    Scripture: Joshua 9–10 1 Peter 5:8 2 Corinthians 11:3

    • A timeless truth: your effort to love God, follow Christ, and be holy will almost always meet immediate resistance from the enemy. Satan has two strategies, and in chapters 9 and 10 Joshua is hit with both.
    • Peter warns that the devil prowls like a roaring lion looking for someone to devour; here the roaring lion is the armies of Canaan, a band of stepbrothers joining forces to overrun Israel. Paul sheds light on the other approach: he fears that just as Eve was deceived by the serpent’s cunning, our minds may be led astray from sincere and pure devotion to Christ.
    • If the Phoenix Zoo tossed a lion in here, everyone would notice. Josh jokes that he asked Elijah to drop a snake in at the start of the service to see how long it took anyone to notice. A roaring lion is more obvious than a slithering snake; with a snake, you won’t know until it’s too late.

    1. 08:24 Gibeon knew the rules of engagement

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 20

      • Gibeon was only 25 miles from Israel’s camp at Gilgal. Somehow the Gibeonites knew Israel could offer peace treaties in conflicts outside the promised land, rules of engagement handed down in Deuteronomy 20, which Josh leaves as homework, and they used them to their advantage.
      • That is why the children of God must carry the word and wear the full armor of God: the enemy knows how to use God’s word to get things done. Cults and false teachers have strategies and charismatic communicators too. It isn’t only the church’s job to proclaim the truth but also to defend it and call out lies: to feed the flock and protect the flock.

    2. 09:37 Protecting the flock isn’t glamorous

      • It is not a glamorous job. People come in calling themselves prophets with a message from the Lord; people with no desire to be led, who don’t tithe or serve, want to tell the church how to do things; people pretend financial difficulty to take advantage of kindness. Many come in with an agenda, meet Josh, and it just doesn’t work.
      • But every now and then someone walks in who loves God and the Scriptures, walks humbly with Jesus, prays for others, is a doer of the word, helps in the mission, and seeks to submit to the authority of the church. That person is rare. That is why the church needs to pray for its pastors and leaders by name.

  4. 11:12 The ruse: worn-out sacks and moldy bread

    Scripture: Joshua 9:3–5

    • When Gibeon heard what Joshua had done to Jericho and Ai, they sent a delegation with worn-out sacks, cracked and mended wineskins, patched sandals, old clothes, and dry, moldy bread, staged to look like a long journey.
    • People walk into the church like this all the time, with a plan for how they want to be seen and used: “I know what you’ve been missing: my gifting.” They bury poor intentions in neatly packaged Christian language. But trust is earned, not given.

    1. 12:07 To those who are new

      • To those fairly new to the church: praise God you’re here. You can grow in your faith here; there are story after story of mistakes turned into ministry. The church wants to walk with you, encourage you, restore you if you’ve fallen, and be there in good times and bad. You aren’t just a butt in a seat; you are someone God loves, cherishes, and sent his Son to save, and they want to preach Christ to you and your family every chance they get.
      • But be patient with them. Even in the summer slump there is a flock of 500 or so souls. It is their job to be welcoming but also watchful, to show hospitality but not act in haste. They aren’t pursuing growth; they are pursuing God. They aren’t trying to be another church or Church 2.0; their strategy is to try to be like Jesus.

  5. 13:59 Broadway comes to Canaan

    Scripture: Joshua 9:9 Joshua 2

    • The Gibeonites come in with props: Broadway has come to Canaan. Look at our wineskins and sandals; did you just stick your stuff in a wine press? They also pull the God card: “Your servants have come from a very distant country because of the fame of the Lord your God.” They are trying to sound spiritual.
    • That is why the church should pray by name for Josh, Chris, Elijah, another Steve, founding pastor Steve, the staff, elders, and pastors: it is really hard to tell the difference between someone like Rahab in chapter 2 and the Gibeonites in chapter 9. Rahab also heard what God had done, drying up the Red Sea, but she wasn’t looking to deceive; she was looking to preserve her family from destruction.

  6. 15:33 How not to discern: they did not inquire of the Lord

    Scripture: Joshua 9:14

    • So how do we discern between a genuine person and a Broadway actor? First, notice how not to do it: the Israelites sampled their provisions but did not inquire of the Lord. The word means they inspected it. They conducted an investigation, were suspicious, and asked the right questions, but never involved God, though he had made clear he was available. They chose expedience, leaned on their own understanding, met in the conference room, and reached their own conclusions, missing the key ingredient.

    1. 16:37 Josh’s confession: never mistake gifting for wisdom

      Scripture: Proverbs 3:5–6

      • Josh confesses to his church family that he is a problem solver who loves to fix things and is gifted at it, and that is the problem. He is slow to criticize Joshua’s team because he is just like them: he thinks it through, and if no one objects, he moves forward. At 38 he needs to work on this, slow down, and inquire of the Lord more. The more he leads and learns, the more capable he feels, and that scares him. He doesn’t want to run out in front of God and only turn back when he makes a mess; he wants to be dragged by God’s hand.
      • There is a balance: God has given gifts, a brain, courage, and boldness to use for his glory. Proverbs 3 says to trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding. It is hard to tell wrong action from inaction. Josh asks the church to pray, for him and for themselves, that he would never mistake his gifting for God’s wisdom.

  7. 18:47 Part two: friends with the enemy

    Scripture: Joshua 9:15 Exodus 34:12

    • Joshua made a treaty of peace to let them live, and the leaders ratified it by oath. There’s no shame in taking time before a decision; it is easy to play Monday morning quarterback. Josh thinks that with another stab at it, Joshua would have welcomed the men but waited a few days on the permanence of the treaty until he’d done his homework. Patience is a fruit of the Spirit, and true faith brings great patience.
    • This wasn’t only about patience. After the exodus Moses warned them to be careful not to make a treaty with those who live in the land, or they would be a snare. Too late now: Joshua has been fooled and has made a friend of the enemy. Scripture calls us foreigners whose home is heaven; we must be careful not to take the enemy at his word in a foreign land but wait on the Lord.

    1. 20:18 Leaders who keep their word

      Scripture: Joshua 9:18–19

      • Being friends with the enemy proved messy, and nobody was happy, but it is hard not to celebrate these flawed leaders for being men of their word. The whole assembly grumbled, but the leaders answered, “We have given them our oath by the Lord, the God of Israel, and we cannot touch them now.” Strong leaders keep their word when the heat is on; let your yes be yes.
      • The people figured an oath obtained by deceit shouldn’t be honored, but breaking it would dishonor the name of the Lord: a rock and a hard place. Our tendency is to bury mistakes, cover them up, sweep them under the rug. Those who walk by faith and understand grace don’t attempt cover-ups; they develop a plan to address the issue.
      • As Josh said last week, and it can’t be over-preached: there are no diminishing returns on God’s grace. When we walk faithfully through our messes, we shine a light on the grace of God and become living testimonies to his goodness and gospel.

  8. 22:10 Mistakes turned into ministries

    Scripture: Joshua 9:20–21 Joshua 9:25–26

    • Joshua’s solution: let them live so God’s wrath won’t fall on Israel for breaking the oath, but let them be woodcutters and water carriers for the community. This is what godly leaders do: they turn mistakes into ministries. The Gibeonites were given jobs in the Lord’s house. Josh jokes that given the choice between dying and serving in the church, you’d probably serve: a new serve strategy at the Cross.
    • There is more to it. In verse 25 they say, “We are now in your hands. Do to us whatever seems good and right to you.” Joshua saved them, and it starts to look like another redemption story; God seems to enjoy those.

    1. 23:28 Surrender and salvation: put yourself in his hands

      Scripture: Psalm 27:10 Isaiah 41:10 1 Peter 5:6 James 4:10

      • Josh prays that people would put themselves in the hands of Jesus the way the Gibeonites put themselves in Joshua’s hands. He hears the sound of God’s salvation in these verses: surrender and salvation. Psalm 27 says even if my father and mother abandon me, the Lord will hold me close. Through Isaiah the Lord says, “Don’t be afraid, for I am with you… I will hold you up with my victorious right hand.” Sin might define your past, but in his hands it doesn’t have to define your future.
      • He invites anyone who hasn’t already to tell God they want to be in his hands, “Whatever seems good and right to you, Lord, have your way,” and the waters of baptism are ready. Peter says to humble yourselves under God’s mighty hand that he may lift you up in due time; James says it too. This is the gospel according to the Gibeonites: I’m in your hands; Jesus gave his life for me, and I want to give my life to you.

    2. 25:48 Did the Gibeonites meet Yahweh?

      Scripture: Ezra 2

      • Canaanite liars forced to serve in the Lord’s house: Josh thinks it is safe to assume, though it is an assumption, that they met Yahweh and put their lives in his hands, because Ezra 2 says the temple servants returned to Jerusalem after the Babylonian captivity. If that’s them, they could have chilled in a nice crib in Babylon, but they wanted to get back to the Lord’s house.
      • This is our God: he forgives sinners and delivers captives, then and now. He turns mistakes into ministries and enemies into friends. The only question is whether you are in his hands, and if not, you need to be.

  9. 26:56 Part three: fighting for the enemy

    Scripture: Joshua 10:1–2 Joshua 13

    • When you make friends with the enemy, their enemies become your enemies. Adoni-Zedek, king of Jerusalem, heard that Joshua had destroyed Ai and that Gibeon had made peace with Israel, and he was alarmed, because Gibeon was a royal city, larger than Ai, with good fighters.
    • With a map, Josh shows phase one of the conquest splitting north from south: Jericho, Ai, the trip up to Shechem for worship, and back down through Gilgal, leaving a rectangle of control north of the southern kings. Gibeon is the high ground before upper Beth Horon on a key road to Jerusalem, like hitting the hill before the plateau driving up to Prescott. If you’re a Star Wars fan, you know how important the high ground is: “It’s over, Anakin.” Fun fact: that land later belonged to the tribe of Benjamin; the land divisions are in chapter 13, homework the series won’t cover.
    • But strategic advantage doesn’t matter when your best warrior is Yahweh. It isn’t about where you fight from but who fights for you; when your best warrior spoke the sun and moon into existence, you don’t need the high ground, though they have it anyway.

  10. 30:02 Joshua marches; then the Lord speaks

    Scripture: Joshua 10:4 Joshua 10:7–8

    • Adoni-Zedek sends an Evite to four other southern kings: come up and help me attack Gibeon, because it made peace with Joshua. As they rush north, Joshua marches from Gilgal with his whole army, and the Lord says, “Do not be afraid of them; I have given them into your hand.”
    • Joshua marched; then the Lord said. Sometimes we hear and then respond; sometimes we act and then hear. That is why inaction cannot be the solution for believers: we don’t want to be careless, but we don’t want to be complacent.

    1. 31:10 When God seems silent, march on what he has said

      Scripture: 1 Kings 8:56

      • Josh recently counseled someone troubled that God had gone radio silent, as though they were talking at God rather than with him. He told them: if we aren’t willing to march on what God has already said, why would he speak again? Sometimes you have to call on the promises of old, as Israel did years later in 1 Kings: praise be to the Lord, who has given rest to his people Israel just as he promised; not one word has failed.

    2. 32:04 “The Lord gave me a word for you”

      • Many believers, to sound super spiritual, have muddied the waters: “The Lord gave me a word for you,” “I had a vision,” “The Lord told me.” Many are always chasing new revelation, and it has gotten so extreme that some churches name their lead pastor and his wife apostles, not the gifting but the office. If he weren’t busy crying, he’d be laughing.
      • Open your Bible: God isn’t dropping new stuff every week; he is dropping the same stuff over and over, probably because we don’t listen. Cessationist, continuationist, or undecided, Josh hopes this body, his responsibility, can agree not to put words in God’s mouth. Instead of asking for a new word, recall the Spirit-inspired Scriptures and ask the Spirit for right understanding, not new understanding. Joseph Smith claimed new revelation; look how that worked out. Ellen White, Mary Baker Eddy, Charles Taze Russell: the list of false teachers is long. Don’t claim words from God; encourage others with the words we know are from God.

  11. 34:14 The Lord fights for Israel

    Scripture: Joshua 10:9–10 Psalm 24:8

    • After an all-night march from Gilgal, Joshua took them by surprise; the Lord threw them into confusion, and Israel defeated them at Gibeon and pursued them toward Beth Horon. Josh read from the NIV, which reads as though the Lord’s only contribution was confusion, but in the original Hebrew the Lord also defeated them, pursued them, and cut them down. He is active in the whole thing.
    • Most of us struggle here because we prefer to shape God into what we want, like pottery growing arms to shape the potter. We like God soft-spoken, distant, around for the holidays, non-combative, affirming. We do the same with Jesus, seeing him less as a warrior for our souls and more as an Old Spice calendar boy with a clean beard and a nice tan. But who is this King of glory? The Lord strong and mighty, mighty in battle. Yahweh is a warrior who crushes the enemy.

    1. 35:54 Hail on command

      Scripture: Joshua 10:11 Job 38:22–23

      • As the enemy fled toward Azekah, the Lord hurled large hailstones, and more died from the hail than by Israel’s swords. Not only hail on command: this was close sword combat, yet Yahweh threaded the tight spots, hitting the enemy and avoiding friendly fire.
      • Josh and his son love a good Nerf battle; his strategy is to hoard the ammo, stashing it in the couch and shutters until everyone wonders where it went. This twisted father’s storehouses are nothing compared to God’s. God puts Job in his place: “Have you entered the storehouses of the snow or seen the storehouses of the hail, which I reserve for times of trouble, for days of war and battle?” God has storehouses full of hail; in 120-degree heat, Josh wants to see the storehouse of snow.

    2. 37:50 Should a Christian prepare for terrible times?

      • A quick subject hop, since many ask: should a Christian prepper prepare for terrible times? We can disagree, but Josh’s short answer is yes, as long as it is done in faith, with contingency plans that are courage-driven, not fear-driven. If being prepared means providing for your family and loving your neighbor, share that 25-year-shelf-life Costco food when things go down. But if you have a biometric bunker full of corn, M16s, and landmines, we need to talk.

  12. 38:56 The sun stands still

    Scripture: Joshua 10:12–14

    • Yahweh’s hailstorm is nothing compared to what comes next. Joshua says before Israel, “Sun, stand still over Gibeon, and you, moon, over the Valley of Aijalon,” and the sun stopped in the middle of the sky about a full day. There has never been a day like it, a day when the Lord listened to a human being. You ask your boss for more time and he adjusts your deadline; Joshua asks God and he adjusts the cosmos.
    • This is the last and most amazing miracle in Joshua, and many have tried to explain it. Josh decided trying would be a bad idea. Yes, we now know the earth rotates, but we still describe the sun as rising and setting, as Eli pointed out in the teaching team. Wouldn’t stopping the earth cause tsunamis and earthquakes? Josh doesn’t know; maybe God slowed it and brought it back, or bent the light. Just remember he has a storehouse full of snowballs.

    1. 40:54 Nothing is too hard for God

      Scripture: Jeremiah 32:17 Genesis 1:1 Psalm 74:16 2 Timothy 3:16

      • His ways are above ours, and the secret things belong to him; that’s why it is called a miracle. We can’t explain or comprehend it; the only answer that makes sense is faith: I believe he can, so he did. Seek answers not from people who pretend to have a word from God but from those who actually had one, like Jeremiah: “Ah, Sovereign Lord, you have made the heavens and the earth by your great power and outstretched arm. Nothing is too hard for you.”
      • Josh finds it hard to believe that in the beginning God made the heavens and the earth and then had no control over them. Psalm 74 says the day and the night are his and he established the sun and moon. Gravity is his design, but he can ignore it if he wants; he is limitless.
      • Curiosity and questions are fine, but if you don’t believe God is a miracle worker, you must discard the Scriptures, which 2 Timothy says are breathed out by God, then the virgin birth, then the resurrection. You cannot be a Christian and not believe in the miracles of Scripture.

  13. 42:37 Why Josh chooses courage over fear

    Scripture: Joshua 10:14

    • Josh loves that there was never a day like it, not because the sun or moon stopped, but because the Lord listened to a human being. That is why he chooses courage over fear: the Lord still listens to those who come to him. He is never alone or without hope; when the enemy says, “Josh, nobody’s for you,” he recalls the promises of old, lets Jesus’ words energize him, keeps praying, and doesn’t lose heart.
    • We are so quick to lose heart. Some have seen God do amazing things, yet when storms come they ask, “God, where are you?” God wants you to call on him in your time of need and also to recall his promises: both.

    1. 43:55 He has already won

      Scripture: John 16:33 John 14:27 1 Peter 2:23 Colossians 1:27

      • God will fight for you wherever you are, but more importantly, he has already won for us. Jesus says, “In this world you will have trouble. But take heart! I have overcome the world.” So it’s time the church starts praying, singing, preaching, and acting like we’ve already won. What can you possibly be afraid of?
      • In John 14 Jesus says, “My peace I give you.” It is the same peace that kept him calm as he was mocked and crucified: they hurled insults, he suffered but made no threats, and gave himself over to the one who judges justly. That is why it was good for Jesus to leave and send the Advocate to bring us his peace until he returns. We have the peace of God because we have the Spirit of God in us, the glorious mystery Paul talked about: Christ in you, the hope of glory. This is why believers choose courage over fear.

  14. 45:30 Closing prayer

    • Josh asks God’s forgiveness for his lack of patience with believers always calling for miracles, signs, and wonders. He knows God is capable and admires their faith, but he cannot get over the miracle already done: salvation for the undeserving, saving him from death and hell by sending Jesus to die and be raised. Having been given God’s Son, he doesn’t want anything more; he just wants God.
    • He asks God to shape him, kill the old desires to walk in the flesh, and continue the miracle of salvation in him and in the church, so that people who walk in would see and feel hope and immediately be told about Jesus, who died to save their souls.
