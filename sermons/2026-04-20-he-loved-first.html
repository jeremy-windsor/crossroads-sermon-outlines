Overview

  1. 00:47 Recite John 3:16

    Scripture: John 3:16

    • Josh greets the church, skips a planned mic bit, and asks someone to stand and recite John 3:16. A brother recites it with only begotten Son and everlasting life.
    • Josh jokes about last week’s lightsaber and says one of the most wonderful verses often becomes one of the most misunderstood.

    1. 01:44 Lazy definitions and the full counsel

      Scripture: John 1:29 1 John 4:8-10

      • Two reasons the verse is misunderstood: lazy definitions, and lack of exposure to the full counsel of God.
      • Any sermon worth hearing must present two things: since God is love, present him as love; since Jesus is the Lamb of God, present him as the atonement for sin. Parting from those does not make a minister creative; it makes him foolish.
      • The Spirit directs, but he will not send you down a path separate from the core doctrines of the faith.
      • Josh reads John’s epistle: whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love. God showed his love by sending his Son that we might live through him. This is love: not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as an atoning sacrifice for our sins.

  2. 03:28 Worldly love versus hesed

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:25

    • Josh and Kristen will celebrate fifteen years in October. Loving her as Christ loved him has been amazing, and some of those years were the hardest of his life.
    • Different church traditions showed up immediately: when he asked for her hand, the pressing concern was whether his church spoke in tongues. She was a single mother and a smoker; he said he would not marry a smoker, and she quit. He was a chewer; she never required him to quit, and that brought years of issues until the Lord gave him new desires.
    • He is not ashamed of the gospel. People have left the church over parts of his testimony.

    1. 05:37 Confession and the wait for forgiveness

      • The summer before freshman year, the internet arrived as his parents divorced. With little accountability he fell into a porn addiction and brought it into the marriage without telling Kristen. It still brought strife. The time came to confess; he was ready for consequences and feared the consequence would be the marriage.
      • Repentance is hard because it requires giving the person you hurt time to heal. The hardest nights were waiting to see if Kristen would find her way to forgiveness.

    2. 06:54 Culture’s dangerous love

      Scripture: Genesis 3:7-8

      • He had been trained by pop songs and sitcoms. What culture calls love is shallow, hollow, fragile—love you cannot feel safe in. Put biblical love next to it and the culture does not admire it; it criticizes it as unhealthy, unstable, controlling, too much.
      • English is awful here. Love has become purely emotional: you fall in and fall out. If it rises and falls on how happy someone makes you, you are not loving them. You are loving yourself, using them to do it.
      • He is not above this. There have been seasons with no peace in the home, because if love is built on feelings, peace is always fleeting.
      • Worldly love has expectations of the other; biblical love has expectations of self. If it is the other person’s job to keep you happy, they cannot be themselves, so we pull an Adam and Eve and hide. In marriage you cannot hide. Weaknesses show, love is tested, we defend and blame, and the marriage becomes a storm instead of a shelter.

    3. 09:26 Hesed: I’ve seen the worst of you

      Scripture: Matthew 5:27-28

      • The Hebrews had a word beyond emotion: hesed—loving-kindness, steadfast love based on promises, not feelings. A hesed marriage says, I’ve seen the worst of you, and I’m not going anywhere. Then you can be fully known. When emotion is gone, commitment remains.
      • He credits God for teaching Kristen to love like that, and her obedience for their joy. She could have left. He never cheated in the flesh, but the Sermon on the Mount made clear that what he was doing was unfaithfulness in the heart. She loved him at his worst. He witnessed the undeserved initiating love of God in his bride.
      • The church uses the same word for barbecue and a spouse. Kristen used to smile when she was annoying him and say, Love me. One time he answered, I’m committed to you. Worldly love prioritizes romance; biblical love prioritizes promises.
      • He is giving this view into the marriage so that when they talk about God’s love it will seem as awesome as it actually is.

  3. 11:57 God so loved the world

    Scripture: John 3:16

    • The love of God becomes immeasurable when you notice it is poured out on a lost, fallen, guilty world. God so agape’d the world: self-giving, sacrificial, unconditional love that seeks the other’s good regardless of worth or response.
    • There was nothing lovable here—enmity, hatred of his commands—yet God so loved the world. He did not love that it was fallen; he loved the people he put there regardless of their condition.

    1. 13:03 Consider the source

      Scripture: 1 John 4:8 2 Peter 3:9 Matthew 11:26 Romans 9:18-21

      • Seven points to deepen understanding. First: consider the source. God’s love springs from himself. He is not simply loving; he is love. He does not look down, find something impressive, and then decide to love. He looks down knowing past, present, and future, and loves anyway.
      • Why does the Lord love this person or that? Josh often says the Lord desires none to perish, yet many will. When pressed why God does what he does, quote Jesus: yes, Father, for this was your good pleasure.
      • Paul does not hide: God has mercy on whom he wants to have mercy and hardens whom he wants to harden. Who are you, a mere human, to talk back to God? Shall what is formed say, Why did you make me like this? The potter has the right over the same lump.

    2. 14:48 God’s love moves first

      Scripture: John 3:16 Matthew 1:23 John 3:17

      • God’s love is initiating, not reactive. For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son. God’s love is not a reaction to your faith; your faith is a reaction to his love.
      • Son of God does not mean God had a heavenly wife and picked Jesus from among God-babies. Begotten is monogenes: only one of its kind, unique. Begotten, not made, from the same substance. He is God in the flesh, Immanuel.
      • God leans down into the mess and sends Christ to be the righteousness we need, because on our own we stand condemned. Verse 17: God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but to save the world through him. He came not on a war path but a rescue mission.

    3. 16:13 Imputed righteousness

      • God in the flesh, not born of a man but of God, lives a perfect life that by grace is given to us. We are wrapped in his righteousness. It is imputed. When God sees us he sees the righteous acts of Christ, not our wickedness.
      • Because of the cross, past, present, and future sin is put on him, and he absorbs God’s wrath completely. How much have you done up to this point?

    4. 17:12 Pursuit through history

      Scripture: Genesis 3:9 Exodus 20:1-2 Hosea 3:1-2

      • God’s pursuit of undeserving sinners can be traced throughout history. In Genesis 3 Adam and Eve hide and cover; God calls, Where are you? There are catastrophic consequences, and a promise of deliverance. God’s reaction to sin in Eden was pursuit.
      • Pop quiz: does the law come before or after the Exodus? After. God did not say, Obey me and I’ll save you. He delivered them, then gave them rules for living like his people.
      • Hosea—Josh says they will do a series on Hosea this year—means salvation, from the same root as Yeshua. Hosea lives out the gospel by pursuing an unfaithful bride. Gomer runs; God tells Hosea to go get her. Not a prodigal waiting halfway. He pursues the unfaithful. Some hearers are in an outright sprint; he is still pursuing with holy hesed.

    5. 19:05 Holy love that acts

      Scripture: 1 John 4:10-16

      • God’s love is holy, not sentimental teenage romance that depends on you. The world’s reactive love leads to sin; God’s initiating love deals with sin. His love does not compete with his justice or force him to compromise holiness. He does not love by ignoring sin or letting us off the hook. He loves by dealing with sin completely.
      • Fourth: it is expressed not in feelings but in action. He demonstrates it. If God’s love were a picture, the most accurate depiction would be Christ’s blood dripping from his feet into the dirt, soaking into a fallen world.
      • John builds on it: this is love—not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as an atoning sacrifice. Since God so loved us, we ought to love one another. No one has seen God, but if we love one another God lives in us and his love is made complete in us. He has given us his Spirit. The Father sent his Son to be the Savior of the world.
      • He did not just say I love you. He proved it by crushing his Son so you could be free. Everything unlovable about you was dealt with at the cross. Now go live for him.

    6. 21:40 Transforming, not just comforting

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:20

      • Fifth: God’s love is transforming. More than that, it is made complete in us. After Kristen said she forgave him and would fight for the marriage, he did not go back to the old life. He left and never looked back.
      • Whether witnessed through Jesus or through one of his adopted sons or daughters, this love does not leave you where it found you. Augustine: love God and do what you will—because real love reshapes your will. That is Josh’s testimony. Justification leads to sanctification that leads to glorification.
      • God’s love is not just acceptance; it is alteration. You were bought at a price. You are mine, and now I will teach you how to look like me.

    7. 23:07 Covenantal love

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:5 John 3:16

      • Sixth: God’s love is covenantal, committed before he spoke light, not conditional, because it is eternal. God describes his hesed in Deuteronomy, and Hebrews quotes him: never will I leave you, never will I forsake you.
      • If God had chosen Josh because of goodness, he would have cast him out because of wickedness. Notice the plan: God so loved the world that he gave his Son, that whoever believes. God loves; the world responds. If his love started with him, it will finish with him.

  4. 24:06 What it means to believe

    Scripture: Revelation 21:27

    • People are all over the map on belief. When the Lord comes, names are looked up in the Lamb’s book of life, every act is evaluated, and there will be surprises: oh, you’re here? Who does Jesus say he is? God. What did he come to do? Save sinners. What does he offer those washed in his blood? Eternal life.
    • Since God does not love with only words, you do not respond with only words. If his Spirit is in you, you demonstrate the effects of his love back to him. That is the biblical definition of belief: you complete the circle of love.
    • Believing in Jesus means you have declared war against the sin in your life, you are serious about knowing God and following Jesus, Jesus is filling more of your life, and the world is emptying to make space for him.

    1. 26:14 Not subtraction—saturation

      Scripture: John 17:17

      • Josh uses a visual of a full life—success, money, sex, family, control, reputation—with no room left. Trying harder to take things out does not work; they find their way back if nothing replaces them.
      • Drop in a rock labeled the Word: sanctify them by the truth; your word is truth. Then fellowship, generosity, prayer, worship. No one told the world to leave. You do not become less worldly by subtraction. You become less worldly by saturation. Fill your life with Christ and the world cannot find a place to fit.

    2. 29:17 Whoever believes

      Scripture: Matthew 5:6 John 3:16 John 6:37

      • If there is no hunger for righteousness, no thirst to look more like Jesus, no growing discomfort with the world’s empty promises, you do not believe in the way Scripture describes belief. God defines belief through his Word.
      • John 3:16 does not only say God loved the world; it tells who receives that love: whoever believes. Whoever is wide open, but it is not careless universalism. It includes anyone who believes and excludes anyone who refuses.
      • You can wreck your life with sin; if you believe in Jesus, you’re in. You can be moral, polished, churchgoing; if you will not trust Christ, you’re out. The line is faith in Christ.
      • Whoever covers all kinds of people and various levels of faith—strong, weak, mustard-seed small. Jesus says he sees it and it is enough; together we can move mountains. Come, and he will never cast you out. That is sovereign saving hesed. Are you in the whoever? The promise stays open until the trumpet blows.

    3. 32:00 Shall not perish

      Scripture: John 3:16 John 10:28

      • Perish means destruction, total loss, no hope, no peace, no light—darkness. God promises believers will not perish. If they could be lost, the promise would be broken.
      • Jesus gives eternal life; they shall never perish; no one will snatch them out of his hand. Josh argues that if you return this gift you never really opened it. No one in their right mind returns a gift of this magnitude.

  5. 32:31 Love that endures to glory

    Scripture: Romans 8:11-14

    • Seventh: God’s love endures to glory. It is not only saving you from something; it is bringing you into something.
    • Paul in Romans 8: if the Spirit who raised Jesus lives in you, he will also give life to your mortal bodies. We have an obligation not to the sinful nature. If you live according to it you will die; if by the Spirit you put to death the misdeeds of the body you will live, for those led by the Spirit of God are the children of God.

    1. 33:46 The war proves God is in you

      Scripture: Romans 7:24

      • John 3:16 says God loved you enough to give his Son; Romans 8 says he loves you enough to put his Spirit in you. The question is not only do you believe that, but is there life in you that proves it. When the Spirit moves in, a war breaks out.
      • Spurgeon said the first week he came to Christ the new life had to fight for its existence. Then he heard a sermon from Romans 7—O wretched man that I am—where the preacher said Paul was not a Christian when he cried that. Josh calls that bogus. Apart from grace Paul would still be chucking rocks. The Spirit is moving in Romans 7.
      • If someone says struggle means you are not a Christian, tell them that is how you know you are. We used to follow sin; now there is tension, conviction, a war. Dead people do not fight. The love of God does not produce perfect people; it produces new people who fight, repent, and get back up.
      • You know this love is yours not because life is flawless, but because your heart is no longer at peace with sin. The cross proves God is for you; the war in you proves God is in you. Grab the armor of God and sprint in with songs of praise.

  6. 37:48 John 4: he demonstrates the love

    Scripture: John 4:6-7 John 4:16-18

    • In John 3 John explains God’s love; in John 4 Jesus demonstrates it. Noon, heat, a woman at the well alone—not because she prefers it. The town knows her story: five husbands, living with the sixth. She comes at noon to avoid the whispers.
    • Someone is there. Jesus is sitting at the well. It did not just happen.

    1. 38:53 He did not go around

      Scripture: Genesis 29:9-11 John 4:12

      • Days earlier Jesus began north from Judea to Galilee. The direct route was through Samaria; Jews went around. Jesus went straight through, not lost, not convenient—pursuing this woman, crossing every boundary.
      • The scene has happened before. Jacob meets Rachel at a well in Genesis 29, sees her, loves her. In John 4 the greater Jacob sits at a well again. Rachel was beautiful, honored, desired. This woman comes ashamed, rejected, discarded.
      • She asks if he is greater than their father Jacob. Josh hears the real question: Jacob loved Rachel because she was beautiful; would he ever love a woman like me? That is our question: can God love someone like me?

    2. 40:51 Living water that cost him thirst

      Scripture: John 4:10 John 19:28

      • Jesus answers by being there. He engages her, exposes her, talks to her, and offers living water. She did not only have a sin problem; she had a thirst problem. Every place she had gone left her empty.
      • If you knew who you were speaking to, you would ask, and I would give you living water. He knows every failure and stays. Not once, Come back when you have cleaned up. He moves toward her in the mess.
      • When he offers living water he knows the cost. Later, in John 19 on the cross, Jesus says, I thirst. The one who offered living water became thirsty so a woman like her could drink and never thirst again.
      • Yes, he is greater than Jacob. Jacob loved the beautiful; Jesus loves the broken. Jacob was drawn to beauty; Jesus creates beauty. You are not too far gone. Run to Jesus and ask for a drink.

    3. 43:28 She ran back to the village that rejected her

      Scripture: John 4:39-42 Revelation 19:9

      • For those who have drunk: notice how quickly bitterness fades. She ran back to the village that rejected her because Jesus accepted her.
      • Many Samaritans believed because of her testimony: he told me everything I did. They urged him to stay two days, and many more believed, saying they had heard for themselves that this man really is the Savior of the world.
      • If you believe that, leave your past and set your eyes on the wedding supper of the Lamb. Soon faith will give way to sight, and whoever believes will sit at the King’s table. Until then, we wait.

    4. 44:55 Closing prayer

      Scripture: John 3:16

      • Josh thanks the Father that through the Spirit a single verse about love can hold the full counsel and still not be contained in a lifetime.
      • Thank you for loving me where I was and into a relationship that looks like you, so others can be blessed through that love and the church can be an extension of it.
      • He asks for an opportunity over the next two weeks to love someone to beauty the way Jesus did—family or stranger—and that those bold enough to pray it would come share testimony. Amen.
