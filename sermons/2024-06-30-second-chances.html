Overview

  1. 00:15 Joshua 5–8: a roller coaster of success and failure

    Scripture: Joshua 8 Joshua 5–8

    • Today the church is in Joshua 8. Surveying the book from 30,000 feet, chapters 5–8 seem to be an intentional section: within the conquest of Canaan is a mini roller-coaster story of success and failure.
    • Jericho fell first, an incredible victory. Next to taste failure were the Israelites themselves, who had to deal with sin in their camp. In chapter 8 Joshua has dealt with that failure, the sin of Achan has been judged, and it is time to move forward to another victory by conquering Ai.

  2. 01:14 The best Christian reclaims mistakes

    • Before getting into chapter 8, Josh doesn’t want the church to miss a crucial lesson: the best Christian isn’t the one who makes the fewest mistakes. The Christian who stands out from the pack wins victories by reclaiming mistakes.
    • Highly effective Christians have really bad memories. They move on from defeat quickly; they live, learn, walk in grace, and leverage those lessons into victories for God. They share an attitude with entrepreneurs: Henry Ford defined a mistake as an opportunity to begin again more intelligently.
    • Josh thinks Joshua had a similar mindset. His approach improves from chapter 7 to chapter 8, and he has learned a couple of lessons the church should take note of.

  3. 02:20 Lesson one: don’t hide your sin, confess your sin

    • It seems like a no-brainer, especially among Christians, but it doesn’t seem to be. When God reveals you’re dealing with sin, don’t ignore the prompt of his Spirit convicting you.
    • One of the most devastating realities of hidden sin in the church: if you sit under faithful preaching week in and week out and have the Spirit of God in you, then to remain in sin you have to become skilled at ignoring the conviction God inevitably lays on your heart as you hear his words declared.

    1. 03:10 Grieving another fallen pastor

      • After church last week Josh talked with a man about their brokenheartedness over yet another well-known pastor falling. Josh has struggled most with the situation surrounding Tony Evans, whose teaching ministry had been like a pastor to him and helped him grow in his faith.
      • The man said it was hard to tune in to their sermons now, which Josh understood, but added, “I’m just glad that I can still enjoy some of their old stuff.” Josh challenged him. Nobody can take away the change it had in him, but if a pastor is living in sin and also disciplined in the word, that man has to reject God’s efforts to reveal that sin almost weekly. Instead of confessing to the elders so they can pray over him for healing and restoration, he continues in sin.

    2. 05:02 Tuesday nights

      • Josh confesses that the hardest day in his home by far is Tuesday, when he writes his sermons. On Tuesday nights he wrestles with conviction brought on by the word and the Spirit, and tells his wife they need to change things, confess things, get rid of things, and do more with the kids. He adjusts his life, marriage, home, and parenting based on what he is convicted by.
      • So for a pastor to be in hidden sin for decades, he has to become skilled at the opposite, convinced he preaches solely to convict others. In reality we should preach and teach after wrestling with God, not after dodging him.

    3. 06:23 Conceal or confess

      Scripture: Proverbs 28:13 Proverbs 28:14

      • If Joshua were listening to Solomon, Josh is sure he’d shout some amens at Proverbs 28: those who conceal their sins do not prosper, but those who confess and renounce them find mercy. The next verse fits too: blessed are those who always tremble before God, but those who harden their hearts fall into trouble.
      • Josh is convinced hidden sin in the life of a Christian is the result of suppressed conviction. The Spirit convicts us of sin, but conviction doesn’t always lead to confession or to repentance.

    4. 07:10 Why we need each other

      Scripture: James 5:16 Ephesians 4:25 1 John 1:6–7

      • That is one of the many reasons we need each other. James 5 says to confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed. He isn’t talking about praying for a brother’s wrist he hurt playing pickleball; he means healing from sin, from temptation, from the tendency to lean into sin.
      • One of the great lies in the church today is that confession is entirely a private act between you and God. It begins there, but we are one body: how we live impacts the body, and where we suffer or rejoice, others do too. Paul says in Ephesians to put off falsehood and speak truthfully to your neighbor, for we are all members of one body. How can we speak truth in love if we aren’t even honest with one another about our sins? Paul says, do not lie to each other.
      • Not everything you confess to God needs to be spilled out in life group prayer time. But if the believers around you never know your struggles, how can you walk humbly with God, and how can they learn to love and show mercy? Secret sin kills the effectiveness of the body of Christ; his church suffers when we suffer alone. First John says that if we walk in the light as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another and the blood of Jesus purifies us from all sin.

    5. 09:15 Find one person

      • There is a difference between someone else shining a light on your walk and you shining a light on your walk. It takes tremendous faith to say, “This is my battle, my weakness, my blind spot. If Satan is coming after me, this is where. Would you just check on me from time to time? There’s nothing for you to do; pray for me, but I need you to be aware.”
      • This isn’t for everyone to know. Just find one person in the church you can trust enough to do this with. If there is no believer you can walk in the light with and you are meandering in the darkness of your sin on your own, what a terrible place for a believer to be. The body of Christ helps you fight your sin and empowers you to live lives that bring glory to God. Don’t hide your sin: confess it to God and to your brothers and sisters in Christ.

    6. 10:47 A word of protection

      • As a protection: if you call this church home, confess your sin to someone, and they don’t seek to restore you with a spirit of gentleness, let Josh know. He will take care of it and deal with them.

  4. 11:01 Lesson two: when you fail, run to God

    • Not if you fail, but when you fail, don’t run from God; run to God. Again it seems like a no-brainer, but it isn’t. When we repent and receive forgiveness, the process of walking in the light and fighting sin should empower and grow us, not crush us.
    • There are no diminishing returns on God’s grace, no account of forgiveness that runs out at zero. Remember the song How He Loves: if his grace is an ocean, we’re all sinking. It is by grace you’ve been saved and by grace you continue; you run to God’s mercy and keep running to it. Maybe you figure things out the third time, the fourth time, or the tenth. Keep working out your faith and don’t give up.
    • The all-knowing, holy, passionate God, abounding in love and faithfulness, with mercy in his eyes, knows that a fallen creation that needed a second chance might need a third. If you’re a true believer, the only way you lose is if you give up. You have to surrender to lose.

    1. 12:54 “I was sober for 10 months and I relapsed”

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 13:5

      • Someone says, “Josh, you don’t understand. I was sober for 10 months and I relapsed.” You’re still here, aren’t you? Praise God; let’s turn this thing around. You’re discounting 10 months of sobriety because of one moment of failure, as though you lost all that good work, because your sponsor won’t give you a medal.
      • God isn’t tracking how many times you trip up. Love keeps no record of wrong. He is rejoicing every time you call on him for help, hit your knees, and trust him. Keep running to him.

    2. 13:45 When shame sets in

      • It breaks Josh’s heart how many believers fall into sin and, because it’s the second or third time, act like the ocean of God’s grace has dried up. Shame sets in and they feel overwhelmed. Don’t let it; that is Satan’s lie. You cannot be overwhelmed and be in the grace of God at the same time. God forgives wickedness, rebellion, and sin; keep calling on the name of Jesus.
      • The only one who cares how many times you fall is you, and if a lot of people in your life keep track, you probably need different people. The lessons you learn from defeat can help you succeed the next time temptation comes. Mount up and fight back: that is the power of God in you. You have everything you need; just don’t give up.

  5. 14:59 Verse 1: “Do not be afraid”

    Scripture: Joshua 8:1 Joshua 1:3

    • Josh turns to verse 1 to watch God help Joshua turn things around: “Do not be afraid; do not be discouraged.” “Thou shalt not” is not the most common command in Scripture. It’s hard to give an exact number across languages and authors, but alongside peace be with you, praise the Lord, rejoice, and give thanks, “fear not, do not be afraid” is given approximately 120 times.
    • What does God want when he commands us not to fear? Josh thinks it’s simple: he wants us to recall and meditate on his promises. Joshua has just dealt with Achan and his disobedient family, comforted 36 families who lost loved ones in the first attack at Ai, and watched the conquest stall in complete failure. God says, remember my promise.
    • Which promise? Probably all of them. Josh jokes that he is Joshua, but not this Joshua. If he were, he would never forget the promise in chapter 1: “I will give you every place where you set your foot, just as I promised Moses.” You did it for Moses, and you’re going to use me.

    1. 17:04 Where the leader goes, God gives victory

      Scripture: Joshua 7 Joshua 8:1–3 2 Chronicles 16:9 John 14:21

      • Surveying the wins and losses in Canaan, Josh keeps coming back to that promise. Joshua seems to have been present for the march around Jericho, and they won. In chapter 7 Joshua sent men to Ai, and while Josh can’t be certain, it doesn’t read like Joshua went with them, and they lost.
      • In chapter 8 the Lord tells Joshua to take the whole army and attack Ai, for he has delivered its king, people, city, and land into his hands; this time they may carry off the plunder and livestock. Yahweh is faithful to his promise to Moses and to Joshua: where his leader goes, God gives victory.
      • This drives home that obedience matters to God. 2 Chronicles says the eyes of the Lord range throughout the earth to strengthen those whose hearts are fully committed to him. Your strength isn’t something you muster up; God gives it on the basis of your commitment. That hasn’t changed: the night before he gave his life, Jesus told his disciples, “Whoever has my commands and keeps them is the one who loves me.” These are the ones who can have confidence in his promises.

  6. 19:07 Build your faith on God’s power, not human wisdom

    Scripture: James 1:5

    • For the first attack Joshua took instructions from the spies, who said they didn’t need many men, and it failed miserably. He learned his lesson: this time he awaited instructions from the Lord.
    • Learn from Joshua’s mistake. Don’t build your faith on human wisdom; build it on God’s power. You have the very words of God available to you: read of your Savior, become a scholar of his promises, seek him morning and night and in everything you do, and watch your faith grow.
    • If you lack wisdom, don’t ask your coworker or your father-in-law, don’t Google it, don’t YouTube Dr. Phil; ChatGPT and AI can’t help you here. James says if any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault.

    1. 20:23 When someone asks you for advice

      • Too many believers would rather have the first advice than God’s advice. When a brother or sister in Christ asks you for advice, keep your wisdom to yourself for a second. First ask whether they’ve spent time in prayer, then whether they’ve spent time in the word and searched the Scriptures; if not, encourage them to start there.
      • Then take your own advice: pray and intercede for them, and search the Scriptures for them so your counsel is biblical and the advice God would give. Where you can’t get there, encourage them to walk in faith.

  7. 21:26 Patience with God’s timing

    • This time God gave the soldiers the right to claim the spoils; they had to burn the city but could keep the loot. How ridiculous does Achan look now? If he had waited a few days he could have picked out a nice robe and displayed his silver on his nightstand instead of burying it, and now he is under some rocks somewhere in Canaan. Josh thinks of the seagulls in Finding Nemo: “Mine, mine.”
    • Wealth isn’t how much you have; it’s how content you are with what he has given you. Many struggle with what is called serpent theology, after the tempter in the garden who emphasized the one restriction God imposed rather than the riches he had lavished: one tree of “thou shalt not,” and no attention to all the other trees of “enjoy me.”
    • The key to a faithful Christian life is recognizing that God is generous. He isn’t trying to bankrupt you; he is trying to lavish you with generosity, love, and his provision, his way. Discontent people are never satisfied with more, so don’t be ruled by what you desire; be joyful in what he offers you.

  8. 23:12 The ambush: God’s promises received in faith

    Scripture: Joshua 8:3–9 Hebrews 4:2 John 5:39–40

    • Next come Yahweh’s instructions for the ambush, God tapping into his inner Call of Duty strategy mode. Josh reads how Joshua sends 30,000 of his best fighting men out at night to lie in ambush behind the city while he and the rest draw the men of Ai out and flee, so the ambush can rise up and take the city. Josh loves that Joshua adds, “Do what the Lord has commanded”: last time they didn’t, and this time when the Lord says burn it, that means burn it.
    • Time and again, unless God’s promises are received in faith, nothing is accomplished. God made Abraham a massive promise, but nothing moved ahead unless Abraham went. Joseph knew God’s promises; Moses claimed them by faith, and so did his successor Joshua. The first attack failed because Joshua and Israel got happy feet and ran ahead of God, operating by human wisdom after hearing the spies.
    • Nothing has changed: even the gospel is of no value if it isn’t received by faith. Hebrews 4 says the good news was proclaimed to us just as to them, but the message was of no value to those who did not share the faith of those who obeyed. Hearing isn’t enough; we must run to God’s promises in faith, becoming doers and not just hearers.
    • In John 5 Jesus tells the Pharisees that they study the Scriptures diligently because they think in them they have eternal life, yet these Scriptures testify about him and they refuse to come to him to have life. It isn’t about what you know or what you do; it’s about who you trust. Joshua learned you can’t walk by faith out in front of God’s promises: you listen, you believe and take him at his word, and then the work begins.

  9. 26:38 Make time every day with the Lord

    • Josh encourages everyone to make time every day with the Lord. He has no hidden cameras in their houses, but if they aren’t doing it, it’s never too late to start. Dust off the Bible, put it where you see it every morning, and spend time with him; it doesn’t have to be an hour and a half. Read a chapter, pray over it, and be on your way.
    • A Christian without the sword of the Spirit, which is the word, is like a gun without bullets, hot sauce without peppers, or a Tesla without a battery.
    • But no matter how unbalanced our priorities, how bad our habits, how undisciplined we are, or how horribly we have failed, we can always get up and start over again. Our God is a God of new beginnings, a God of second chances. Don’t give up.

  10. 27:59 Evaluate every opportunity on its own

    Scripture: Joshua 8:10–13

    • Josh reads how Joshua musters the army early the next morning, sets camp north of Ai with the valley between them, sets about 5,000 men in ambush to the west, and goes into the valley that night.

    1. 28:33 God enjoys variety

      • We should evaluate every opportunity on its own. It is easy to say God gave us Jericho, so let’s keep rolling with what’s working. But chapter 8 shows God seems to enjoy variety: from a daytime trumpet-blast approach to a midnight sneak attack with an ambush.
      • It cracks Josh up, because he can’t think of a group that loves tradition more than Christians. Not all tradition is bad, and he isn’t anti-tradition, but to those who want to face off in the lobby over it he says: what if God wants to do something new, wants a little variety, or decides to adopt a new strategy? Who are you to say it is or isn’t from him?
      • So watch your tongue about different ministry strategies, like churches that pop up in nightclubs or bars, or the XXX church lifting people out of the porn industry and setting them on fire for Christ. Just because that isn’t how you do it doesn’t mean God can’t use it for his glory. Bruce Barton said, “When you’re through changing, you’re through.” Don’t get stuck in your ways; seek his ways, and don’t assume what worked back then will work again.

  11. 30:47 God rewrites our mistakes into victory

    Scripture: Joshua 8:14–17

    • The king of Ai hurries out to meet Israel in battle, not knowing an ambush is set behind the city. Joshua and all Israel let themselves be driven back toward the wilderness, and every man of Ai and Bethel pursues them, leaving the city open.
    • This is what God does with his adopted sons and daughters: he takes your mistakes and rewrites them to bring about victory, and he is really good at it. So it shouldn’t surprise us when our greatest failure is also the key to our greatest success.

    1. 31:44 What Josh is most proud of in his ministry

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:17

      • What Josh is most proud of isn’t his sermons. Preaching is a big part of the job and takes most of his hours, but he is nothing special; the only reason he has anything worth saying is that God said it first. If great preaching is all you care about, you will find a better communicator elsewhere. Josh will take an average communicator with integrity over a charismatic one without it, all day.
      • What he boasts in is that God took some of the most painful experiences of his life and uses them almost every week to help somebody avoid that pain. His humiliation has become God’s victory: he went from addict to someone God uses to help others out of addiction, and from a child of divorce to helping others fight for their marriages. The things he was most angry at God for allowing are the things he is most grateful for today.
      • God rewrites stories. We have been washed by the blood of Jesus so we can have a clean slate and he can write a new story: the old has passed away, the new has come. If you came in beaten down, tired, and in failure, don’t get hung up on the failures of chapter 7. Wait on the Lord, stay faithful, and in time he will show you what chapter 8 has for you.

  12. 34:12 The javelin held out

    Scripture: Joshua 8:18–22 Exodus 17

    • The Lord tells Joshua to hold out his javelin toward Ai. When he does, the ambush rises, takes the city, and sets it on fire; the men of Ai see the smoke with no chance to escape, Israel turns back on its pursuers, and the men of Ai are caught in the middle with Israelites on both sides.
    • Joshua holding up his javelin until victory is won recalls the battle Joshua fought against Amalek in Exodus 17, except that time Moses held up his hands to the Lord. When Christian leaders are faithful, God gets all the glory, not man.

  13. 35:33 A difficult text: judgment, not the killing of innocents

    Scripture: Joshua 8:23–26

    • They take the king of Ai alive, and 12,000 men and women fall that day, for Joshua did not draw back the hand holding the javelin until all who lived in Ai were destroyed.
    • Josh understands this is a difficult text, and their study through Joshua has no shortage of those, but please don’t mistake it for the killing of innocent people. For a picture of that, look at the estimated 1 billion aborted babies in 2023. Everyone wants to read this as God leading an army of barbarians, but Joshua’s conquest is PG compared to the mass slaughter of babies in this country today: 2,500 abortions daily in the US, about 106 during this service, one every 35 seconds. That’s barbaric.
    • These people were under God’s judgment. They had their chance and refused it, and the Canaanites were sentenced by the fairest judge in all the land. Don’t blame Israel and Joshua; they were carrying out the sentence.

  14. 37:15 Don’t look away from the king on the pole

    Scripture: Joshua 8:27–29 Deuteronomy 21:22–23

    • Israel carries off the livestock and plunder as the Lord instructed, and Joshua burns Ai into a permanent heap of ruins. He impales the body of the king of Ai on a pole until sunset, then has it thrown down at the city gate under a large pile of rocks that remains to this day.
    • Did your parents ever cover your eyes during a gruesome part of a movie? Josh understands protecting young eyes from what they can’t process, but every believer needs to see the king of Ai on a pole. Don’t look away.
    • Deuteronomy 21 says the body of anyone put to death for a capital offense and exposed on a pole must be buried the same day, because anyone hung on a pole is under God’s curse. The body wasn’t under God’s curse because it was put on a pole; it was put on a pole because it was under God’s curse. It is a warning we should open our eyes to see; without visuals like this, would we really fear sin and its consequences?

    1. 39:00 “Just an Old Testament thing”?

      Scripture: Galatians 3:13–14

      • Many Christians today think God’s judgment is a thing of the past, “just an Old Testament thing.” This was 1400 BC; what does it have to do with me?
      • In an effort to call you to faith, God gave another picture of judgment. A little over 1,400 years after the king of Ai hung on a tree in Canaan, the King of the Jews hung on a tree in Jerusalem. Galatians 3 says Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us, for it is written, cursed is everyone who is hung on a pole, so that the blessing given to Abraham might come to the Gentiles through Christ Jesus and by faith we might receive the promise of the Spirit.

  15. 39:58 From war to worship at Shechem

    Scripture: Genesis 12:6–7 Joshua 8

    • Josh’s family called a too-quick change of subject “topical whiplash,” complete with “squirrel!” That is what the last verses of chapter 8 feel like: from war to worship real quick. To the reader it’s quick, but to the Israelites it was a 30-mile journey north to Shechem, where Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim are, about 40 miles north of Jerusalem.
    • The location matters because it was in this valley that Abram first received the promise of the land. Genesis 12 says Abram traveled as far as the great tree of Moreh at Shechem while the Canaanites were in the land, and the Lord appeared to him and said, “To your offspring I will give this land,” so he built an altar there.
    • Now in Joshua 8, Abram’s offspring return to the place of the promise as they experience its fulfillment: proof that God is faithful and his word never fails. Time may pass and generations come and go, but Yahweh has never failed, and he isn’t about to start now.

    1. 41:49 Reading the law in the land of promise

      Scripture: Joshua 8:34–35 Matthew 6:33

      • To celebrate, the people gather just as Moses commanded, and Joshua reads all the words of the law, the blessings and the curses; there was not a word Moses had commanded that he did not read to the whole assembly, including women, children, and foreigners. They received the law at Sinai and Moses explained it just outside Canaan, blessing for obedience and curse for disobedience; now Joshua leads them to renew their commitment in the land of promise. If the past is any guide, they’d be shouting “Amen” as each blessing and curse was read.
      • Josh loves that while armies are developing in the west to retaliate, this young leader pauses to read and remind the people of their commitment to obey their Lord. When you rest in God’s promises, you don’t lose sleep worrying about the enemy. Josh is reminded of Jesus’ words, “Seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well.” That is what Joshua is doing at Shechem.

  16. 43:38 Not under the curse, but the law still matters

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:3 Romans 8:3–4

    • Believers today aren’t living under the curse of the law, because Jesus bore that curse on a tree. In Christ, believers are blessed with every spiritual blessing; Josh could spend his entire life trying to understand the magnitude of that, all because of the grace of God.
    • Don’t take that to mean that because we’re under grace, the law of God no longer matters. It does. We aren’t saved by keeping the law; it reveals our sinfulness but cannot save us. Romans 8 says what the law was powerless to do, weakened by the sinful nature, God did by sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful humanity to be a sin offering, so that the righteous requirement of the law might be fully met in us who live according to the Spirit.

  17. 44:43 “It is finished”

    Scripture: John 19:30 1 Corinthians 15:57

    • “It is finished” were the last words Jesus spoke on the cross before he died. The Greek word, tetelestai, had several uses: on ancient business receipts to show a debt paid in full, as a legal term releasing someone from a court sentence, and on the battlefield to declare victory.
    • So when Jesus gave his life as the sin offering, he declared that your debt of sin is fully paid, the sentence for your crime is satisfied, and the spiritual war against death, sin, and Satan has been won. Believer, what more is there to say but thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.

  18. 45:53 Closing prayer

    Scripture: 1 John 1:9

    • Josh prays for those in the room struggling to have hope in a difficult time, drowning in shame and beating themselves up over past mistakes they keep hanging on to, whether because of the pain it caused loved ones or because they feel they deserve not to be released. He wants to lead them to God’s throne, because God says that if we confess our sins he will forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness.
    • When we fall into sin it feels like we’ve ruined everything and shame sets in quickly, but into that shame he speaks the name of Jesus, whose blood cleanses us. He prays for a cleansing in the room and that everyone would embrace their second chance, which may lead to more chances. We will not achieve a sinless state as Jesus did, but we can walk in the Spirit with confidence if we love him and commit our lives to him.
