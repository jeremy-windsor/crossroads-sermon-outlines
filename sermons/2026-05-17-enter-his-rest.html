Overview

  1. 00:39 Fix your thoughts on Jesus, the believer’s confidence and calling

    • Josh opens the series’ most ambitious week with one warning: do not harden the heart and miss the rest only Christ gives. Hebrews addresses people who need both a message from God and a way to God, and Jesus supplies both.
    • Because even people who have heard God’s message can harden themselves, Josh wants every hearer to know whether he or she belongs to Christ; he also holds out hope that a crusted heart can be softened again.

    1. 00:39 The warning that governs the message

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:7-11

      • The warning ‘Do not harden your heart’ anticipates the Spirit’s repeated word later in Hebrews 3 and frames the whole sermon around entering Christ’s rest today.
      • Josh presents Jesus’ revelation as God’s word to humanity and Jesus’ reconciling work as the opened way home to God.

    2. 02:08 Holy brothers and sisters are called to consider Jesus

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:1 Hebrews 2:18

      • Beginning Hebrews 3:1, Josh pauses over ‘therefore’ and reaches back to the prior week: Jesus suffered temptation and is able to help the tempted, so believers should ask for that help more often.
      • Hebrews dignifies Christians as holy brothers and sisters who share a heavenly calling and commands them to fix their thoughts on Jesus, acknowledged together as apostle and high priest.
      • Josh contrasts the beautiful names Scripture gives believers with the names people call one another and says the call from heaven is not about self-improvement but shows the way home.

    3. 03:35 The heavenly calling directs pilgrims toward God’s city

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:10 Hebrews 11:13

      • While the world waits for another leader, awakening, or revival, Christians ultimately look for the city whose builder is God.
      • Present good can be celebrated, but Josh says believers remain strangers and pilgrims until Jesus returns and they are finally home. Their hope rests not on their record but on Christ.

    4. 04:49 Christian confidence grows by seeing Christ clearly

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:1

      • Every kind of sinner is present in the church, with some sins public and others known only to the sinner and God. If confidence before God rested on performance, everyone would leave hopeless.
      • Hebrews therefore says to consider Jesus: think, study, sing, teach, and treasure him. Christianity is not ultimately trying harder but seeing his perfection, mercy, sufficiency, lordship, and kingship more clearly.
      • Josh defines preaching, worship, discipleship, and Christian conversation as means of magnifying Jesus rather than offering self-help or ordinary small talk. The command is addressed to believers, showing that Christians can fail to think about Christ.

    5. 06:05 Grace sustains believers by dependence, not graduation

      Scripture: Galatians 3:2-3 Philippians 1:6

      • Christians are sustained the way they were saved: by grace. Strength is not independence but deeper dependence, because no Christian outgrows grace.
      • Paul’s challenge to the Galatians exposes the folly of beginning by the Spirit and attempting to finish by human effort. Josh joins that with the promise that the one who began the good work will complete it.
      • The believer’s role is not to complete Christ’s work but to consider Christ—to become, in Josh’s phrase, a ‘professional Jesus thinker.’

    6. 07:07 Jesus is God’s sent apostle and humanity’s merciful high priest

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:17 Hebrews 3:2 Hebrews 4:14 John 1:1, 14; 14:6

      • Jesus can be considered in his person, work, and offices. As apostle—the one sent—he represents God to humanity; as high priest—the go-between—he represents humanity before God.
      • Josh rereads Hebrews 2:17: Jesus became like his brothers and sisters so he could be a merciful, faithful high priest and make atonement for sins. Hebrews 3:2 then compares his faithfulness with Moses’ faithfulness in God’s house.
      • Moses sometimes acted as prophet and priest, but Aaron held the high-priestly office. Jesus alone is the ‘great high priest’ who is God’s final word and the reconciler sinners need.

    7. 08:55 The church unites in one confession about Jesus

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:13-14 John 14:6

      • Holy brothers and sisters acknowledge the same apostle and high priest. Their backgrounds and testimonies vary, but together they confess who Jesus is, what he has done, and what he will yet do.
      • That shared confession gives confidence that God has spoken through his Son and made peace through his Son’s work. Josh says believers are sealed by the Spirit, who guarantees their inheritance until Christ returns.
      • The confession culminates in Jesus’ claim to be the way, truth, and life: no one comes to the Father except through him.

  2. 10:04 The Son is greater than Moses and God’s people are his house

    • Josh follows Hebrews’ comparison between Moses and Christ into Numbers 12, the identity of God’s house, and the church’s life together. Moses was a faithful servant within the household; Jesus is the Son over it and its divine builder.
    • God’s dwelling is not a structure or institution but his people. That identity offers weary sinners a home in Christ and asks whether the church’s holiness, peace, sacrifice, and love make Jesus welcome in his own house.

    1. 10:04 Hebrews measures Moses’ greatness against Christ’s infinite superiority

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:3-6

      • Josh reads Hebrews 3:3–6: Jesus deserves greater honor than Moses as a builder deserves greater honor than a house; God builds everything; Moses served faithfully in the house, but Christ is the faithful Son over it.
      • The passage ends with both an identity and a condition: believers are Christ’s house if they hold firmly to their confidence and hope. Considering this superiority is meant to strengthen faith and resolve.

    2. 10:51 God publicly defended Moses as his faithful mediator

      Scripture: Numbers 12:1-2 Exodus 14:21-31 Exodus 16:4-15 Exodus 17:1-7 Exodus 13:21-22 Numbers 12:5-8

      • Josh sets Numbers 12 after Israel has watched God part the sea, provide manna and water, and lead the people by fire through the wilderness, yet rebellion continues.
      • Miriam and Aaron’s apparently spiritual complaint against their brother is driven by pride and jealousy. God descends in the cloud and distinguishes Moses from ordinary prophets: Moses is faithful in all God’s house, speaks with God face to face, and sees the Lord’s form.
      • By defending Moses, God establishes the chosen mediator through whom the law will come; Hebrews then points through that mediator to Jesus, God’s final and greater revelation.

    3. 12:26 The servant in the house points to the Son over the house

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:3-6

      • Josh says Hebrews’ comparison shows the new covenant instituted by Jesus superseding the old covenant associated with Moses.
      • Moses remains a vital servant within the household; Jesus stands over it as Son and built it. Since God builds everything and Jesus built God’s house, Josh presents the comparison as an argument for Christ’s deity.
      • He recommends this passage when responding to Jehovah’s Witnesses at the door because the builder logic identifies Jesus as God.

    4. 13:18 God’s presence cannot be contained in human buildings

      Scripture: Isaiah 66:1 Acts 17:24

      • God has always wanted his people to know he is with them, but the temple was a picture rather than a prison or lockbox for his presence.
      • Isaiah’s throne-and-footstool question and Paul’s declaration in Acts 17 both reject the idea that the Creator can be contained by walls or human hands.
      • The law, Moses, and temple prepared the stage for someone greater. Entering a church building is therefore no more able to make a person right with God than putting on a swimsuit can create a model.

    5. 14:18 God’s house is the people whose hearts belong to him

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:6

      • Hebrews answers the question of where Jesus’ house is: ‘We are his house.’ It is built not from brick, steel, drywall, or institutional machinery but from men and women who belong to God.
      • Josh compares this with his own family: home is not merely an Anthem address but Christian, Madison, and Nolan, wherever they happen to be.
      • God’s invitation is to come home, stop pretending, and be known, loved, and forgiven. Rest cannot finally be secured through money, achievement, approval, sex, career, success, or relationships; it is found in Jesus, the dwelling God built for the soul.

    6. 16:13 Would Jesus recognize what the church has done with his home?

      • Josh asks whether Jesus would be pleased or grieved by the church—not its building, but the unity and life of his people. He asks whether peace decorates the house, obedience hangs on its walls, and love, holiness, and sacrifice fill it.
      • Madison’s Mother’s Day gift was mailed to the Wyatt family’s five-years-former address. When she approached that old home, being on the side of the door that knocks felt wrong.
      • The story becomes a church examination: how are believers treating one another and outsiders, loving God, answering their calling, and maintaining their confession? Josh asks those who love Crossroads to pray that it becomes a home that makes Jesus smile.

    7. 18:24 The great ‘if’: Christ’s house holds its confidence to the end

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:6 Hebrews 10:35-36 Ephesians 1:4

      • Josh repeats Hebrews 3:6 and calls its ‘if’ enormous: those who persevere in grace are Christ’s house. Hebrews 10 similarly commands believers not to throw away confidence but to persevere and receive what God promised.
      • He argues that temporary Christianity is not real Christianity. Perseverance is the fruit of God choosing and setting apart his people before the world’s foundation; abandoning hope does not cancel salvation but reveals that the person was never home.
      • Josh identifies the passage as teaching eternal security: true Christians boast in Jesus through the end.

  3. 19:51 Israel’s wilderness rebellion warns the church against unbelief

    • Hebrews applies Psalm 95 to the church: the Spirit speaks through David and makes Israel’s refusal at Canaan a present warning. The people experienced deliverance and daily provision yet hardened themselves until they could not recognize God’s goodness while standing in it.
    • Josh overlays their story on Christian lives. Deliverance should lead into trusting obedience, but believers can linger at the edge of promise, pull back from commands, and practice unbelief until it becomes habitual.

    1. 19:51 The Holy Spirit speaks Psalm 95 as a present warning

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:7-11 Psalm 95:7-11

      • Josh reads Hebrews 3:7–11: today, hear God’s voice without hardening the heart as in the wilderness testing, where a generation saw God’s work for forty years yet went astray and was barred by oath from rest.
      • The passage reproduces Psalm 95. Josh says later Hebrews identifies David as the human author, but here names the Holy Spirit to emphasize Scripture’s divine authorship.

    2. 20:38 Human prophets spoke as the Spirit carried them

      Scripture: 2 Peter 1:21

      • Second Peter explains the relationship between David and the Spirit: prophecy did not originate in human will; human prophets spoke from God as the Holy Spirit carried them.
      • The Spirit-carried David therefore warns God’s chosen people not to harden their hearts.

    3. 21:19 Deliverance from Egypt leads to a command to trust

      Scripture: Numbers 13:25-14:35 Exodus 12:21-32 John 1:29 Exodus 14:21-31 Exodus 16:4-15 Exodus 17:1-7 Numbers 11:31-32

      • Because Jesus is greater than Moses, Josh asks hearers to compare Israel’s poor response to Moses with the greater seriousness of their own stubbornness toward Christ.
      • Israel’s slavery and rescue become a picture of bondage to sin and deliverance through Jesus. As Israel left Egypt through lambs’ blood and God’s power, a believing sinner is freed because Jesus, the Lamb of God, died and rose.
      • God did not intend Israel to remain where it had been delivered. He parted the sea, crushed Egypt’s pursuit, led by cloud and fire, fed the people with bread and birds, and gave water from rock before bringing them to Canaan’s edge.
      • The promise was Canaan and the command was to enter. Ten spies magnified the inhabitants, while Joshua and Caleb trusted God’s power; the generation refused, not because God failed or lacked power, but because it did not believe him.

    4. 22:53 The same promise met faith in Joshua and unbelief in the wilderness

      Scripture: Joshua 1:3 1 Samuel 17:45, 49

      • Josh quotes God’s promise at the beginning of Joshua to give every place Joshua’s foot touches, just as promised to Moses. The promise did not change; the people’s response did.
      • Like Israel between Egypt and Canaan, genuinely delivered and loved believers can still wander because they refuse the next act of obedience—confession, surrender, forgiveness, generosity, kindness, sacrifice, ending a destructive relationship, quitting a habit, or seeking help.
      • When Christians meet a giant, Josh urges the posture of David moving in the Lord’s name rather than paralysis through unbelief.

    5. 24:15 Repeated unbelief hardens people inside God’s continuing provision

      Scripture: Exodus 16:4-15; Deuteronomy 1:27 Matthew 14:13-21; Mark 6:30-44; Luke 9:10-17; John 6:1-15

      • The frightening fact is not a failure of grace: God continued feeding, leading, and providing for complaining Israel. Their hearts instead became unable to recognize goodness while eating heaven’s bread.
      • Josh compares that blindness to someone among the five thousand asking whether Jesus cares immediately after he created enough bread to fill everyone and leave twelve baskets.
      • Unbelief turns grace into grounds for complaint and makes the heart evil and selfish.

  4. 25:43 Daily warning and encouragement resist sin’s quiet hardening

    • Hebrews tells brothers and sisters to guard against an unbelieving heart and encourage one another every day. Josh exposes sin’s disguises, defines warning as loving encouragement, explains final perseverance, and compares gradual apostasy to a pond freezing unnoticed.
    • The warning is not that every struggle, doubt, or battle proves hardness. The danger is unrepentant resistance without conviction or concern; even small grief over sin is evidence that the Spirit is still at work.

    1. 25:43 See to it that no one turns away from the living God

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:12-13

      • Josh reads Hebrews 3:12–13: believers must see that no brother or sister develops a sinful, unbelieving heart and must encourage one another daily while it is called today so sin does not deceive and harden them.
      • He describes the ensuing warning as genuine encouragement—a loving heads-up about a danger people do not naturally see.

    2. 26:06 Sin arrives as a guest in costume, not an announced executioner

      • Sin does not introduce itself as the force that will steal joy, wreck marriage, destroy peace, harden the heart, and leave spiritual bankruptcy. If it advertised destruction, people would refuse it.
      • Instead it dresses as freedom, relief, comfort, control, deserved reward, secrecy, manageable compromise, or a one-time choice. It advertises pleasure while hiding the hook and sells freedom while building chains.
      • Sin asks only to be a guest. Once welcomed and fed, it becomes a master.

    3. 28:00 Encouragement tells the truth a sick person needs

      • Josh asks whether a person wants a doctor who names cancer or one who gives a false clean bill of health. Christian encouragement is not whatever a listener wants to hear but what the listener needs.
      • He connects that standard to Crossroads’ Sunday attendance: Easter shows the church can reach more than the roughly four hundred adults it averages, but some people leave offended when they should drive home grateful.
      • He says Crossroads will continue to teach God’s word passionately and unapologetically while it is called today.

    4. 28:51 Sharing in Christ is evidenced by holding the original conviction

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:14-15

      • Hebrews 3:14–15 repeats both the condition and warning: those who share in Christ hold their original conviction firmly to the end and do not harden themselves when they hear God’s voice.
      • Josh interprets permanent departure not as salvation being lost but as evidence that salvation was never possessed, while acknowledging that people cannot readily identify whether someone has truly abandoned Christ permanently. He names Crossroads’ position as the final perseverance of the saints: God keeps true believers, genuine faith persists and bears fruit, and even believers who stumble badly are preserved to the end.

    5. 30:38 God preserves his people through the warnings themselves

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:12-15

      • One means by which God keeps his people is warning them. Hebrews’ commands prevent true believers from remaining at the fringe of rest while sin continues its hardening work.
      • Hardness rarely arrives overnight. Josh’s frozen-pond picture begins with nearly invisible shoreline ice, progresses through a thin surface, and eventually becomes strong enough for a hockey player; spiritual coldness develops in the same quiet increments.

    6. 31:58 The first layer of ice is one thing valued above Jesus

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:15 Hebrews 3:1

      • Apostasy usually begins not with a dramatic farewell to Jesus but with one small thing becoming more important than him. Prayer is neglected; compromise, hidden sin, bitterness, unforgiveness, and ignored conviction accumulate; worship becomes mechanical and Christ’s riches seem ordinary.
      • Josh repeats, ‘If you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts.’ The danger signs are ongoing resistance without repentance, sin that no longer grieves, obedience treated as optional, and drift that causes no concern.
      • Struggle, temptation, and occasional doubt are not themselves proof of a hard heart. The desire to consider Christ and any real trouble over sin testify that the Spirit is at work: hard hearts do not fear becoming hard, but soft hearts do.

  5. 34:10 The wilderness verdict exposes unbelief and commends minority faithfulness

    • Hebrews closes chapter 3 by naming unbelief as the reason the exodus generation could not enter. Josh intensifies responsibility for people who repeatedly hear the gospel, then encourages faithful Christians not to let the surrounding majority’s unbelief reproduce itself in them.
    • Joshua and Caleb were not sinless, but they were willing to move forward because they knew God was greater than the opposition. God notices and leads even a very small faithful remnant.

    1. 34:10 Those who heard rebelled and could not enter because of unbelief

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:16-19

      • Josh reads Hebrews 3:16–19 as a chain of questions: Moses led the people out, yet those who sinned died in the wilderness, and the disobedient were barred from rest.
      • The concluding diagnosis is unbelief. God’s power and promise were present, but the people did not enter.

    2. 34:34 Greater light makes continued sin more serious, not less

      • Josh recalls hearing a Christian argue that a believer’s sin is somehow less serious. He answers that sin after knowing better and hearing the gospel repeatedly is worse, not better.
      • The application confronts hearers who receive God’s word week after week yet continue unchanged.

    3. 35:42 Faithful people must not borrow the crowd’s unbelief

      Scripture: Numbers 14:6-10, 30, 38

      • Josh encourages believers not to lose heart when unfaithfulness surrounds them or allow other people’s sin to awaken sin in themselves. Even if only two among millions are faithful, God knows, sees, records, and leads them.
      • Joshua and Caleb still sinned, but God spared them because they were prepared to advance by faith. They lacked every answer but knew God was bigger than the giants.
      • The direct command is: do not let unbelief around you create unbelief in you.

  6. 36:40 Creation, Canaan, and salvation point to the rest still open today

    • Josh turns to Hebrews 4 and bookends a section he cannot read in full. The author layers God’s seventh-day rest and Israel’s Canaan rest over the spiritual reality offered to believers.
    • Those earlier rests point to salvation rest now and its greater completion when Christ returns. The proper effort is not self-saving work but strenuous reliance on what Jesus has freely provided by grace.

    1. 36:40 The promise of entering God’s rest still stands

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:1

      • Hebrews 4:1 says the promise remains and therefore calls the church to take care that no one falls short.
      • Josh uses that still-open promise to begin the sermon’s closing movement.

    2. 36:58 A Sabbath rest remains for God’s people

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:9-10

      • Josh reads Hebrews 4:9–10: a Sabbath rest remains, and those entering God’s rest cease from their own work as God ceased from his.
      • He receives the sentence as good news and thanks God aloud for the rest.

    3. 37:27 Two Old Testament rests become pictures of spiritual reality

      Scripture: Genesis 2:2 Romans 8:17

      • The first picture is God’s creation rest on the seventh day; the second is Israel’s rest in Canaan.
      • Sabbath rest pictures salvation in Christ, where believers rest from their work as God rested from creation. Canaan rest connects with inheritance and the identity of believers as co-heirs with Christ.
      • Josh notes that the term translated ‘Sabbath rest’ occurs only here in the New Testament.
      • Josh summarizes the sequence as creation rest for God, Canaan rest for Israel, salvation rest for God’s people now, and fuller rest when the Lord returns.

    4. 38:23 Joshua could enter land, but only Jesus brings sinners into true rest

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:8

      • Hebrews 4:8 says that if Joshua had supplied the final rest, God would not later have spoken of another day. Canaan was real but not ultimate.
      • Salvation rest remains open. Joshua could lead Israel into land; Jesus can lead sinners into true rest. Josh notes that ‘Jesus’ is the Greek form of ‘Joshua,’ the same name.

    5. 38:58 Make every effort to enter rather than repeat disobedience

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:11 Hebrews 4:7

      • Josh reads Hebrews 4:11 as a call for total rather than lazy effort so that no one follows Israel’s example and perishes in disobedience.
      • The repeated ‘Today’ comes through David long after Israel occupied Canaan, proving that God still holds out another rest. If it is called today, the offer remains.

    6. 40:05 The labor of rest is believing Christ rather than one’s own work

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:3 Matthew 11:28-29

      • Hebrews 4:3 defines those who enter as those who believe. Josh says the heavenly calling is to stop trusting personal works and rest in Christ’s grace.
      • That dependence is difficult because self-reliance feels easier; Israel’s two faithful spies show how rare it can be to walk into a promise.
      • Jesus’ invitation to the weary and burdened answers the difficulty: come to him, take his yoke, learn his gentle and humble heart, and receive rest for the soul.

  7. 40:59 The heavenly call invites sinners through the door of Christ’s finished rest

    • Josh closes by preaching Christ’s sufficient sacrifice, preserving strength, and exclusive salvation. The weary and ungodly are not disqualified by unworthiness; they are the people for whom Jesus came and died.
    • He calls listeners to enter by faith, repentance, and baptism, then asks that communion mark a remembered entrance into Sabbath rest. His prayer gives up exhausting self-effort, thanks Jesus for Calvary and the covenant in his blood, and asks for daily trust.

    1. 40:59 Christ’s once-for-all sacrifice saves and keeps believers

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:10 Jude 24 John 10:28-29

      • God gave his Son as the once-for-all sacrifice for sinners. Those who believe, rest, lean, depend, and call on him are forgiven, accepted, adopted, redeemed, and saved forever.
      • Their security rests not in their grip on Jesus but in his grip on them. He keeps, sustains, and carries his people until he presents them faultless before the Father with joy.

    2. 41:40 There is no other Savior or rest for the soul

      Scripture: Acts 4:12

      • Josh has shown the door and asks whether the hearer will enter by faith. Refusing Christ leaves nowhere else to go: invented saviors die while Jesus reigns.
      • He quotes the apostolic claim that no other name under heaven is given by which people must be saved.

    3. 42:17 Unworthiness is not a barrier to the Savior of sinners

      Scripture: Matthew 9:13; Mark 2:17; Luke 5:32 Romans 5:6

      • Josh rejects the thought that someone is too broken, sinful, or far gone. Unworthiness did not deter Christ; Josh says it was his motivation.
      • Jesus did not come for the polished, perfect, or prestigious but for sinners and the weary. Christ died for the ungodly.

    4. 42:49 Stop watching the promise from outside and step in

      Scripture: Acts 2:38 Hebrews 4:9

      • Having heard heaven’s call, listeners should come to Christ, trust him, repent, turn from sin, be baptized, and rest forever in his promise.
      • Josh prays that the day’s communion will mark the moment someone finally enters Christ’s Sabbath rest.

    5. 43:18 The communion prayer relinquishes exhausting effort

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:1 Matthew 26:26-28; Luke 22:19-20; 1 Corinthians 11:23-25 Hebrews 8:6, 13

      • Josh begins in amazement at God’s goodness, not wanting to take his eyes off him or stop speaking and hearing about him. He thanks God for new believers’ excitement and admits that longer faith can grow rusty.
      • He confesses the exhaustion of constant pushing and asks to live every day by giving the work up to God and walking into promised rest.
      • Holding the bread and juice, he thanks Jesus for the body torn for believers and blood poured out for forgiveness and for the newer, better, greater covenant in that blood.
      • The prayer closes in love and worship at 45:00.
