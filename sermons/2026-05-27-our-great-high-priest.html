Our Great High Priest

Overview

  1. 00:05 Steve passes the teaching baton to the next generation

    • Steve celebrates the children and students who have led Next Gen Sunday, recalls his own first nervous sermon, and presents the morning itself as a generational handoff of faith.
    • He then locates the message in Hebrews: God addresses his people through his Word, and his people approach God through Jesus, the great and final high priest.

    1. 00:05 Next Gen Sunday displays a living faith

      • Steve says the service has looked different because children and students have led it, but their leadership demonstrates that the church’s next generation and its faith are alive and well.
      • He announces that two high-school students will also preach and explains that he studied and wrote with them as they prepared.

    2. 00:45 A first sermon on endurance becomes a picture of generational faithfulness

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:1-2

      • Steve remembers preaching as a nervous nineteen-year-old in a gym, surrounded by teenagers, on Hebrews 12’s race, endurance, cloud of witnesses, and call to look to Jesus.
      • The memory fits Next Gen Sunday: Christian faith is not generational slang that others wait to see disappear. It is shared and passed on like a relay baton because the difficult race carries eternal rewards.
      • The old message did not need a polished messenger. Steve had obeyed God’s call, stepped beyond his comfort, developed his gifts, and declared a Word that cannot fall short even when its speaker feels shaky.

    3. 02:20 Hebrews moves from promised rest to the Word and the final high priest

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:16-19; 4:8-11 Hebrews 4:12-16

      • Steve recalls Israel’s unbelieving generation missing the promised land and Joshua leading the next generation into earthly rest. Hebrews 4 treats that land as a shadow of the greater, eternal rest found in Jesus alone.
      • The primarily Jewish-Christian audience now hears two benefits of salvation: God speaks to believers through his powerful Word, and believers speak to God through Jesus his Son, the great and final high priest.

    4. 03:13 Caleb and Levi receive the teaching baton

      • Steve introduces Caleb Bromley and Levi Cuevas by name, honors their preparation, and hands the teaching first to Caleb and then to Levi.

  2. 03:36 Caleb explains how God’s living Word exposes and changes the heart

    • Caleb Bromley works through Hebrews 4:12–13: Scripture is not merely informative or temporarily inspiring, but living, active, sharp enough to expose the inner person, and authoritative before the God to whom everyone gives account.
    • His own conversion and transformation illustrate the movement from hidden unbelief to a heart opened and changed as he drew near to God and remained in the Word.

    1. 03:36 A shy student takes a public step of bold faith

      • Caleb names his nervousness and shyness, introduces himself as a newly finished high-school sophomore, and says that before freshman year God found and saved him.
      • Standing on stage is a major step in his effort to become bold in faith, and he is excited to open God’s Word with the church.

    2. 04:10 Hebrews 4:12–13 lays every heart bare

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:12-13

      • Caleb announces Hebrews 4:12–13 and reads that God’s Word is alive and active, sharper than a double-edged sword, able to divide soul and spirit and judge the heart’s thoughts and attitudes.
      • He completes the reading with the warning that nothing is hidden from God; everything is uncovered before the one to whom people must give account.

    3. 04:44 Scripture is more than a respected source of information

      Scripture: John 5:39-40

      • Caleb contrasts calling the Bible a good historical or inspirational book with receiving it as God’s powerful Word. He cites a study claiming that only about seventy-one percent of American Christians identify it as God’s Word and warns against treating it as helpful but powerless or informative but not transformative.
      • He distinguishes Scripture from Jesus, the Word embodied, while insisting that Scripture points to Jesus. Jesus’ words in John 5 identify the diligently studied Scriptures as witnesses to him and direct people beyond the text to the life found in him.

    4. 05:53 Alive: God’s Word changes hearts rather than momentary feelings

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:12 1 John 4:9

      • Caleb isolates ‘alive’ and ‘active’ in Hebrews 4:12, beginning with the contrast between something living and something dead.
      • He sets Spider-Man’s maxim about power and responsibility beside 1 John 4:9, where God shows love by sending his Son so people may live through him. A comic-book line may motivate, but God’s Word can be meditated upon and changes the heart rather than merely stirring a feeling.

    5. 06:53 Active: the Word retains its power beyond today’s news

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:12

      • Caleb notes that some versions render ‘active’ as ‘powerful,’ but he does not name a translation. Daily news may be exciting for a day; the Bible stands the test of time.
      • Every line, thought, and symbol remains active and valuable now, just as when it was written.

    6. 07:26 Sharp: the double-edged sword becomes a surgeon’s scalpel

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:12

      • Unlike a machete with a dull edge, God’s Word has no dull side and cuts in every direction. Caleb explains machaira as a short sword or dagger, an instrument normally imagined as a weapon.
      • In the hands of a skilled surgeon, however, something sharp can repair and heal. Caleb applies the image to God cutting out sin, giving new life, and using his Word as a spiritual scalpel that reaches between soul and spirit.
      • Caleb describes the soul as the natural self—mind, personality, and feelings—and the spirit as the deepest place of connection to God through the Holy Spirit.

    7. 08:39 The Word distinguishes spiritual guidance from self-deception

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:12

      • Caleb says the joints-and-marrow image explains how easily people confuse their own intentions with the Holy Spirit’s guidance; the verse then turns directly to God’s judgment of those intentions.
      • He testifies that he attended church while disbelieving and pretended to be Christian because his family and friends were. His family began attending Crossroads in 2022 after needing a new church, and Caleb had recommended it because his friend Brayden de Wald kept inviting him.
      • After giving his life to Christ at summer camp before freshman year, old habits and thoughts remained, but closeness to God and time in the Word opened his heart, cut out what was wrong, and revealed truth.

    8. 09:59 Exposure before God makes mercy in Jesus the only safe response

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:13 Revelation 20:12 Hebrews 4:16

      • Caleb rereads Hebrews 4:13 and asks listeners to imagine every thought displayed over their heads. People hide, but God already sees their thoughts and intentions.
      • Revelation 20:12 supplies the final-accountability warning: the dead stand before the throne, books are opened, and people are judged according to what they have done.
      • Caleb contrasts being judged on one’s own record with being received on the basis of Jesus’ work. Because present exposure anticipates future accountability, the proper response is to run to Jesus’ mercy and grace.
      • He closes with a diagnostic question: when God searches the heart, will he find Christ?

  3. 11:41 Levi presents Jesus as the sympathetic high priest who opens the throne of grace

    • Levi Cuevas reads Hebrews 4:14–16 and develops its commands to hold firmly to faith and approach God confidently. Jesus entered human weakness without sin, completed the atoning work pictured by Israel’s priesthood, and therefore replaces fear of punishment with confidence in divine love, mercy, and grace.
    • A mountain guide who has climbed the route without taking a shortcut pictures why Jesus is the trustworthy guide believers want beside them.

    1. 11:41 Levi credits the Holy Spirit for courage to speak

      • Levi introduces himself as a student involved in the youth ministry since sixth grade, newly finished with his junior year at Anthem Prep, anxious about college decisions, and sometimes serving with the church’s tech team.
      • Because many congregants have probably never heard his voice, he calls it a miracle that he is speaking and admits that public speaking has always terrified him.
      • He initially left Steve’s invitation unanswered, but says the Holy Spirit—not Levi himself—is the reason he stands before the church, even though the experience remains as frightening as expected.

    2. 12:46 Hebrews 4:14–16 joins firm confession with confident access

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:14-16

      • Levi reads that Jesus the Son of God is the great high priest who ascended into heaven, so believers must hold firmly to their professed faith.
      • Because this high priest was tempted as his people are yet did not sin, he can empathize with weakness; believers may therefore approach the throne of grace confidently to receive mercy and timely help.

    3. 13:13 The ‘therefore’ answers unbelief and ultimate judgment with Christ’s atonement

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:2 Hebrews 4:12-13 Leviticus 16:11-17 Hebrews 4:14; 9:11-14

      • Levi asks what the ‘therefore’ is there for. Hebrews 4:2 warns about hearers who received good news yet remained in unbelief; believers must instead soften their hearts and submit to God’s voice.
      • Caleb’s preceding text establishes that the Word judges thoughts and intentions. Levi says Christ spares believers from God’s ultimate judgment.
      • He compares Aaron’s entry through the curtain and blood ministry on the Day of Atonement with Christ passing through the heavens and using his own blood to atone once for all. The old action is a picture; Christ is its fulfillment, received by faith.

    4. 14:01 Hold firmly to the confession that Jesus alone is Lord

      Scripture: Romans 10:8-9 Hebrews 4:14

      • Levi connects Hebrews 4:14 with Romans 10:8–9 and reads of the nearby word of faith: confess Jesus as Lord, believe that God raised him from the dead, and be saved.
      • Salvation does not arise from personal power. It is the Father’s grace received through the Son’s sacrifice, and the church must hold firmly to that confession because Jesus is its high priest.

    5. 14:33 The sinless high priest has climbed the human route

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:15 John 1:14 2 Corinthians 5:21

      • As Israel’s high priest was human, Christ became human and dwelt among his creation. He experienced ordinary struggles and temptation but never sinned, living the perfect life no one else could.
      • Levi quotes 2 Corinthians 5:21: the sinless one was made sin on behalf of others so they might become God’s righteousness in him. Christ’s humanity comforts believers because the God who set the stars in motion understands their struggles from experience.
      • Levi’s mountain-climbing analogy rejects both an inexperienced guide shouting from below and a guide who cheats by taking a helicopter to the summit. Jesus completed the climb and promises to accompany his people, making him the guide worthy to direct their lives.

    6. 15:54 Perfect love casts out fear at the throne of grace

      Scripture: 1 John 4:16-18 Hebrews 4:16 1 John 4:18 Ephesians 2:8-9

      • Levi reads 1 John 4:16–18: God is love, abiding in love means abiding in God, perfected love creates confidence for judgment, and perfect love casts out punishment-fearing fear.
      • Through the Holy Spirit, God leads believers to follow Christ’s self-giving love. Contemplating the lengths to which Jesus went to save sinners should change how they look toward God.
      • Levi returns to Hebrews 4:16: approach the throne confidently because love has cast out fear. Echoing Ephesians 2:8–9, he locates salvation in grace through faith rather than human power or grounds for boasting.
      • He challenges the congregation to practice that confident approach during the coming week.

  4. 17:11 Steve shows Jesus superior in selection, sympathy, and salvation

    • Steve returns to explain Hebrews 5:1–10 through three contrasts with the old priesthood. The Father selected the Son into an eternal royal priesthood; the incarnate Son sympathizes perfectly without sharing human sin; and the obedient Son became the once-for-all sacrifice and source of eternal salvation.
    • He closes by joining Christ’s unique high-priestly work to the church’s derivative royal-priestly calling: believers carry others before God in prayer, which the congregation immediately practices for promoted students and graduating seniors.

    1. 17:11 Hebrews 5 explains why the new high priest is better

      Scripture: Hebrews 5:1-10

      • Steve praises Caleb and Levi, then says the opening of Hebrews 5 anticipates questions about who this great high priest is, what he brings, what he does, and why he is better than Israel’s familiar system of sacrifice and access to God.
      • Because the original readers were Jewish believers, returning to the old covenant’s rituals could feel familiar even though something better had arrived in Jesus.

    2. 17:53 Returning to the old covenant would trade the better reality for the old pattern

      Scripture: Exodus 16:2-3; Numbers 14:1-4

      • Steve compares the readers’ temptation with Israel’s wilderness desire to return to Egypt rather than follow God obediently toward his promise.
      • A generational movie-night analogy makes the old system feel cumbersome: streaming requires a few clicks, while renting from Blockbuster required driving, choosing one tape for one television and VCR, watching previews, rewinding, returning it on time, and protecting it from Phoenix heat.
      • Nostalgia hides exhaustion. In the same way, the old model for being made right with God is no longer the path and cannot rival the better access already present in Jesus.

    3. 20:00 1. Jesus is superior in selection

      Scripture: Hebrews 5:1, 4-6 Hebrews 5:1, 4 Hebrews 5:5-6 Hebrews 7:13-17 Hebrews 7:1-10 Genesis 14:18-20

      • Steve reads Hebrews 5:1 and 4–6: a high priest is selected from the people to represent them and offer gifts and sacrifices; no one takes the honor personally, and Christ likewise received the appointment from God, who called him Son and priest forever in Melchizedek’s order.
      • The old office was neither self-appointed nor open to volunteers. God selected fallible men from Aaron’s family in Levi’s tribe, giving them real authority and responsibility among Israel.
      • Jesus did not crown himself, campaign, or seize priestly power. The Father chose him directly as Son and eternal priest.
      • Jesus’ descent from Judah appears to disqualify him from Levi’s priesthood, but that is precisely the point: he belongs to another, royal order that predates Levi and Aaron.
      • Steve identifies Melchizedek’s first appearance in Genesis as both king and priest. Jesus’ authority is therefore higher and unbroken; as priest and king forever, he never passes the baton to a successor.

    4. 22:48 2. Jesus is superior in sympathy

      Scripture: Hebrews 5:2, 7 Luke 2:7 Matthew 1:23 Matthew 4:1-11 Hebrews 4:15

      • Human priests could deal gently with ignorant and wandering people because they shared human weakness. Steve reads that comparison from Hebrews 5:2 and then reads verse 7’s portrait of Jesus offering prayers and petitions with fervent cries and tears in reverent submission.
      • Jesus came incarnate, fully God and fully man: a baby laid in a manger, Emmanuel—God with us. He knew bodily pain, ministry exhaustion, emotion for friends, and wilderness temptation, yet remained without sin.
      • Because Jesus truly knows hurt, tears, and grief while remaining perfectly strong where the old priests were weak, failures brought to him meet complete kindness rather than apathetic irritation.

    5. 24:28 3. Jesus is superior in salvation

      Scripture: Hebrews 5:3, 8-10 Hebrews 5:3 Hebrews 5:8-9 Philippians 2:8 Hebrews 7:24-27 John 19:30 Hebrews 10:12 Hebrews 10:11-12

      • Steve reads Hebrews 5:3 and 8–10. Traditional priests needed sacrifices for their own sins as well as the people’s, while the Son learned obedience through suffering, became the source of eternal salvation for those who obey, and was designated high priest in Melchizedek’s order.
      • Sinful priests could offer only temporary help. Repeated animal sacrifices covered sin without finishing the work; Jesus did not merely bring a sacrifice but became the sacrifice and washed sin away.
      • Steve clarifies that being ‘made perfect’ does not imply that Jesus was formerly sinful. His already-perfect obedience was completed in action and suffering at the cross. Children’s promises to do chores illustrate the difference between verbal agreement and costly follow-through, down to taking out trash that spills coffee grounds and cleaning up after the dog.
      • Christ’s death and resurrection completed the work. His once-for-all, everlasting priesthood means mercy and grace do not expire and sacrifice need not be repeated.
      • Steve quotes Jesus’ final cry, ‘It is finished,’ then Hebrews 10:12: after offering one sacrifice for sins for all time, Jesus sat at God’s right hand. Standing Levitical priests could not rest because their work continued; Jesus’ seated posture declares payment complete.

    6. 27:35 Access through the great high priest leads to a royal-priestly calling

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:16 1 Peter 2:9 Revelation 1:6 Exodus 28:29

      • Steve marvels that people may bring tears, fears, stress, struggles, and sin to the Father because Jesus represents them before the throne of grace. The access is easy to take for granted, so believers must remember and use it.
      • He then changes direction: Jesus alone is the great high priest, yet believers also have a priestly role. He reads 1 Peter 2:9’s identity—chosen people, royal priesthood, holy nation, and God’s possession—called to declare the praise of the one who brought them from darkness to light.
      • As a kingdom of priests, believers stand in the gap for others. No animal sacrifice, bloodshed, special clothing, or change of location is needed.
      • Aaron carried Israel’s tribal names over his heart into God’s presence; likewise, every believer may carry people and needs to God in prayer.

    7. 29:13 Promotion Sunday turns priestly teaching into intercession

      • Steve announces Promotion Sunday: fifth graders enter junior high, eighth graders enter high school, and graduating seniors move into a new chapter. He asks those groups to stand so the congregation can recognize them.
      • After the applause, he directs the church to notice the entire next generation, extend a hand toward a nearby child or student, and silently intercede while he prays. The application enacts the priestly role rather than leaving it as an idea.

    8. 30:27 The closing prayer entrusts the next generation to God

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:2 1 John 2:1 Psalm 119:105 Psalm 23:3

      • Steve thanks God for the privilege of speaking and hearing the Word and asks that it sink into hearts and stir affection for God.
      • He thanks God especially for Jesus, the author and perfecter of faith and the perfect great high priest who makes present access possible.
      • The congregation lifts every young person to God, asking that they experience his faithfulness, pursue his will, and remember Jesus as advocate and friend.
      • Steve asks that the Word become light and delight to them, that God lead them in paths of righteousness, and that they discover the blessings of obedience and faith. He closes in Jesus Christ’s name.
