Overview

  1. 00:05 “Lord, I need you”

    • Josh is moved by the heart of dependency the song encourages: “Lord, I need you” is the most honest confession a seasoned Christian can make. You may have him, Christian, but you still need him like the day you first met him. The more he works in you, the easier it is for the evil one to convince you that you’re something special: look how far you’ve come, no one would recognize who you were a decade ago.
    • Be careful: the stumbling block for all of us is looking down on others from the position Jesus lifted you to. You wouldn’t be there without him. We can’t afford to be heartless. Many wield the truth like a weapon, and it is, but point it at flesh and blood, at our neighbor, which violates the Christian rules of engagement. We fight the evil one with a weapon; we witness with our testimony.

  2. 01:35 Built on his faithfulness or yours?

    • A major issue in today’s church is building a religion around our faithfulness when the world needs his true church built on his faithfulness. What is your foundation built on? In church everyone wants to shout “his,” but anyone can claim anything; what if we inspected the evidence flowing from your life, your marriage, your home?
    • Does it sound like, “As long as I do all the things right, everything will be fine; as long as I remain faithful”? That sounds exhausting. What sounds liberating is this: he finished all things. Everything is fine even when it’s not; if I fail today, he still lays me down to rest tonight; even when I’m not faithful, he is.
    • Depending on our own faithfulness has produced millions of heartless Christians so worried about their own standing with God that they have no time to worry about the stranger’s. The Christian journey is about the faithfulness of God, not the faithfulness of you.

  3. 03:10 Every generation needs reminding that God is faithful

    Scripture: Deuteronomy 7:9–10 1 Thessalonians 5:24 2 Timothy 2:13 John 3:16–18

    • All the way back to Moses, the message before Israel entered Canaan was: know therefore that the Lord your God is God, the faithful God keeping his covenant of love to a thousand generations, and repaying to their face those who hate him. Every generation needs constant reminding that God is faithful, and that’s why we gather. Many years later Paul told persecuted young Christians in Thessalonica, “The one who calls you is faithful. Hang in there. He will do it.”
    • God’s promises aren’t the empty promises of a politician; they are divine guarantees. As certain as you are that the sun will rise tomorrow, that’s how certain Josh is that his Son will return.
    • In The Knowledge of the Holy, A. W. Tozer wrote that God, being who he is, cannot cease to be what he is, cannot act out of character with himself, and is at once faithful and immutable. Josh couldn’t stop thinking about that line all week. The Christian journey is less about your pursuit of faithfulness and more about your view of the faithfulness of God.
    • Paul told Timothy that if we are faithless, he remains faithful, for he cannot disown himself: the Lord won’t back away from his promise to save those who trust him, just as he won’t back away from his promise to condemn those who don’t. Everyone’s theology would improve if they kept reading past John 3:16: God did not send his Son to condemn the world but to save it; whoever believes is not condemned, but whoever does not believe stands condemned already.

  4. 05:53 Even your faith can become an idol

    • Many Christians have become so focused on their faith that they have taken their eyes off the faithful one. Some trust the strength of their own faith rather than God himself; faith becomes what they celebrate and what carries them. That is pride decorated in religious wrapping paper; it leads to legalism and kills your witness.
    • Others use faith to control God: if I just believe enough, God will bless me; if I come to church, maybe God will save my marriage or my children will call me back. That is a self-centered faith, not a God-centered one. Faith isn’t a tool to get what you want; it is a gift from God to illuminate one truth: you need Jesus Christ.
    • For some, being a Christian has become more about belonging to a group, denomination, or tradition than knowing Christ and sharing him. Many Christians idolize community and, to protect the idol, call it Christian community, as if the title made it an acceptable replacement for a personal relationship with the living God. Josh says these are hard things he has wrestled with all week, but he is commanded to remind God’s people: “If you don’t like it, take it up with him.”
    • True faith always keeps God at the center: not trusting our ability to believe, obey, or belong, but saying, “Lord, I need you every hour.”

  5. 08:07 Ezra 4–6: when God launches a work, expect pushback

    Scripture: Ezra 4–6

    • Josh hopes surveying Ezra 4–6 brings the faithful one back into focus. The Jewish remnant depended on God’s faithfulness through a difficult time; if his promises were only words with no work behind them, the rebuilding would be hopeless.
    • Newton’s third law, that for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction, is true of the spiritual world too. A rocket launches by pushing gases downward; when God launches a work, Satan doesn’t like it. Eli showed last week that God turned on a light in Jerusalem, and in chapter 4 the bugs start circling the light. New Christians should remember that God’s work frustrates the enemy and expect pushback; it’s called spiritual warfare.
    • The year is 535 BC. The seventy-year Babylonian captivity has ended, Cyrus the Great is dead, and the temple foundations are laid.

    1. 09:35 “Let us help you build”

      Scripture: Ezra 4:1–2 Ezra 4:3

      • In verses 1–2, the enemies of Judah and Benjamin come to Zerubbabel and the family heads: “Let us help you build, because like you, we seek your God,” sacrificing to him since the king of Assyria brought them there. Cyrus sent people back to their homeland; the Assyrian king’s policy was to capture and mix up peoples so they couldn’t unify and rebel. It is a contrast between a king moved by God and a king moved by self: unity versus disunity, sacrifice versus selfishness, honesty versus lies, pride versus humility.
      • The bugs have arrived intending to destroy Jewish identity and dismantle any community built since they first set up the altar. But Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the family heads answer, “You have no part with us in building a temple to our God. We alone will build it.”

    2. 11:04 Opportunity and opposition go together

      • Here is a spiritual law of motion: opportunity and opposition usually go together. Whenever God uses the church to take ground for the kingdom, part of Josh expects retaliation from Satan. It isn’t fear or anxiety; he just knows it is coming.
      • We can’t all walk around with pitchforks, but the church needs elders, men of God, being watchful. Elders and pastors, be warned: don’t assume Satan can only use outsiders. Stay humble and vigilant; he might intend to use you. Lord, we need you.

  6. 11:59 The first attack: a counterfeit offer

    • The first attack comes from the people of Samaria: we’re under the same God, we’re basically best friends, so let us help you. Josh says the same thing happens today with the Mormon cult and the Jehovah’s Witnesses: they claim to be Christian and to seek our God, but have perverted the gospel and changed his word while calling themselves lovers of it.
    • This is one reason church membership matters. We still have an obligation to protect ourselves, our children, and our families from corruption. It isn’t about traditions or denominations; it is about the gospel. If people who claim to love God but carry a different message are let in, everything built from there falls, because the foundation is rotten.

    1. 13:04 If you aren’t a member, you need to be

      • Josh lists what members can count on, and invites the church to hold the leaders to it: we will teach you the truth; we will confront your sin, the one everyone hates; we will accept you as family, hold you accountable, help you grow, keep your eyes on Jesus as we keep ours on him, and equip you to share your hope.
      • What is asked of members is easier, which is why no one goes into ministry: let us lead you. When you hear the truth, act on it. When we confront your sin, be humble; we’re all sinners. Failing to deal with sin doesn’t make the church more loving; it makes it less loving. Tolerance is not a greater love.
      • When a soldier falls in battle, no one admires the platoon for leaving him behind; we admire “no man left behind, let’s fight our way out together.” That is the church’s mentality. Jesus sat with sinners, but he didn’t sin with sinners. When we say give, get in a life group, or serve and meet needs, we aren’t lording it over anyone; it is our job to call you to obedience as we display obedience ourselves.

  7. 15:11 Why the outsiders’ help was dangerous

    Scripture: John 4:9 2 Corinthians 6:14–15

    • The Samaritans were part Jewish and part many other peoples, which is why in Jesus’ day Judeans saw them as half-breeds and not true worshipers of Yahweh; in John 4 the woman says, “You are a Jew and I’m a Samaritan woman. How can you ask me for a drink?”
    • Why is their help a big deal? If you start building a temple together, you might start building a life together. Josh imagines the invitation to ride donkeys after finishing the west wall; they start hanging out, then intermarry, which the law of Moses forbade. Israel was a nation set apart, called to be a conduit for God’s messianic worldwide blessing; if Israel is corrupted, the blessing is impacted. Israel’s battle with corruption isn’t unlike ours with the false gospel today.
    • The church has to stop involving itself in things that could compromise its testimony. Paul tells the Corinthians not to be yoked together with unbelievers: what fellowship can light have with darkness, and, Josh’s favorite, what does a believer have in common with an unbeliever? The church has given up much ground here, and Josh believes the Lord is trying to take some back today.

    1. 17:14 Don’t build a life with an unbeliever

      • To anyone with influence over a young unmarried person, whether a three-year-old daughter, an eight-year-old son, or, in Josh’s case, an eighteen-year-old daughter: if you train your children in righteousness but fail to warn them of the devastation of not marrying a Christian, you have failed. To young adults in such a relationship: get out now. Run.
      • Like a doctor’s note that gets you out of work or a family reunion, Josh offers to write a custom pastor’s note excusing you from that relationship. If God hasn’t changed their heart, do you really think you can? A single believer can live a more fulfilled life than a believer married to an unbeliever.
      • Marriage and sex are amazing when used according to God’s blueprints and awful when not, but they are not the pinnacle of the human experience; knowing and following Jesus is. Believers live in Christ’s kingdom of righteousness, freedom, light, and eternal life; unbelievers in Satan’s kingdom of lawlessness, captivity, darkness, and death. With different standards, convictions, morals, hopes, and dreams, how will you build a home, or teach your children to obey the Lord when your spouse doesn’t? In short, you can’t.
      • Josh offers himself and Kristen to sit down with any couple. Don’t get married until you’ve gone through Christian-led, biblically based premarital counseling. Yes, it costs money, but not as much as a divorce. If the relationship is real and God’s best for you, it will work.

    2. 20:15 In the world but not of it

      Scripture: John 17:14–18 Matthew 1

      • We can’t isolate ourselves from unbelievers entirely, because we need to be a witness. But we can set boundaries, and we are never required to violate them to witness. Jesus sought out sinners to win them; he never sinned with them.
      • Josh reads Jesus’ prayer for his disciples: they are not of the world any more than I am; my prayer is not that you take them out of the world but that you protect them from the evil one; sanctify them by the truth; as you sent me into the world, I have sent them. Verse 18 is huge: why did God send Jesus into the world? Matthew 1: to save sinners.
      • So there is only one reason to have unbelievers in your life: to tell them about Jesus. It isn’t a popular take, but it isn’t to foster a friendship for years until you finally feel comfortable sharing the gospel. Are you the Holy Spirit? Can you read hearts? Tell them now and tell them often. They may reject you, but take heart, believer: they hated him first.

  8. 22:00 Learn from the old-timers

    • Zerubbabel and Joshua set an example the local church needs more of. Just because someone wants to be here doesn’t mean they should be allowed; just because someone wants to be in your life doesn’t mean they should be; just because someone calls themselves a Christian doesn’t mean they are one. They may say they are here to rebuild with you but intend to tear you down.

  9. 22:26 Satan’s playbook: deceive or devour

    Scripture: Ezra 4:4 Ezra 4:2 Ezra 4:5

    • Remember the law of motion: turn them away the first time and they won’t stop. Next, the peoples around them set out to discourage Judah and make them afraid to build.
    • Satan’s playbook isn’t very thick. Like football’s pass or run, it is deceive or devour. In verse 2 he took the role of a serpent and tried to deceive: let us help, we’re cut from the same cloth. Israel’s leaders said no.
    • If you get nothing else, get this: if you think victory comes after the first battle, you are in for a rude awakening. He’ll be back, and if he can’t deceive you like a serpent, he’ll devour you like a lion. If they can’t tear the project down from within, they’ll appeal to the king to stop it, and it worked: verse 5 says they bribed officials to frustrate their plans from Cyrus down to Darius.

    1. 23:53 Rehum’s letter and the work halted

      Scripture: Ezra 4:21 Ezra 4:24

      • Rehum, the commanding officer, writes to King Artaxerxes with four reasons to stop the rebuilding. First, Jerusalem was a rebellious and wicked city, and honestly, he isn’t wrong. That is in Satan’s playbook: he will use your past against you; don’t let him. There were mighty kings like David, Solomon, Josiah, and Hezekiah, but rebellious ones too, and Rehum argued a restored Jerusalem would rebel.
      • Second, the loss of revenue to the empire. Third, a successful rebellion would dishonor the king. Fourth, the Jews could conquer the whole territory. In other words: defend your honor, king; stop this build.
      • After searching the records, Artaxerxes declares in verse 21, “Issue an order to these people to stop work,” and in verse 24 the work comes to a halt. They had worked six years, but in 530 BC it stopped, and it didn’t start up again for a decade.

  10. 25:27 Haggai and Zechariah: get up and build

    Scripture: Ezra 5:1 Haggai 1:2–4 2 Timothy 3:16–17

    • Here is where it gets interesting. Chapter 5 verse 1: Haggai and Zechariah prophesied to the Jews in the name of the God of Israel. Why didn’t the work restart when Darius took the throne? They are in the second year of his reign, so the remnant may have lost a year or more. The book of Haggai tells why: the people say the time has not yet come to rebuild the Lord’s house; is it a time for you to live in your paneled houses while this house remains a ruin?
    • What does this have to do with us? All Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training in righteousness, and Josh believes there is a timeless truth here. The decree goes out and the work comes to a complete standstill, and much of the modern American church would applaud: look at those obedient, law-abiding Christians.
    • Then two minor prophets show up, and the gist of their sermon series is: what are you doing? You are perfecting your backyard barbecue and the guest house while leaving God’s house in ruins. Get up and build; do what God sent you here to do.
    • Josh imagines someone emailing Haggai and Zechariah: don’t you know that’s against the law, that it violates the king’s edict? He can see Haggai drafting a response, “because I’ve done this before”: yes, the law says don’t build; the law of God says build. So get up and build.

    1. 28:16 When the law says stop and God says don’t

      Scripture: Romans 13 Acts 5:29

      • If the law says stop and God’s law says don’t stop, we don’t stop. Obedience to earthly authorities is required, since they have been instituted by God, Romans 13; but we are to reject laws that contradict God’s commands. Christians are called to be the best citizens, but our ultimate loyalty is to God. If the law says don’t pray, but God’s word says pray always, will you stop?
      • Daniel kept praying when it was illegal, and God delivered him from the lions’ den. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego refused to bow to Nebuchadnezzar’s idol. When a powerful institution tried to shut down Peter and the apostles, they kept preaching Christ crucified and raised, saying, “We must obey God rather than human beings.”
      • If a Christian must be disobedient, we are called to be the most wonderful, peaceful, humble, kind disobedient citizens ever, like the disciples leaving the Sanhedrin rejoicing that they got to suffer for the name. Spoiled American Christians may never face this dilemma, but we should be ready in case the Lord considers us worthy to suffer for the name.

  11. 30:16 God’s word spurs God’s people to God’s work

    Scripture: Ezra 5

    • Another timeless truth in Ezra 5: God’s word spurs God’s people on to keep doing God’s work. Just as the gospel can rescue horrific men like Josh, God’s word can stir revival in the most horrific institutions. Give him a crooked church, the word of God, and a few good men, and it can be straightened out; give him the most messed-up man and the gospel of Christ, and that can be fixed too.
    • The rebuilding continued because a couple of men started preaching the word. God speaks and creation begins and obeys, and so do his people. When God needs to wake his people from slumber, he sends preachers: Martin Luther and the Reformation, which transformed Germany and the whole Christian world; John Wesley in Great Britain, whose revival many historians credit with sparing England the bloodbath France experienced in its revolution. Don’t underestimate what God can do with a preacher who loves his word.
    • Josh couldn’t shake Spurgeon’s line that the man who preaches the word of God stands not on a mere platform but on a throne. Haggai launched his preaching ministry in 520 BC; five of his messages are recorded in his book, though not on YouTube like Crossroads’. The preaching led to the work continuing.

  12. 32:17 Opposition returns: dealing wisely with Tattenai

    Scripture: Colossians 4:5

    • And, you guessed it, opposition returns. Tattenai, governor under King Darius, asks two questions: who gave you authority to do this, and what are the names of the men building? Not all outsiders are bad. The Jews were respectful with Tattenai, which mattered, because he let the work continue while he checked with Darius; if they had been rude, he could have shut it down.
    • Once again the old-timers take us to school: how we deal with people matters, at work, on the freeway, even in the checkout line at Costco. Paul tells the Colossians to be wise in the way you act toward outsiders and make the most of every opportunity.
    • Is it possible you need to slow down to look for opportunities? Are you missing God-given opportunities because you are living too fast? We want to check the box so badly that we forget to check on the people right next to us. We desperately need to slow down and notice the needs of others.

  13. 33:43 Darius finds Cyrus’ decree and adds to it

    Scripture: Ezra 6:8–12

    • In chapter 6 Darius orders a search of the archives to confirm the history the Jews gave Tattenai, and he finds the decree of Cyrus the Great. Someone in the church 3D-printed a replica of the Cyrus Cylinder, with translations, and Josh invites the church to check it out.
    • Darius also adds to the decree, and Josh reads it: expenses fully paid from the royal treasury so the work won’t stop; whatever is needed for burnt offerings given daily without fail so they can pray for the king and his sons; and anyone who defies the edict is to be impaled on a beam from his own house. “I, Darius, have decreed it. Let it be carried out with diligence.”
    • And it was. The temple was completed on the twelfth day of the last month, about five and a half years after Haggai and Zechariah started preaching.

  14. 35:56 Verse 14: prospering under the preaching of the word

    Scripture: Ezra 6:14

    • Josh says that if he could go back, he would write the whole sermon on verse 14: the elders of the Jews continued to build and prosper under the preaching of Haggai and Zechariah. God’s people prosper when they rest under the preaching of God’s word. That is why the church comes together; when the church has the word of God, anything can happen.

  15. 36:27 Verse 16: dedication with joy

    Scripture: Ezra 6:16 Ezra 6:22

    • Verse 16: the people celebrated the dedication of the house of God with joy, which the church will have an opportunity to do later in the service. There was no ark in the Holy of Holies and no glory filled the house, but they had joy, because it was the house of the Lord. God’s people don’t spend time whining about what they don’t have; they rejoice in what they do have. Gratitude is the attitude that pours out of a life of faith.
    • When Solomon dedicated the first temple there were so many sacrifices nobody bothered to record the number. The remnant’s were easy to count: 712. And the number 12 stands out: twelve male goats as sin offerings, one per tribe, because they wanted the Lord to forgive their sins and give them a new beginning.
    • It came just in time for Passover. For seven days they celebrated the Festival of Unleavened Bread with joy, because the Lord had filled them with joy by changing the attitude of the king so that he assisted them in the work of the house of God.

  16. 38:06 Why did you stop?

    • How has the Lord filled you with joy? Recall what he has done. For many, the joy of salvation led them to a great work: joining him in building his kingdom, fueling love for the church, service to others, love for God and neighbor. So Josh asks: why did you stop?
    • How many have gotten distracted and sidetracked, with all their time and energy going into building their own home and none into building the house of the Lord? Do what he put you here to do. Reclaim your purpose and pursue it. The temple was dedicated right before Passover, and what is the point of dedicating the temple if the people aren’t dedicated to the Lord?

  17. 39:17 “Great is thy faithfulness”

    Scripture: Lamentations 3:23 John 16:33 Romans 10:13

    • No matter what happens or what our circumstances, we can trust God to be faithful always. “Great is thy faithfulness” is so much more than a verse or a song; it is a timeless truth on which to build our lives and anchor our hope however hard life becomes.
    • Never forget Jesus’ words: in this world you will have trouble; take heart, I have overcome the world. God’s faithfulness is best seen in the person and work of Jesus Christ: he died to save sinners and was raised to give us everlasting life, and everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.

  18. 40:13 Closing prayer before communion

    • Josh calls on the Lord’s name: he needs him, and the church needs him. We can come up with our own programs, strategies, and plans, but if God isn’t in it we can do nothing. He prays for protection from the evil one and for discernment about whom to let into our lives and whom to let out.
    • Turning to communion, he explains the items are reserved for believers, held to remember that Jesus died for our sins and was punished so we could have peace with God. Believers, take heart: he will do everything he said he would; he will come back and take us to be with him, so you can endure. But unbeliever, if you don’t have Jesus, you have nothing: turn to him, give him your life, and surrender. He closes by calling the church to be filled with joy in worship and giving, because he is faithful always.
