Overview

  1. 02:04 Who do you think you are?

    • Over the next four weeks the church will wrestle with identity, hearing from a few different people and a panel. The big question: who do you think you are?
    • We start working this out early in life, and it picks up speed in high school as we find our group: athlete or mathlete, gamers, nerds, and even the outcasts who form a group together.

    1. 02:49 Josh’s identity crisis

      • Josh thinks his rebellion came from an identity crisis in a pivotal year. He was a jock with a letter jacket on an undefeated football team and thought it might be his meal ticket. Then he blew out his knee, his parents divorced, and the family moved across the country. It took a long time to figure out who he was and where he fit. His place was with Jesus all along, but he couldn’t see it because he was so angry at him.
      • Some experienced the same thing: you thought you had your place, your group, your people, and then something big happened and you had to redefine everything.

    2. 03:46 Identity doesn’t fade with adulthood

      • Some say identity fades as you start adulting, but Josh thinks it just changes. We define ourselves by race, career, which side of the aisle we’re on, net worth, neighborhood, appearance, children, grandchildren, and connections. Social media is addicting and dangerous because it lets you be seen exactly how you want to be seen, which usually isn’t how those closest to you see you.
      • Questions like where you grew up, whether you’re married, and what you do help us understand someone. The problem comes when we take one part of our identity and define everything else by it. Josh grew up in Indiana with a Hoosier heart; what happens when he moves across the country? If your life centers on your marriage, children, or career, what happens when the marriage ends, the kids grow up, or you get laid off? Asking “what if” usually isn’t healthy, but with identity it is productive.

    3. 05:58 Value in what we create instead of our Creator

      • Forty percent of all résumés contain false information. Whether it is a group we want to join or a job we want to earn, we fear being inadequate: not good enough, smart enough, or unique enough. So instead of finding value in our Creator, we find value in what we can create for ourselves.

  2. 06:34 Ephesians: Paul writes to a melting pot

    • To course-correct a broken identity, the opening chapter of Ephesians will direct the series. Paul went from participating in the murder of Christians to becoming one, from leading persecution to leading the church. He is an apostle, and the church is built on the apostles’ teaching, so when Paul speaks, the church should listen.
    • Ephesus sat on the west coast of Asia Minor, founded by the Greeks in 1000 BC and under Roman control from 133 BC. It was the third-largest city in the Roman Empire, around 250,000 Jews and Greeks, a major commercial port and tourist draw. Its most notable religion was the worship of Artemis, goddess of fertility, magic, and astrology, viewed as a savior who answered prayers, among up to 50 gods and goddesses. Establishing the church on a strong foundation there would be incredibly difficult.

  3. 08:18 Two questions that form our identity

    • Paul starts by addressing the most important question of all: who is God? Your family, job, friendships, and life won’t reflect the fullness God intended unless you know who he is. If everything that makes you you isn’t connected to the living God who made you in his image, there will always be an emptiness you can never fill.
    • Two questions form our identity: what does God say about himself, and what does God say about you? Not who you say or think God is; the only reliable source on God is God himself, and he has revealed himself in his word. Some things about him are mysterious, but much has been revealed.
    • Only after answering who God is and who he says we are can we discuss church, family, parenting, marriage, sexuality, career, spiritual warfare, and suffering. We have to do the legwork first.

  4. 10:02 One long sentence: Ephesians 1:1–14

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:1–2 Ephesians 1:3–14

    • Josh reads Paul’s greeting to God’s holy people in Ephesus, then warns he is about to read a lot: in the Greek, verses 3–14 are one sentence. Praise be to the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in Christ; he chose us before the creation of the world, predestined us for adoption, redeemed us through his blood, made known the mystery of his will to bring unity to all things under Christ, and marked us with the seal of the promised Holy Spirit, a deposit guaranteeing our inheritance, to the praise of his glory.
    • That one sentence answers both identity questions: who God is according to God, and who we are according to God. Most of today’s focus is the first.

  5. 12:23 Secret things and revealed things

    Scripture: Deuteronomy 29:29

    • Answering who God is runs into big things theologians have discussed for thousands of years, like being chosen from all eternity to be his child. That should bring incredible joy and fuel love for the one who loves you, but we have reduced it to foolish debate. Questions like why God chose me, why not this person, or how predestination fits with free will are great questions, but we have to trust God with the unanswered ones.
    • Bible memory alert, one Josh’s family just added: Deuteronomy 29:29. The secret things belong to the Lord our God, but the things revealed belong to us and to our children forever. Show obedience to the things we know and faith in the things we don’t.
    • Josh is saved because God chose him and drew him, yet he also knows God wants no one to perish and is being patient. He isn’t sure how those fit together, but he doesn’t need it to make sense; he believes both are true and that God is good. Intellectual Christians, be careful: making the secret things revealed isn’t in your job description.

  6. 14:51 Who is God? There is only one

    Scripture: Psalm 14:1 Ephesians 4:4–6 Isaiah 45:18 Isaiah 45:21–22 Deuteronomy 6:4

    • Throughout history there have been three answers: atheists say there is no God, polytheists say there are many, and monotheists say there is one. Christians, Muslims, and Jews are monotheists; Hindus and Mormons are polytheists; Jehovah’s Witnesses are monotheists who deny the Trinity and that Jesus is God in the flesh, which the series will address. To the atheist, David says, “Fools say in their hearts, ‘There is no God.’”
    • We believe there is only one God because God revealed it. Paul tells the Ephesians there is one body and one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all. Through Isaiah the Lord says he created the heavens and formed the earth to be inhabited: “I am the Lord, and there is no other… there is no God apart from me, a righteous God and a Savior… Turn to me and be saved, all you ends of the earth.” One God who is Father, Son, and Spirit; as Deuteronomy says, “Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one.”

  7. 17:04 The Trinity: intimacy, diversity, unity

    Scripture: John 10:30 Matthew 28:18–20 1 Corinthians 3:16

    • For 3,000 years Jews believed in one God; then Jesus says in John 10, “I and the Father are one,” and everyone freaks out: blasphemy, angry leaders, confused disciples. The Spirit descends, the Gospels, Acts, and Epistles are written, the early church fathers study what happened, and the doctrine of the Trinity is born: each member of the Godhead has a unique role in perfect unity.
    • At Jesus’ baptism the Father says he is proud of his Son, and the Spirit descends like a dove. In the Great Commission in Matthew 28, Jesus says all authority has been given to him: go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and surely I am with you always.
    • Does Josh fully understand the Trinity? No. But he sees intimacy, diversity, and unity: three persons, each with a distinct and crucial role. He praises the Father who made a way to peace through Jesus, the Son who is the suffering servant and Savior of his soul, and the Spirit who lives in him and convicts him. That is why Paul asks, “Do you not know that you are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit dwells in you?”

    1. 19:25 Made in his image to join that relationship

      Scripture: Genesis 1:27

      • Before this adoption, God made us in his image and likeness: male and female he created them. The Trinity was in relationship at creation, the Spirit hovering over the waters and the Word with God in the beginning, so we were created to join that intimacy, diversity, and unity. As the church we are the bride of Christ.
      • Josh has been reading church history. The Westminster Shorter Catechism, written in 1646 by English and Scottish theologians who were smart and loved Jesus, asks what the chief end of man is: to glorify God and enjoy him forever. Maybe put down Joe Rogan and read some of the old stuff.

    2. 20:59 Built for connection, separated by sin

      Scripture: Romans 5:8 Ephesians 1:7 1 John 1:9

      • After making Adam, God said, in effect, “This is no bueno; you can’t be alone.” Made in his image, we were built for connection, love, and intimacy. But we sinned, and the result is separation from God and each other. Because God is holy and righteous he can have no part in evil; he isn’t the author of evil but the author of life.
      • Here the news turns good. As with the Israelites in Egypt, the separation didn’t go unnoticed. God saw our need and demonstrated his love: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Verse 7 of the long sentence: in him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, in accordance with the riches of God’s grace that he lavished on us. Sweeter words have never been spoken. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness.

    3. 22:39 God follows through, and he reversed the curse

      Scripture: Genesis 2:17 2 Corinthians 5:21 Hebrews 4:15 Ephesians 1:3

      • Whenever you hear “redemption through his blood,” think back to the garden: do not eat or you will die. Obey me, or there will be consequences. Parents know: “Don’t touch your sister.” “You can have one Oreo,” and yes, there are groundings over Oreos in Josh’s house. If the child goes for four and you look the other way, that doesn’t make you a good or loving parent. You must follow through.
      • God promises punishment for disobedience because he is holy, perfect justice, and unblemished integrity; he can’t turn a blind eye. But because he is also merciful and kind, he develops a plan, and while we sit in the curse eating our Oreos, he works to restore every spiritual blessing in Christ.
      • God’s justice and mercy meet at Golgotha, and his wrath is satisfied on the cross: God made him who had no sin to be sin for us. Jesus was tempted in every way yet without sin; every spiritual blessing belonged to him, but he willingly became the curse. We lived lives worthy of the curse, but through Christ we receive every spiritual blessing. He reversed the curse; we can be forgiven.
      • Then Jesus was raised to claim victory over sin and death and prove he is the one true God come to save sinners, the promised Messiah, the lover of souls who reconciles us to God forever.

  8. 26:01 Why did God do it? Unity under Christ

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:10 Philippians 2:10–11 2 Corinthians 4:4

    • Why every spiritual blessing, why chosen, predestined, redeemed, forgiven, and lavished with grace? Verse 10: to bring unity to all things in heaven and on earth under Christ. The unity Adam forfeited in the garden will be perfectly restored in Christ. At the name of Jesus every knee will bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord. They’ll praise him now or they’ll praise him then, but everyone will praise him.
    • Right now the world is divided and corrupt; Scripture calls Satan the god of this world. But on that day he will be cast out, every trace of evil thrown out, and God will dwell among his people. Josh doesn’t care about your eschatology if it doesn’t include that phrase: it will end with unity under Christ. This has been God’s plan from the beginning, and he is still exercising his sovereignty to work everything out according to his will.

  9. 27:45 Why is the church so worried?

    Scripture: Psalm 2:3–6 John 19:11 1 Thessalonians 5:24

    • Josh doesn’t get why the church seems so worried. Christians are freaked out about every little thing, maybe from too much Fox News or CNBC. For several elections he has heard that this is the most important election ever, every time. He isn’t saying don’t vote or speak up: if you’re a Christian here and not registered to vote, you are failing as a citizen; go register. We should take strong stances on important issues.
    • But Psalm 2: “Let us break their chains,” say the nations, and the One enthroned in heaven laughs; the Lord scoffs at them and says, “I have installed my king on Zion, my holy mountain.” Josh wonders what God’s laugh is like, maybe contagious, but he loves that God doesn’t just laugh; he is a doer who has installed his king.
    • Imagine if every believer truly believed this King will not lose. Many act like it is a jump ball: “Come on, God, try harder; let me help.” No, stop; it is already done. Josh prays for unshakable confidence in the divine execution of God’s plan, like Jesus before Pilate: “You would have no power over me if it were not given to you from above.”
    • Pilate had a choice, but either way Jesus would have found his way to bear our sins on the cross. God’s sovereignty is breathtaking: he guides man’s plans and lets us choose freely, and Josh doesn’t know how. When the world turns its back, he calls Noah; when a nation is enslaved, Moses; when land must be conquered, Joshua; to announce the Messiah, Isaiah; to call for repentance, John the Baptist; and to take the gospel to the nations, fishermen, cowards, and murderers. The one who calls you is faithful, and he will do it.

  10. 31:52 Election, predestination, and the arrogance of getting hung up

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:4–5 John 6:44 John 6:48 Ephesians 2:1

    • Election and predestination overwhelm many people, so avoid labels. People try to label Josh, and sometimes he veers a little just to throw them off. The goal isn’t to label people but to hear what the text says and how to respond.
    • God, the lover of our souls, worked out a plan over thousands of years to save us, and we get hung up on how he did it. Sit in the arrogance of that. Verses 4 and 5 say he chose us before the creation of the world and in love predestined us for adoption. That should be the most comforting statement we ever hear, yet many sit appalled: “My life, my choice; my free will has been violated,” treating it like a date gone horribly wrong and drafting the restraining order. Our response should be, “Praise be to God; he made a way for us.”
    • Jesus says no one can come to him unless the Father draws them, and later that he is the bread of life. You only eat bread when you’re hungry, and guess who gave you a hunger for Jesus? God changes your heart so you want him. The next chapter of Ephesians says we were dead in our sins; when did you last see a dead person perform CPR on themselves? (There is a defibrillator by the back doors; help yourself.) The heart change that lets you see your offense and call on God is the work of his word and his Spirit.

    1. 35:01 Parents overrule their kids’ wills to protect them

      • Parents know what it is to overrule a child’s will to protect them, holding them while they kick and scream, “Let me go,” because they don’t know any better. Our job is to override their twisted desires with our love.
      • Our rebellion against God is no different. He steps in and says, “That’s my son, that’s my daughter,” even while we scream that we want to be free: “That’s not freedom; it leads to death, and you can’t see it yet, but trust me.” The only reason you chose God is that God made the choice possible. He predestined us for adoption to sonship.

  11. 36:17 God is love, and he welcomes sinners into his family

    Scripture: John 15:15

    • Back to the question, who is God? God is love, and in his love he has welcomed redeemed sinners into his family. In John 15 Jesus says, “I no longer call you servants… instead I have called you friends.” See the status change: Jesus calls us friends, God calls us children, and when you were born again you were born into God’s family.

    1. 37:04 Madison: adopted, and fully family

      • Josh thinks of his 18-year-old daughter Madison, who doesn’t share his DNA. He and Kristen met when she was two and married when she was four. When they planned another child, Josh told Madison he was praying for a boy because he already had a girl. They have different last names, but he tells her all the time that he considers her more family than most of the people who share his name. She looks a lot like him, loves Jesus, serves Jesus, and has experienced his grace.
      • Madison and Nolan have complete equality in his home: equal access to Dad, Mom, resources, and inheritance, though as a pastor he can promise more of a trust piggy bank than a trust fund.
      • The one thing you can never give an adopted child is your nature. Madison will never have Josh’s nose (she is probably excited; she got her mom’s) or his blue eyes. What amazes Josh is that those God adopts begin to take on the family resemblance. He makes them sons like his divine Son, people who reflect his values and character and bring him glory.

    2. 39:33 Christian, you are chosen

      • See the Godhead working the plan of salvation: the Father planned it before creation, Jesus came to accomplish it, and, as next week will show, the Holy Spirit seals and guarantees it.
      • Christian, you are chosen. You are an image bearer with dignity and worth; chosen, loved, adopted, called sons and daughters, a co-heir with Christ, forgiven, and his forever. That is what God says about those who love him.

  12. 40:05 How do you know that’s you? Praise

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:3, 6, 12, 14 Ephesians 2:8–9

    • How do you really know you’ve been forgiven, redeemed, adopted, and chosen? You will be marked by something that appears four times in that one long sentence: praise, in verses 3, 6, 12, and 14.
    • You don’t just know in your head that you’re saved by grace through faith and not by works so that no one can boast; that’s memorizing, not believing. The word Jesus uses for believe means to have faith, entrust, commit, give yourself. It isn’t just in your head; it blows you away every time, stirs your affections, and makes you want to sing, serve, kill your sin, and praise God. That is what the blessings do: they stir praise.
    • So ask yourself what captivates you, what rules and reigns in your heart. If you know Jesus, what used to grab your attention won’t anymore. Under his authority and reign as Lord, your entire identity will change.

  13. 41:54 Give your heart to Jesus

    • Josh pleads with the church to give their hearts to Jesus. If you are ready to say yes, nothing else matters: stand up while he prays, walk to the baptistry, and he will meet you there. God loves you and is welcoming you into his family; don’t wait. Get baptized and follow Jesus.

  14. 42:50 Closing prayer before the table

    • Josh thanks the Father for embracing him and welcoming him into his family. He will never grow tired of reading about his redemption and adoption. What God has revealed about himself is good enough; he doesn’t need to know what God hasn’t revealed, and he anticipates it: “Maranatha, come, Lord Jesus.”
    • Until then, he thanks God for revealing himself in his word, reshaping hearts by his Spirit, and turning image bearers into children who are his for a purpose, and asks God to lead them to understand that purpose through the series. He thanks God for the table and the sacrifice of atonement, where he took the curse so they could have every spiritual blessing in Christ.
