Overview

  1. 00:03 A front-row seat to what God is doing

    • Josh is encouraged by this body of believers. Many are being hit on every level and going through really tough times, but the faith Jesus is building in them is awesome. As their pastor he has a front-row seat as believers fix their eyes on Jesus and embrace the Spirit’s ministry, through trouble or triumph.
    • Addicts are turning from addiction, finding healing in Jesus’ name, and moving from idols into kingdom work. Almost weekly, men tell him they are no longer content with the world’s mold of a father; some who knowingly were bad fathers a year ago now see their duty as provider, protector, and priest in the home, leading their wives and children in the ways of the Lord. Over time God helps men realize they have failed if their family learns to depend on them; their job is to teach their families to depend on the Lord.
    • The women of the church are capable of extraordinary forgiveness, the kind that had Josh scratching his head at first. It is beautiful and convicting to watch something with no hope at all reach complete restoration, with people we would have walked away from.

    1. 03:06 Feeling like Peter in the waves

      • Walking through these stories, Josh sometimes feels like Peter drenched in salt water after failing the faith test. Jesus calls Peter out of the boat, and Josh admits he would have said, “Why don’t you come over here? It’s a perfectly good boat.” Peter walks, becomes aware of his circumstances, takes his eyes off Jesus, and sinks.
      • Looking at a situation, his own or someone else’s, Josh thinks there’s no way: the waves are too choppy, the storm too relentless, the water too deep, cold, and dark. Then Jesus takes somebody’s hand and reminds him there is nothing he can’t do and no one too far gone. These stories are swarming around the church: people turning from sin, truly repenting, surrendering their lives as testimonies of God’s transforming grace. Josh thanks those who had the boldness and courage to share them.

  2. 04:46 The graveyard of testimonies

    Scripture: Acts 1:8

    • Many testimonies in the church never get shared; they end up buried, and Josh thinks the graveyard of testimonies in the church could rival Arlington. Shame is a powerful tool Satan uses to silence God’s people, but we refuse to let him. We have all been tempted and fallen short; the greater tragedy isn’t the sinner or even the sin, but the rescued sinner who is scared to testify.
    • The unstoppable movement of the Lord’s church is built on testimony: from zero Christians to over 5,000 in one holiday weekend, because witnesses testified, which was the plan all along. Jesus said, “You will be my witnesses.” Josh believes Satan has convinced an overwhelming number of Christians to bottle up their testimony, and it’s time to push back.

  3. 06:02 Two points from Acts 3 and 4

    Scripture: Acts 3–4

    • Josh takes a unique approach, using two chapters, Acts 3 and 4, to show two simple things, because something connects them he doesn’t want the church to miss. First, there is power in the name of Jesus. Second, some are amazed at this, and some are annoyed.

  4. 06:26 Amazed at the source, not the miracle

    • Extraordinary things are happening in Jerusalem after Pentecost; Jesus is showing off through the apostles. Josh won’t tell anyone these things don’t happen anymore. People try to label him with man-made doctrines, but he dodges the labels; he isn’t a cessationist. God works how he wants, when he wants, period.
    • When amazing things happen, he isn’t amazed at the things themselves or the people God works through, but at the source of the power. He isn’t amazed at the Red Sea splitting: God spoke creation into existence and turned words into water, so holding it back for a while is small work. He isn’t amazed at a flawed man driving a stick into the sand. He is amazed that God cares for his people in need, chose the most unimpressive misfits to be his people, and is concerned for those everyone else ignores. He could have chosen Egypt, but he chose her slaves.
    • Josh saw a headline about a believer whose sight was restored after baptism, and a testimony of a young woman raised in a cult whose body convulsed as evil spirits came out of her when she was baptized in Jesus’ name. Does God still do these things? Yes. Nothing is too hard for him. Why does he do them? To glorify himself, draw people to himself, and reveal himself to those whose eyes have been pulled shut.

    1. 09:05 Jesus has always been the Healer

      • Jesus has always been the Healer: not the apostles, not self-proclaimed faith healers, not your favorite televangelist swinging things around on stage. Watch how the Lord uses men in the streets, especially Peter, but this isn’t about Peter for a second. It is always about souls being directed to the God of the universe, because the one who can heal you physically really wants to heal you spiritually.

  5. 09:43 The lame man at the gate called Beautiful

    Scripture: Acts 3:1–10 Acts 3:6

    • Chapter 3: Peter and John go up to the temple at three in the afternoon. A man lame from birth, carried every day to the temple gate called Beautiful to beg, asks them for money. Peter looks straight at him: “Look at us.” Expecting something, he hears, “Silver or gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, walk.” Peter takes his right hand, his feet and ankles become strong, and he goes into the temple courts walking, jumping, and praising God. The people recognize him and are filled with wonder and amazement.
    • Verse 6 is the key verse: in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk. The apostles healed through the Holy Spirit’s power, not their own.

    1. 11:01 The Spirit’s focus: the man in the street

      • The gate called Beautiful was made of Corinthian bronze and shimmered in the sunlight. Thousands would have rushed past the lame man to present their prayers in the temple, yet the Lord led Peter and John to stop, speak with him, and heal him.
      • Notice the Spirit’s focus. The religious are in a mad dash to the temple to be noticed and have their fancy prayers heard, while the Spirit leads Peter and John to a man lame from birth. Crowds rush to the temple; meanwhile the Lord builds his church in the streets.

    2. 11:55 Spark or spectator?

      • What percentage of your ministry effort happens outside these walls? Are you a spark for his kingdom or a spectator, comfy with your life group and church clique, or looking for opportunities to do his work in the streets?
      • On the way to Leslie’s for a bag of salt, Josh’s family came upon a car dead in the middle of the street, blocking McDonald’s, Taco Bell, and Safeway all at once. His first thought was how to get around it. He has learned that your first instinct usually isn’t the one to go with, and the next thought, park and help, was from the Lord.
      • A frightened teenage girl was afraid to get out of the car, and a woman trying to help couldn’t get it into neutral. They jumped it, got it into neutral, and pushed it to safety. The woman told Josh she had counted more than ten vehicles honking as they drove by, and he was the first to get out and help; after that everybody started helping. He told her Jesus had called him out on his selfishness or he would have driven by too. The Lord used it to show her Jesus and to show his son that they are the kind of people who stop in the street. We don’t need more Christian honkers.

    3. 14:20 Ministry outside these walls

      • Jerusalem wasn’t amazed at the early Christians for their hours of prayer; it was their ministry in the streets that stood out. That is why the church launched Crossroads Cares in 2021, meeting individual needs outside its walls. People in the school districts are talking about the church’s generosity and love, and the church tells them it is just Jesus.
      • Josh asks people to reconsider their involvement. A small group carries most of the weight, but two hours a week, one for a meeting and one for work, can send you into the community on fire for Christ. How many more needs would be met if you just asked your neighbor or your server, “How can I pray for you?” They might spit in your food, or they might say they need prayer, and with permission you can bring the need to the church.
      • To those who are late every week, Josh, an optimist, likes to assume they stopped at the gate called Beautiful to help someone in need.

    4. 16:14 “No one has ever asked me that”

      • Eight months after Josh invited a man to church, he started coming; sometimes the effort takes time to bear fruit. Last Monday the Lord prompted Josh to text and ask how he could pray for him, and the man, who grew up in the Catholic Church, replied, “No one has ever asked me that before.” If we aren’t asking how to pray for each other, we certainly aren’t preaching the gospel to each other.
      • This isn’t a country club; Josh will happily give you a ride to one. This is a church of action. Stop talking to the same people in the lobby; find someone you don’t know, someone talking to no one, and blow them away by asking how you can pray for them. Too many Christians are content to watch ministry happen and leave it to the pastoral team. Who are you trying to bless in Jesus’ name? Prayer meetings matter, but they aren’t more important than people who need prayer; ask them, and pray right then and there.

  6. 18:04 Peter’s second sermon: by faith in the name of Jesus

    Scripture: Acts 3:11–16

    • Last week Josh showed Peter’s first sermon; now comes the second. In verse 11 the people run to Solomon’s Colonnade, and Peter asks why they stare as if by their own power or godliness they made this man walk. The God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob has glorified his servant Jesus, whom you handed over and disowned before Pilate; you asked for a murderer and killed the author of life, but God raised him from the dead, and we are witnesses. By faith in the name of Jesus this man was made strong; it is Jesus’ name and the faith that comes through him that has completely healed him.

    1. 19:12 All power in heaven and on earth

      Scripture: John 1:3 Colossians 1:16–17 Isaiah 9:6

      • Jesus doesn’t just have great power; he has all the power in heaven and on earth, because our Redeemer is also the Creator. John 1: through him all things were made. He sustains it all and is the glue holding it together; Colossians 1 says all things were created through him and for him, and in him all things hold together.
      • He has been given the name above all names. Long before Bethlehem, Isaiah used titles reserved for the one who hung the stars: for unto us a child is born, and he will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. What were you thinking naming your baby boy Fred?
      • Remember how the demons reacted to his name: “What do you want with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are, the Holy One of God.” They weren’t mocking him; they legitimately knew who he was, and Jesus told them to be quiet because it wasn’t time yet. The name of Jesus has infinite power.

    2. 21:01 Proclamation and demonstration

      Scripture: James 1:22

      • Josh’s concern is that we don’t give God enough opportunities to demonstrate his power. Christians share their amazing message not only through words but through actions; the church is called to make proclamations and demonstrations. Many churches drift to one extreme, all demonstration without the words or all words without action, but the truth is down the middle. Jesus and his disciples never showed up with a message without some blessing or healing that showed it had real power and authority.
      • So Peter says: by faith in Jesus’ name this man has been healed, and by faith in Jesus’ name you can be healed too. The gospel has power to heal ankles and to save your soul. That is why James implores us not only to listen to the word but to be doers of it; then the word comes alive in our actions, and when the gospel is heard and seen, people are amazed.

  7. 22:46 Faith as the conduit

    Scripture: Matthew 14:31 Matthew 17:20

    • The lame man wasn’t amazed until Peter helped him up. Before landing on those divinely fused ankles he had no faith at all; he was looking up with his canister expecting money, not expecting to join the temple dance team that afternoon (Josh is kidding; that isn’t biblical). It was Peter’s faith that was the conduit for Jesus’ healing power, and that checks out with the Gospels: miracles from the spiritual gift always depend on faith.
    • Peter stepped out of the boat by faith and walked on water, but his circumstances overwhelmed his faith and he began to sink; Jesus caught him and said, “You of little faith, why did you doubt?” When the disciples failed to cast out a demon and asked why, Jesus said, “Because you have so little faith.” They had some faith; they had dropped their nets and tried to heal the boy. What they lacked was faith in the power of the one they called on.

  8. 24:35 Faith, healing, and God’s will

    Scripture: James 5:16

    • Since the apostles there has been the idea that enough faith lets us work miracles too, or the negative version: if you aren’t receiving your healing, you don’t have enough faith. Josh is clear: if you haven’t been given the power to do it, no amount of faith can do it.
    • James 5 says the prayer of a righteous person is powerful and effective, and it is; we should pray for healing. But those prayers don’t operate outside God’s will. Josh has been on both sides: he prayed for healing from cancer for his mother-in-law and his stepmom, and neither healing was in God’s will; now he is starting that prayer for his stepdad. He has also prayed over cancer patients outside his family whose doctors found nothing the next day. The prayer of faith is the conduit for the Lord’s healing power, but no amount of faith overrides God’s will.

    1. 26:09 Prayer pulls you in line with God

      • This is where so much Christian frustration with prayer lies. The Lord teaches us to pray that his will be done, but many pray it while still wanting their own will. Eventually it sounds like: Why won’t God do this? Why was that person healed and not this one? Doesn’t he care about me? He doesn’t answer my prayers. Insert the “ye of little faith” speech here.
      • Is God not answering, or not answering the way you want? Prayer was not established so you can pull God in line with your plans, but so God can pull you in line with his. Impatient prayer lives keep countless believers from spiritual blessings like joy, forgiveness, freedom, wisdom, comfort, and peace.
      • Last week a woman told Josh she had prayed since her children’s births that they would find spouses who love Jesus and a faithful church home. Now in their late 20s, after 20 years of prayer, they had been brought together and brought to this church. Josh believes that is an answer to prayer. Christians get upset that God won’t buy them a mansion, but this woman just wants her grandbabies to know Jesus. We need more of that.

  9. 28:23 Little faith and big faith

    • The healing led to dancing, jumping, and outbursts of praise, but we don’t know how the beggar’s faith journey continued. There was probably hardship: he was used to living off gifts, and now he could earn a wage, and Josh can assure you he got taxed on it. Would he still be dancing in a week or a month? Would he join the apostles and plant a church, or use his new freedom to build a name and a business? Josh doesn’t know.
    • Scripture shows a clear difference between little faith and big faith. Little faith believes when the checking account is flush, the marriage rocking, the kids thriving, the 401(k) growing. Jesus rebukes it in the disciples: they get out of the boat, but when circumstances get sketchy they doubt, and he doesn’t say, “Good job, you’re halfway there.” We can all relate: when our needs are met our faith is strong, and when we are in need, doubt sets in.
    • Big faith, or mature faith, stays fixed on Jesus through good and bad. Layoffs hit: God has a plan. A health scare: I don’t know what he’s doing, but I trust him. The loss of a loved one: my heart is broken, but my faith is not shaken. Big faith trusts God while the winds blow and the boat shakes.

    1. 30:38 “Josh, I’m not worried”

      • A brother in the church, Dan, has been in the hospital almost a month fighting for his life. Before his surgery Josh prayed over him: “Heal Dan, Lord. Give us more time with this brother,” who has taught him so much about leadership, “but your will be done.” Dan, who usually gives Josh a hard time, looked at him seriously and said, “Josh, I’m not worried. My heart isn’t doing well, but God has given me a hope and a joy in him that even a failing heart can’t touch.” That kind of big faith is how the world is amazed.
      • This is why God allows us to suffer: so we can show a joy that can’t be shaken even in the most difficult circumstances. Everyone wants the healing, and we want to be with those we love as long as possible, but God is glorified not only in miraculous healings but in peaceful goodbyes. Dying well brings just as much glory to God as being healed. Healings make headlines, and they should, but Josh finds just as much power sitting with someone as they sing their way into the Lord’s presence. Whether healing or heaven, praise God.

  10. 32:34 Some are annoyed

    Scripture: Acts 4:1–2 Acts 4:3–4 Acts 2

    • Any name with this much power will bring a power struggle. In chapter 4 the priests, the captain of the temple, and the Sadducees come upon them, greatly annoyed that they are proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection of the dead. The Sadducees loved their power and status and rejected resurrection, so this is political and religious: Jesus has more power than they do, and the apostles are claiming he was raised.
    • The Sadducees are a Christian comedy sketch waiting to happen. Josh pictures two of them standing at the empty tomb: “What now?” Instead of being convinced by evidence they were wrong, they ask how to bury and twist it. Their well-thought-out plan: arrest Peter and John and hold them overnight. But many who heard believed, and the men numbered about 5,000.
    • Jerusalem’s population was perhaps 40,000 to 45,000, only heads of households were counted, and the 3,000 from Pentecost must be added, so probably five to seven thousand. Christianity literally exploded over one long weekend in Jerusalem. Acts 2 is the birth of the church; Acts 4 is the birth of persecution. Satan starts his work immediately at the church’s first signs of success.

    1. 34:52 True persecution

      • Next week they will look at men like Stephen. Josh describes the persecution under Nero, who sewed Christians into animal skins and loosed hungry dogs on them, and dressed others in wax clothing and lit them as torches in his garden, and under Domitian, who considered himself Lord and God and cooked, seared, boiled, and branded Christians.
      • That is what the early church endured. Stop claiming persecution because some liberal Christian posted yuckies on your Facebook page. The early church knew true persecution, and it traces back to this moment in Acts 4.

  11. 35:50 Before the council

    Scripture: Acts 4:5–7

    • The next day the rulers, elders, and scribes gather with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and the high-priestly family, and ask, “By what power or by what name did you do this?” For Annas it was a family meeting; he basically had the whole court in his family. This is the same council that condemned Jesus. It was within its rights to investigate a big shift in Jerusalem, but it had no right to jail innocent men and ignore evidence, and it did both.

    1. 36:41 People will be annoyed that you’re a Christian

      Scripture: Acts 4:8–10

      • Learn from Peter: people will be annoyed that you’re a Christian, and it will only get worse. You can still be polite, respectful, caring, and loving. Josh gets the temptation to want to punch someone in the face; the first reaction is probably from the flesh, so shove it down, wait for the Spirit, and speak calmly and truthfully.
      • Filled with the Holy Spirit, Peter answers: if we are being examined for a good deed done to a crippled man, know that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified and God raised from the dead, this man stands before you healed. Imagine their frustration hearing that name again: didn’t we deal with this prophet from Nazareth? Now his followers are telling everyone that the man they publicly crucified is alive.

    2. 38:10 The stone the builders rejected

      Scripture: Acts 4:11–12 Psalm 118

      • The Spirit leads Peter to tell the council the foundation on which God builds his kingdom: this Jesus is the stone you builders rejected, which has become the cornerstone, and there is salvation in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven by which we must be saved.
      • Peter quotes Psalm 118: you are the builders, and you rejected God’s stone. These Old Testament experts knew the rock was a symbol of God and that the prophet Daniel used it for the Messiah and his coming kingdom. If you checked out, come back: Peter is preaching the gospel of Christ to the people who killed Jesus. Turn to the one you killed in order to be saved. Jesus is the source of this man’s physical healing and of all spiritual healing, and you need it every bit as much as I do.

  12. 39:19 You don’t get to pick who you preach to

    • This is huge, because many Christians are selective in their preaching. Nobody says in the lobby, “You’re rude, so I won’t tell you about Jesus,” but we act like it, and stop preaching to difficult people when they need it most. You don’t get to pick who you preach to; be faithful to the opportunities God puts before you. Salvation was offered even to those who killed God’s servant. The only problem is they rejected the message.
    • How have you responded to the message of Christ? Jesus is the great physician, the only way for humanity to deal with what truly ails it: sin.

    1. 40:37 An exclusive Christ in an inclusive world

      Scripture: John 14:6 Matthew 7:13–14

      • Take a page from Peter’s playbook, which he borrowed from the Lord’s. Peter says there is no other name, but Jesus said it first: I am the way and the truth and the life; no one comes to the Father except through me. Christians preach an exclusive Christ in a completely inclusive world, and they won’t like you for it. If you are faithful, most will call you narrow-minded, unloving, intolerant, not inclusive enough; they want you to say many paths lead to God, just follow your heart. Even some Christian leaders, teachers, and preachers cling to a universal message that all people will be saved.
      • There are two paths, but only one leads to God: the wide road, roomy, taken by most, paved by works, leading to destruction; and the narrow path, paved by faith in Jesus Christ, leading to eternal life. Jesus says only a few will find it. Josh doesn’t think that is because it is hard to see, but because it is hard to travel.

    2. 42:17 Jesus, the true gate called Beautiful

      Scripture: John 11:25–26

      • Salvation is a gift from God to all people that most will reject. Everyone has a sin debt. Like the lame man, you were crippled on the ground under a debt so big you couldn’t stand. Break one law and you broke it all. The world says you’re better than him, so it’ll take you, but God sees filth, because the standard was perfection set by the Creator, and you didn’t make it out of your mother’s womb.
      • He knew you couldn’t, and he loved you. So Jesus, the true gate called Beautiful, took on flesh, lived the life you couldn’t, handed himself over willingly to be crucified, and died the death you deserved. What did you deserve? The moment Jesus cried out to God from the cross and got nothing: silence. Jesus was forsaken so you could be forgiven and have peace with God. He was raised, appeared to hundreds, and ascended to the Father’s right hand. There is no other name.
      • Jesus says in John 11: I am the resurrection and the life; whoever believes in me will live, even though they die. Do you believe this? If yes, it changes everything. You don’t need every question answered, but you need to answer this one: believe and be saved.

    3. 44:09 The narrow road of full surrender

      Scripture: Isaiah 6:8

      • It is hard to hear, but don’t assume you’re saved because you’re here. The church is full of people who aren’t offended when Jesus is preached; to many it is flattering to hear how much God loves them. The problems start when Jesus asks them to deny themselves, love the unlovable, forgive the unforgivable, give their money to the church’s ministry of meeting needs and sharing hope, and say no to their sinful desires: “Lord, this is too much.”
      • That is the narrow road. True believers see this path, count the cost, and invite Christ to reign in their lives no matter what: here is everything I’ll have to lose, and I’m still good with it. He is Savior and Lord. The Lord wants your full surrender and full trust, and as one who has walked in it, Josh says he will not disappoint. If you don’t know how to start, approach it like Isaiah: “Here I am, Lord. Send me.”

  13. 46:03 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks God for mercy and grace, for seeing him on the ground, beaten and drowning in sin, and making a way for him to find God again; he will never forget how God loved him. He prays for anyone who has been watching, waiting, and absorbing, that they would see how God is moving in people’s lives and stop being closed-minded and skeptical about lives changing because of his presence, goodness, mercy, and grace.
    • Everything good we have, and every decent person we know, is from God. He asks God to do more, and the church surrenders to the process: even if a whole branch of their lives needs to be cut off, “cut it,” because what is the point of gaining everything here and losing you?
