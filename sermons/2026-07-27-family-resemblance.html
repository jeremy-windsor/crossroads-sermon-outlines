Detailed chronological outline

The sermon begins at 00:36 and continues through the closing prayer at 48:20. Josh presents Hebrews 13 as the family resemblance produced by the gospel, not an entrance fee for earning God's favor. Because Jesus gives his people new hearts, the church is called to sustain brotherly love, welcome strangers, remember prisoners and the mistreated, honor marriage and sexual holiness, reject the love of money, imitate faithful leaders without idolizing them, submit to accountable shepherding, and bear Christ's disgrace outside the camp. The sermon closes by locating every command in the unchanging Christ, inviting hearers to repent and trust the risen Shepherd, and praying for power to walk in his grace.

  1. 00:36 The gospel creates a family resemblance

    • Josh reframes biblical commands as the fruit of salvation: Christ does not merely improve rules but gives new hearts, and Hebrews 13 describes life among people who already belong to God's unshakeable kingdom.

    1. 00:36 From an impossible rulebook to transformed desires

      Scripture: Ezekiel 36:26-27 Hebrews 8:10

      • Josh opens with a common objection to Christianity: its commands can look like an exhausting list of prohibitions and an impossible demand for perfection. His rapid examples include "Don't have sex outside of marriage," "Don't love money," "Love your enemies," "Love strangers," and "Honor marriage."
      • The gospel is not merely that Christ teaches better behavior; Christ gives new hearts so that forgiven sinners begin to love what he loves, finding joy and freedom in obedience.
      • God does not simply hand saved people a kingdom handbook: "He puts his spirit within them," gives them power, changes their desires, and enables a new life.
      • Josh says, "He writes his law on their hearts," so the order is grace and inward renewal before the commands that follow.

    2. 02:59 Hear Hebrews 13 as a Father's description of family life

      • Josh asks listeners not to picture a grumpy teacher with a clipboard when Hebrews 13 gives commands, but a loving Father who knows actions, thoughts, and motives.
      • These commands are not entry fees for God's family. They are values for children who already belong and already have their feet on the coffee table.

    3. 03:34 Come down from the mountain into kingdom life

      Scripture: Matthew 17:1-8 Hebrews 12:22-29

      • Josh compares the arrangement of Hebrews 12–13 with "the mountain of transfiguration in [Matthew] 17": Peter wanted to remain on the glorious mountain, but the disciples came down.
      • Likewise, "Hebrews 12 ended with all God's people rejoicing together on Mount Zion," yet Scripture continues by showing what salvation joy produces on earth.
      • The finale asks what life looks like inside God's unshakeable kingdom while it is still present in the world and what the Spirit produces in people redeemed by Christ's blood.

    4. 04:48 The fruit is resemblance, not admission

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:1

      • Josh states the controlling distinction: the fruit under discussion is not an entry fee but "the family resemblance."
      • Hebrews 13:1 begins, "Keep on loving one another as brothers and sisters." After thirteen weeks declaring that Jesus is better, Hebrews shows that those who follow him are being made better too.

  2. 05:06 Love the family and welcome the stranger

    • The Spirit preserves both Philadelphia, brotherly love within the church, and philoxenia, love for the stranger. Josh warns against forces that kill fellowship, illustrates the church's historic hospitality, and turns the text into concrete life-group and table fellowship applications.

    1. 05:06 Protect Philadelphia from the enemies of love

      Scripture: Philippians 2:1-11 Matthew 20:28; Mark 10:45 John 13:4-15 Luke 23:34 John 15:13 1 John 4:19

      • Because believers must be told to keep loving, Josh reasons that congregational love can die and therefore must be protected.
      • Pride resists washing feet, bitterness keeps a record of wrongs, and selfishness asks what others can provide; busyness, envy, gossip, favoritism, unresolved conflict, fear, materialism, bad teaching, and bad shepherding also threaten love.
      • For an attitude adjustment, Josh tells Christians to "go read Philippians 2" repeatedly until Christ's humility confronts them.
      • Christ is the one who "came to serve, not be served," "washed dirty feet," "prayed for their forgiveness while they mocked him," and "laid down his life for his friends."
      • With eyes on Jesus, love is not manufactured; believers "love because he first loved us." The preservation of this love for two thousand years displays God's power.

    2. 08:06 Move from Philadelphia to philoxenia

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:2

      • Hebrews 13:2 says, "Do not forget to show hospitality to strangers, for by so doing some people have shown hospitality to angels without knowing it."
      • Josh explains the Greek wordplay: Philadelphia is love for brothers and sisters, while philoxenia is love for the xenos—the stranger or foreigner—and is the opposite of xenophobia.
      • Gospel love starts in the church but burns warmly enough to reach the streets: instead of fearing or avoiding outsiders, believers open the door.

    3. 09:15 Reject cliques without neglecting the body

      • Josh rejects an "us four and no more" church mentality and urges proactive new relationships rather than dependence only on old connections.
      • The opposite error is caring for outsiders while overlooking the person drowning nearby inside the body; love formed by Christ works at both hospitality and congregational care, imperfectly but consistently.
      • As an historical illustration, Josh cites Emperor Julian's complaint that Christians' charity cared not only for their own poor but also for his people's poor. Even an opponent noticed their love.

    4. 11:20 Open homes and pursue life-group community

      Scripture: Luke 19:5

      • First-century Christian travelers depended on believers' homes. Josh notes that "Jesus didn't even have a home" and recalls that Jesus told Zacchaeus, "I'm coming to your house today."
      • Josh and his wife recently housed a Christian who needed it, illustrating preparedness to meet needs rather than waiting for ideal circumstances.
      • Because ministry in one another's homes is powerful, Josh presses listeners to join life groups, asks existing leaders to seek people who need community, and says members need not wait for staff or an intake form before extending an invitation.
      • He challenges mature members who are ready to lead, offering help from Josh and Mike to equip them for that ministry.

    5. 13:05 Trade privacy for wise table fellowship

      • Some will never entertain angels because they refuse to host anyone; modern Christians prize privacy, while the early church used homes as tools for public ministry.
      • Josh assigns concrete homework: before the end of August, ask someone at church, "Do you have someone to share a meal with this week?" If not, invite that person to the table and report what was learned.
      • Hospitality should include discernment: involve a third party or meet publicly when appropriate, screen wisely, and account for the dynamics and safety of the home rather than inviting carelessly.

    6. 14:26 The Mormon missionaries and Abraham's unexpected guests

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:8 Genesis 18

      • Josh remembers welcoming two Mormon missionaries for water and Cheez-Its, listening to their presentation, discussing Jesus, and ending with the declaration that "salvation is a gift from God"; the family ministry moment remained memorable.
      • Josh says, "In Genesis 18, Moses gives the story of Abraham showing hospitality to Jesus Christ and two of his angels." Abraham welcomed them before recognizing who they were.
      • Even if a stranger is not literally an angel, that person may become a messenger of blessing. Josh asks the church to pray about using its homes for kingdom ministry and hopes for more life groups, locations, and meeting times.

  3. 16:22 Remember prisoners and the mistreated

    • Hebrews calls the church to identify with suffering believers. Josh contrasts the state's limited role with the church's ministry of mercy and recounts early Christians who chose imprisonment alongside persecuted brothers and sisters.

    1. 16:22 Remember suffering as though it were your own

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:3

      • Hebrews 13:3 commands, "Continue to remember those in prison as if you were together with them in prison, and those who are mistreated as if you yourselves were suffering."
      • Josh explains that Roman prisoners could depend on friends for food and survival, so serving an imprisoned Christian in Christ's name was urgent, costly care.
      • Paying taxes supports a justice system, but the state cannot be the church: it cannot offer Christ's love, care for a broken family, read Scripture with a prisoner, or pray for a child whose father was incarcerated.
      • Christians therefore support justice while personally showing mercy and supporting gospel ministry inside prisons.

    2. 17:54 Serving the least is serving the King

      Scripture: Matthew 25:36-40

      • Josh quotes Jesus: "I needed clothes and you clothed me. I was sick and you looked after me. I was in prison and you came to visit me."
      • The righteous ask when they fed, welcomed, clothed, or visited him, and the King replies, "Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me."

    3. 18:22 A radical solidarity that leads into holy conviction

      • Josh recounts ancient Christians who protested the imprisonment of believers and, when protest failed, entered prison so they could sleep and eat beside them.
      • This anti-cultural love holds together justice, generosity to the poor, and strong convictions about marriage and sexuality; compassion and holiness are not rival categories.

  4. 19:07 Honor marriage and bring sexuality under Christ's lordship

    • Josh treats marriage as a gift that all believers must honor and sexual intimacy as good within its covenant. He confronts sexual sin within the church, warns of judgment, and shepherds ashamed strugglers toward the cleansing, power, and new identity available in the risen Christ.

    1. 19:07 Honor the covenant and keep its bed pure

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:4

      • Hebrews 13:4 says, "Marriage should be honored by all, and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral."
      • Josh distinguishes adultery—sexual activity outside one's marriage—from porneia, sexual immorality that includes sexual activity outside marriage during singleness.
      • The church's generosity toward the poor and convictions about sex resist easy cultural or political categories; Josh says there is a left side, a right side, and the truth.

    2. 20:12 Sex and money are gifts that make terrible gods

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:5-6

      • To complete the worldview he sees in the paragraph, Josh reads Hebrews 13:5–6: "Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content with what you have" because God will neither leave nor forsake his people; therefore, "The Lord is my helper. I will not be afraid. What can human beings do to me?"
      • The gospel changes whom and how believers love, how they suffer, what they do with their bodies, and what they do with money, talents, and gifts.
      • A Spirit-filled Christian cannot reserve Sunday for Jesus while claiming the body, sexuality, bank account, or future as private property: if Jesus is Lord, he is Lord of all.

    3. 21:19 Everyone has a way to honor marriage

      • Married people honor marriage by keeping their promise; singles honor it by refusing marital privileges outside marriage; children honor their parents' covenant by respecting and celebrating it.
      • Churches honor marriage by teaching truth, supporting struggling couples, confronting adultery, helping wounded spouses, and refusing to redefine what God established.
      • Josh directs couples needing support to the church's marriage and family page and the forthcoming Bind event for laughter, tears, prayer, encouragement, and mutual strengthening.

    4. 22:20 Protect the Creator's definition and the gospel picture

      Scripture: Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:4-6 Ephesians 5:22-33

      • Marriage is God's gift, will be attacked, and is worth protecting. Josh states its definition as "one man, one woman, one flesh for life."
      • Believers honor this design not from mere traditionalism but as sons and daughters of the one who made it.
      • Josh explains the gospel picture: "every husband who sacrificially loves his wife tells the story of Christ" and "every wife who gladly submits to her husband tells the story of the church."
      • A kept covenant proclaims the goodness of the promise-keeping God; dishonoring marriage distorts the picture God intended to paint through it.

    5. 23:35 Receive marital intimacy as a good gift

      • After humorously offering parents a moment to take young children for water, Josh says plainly that the marriage bed is good and sex is not dirty.
      • Sexuality was created by God rather than Hollywood, the internet, or the sexual revolution.
      • Within marriage, intimacy is to be received with joy, gratitude, tenderness, faithfulness, safety, love, and playfulness; husband and wife may enjoy the gift together.

    6. 25:09 Christian discipleship is self-denial, not self-expression

      Scripture: Matthew 16:24-26

      • The sexual ethic is straightforward: intimacy belongs within marriage between a man and a woman; adultery, premarital sex, pornography, lust, using another for pleasure, and redefining sexuality against God's design are sin.
      • Josh quotes Jesus: "Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves, take up their cross and follow me," warning that gaining the world while forfeiting one's soul is no gain.
      • Christianity does not command people to discover and obey every inner desire; it calls everyone to bring every desire under Jesus Christ's lordship.

    7. 26:02 Let holiness confront the church first

      Scripture: Matthew 7:3-5 Hebrews 13:4

      • The Christian sexual ethic is not a weapon aimed first at outsiders. Josh asks the church to deal with "the plank in our own eye" before pointing outward.
      • It confronts unmarried couples sleeping together, women and men using pornography, emotional affairs, private fantasies, selfish sexual use, and manipulative withholding of affection because all believers are called to holiness.
      • Josh repeats the warning: "God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral." Culture, media, friends, churches, politicians, or courts may normalize sexual sin, but God will judge it.

    8. 27:11 Refuse excuses and fight the liar

      • Josh describes judgment as both active within creation and future, saying he has watched adultery consume people and that brief pleasure is not worth its misery.
      • Neither "God knows my heart," professed love, nor cultural prevalence turns disobedience into holiness; each person will stand before God rather than before the crowd.
      • He shepherds exhausted hearers who have promised "never again" and now feel ashamed: temptation promises freedom, pleasure, and life but leaves chains, shame, and death.
      • Believers fight not because they are strong but because Jesus is alive; the Christ who conquered sin, death, and the grave lives in his people.

    9. 29:08 Judgment is not the last word: Christ cleanses

      • God's word offers healing, forgiveness, restoration, new starts, and power to change; Christ's blood is sufficient for adulterers, pornography addicts, the abused, the ashamed, and all who come repentantly by faith.
      • Jesus does not forgive sexual sinners only to leave them enslaved: he cleanses, purifies, and gives his Spirit. Heaven's message is not merely "try harder" but God's presence with his people.
      • Correcting a billboard that advertised zero perfection and complete forgiveness, Josh insists that believers possess Christ's perfect righteousness imputed to them and everything needed in the resurrected Christ.

    10. 30:38 The worst sin or wound does not permanently define the believer

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:11

      • The gospel says believers are not permanently defined by the worst thing they have done or the worst thing done to them; there is healing in Jesus' name.
      • Josh echoes Paul's words: "That's what some of you were ... but you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God."

  5. 31:09 Reject covetousness and rest in God's presence

    • Josh defines covetousness as love of money, distinguishes money as a useful tool from money as a cruel master, and applies the text through personal questions, consistent tithing, church support, generosity, and confidence that God will never leave his people.

    1. 31:09 More possessions cannot produce enough

      Scripture: Luke 12:15

      • A millionaire's answer that satisfaction requires one million more illustrates covetousness: it wants more regardless of need or use.
      • Even in a wealthy society, people struggle to say, "I have enough." Material things cannot satisfy the heart; only God can.
      • Jesus warns, "Watch out. Be on your guard against all kinds of greed. Life does not consist in an abundance of possessions."

    2. 32:02 Money is a tool; loving it is slavery

      Scripture: 1 Timothy 6:10

      • Against the world's claims of ownership, Christianity says both money and body belong under God's choice and authority.
      • Paul says, "For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil." Josh stresses that the text does not call money itself evil.
      • Money can feed, house, support missionaries, care for children, provide for families, and sustain a church; love of money instead promises security while producing anxiety, promises freedom while creating slavery, and promises significance while displaying what others have.

    3. 32:55 Ask not how much you have, but how much it has you

      Scripture: Psalm 24:1

      • Poor people, rich people, and pastors can all love money. The diagnostic questions are whether money supplies identity or security, directs decisions, disturbs sleep, or makes teaching about generosity and tithing feel threatening.
      • Josh explains his own practice from Psalm 24:1: "The earth is the Lord's and everything in it, the world and all who live in it." He tithes because everything already belongs to God.

    4. 33:46 Give first fruits consistently and loosen the idol's grip

      Scripture: Matthew 6:33 Matthew 6:21

      • Josh and Kristen give ten percent of all income to the church's general offering so the church can rely on a consistent gift and plan responsibly; other offerings and benevolence are additional rather than deductions from the tithe.
      • Josh quotes Jesus, "Seek first the kingdom of God," and says his household answers, "Yes, Lord," in all things.
      • Because "our treasure and our heart are connected," consistent tithing helps loosen money's grip as a leading idol.

    5. 34:45 Support an independent church because Christ made his people rich

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 8:9

      • Josh and Kristen give because they believe in Crossroads' gospel ministry. As an independent church it has no denominational subsidy or outside body to rescue it financially; its elders are local and raised up within the congregation.
      • Their giving testifies that the gospel is destroying greed and growing generosity.
      • Josh applies Christ's grace personally: "Jesus was rich and for my sake he became poor so that by his poverty I might become rich." He is rich because he has Jesus and therefore gives as one who already has more than he deserves.

    6. 35:39 Contentment rests on the God who stays

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:5

      • The foundation of contentment is not having enough money but having a God who will never leave.
      • Accounts, jobs, health, homes, economies, and relationships can change, but the Lord says, "I will never leave you and I will never forsake you." This promise defeats covetousness.

  6. 36:03 Follow the unchanging Christ through accountable leaders

    • The closing movement anchors kingdom life in Jesus Christ's immutability. Josh calls the church outside the camp, remembers faithful teachers, rejects pastor-centered dependence, describes Christ's unchanged saving work, teaches reciprocal accountability between leaders and congregation, applies the benediction, summarizes the family resemblance, invites repentance, and prays.

    1. 36:03 An unshakeable kingdom is led by an unchanging Christ

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:7-16

      • Josh reads Hebrews 13:7–16, beginning, "Remember your leaders who spoke the word of God to you," commanding imitation of their faith, and declaring that "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever."
      • The passage warns, "Do [not] be carried away by all kinds of strange teachings," because hearts are strengthened by grace rather than ceremonial food.
      • Jesus suffered outside the gate to make the people holy by his blood, so believers are to go to him outside the camp, bear his disgrace, seek the coming city, offer praise, do good, and share: "with such sacrifices God is pleased."

    2. 37:23 Go to Jesus outside the camp, whatever it costs

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:13

      • Josh lingers over Hebrews 13:13: "Let us go to him outside the camp."
      • He applies the call to evangelistic sacrifice: if pointing someone to the Redeemer costs money, time, or convenience, give it; the cost is nothing beside what Jesus paid to bring his people home.

    3. 37:42 Remember the people who first taught you God's word

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:7

      • Josh pauses for ten seconds and asks everyone to remember the first person who taught them God's word, then to thank God for that person.
      • As their pastor, Josh hopes the church will see in his way of life a faith worth imitating; he wants not only his words but his life and conduct toward his family to point people to Christ.

    4. 38:44 Do not build a life around any servant of God

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:8 Ephesians 1:4-5

      • Josh warns that people attach themselves to pastors in unhealthy ways and that troubled shepherds may thrive on the attention; some listeners have seen or suffered this abuse.
      • His instruction is direct: "Don't depend on me. Depend on the Lord." Never build life around a servant of God; build on Jesus Christ and his unchanging truth.
      • Josh repeats, "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever." Before Bethlehem, the cross, Adam, or the stars, the Son had set his love on his people; the Father chose a people, and the Son willingly came to redeem them.

    5. 39:54 The same Jesus still forgives, welcomes, convinces, and gives life

      Scripture: John 21:9-19 Luke 23:39-43 John 20:24-29 John 11:43-44 John 6:37

      • Kingdoms, presidents, elections, friends, churches, bodies, memories, and wealth change, but Jesus Christ remains the same forever.
      • The Jesus who sat by the fire and "forgave Peter for denying him still forgives today."
      • The Jesus who told the dying thief he would be "in paradise that day" still welcomes sinners, even as Josh notes that the thief had no time for baptism or a polished doctrinal statement.
      • The Jesus who appeared behind locked doors to "doubting Thomas" still turns doubt into belief.
      • The Jesus who "called Lazarus from the grave" still gives life to the dead.
      • His promise remains: "All whom the Father gives me will come to me and whoever comes to me I will never drive away." Because Jesus does not change, neither do his promises.

    6. 41:07 Submit to leaders who know they need prayer

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:17-18

      • Hebrews 13:17–18 says, "Have confidence in your leaders and submit to their authority because they keep watch over you as those who must give an account," and asks the church, "Pray for us."
      • Josh sees the connection as a safeguard against authoritarianism: leaders worthy of confidence cherish prayer rather than celebrating power.
      • A shepherd answers to God and needs the church's prayers because the church is ordained to play the gospel's song while every human leader remains flawed and vulnerable to deception.

    7. 42:29 Handle leadership conflict biblically and assume the best

      Scripture: Matthew 18:15-17

      • Members who call Crossroads home should consent to its leadership except where leaders clearly violate Scripture, but a leader's sin does not authorize an unbiblical response.
      • Josh says to "activate Matthew 18": go directly to the person, take a witness if needed, then bring the matter to the elders under Crossroads' application of the final step.
      • If resolution remains impossible, leave without division or gossip. Give leaders the benefit of the doubt and assume the best about motives until evidence shows otherwise.
      • Josh admits that he held ill will toward leaders for a decade and closes the counsel with the reminder that pastors are sheep too.

    8. 43:43 Pray the Shepherd's benediction over the shepherds

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:20-21 Hebrews 13:21

      • Josh offers Hebrews 13:20–21 as a prayer: "May the God of peace, who through the blood of the eternal covenant brought back from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, equip you with everything good for doing his will."
      • The benediction asks God to work in his people what pleases him through Jesus Christ and gives Christ glory forever.
      • Flawed men can watch over souls because the great Shepherd equips his people. Josh assigns the church to find the staff and elder pages and pray, "May the God of peace equip you with everything good for doing his will," over its leaders.

    9. 44:33 The Hebrews finale displays what faith produces

      • After thirteen weeks seeing Jesus from every angle, the finale shows faith's fruit: a church that trusts God, loves one another and strangers, honors marriage, resists love of money, prays for leaders, and is a joy to lead.
      • The greatest gift the church can give Josh is neither a compliment, card, attendance, nor scheduled tithe, but to know Jesus, stop resisting his voice, trust him, and walk in his power.
      • Josh anticipates the next week's baptisms as fruit of members leading people to Jesus, praising God for the spiritual fire he sees in the body.

    10. 45:59 Come home to the great Shepherd today

      Scripture: Hebrews 3:7-8, 15 John 10:11 Matthew 26:28 John 6:37

      • A shepherd's greatest joy is "carrying one of his sheep back home." Josh urges those delaying repentance: "If you hear his voice today, surrender."
      • He invites hearers to kneel or fall on their faces if needed and surrender the heart to Jesus, "the great shepherd. He laid down his life for his sheep."
      • Josh declares, "His divine blood was poured out for the forgiveness of sins," God raised him from the dead, and "he will receive every sinner who comes to him in faith."
      • The invitation is to come, turn from sin, trust Christ, and walk in his power rather than leave without responding.

    11. 46:46 Closing prayer: thank Christ for the cross and ask for power

      Scripture: Colossians 1:16 Matthew 27:42 Hebrews 12:2

      • Josh thanks the Father for Scripture, Hebrews, and the supremacy of Jesus from beginning to end.
      • He thanks Jesus for "humbling yourself, taking our flesh," living the life sinners could not live, dying the death they deserved, and taking their place.
      • Addressing the crucified King, he prays, "All things were made through you and for you," yet the creation mocked its maker.
      • Josh recalls the mockery, "You can save others. Why can't you save yourself?" and says Jesus stayed on the cross because he saw "this joy"—a redeemed family of faith called to trust, serve, love, and change the world.
      • He asks the Lord for needed power, help to forgive others and themselves, and grace to walk faithfully; he closes by thanking God for the series, Jesus, and the cross, then prays in Jesus' name, "Amen."
