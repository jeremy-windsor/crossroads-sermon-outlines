Overview

  1. 00:55 Communication methods expose the need to hear clearly

    • Josh moves from comic observations about letters, calls, voicemail, and texts to the relational cost of broken communication, then frames Hebrews as God’s answer to a receiving problem.

    1. 00:55 Letters and cards create social pressure

      • After correcting an earlier announcement about the students, Josh asks the congregation to identify its preferred communication method.
      • He jokes that receiving a handwritten card creates dilemmas about how long it must remain displayed, when it may be discarded, and whether a reply is required.

    2. 01:50 Phone calls and voicemail complicate a simple reply

      • Phone-call devotees often leave voicemails, which Josh portrays as an elaborate way to say what a three-second text could communicate: “Call me back.”
      • His imitation of a voicemail that sounds like a live conversation underlines how a communication tool can produce confusion rather than clarity.

    3. 03:03 Texting turns punctuation and read receipts into emotional signals

      • Email and texting are convenient, but Josh says they turn recipients into anxious mind readers who interpret capitalization, periods, exclamation marks, ellipses, and delayed replies.
      • Read receipts can strain friendships, and a medium intended to simplify communication instead leaves people scrutinizing every word.

    4. 04:27 Matthew 18 calls the church to face-to-face conflict resolution

      Scripture: Matthew 18:15

      • Josh argues that avoiding difficult conversations is tactical rather than generational: even people who request face-to-face communication may send a text when they have a complaint.
      • He applies Matthew 18 to people who have sinned against one another and says Crossroads commits to healthy, direct conflict resolution.
      • Letters, ordinary calls, texts, and email conceal too much; when distance prevents an in-person meeting, seeing the other person by video is better than trying to resolve sin through detached words.
      • He distinguishes biblical conviction from closed-mindedness: living by God’s word is, in his formulation, Christ-minded, and truth and love belong together.

    5. 06:44 Broken lines of communication damage every relationship

      • Josh says diplomats, leaders, and businesses understand that some conflicts require sitting down together, while churches too often tolerate poorer communication.
      • Family drama, marital strain, church conflict, and church hurt grow when communication is severed.
      • Friendship with Jesus likewise requires communication through prayer and his word, as well as healthy communication with others.

    6. 07:53 Hebrews addresses a receiving error under the better covenant

      • Communication fails through bad sending or bad receiving; Josh presents Hebrews as a letter to New Testament believers learning to live in a new and better covenant by faith rather than relating to God through law-keeping.
      • The problem is not that God sent his message badly but that the hearers need their reception restored.
      • Josh says he believes Paul wrote Hebrews and promises to give reasons during the series while acknowledging that the authorship debate will remain.

  2. 08:40 God’s final speech governs every claim of revelation

    • Hebrews 1 contrasts partial prophetic speech with God’s complete revelation in the Son. Josh uses that contrast to permit fallible spiritual impressions only under infallible Scripture and to reject any modern revelation treated as doctrine or authority equal to the Bible.

    1. 08:40 God spoke through prophets and has now spoken by his Son

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:1-2a Hebrews 1:1

      • Josh reads the opening contrast of Hebrews: in the past God spoke to the ancestors through prophets at many times and in various ways, but in the last days he has spoken by his Son.
      • Unlike forms that ask for a preferred contact method, God chose his saving communication plan in Christ without consulting its recipients.
      • Josh explains the prophets as representatives of God to the people and the phrase “many times and in various ways” through the Greek word *polymerōs*, which he summarizes as incomplete or partial.
      • The old revelation was neither wrong nor flawed; it was intentionally partial so that the Father’s full and better self-revelation in Jesus could follow.

    2. 09:49 A continuationist position with a speed limit

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:9-10

      • Josh pauses the exposition to address Christian disagreements about prophecy, tongues, and healing—gifts named in the New Testament.
      • Cessationists believe supernatural gifts have ceased; continuationists believe they continue. Josh identifies himself as a “continuationist with a speed limit.”
      • He has seen gifts expressed in healthy and extremely unhealthy ways, so continuation cannot mean accepting every spiritual claim.

    3. 10:46 Fallible people must subordinate gifts to infallible Scripture

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:31; 14:3

      • A healthy expression of supernatural gifts is subordinate to Scripture. Josh calls God’s word infallible, without error, while the same gifts in believers’ hands are fallible because their human vessels can be wrong.
      • The gift is not deficient, but flawed Christians make ministry messy.
      • His governing limit is that the greater gifts are for encouragement, not the creation of new doctrine.

    4. 11:46 Authoritative “God told me” claims enable abuse

      • Josh warns the congregation to avoid the New Apostolic Reformation because of its emphasis on modern apostles and prophets who claim authority with the phrase “God told me.”
      • He grieves abuse and crimes committed against God’s children under such claims, apologizes to victims, and entrusts final vengeance to King Jesus.
      • His concern is not merely unusual language but leaders using claimed revelation to control people in God’s name.

    5. 12:59 Word-of-faith ambition must be tested and the flock protected

      Scripture: 1 John 4:1 1 Corinthians 16:13

      • Josh also warns against word-of-faith and prosperity circles that claim to declare realities into existence with authority like Scripture; in such settings, God’s voice and personal ambition become difficult to distinguish.
      • These teachings can enter an ordinary church unless believers test the spirits, stay watchful, report troubling behavior, and protect the flock.
      • He urges men in particular not to be passive: standing for what is right requires knowing God’s word and defending it.

    6. 14:01 “God put this on my heart” still requires biblical confirmation

      • Josh calls “God put this on my heart” a softer form of the same claim but does not prohibit the language.
      • If an impression is from God, the believer should do the work of finding where it aligns with Scripture, then take God’s word to the other person for encouragement.
      • The manner of sharing must elevate the Lord rather than the messenger or the messenger’s gifting.

    7. 14:57 Any ongoing revelation that functions like Scripture must be rejected

      • Josh broadens the warning beyond groups commonly labeled non-Christian: any continuing revelation becomes a problem when it prescribes church life or claims authority equal to Scripture.
      • He names prosperity teachers whose message, in his assessment, replaces repentance and the cross with self-improvement, health, wealth, and material blessing; if money or health is the reason for coming to Jesus, that benefit has become the worshiper’s god.
      • He separately names self-proclaimed apostles and prophets whose claimed revelation is expected to function like Scripture and tells the congregation to avoid them.

    8. 16:49 Progressive language can preserve Christian words while changing their meaning

      • Josh calls progressive Christianity especially dangerous because it uses recognizable Christian vocabulary while redefining core doctrines.
      • He cites the denial or reinterpretation of hell, substitutionary atonement, and Christ’s exclusivity as examples of cultural sensibilities displacing biblical authority.
      • His application is to become students of the word, test teachers—including Josh—and reject what is not biblical.

    9. 18:13 Scripture repeatedly warns against another gospel and false teachers

      Scripture: Galatians 1:6-9 2 Peter 2:1 2 Timothy 4:3 Matthew 7:15

      • Josh invokes Galatians, where Paul is astonished that people desert Christ’s gospel and calls down a curse on those preaching another one.
      • He quotes Peter’s warning that false teachers will secretly introduce destructive heresies and deny the sovereign Lord who bought them.
      • He quotes Paul’s warning to Timothy that people will reject sound doctrine and collect teachers who satisfy itching ears, then recalls Jesus’ warning about predators dressed as sheep in Matthew 7.
      • These warnings establish the need for his next movement: presenting Christ’s true person, work, worth, and deity so counterfeits can be rejected.

  3. 18:55 First portrait: Jesus is God’s prophetic voice

    • Josh begins an eight-part portrait of the majestic Christ. The first claim is that Jesus fulfills earlier revelation as God’s full, decisive, final, and perfect speech without making the Old Testament disposable.

    1. 18:55 Eight truths will display the majestic Christ

      • Because the world and parts of the church diminish Jesus to a social reformer or deny his equality with God, Josh says he will use his remaining time to show Christ’s true person, work, value, and divinity.
      • The standard is not intensity for its own sake but recognition strong enough to reject any substitute Jesus.

    2. 19:39 Christ fulfills the partial revelation that preceded him

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:1-2a Matthew 5:17 Matthew 17:1-5; Mark 9:2-7; Luke 9:28-35

      • Jesus does not break with Israel’s past; he brings it to fulfillment. Without him, Old Testament revelation remains partial and fragmented, but in him God has spoken fully and finally.
      • Josh uses the Mount of Transfiguration as a guide to the Testaments: Moses and Elijah appear with Jesus, and the Father commands the disciples to listen to his Son.
      • The finality of the new covenant does not diminish the Old Testament. Christ enables believers to see its divine Author more clearly, and Josh commends mature Christians who search for Jesus there and submit to all Scripture.

    3. 21:13 The last days began with the Son’s arrival

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:1-2a Matthew 24:36; Mark 13:32

      • Josh reads Hebrews 1:1–2a again and identifies the period from the birth of God’s Son to the present as the last days.
      • Though that period feels long, only the Father knows its length.
      • The theological point is that what God intended to communicate through Jesus has been said; no additional prophetic communication is needed to complete it.

  4. 21:48 Second portrait: Jesus is God’s Son

    • The title “Son” grounds Christ’s equality with the Father, the comfort of his divine power to save, and the call to put hope and trust in him.

    1. 21:48 The familiar title “Son” carries a world-dividing claim

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:1-2a

      • Josh reads again that God has spoken by his Son and warns the church not to take that statement for granted merely because Christians unite around it.
      • Explanations of the Father-Son relationship can fall short, so Josh values the Nicene Creed’s language: Christ is “God of God, light of light, very God of very God,” fully God and fully man, coequal with the Father.
      • He admits that the mechanics exceed his understanding while emphasizing the Father and Son’s relationship of love, delight, and dedication.

    2. 23:09 Seeing Jesus is seeing the Father

      Scripture: John 14:8-10

      • Josh quotes Philip’s request in John 14 to see the Father and Jesus’ answer that anyone who has seen him has seen the Father.
      • Jesus asks Philip to believe the mutual indwelling of Father and Son; Christ therefore cannot be treated as a merely optional or secondary doctrine.

    3. 23:54 The Savior’s deity is the believer’s greatest comfort

      Scripture: Luke 2:13-14

      • Josh calls Christ’s divinity the greatest comfort of his life: the one sent to save sinners is divine, and the Creator cannot fail in saving.
      • Because Jesus is omnipotent, no class, history, ethnicity, wealth, or sin places a person beyond his reach.
      • The angels’ praise when Christ appears leads Josh to ask how the hearer will respond and whether the hearer is ready to trust Jesus.

  5. 24:36 Third and fourth portraits: Jesus is heir and Creator

    • Everything belongs to the appointed heir, and everything that exists came through the Creator. His unlimited authority supplies salvation, and his creative power means no sinner is beyond renewal.

    1. 24:36 The appointed heir possesses all things

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:1-2b Hebrews 2:8 Philippians 2:10-11

      • Josh reads that the Son was appointed heir of all things and explains that all authority, creation, and power belong to him.
      • Christ’s rule is not yet fully visible, but every angel, ruler, molecule, and rebel remains under his authority.
      • One day every knee will bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.
      • This matters now because the Savior lacks neither power, grace, nor mercy; everything necessary to save has already been possessed and poured out.

    2. 25:45 Christ is no uncertain counselor, and his people share his inheritance

      Scripture: Isaiah 9:6 Colossians 1:16 Romans 8:16-17

      • A comic comparison with an underqualified therapist who must send a difficult client elsewhere emphasizes that Jesus never discovers a sinner beyond his expertise.
      • Josh calls him the wonderful Counselor who holds all things in his hands, and he quotes the claim that all things were created by and for Christ.
      • Because the Spirit testifies that believers are God’s children, they are heirs of God and co-heirs with Christ, sharing both his sufferings and his glory.
      • Josh compresses the application into a promise: trust Jesus and lack nothing.

    3. 27:05 The Son made the universe

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:1-2c

      • Josh reads that God made the universe through his Son and extends the claim beyond earth to every planet, galaxy, star, atom, and strand of DNA.
      • His memories of looking at the night sky with his son Nolan lead into playful uncertainty about extraterrestrial life, but the conclusion does not change: wherever anything exists, Christ caused and governs it.
      • Nothing discovered now or later can fall outside Jesus’ creative power.

    4. 29:00 The one who made everything can remake sinners

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:1-2c

      • Josh applies cosmic creation personally: the one who spoke the universe into being can speak life into a dead heart and form holiness in a sinner.
      • Human inventions such as voicemail are trivial beside Christ’s creation of new life.
      • No person is too far gone or beyond repair for the maker of the universe; the one trusted with planets, orbits, seasons, and time can be trusted with a soul.

  6. 29:51 Fifth portrait: Jesus is God’s exact representation and sustainer

    • Hebrews identifies Jesus as the radiance and exact imprint of God’s own being. Israel’s visible encounters with glory pointed toward this person, whose powerful word now upholds creation and can uphold suffering believers.

    1. 29:51 Hypostasis names God’s actual essence

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:3a

      • Josh reads that the Son is the radiance of God’s glory and the exact representation of his being.
      • He explains the Greek *hypostasis*, sometimes rendered “nature,” as God’s actual essence or substance.
      • Jesus is therefore not a copy or shadow that merely reflects glory; he is the light itself shining from God, the exact imprint of God’s nature.

    2. 30:16 Israel’s visible glory anticipated glory in a person

      Scripture: Exodus 19:16-20 Exodus 40:34-35 Exodus 25:22 1 Samuel 4:21-22 Exodus 3:1-6

      • Josh recalls Sinai burning with God’s glory, the glory filling the tabernacle, and God’s presence above the ark between the cherubim.
      • When the Philistines captured the ark in 1 Samuel 4, Israel lamented that the glory had departed.
      • Generations longed for its return, and Hebrews announces that it appeared—not in a cloud, mountain, veil, or burning bush, but in Jesus Christ.

    3. 31:10 Jesus does not wear holiness; he is God’s holiness in flesh

      Scripture: Exodus 28:36-38 Hebrews 1:3a Exodus 3:5

      • The high priest wore “Holy to the Lord” on his forehead as a symbol, but Josh says Jesus embodies rather than merely wears God’s holiness.
      • The bush, Sinai, tabernacle, and temple appearances all pointed toward the fullest, clearest display of divine glory in Christ.
      • The decisive question is not where God’s glory can be found but how a person will respond to the one who is God’s glory.

    4. 32:14 The word sustaining the universe can sustain the suffering believer

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:3b

      • Josh returns to Hebrews 1:3 and emphasizes that Christ sustains all things by his powerful word; without that sustaining speech, creation would cease.
      • As a foundation supports a house, Christ holds up all things.
      • Because he upholds the universe, he can uphold people who have endured great pain. Nothing has escaped his word, and that same word can surround the believer who trusts him.

  7. 32:56 Sixth portrait: Jesus finished purification for sins

    • Josh treats sin as universal unbelief rather than an external problem, then reads Christ’s purification and heavenly session as completed work. The gospel joins justice and mercy, forgiveness and transformation, perfect standing and continuing holiness.

    1. 32:56 Purification confronts the reality of sin

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:3c-d

      • Josh reads that after providing purification for sins, Jesus sat down at the Majesty’s right hand.
      • He calls this his favorite phrase in the passage because the church may avoid the word “sin,” yet the world, the headlines, and each person’s internal conflict make sin undeniable.
      • Sin is not merely an outside contamination from which respectable people can isolate; it lives within Josh and every hearer.

    2. 33:52 The accuser’s categories yield to level ground at the cross

      Scripture: Revelation 12:10

      • Josh points to Revelation’s name for Satan as the accuser and links the Greek term to categorizing people.
      • “Us versus them” places blame elsewhere, but the ground at the cross is level: Christians do not stand beside it ordering others to improve; they kneel and say there is room for others too.

    3. 34:24 At its core, sin refuses to trust God’s way

      • Josh defines sin’s root as unbelief: telling God that his way is not better and choosing self-direction instead.
      • That posture then spreads into marriage, relationships, decisions, discipleship, and private life, leaving people fractured and empty.
      • He calls personal sin against a holy God the greatest injustice, preparing the contrast between divine justice and mercy at the cross.

    4. 34:58 The cross displays justice against sin and mercy toward sinners

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:3c-d Leviticus 16:1-34

      • The cross displays God’s justice against sin and God’s mercy toward sinners at the same time.
      • Josh emphasizes Hebrews’ past tense: Jesus “provided” purification and then “sat down,” signifying that the saving work is finished.
      • The annual Day of Atonement and repeated animal sacrifices no longer continue, because Christ shed his own blood and has nothing left to add.
      • Josh interprets the temple’s fall in AD 70 as another declaration that the sacrificial work is done.

    5. 36:08 In Christ, sins are dealt with rather than awaiting payment

      Scripture: Ephesians 4:15

      • For those in Christ, past, present, and future sins are not being dealt with; they have been dealt with and purified.
      • Josh acknowledges that this message is dangerous to self-righteousness, control, pride, boasting, and every attempt to earn access to God.
      • A person who treats grace as permission to sin has misunderstood the gospel and does not know Christ; the same grace that forgives also changes.

    6. 37:13 One sacrifice makes perfect those being made holy

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:14

      • Josh quotes Hebrews 10:14 and says the series will revisit it: by one sacrifice Christ has made perfect forever those who are being made holy.
      • The apparently strange grammar expresses Christian life’s tension—full acceptance alongside continuing transformation.
      • The question is whether the hearer trusts the only one who purified sins and can truly make a person new, or still insists on running life independently.

  8. 37:56 Seventh portrait: Jesus is superior to angels and every earlier figure

    • Hebrews 1 names the Son superior to angels, and Josh turns that claim into a Christ-centered method for reading all Scripture. Adam, Isaac, and Solomon cannot save; God’s own testimony identifies Jesus as Son, God, righteous King, eternal Creator, and enthroned Lord.

    1. 37:56 The whole book announces Christ’s preeminence

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:4

      • Josh reads that Jesus became as much superior to the angels as his inherited name is superior to theirs.
      • He summarizes Hebrews as one claim: Christ is superior to every prophet, angel, and messenger; Jesus is preeminent.
      • The letter moves Jewish believers from the old to the better covenant by showing that Jesus is greater.

    2. 38:29 Hermeneutics draws God’s meaning out before applying it

      • Josh defines hermeneutics as reading the Bible rightly—drawing God’s meaning out rather than importing the reader’s ideas.
      • Personal relation and application are legitimate, but meaning comes first and application follows.
      • His central interpretive rule is simple: God is the Author inviting the reader to see Jesus.

    3. 39:11 Adam, Isaac, and Solomon point beyond themselves to Jesus

      Scripture: Romans 5:12-19 Matthew 4:1-11; Luke 4:1-13 Genesis 22:1-14 Ecclesiastes 1:9 Revelation 21:5 2 Corinthians 5:21

      • Humanity waits through history for a savior. Adam fails through passivity, but Josh presents Jesus as the greater Adam who answers Satan’s wilderness temptation with God’s word and credits his obedience to his people.
      • Isaac is spared when heaven says stop, but Jesus is the greater Isaac who is not spared and becomes the sacrifice for sinners.
      • Solomon can only say there is nothing new under the sun, while the greater Solomon declares that he makes all things new.
      • These contrasts train the church to look beyond every partial figure toward Christ.

    4. 40:08 God testifies that the Son is greater than angels

      Scripture: Romans 7:24-25 Hebrews 1:5 Hebrews 1:6-7

      • Josh joins Paul’s cry for deliverance from the body of death to the repeated failure of Moses, David, Jonah, Joshua, and every other human candidate to save.
      • He then reads Hebrews 1:5–7: God never addressed an angel as his Son, commands the angels to worship the firstborn, and describes angels as spirits and servants like flames of fire.
      • The angels serve as messengers; Jesus is God’s own Son. Josh says the writer’s use of Psalms supplies a Christ-centered hermeneutic.

    5. 41:04 The Father addresses the Son as the eternal God and righteous King

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:8 Psalm 45:6 Hebrews 1:9

      • Hebrews addresses the Son: his throne as God lasts forever, and justice is the scepter of his kingdom.
      • Josh explicitly identifies the throne line as Psalm 45:6 and argues that, whatever possible relation it has to Solomon, its original and exclusive focus appears to him to be the Messiah.
      • Jesus is the true temple, prophet, priest, king, sacrifice, lamb, light, and bread; God sets him above his companions because he loves righteousness and hates wickedness.

    6. 41:43 Christ’s righteousness teaches his people to hate wickedness

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:21

      • Josh pauses over the claim that hating wickedness is godly and resists a culture that makes Christians feel unloving for doing so.
      • He quotes 2 Corinthians 5:21 and explains the “double imputation”: Jesus receives what sinners deserve, while believers receive a righteousness they did not deserve.
      • Those who have become God’s righteousness should increasingly love what Jesus loves and hate what he hates.

    7. 42:34 The Son remains when creation wears out

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:10-12 Hebrews 1:13-14

      • Josh lists Christ’s superior name, dignity, nature, role, example, work, achievement, and destiny.
      • He reads Hebrews 1:10–12: the Lord founded earth and heaven, creation will perish and wear out, and he will roll it up like a garment while remaining unchanged forever.
      • He then reads that no angel was invited to sit at God’s right hand until every enemy became a footstool; angels are ministering spirits sent to serve those who will inherit salvation.

    8. 43:23 The Father’s love for his Son fills heaven with service

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:13-14

      • Josh delights in hearing God testify to his beloved Son’s superiority and compares that delight, cautiously, with his own pride and care as a father.
      • If an earthly father loves his son deeply, God’s love for his beloved Son is immeasurably greater.
      • The chapter’s closing line also comforts future heirs: God assigns angels to minister to his children, as though sending heaven after them.

  9. 44:07 Eighth portrait: Jesus is heaven’s champion who makes all things new

    • The unchanging Son will fold up fallen creation, establish the promised world without death, and bring purified heirs home. Josh closes by calling unbelievers to release what is killing them, trust Christ, repent, be baptized, and listen to God’s final speech in his preeminent Son.

    1. 44:07 The day of the Lord will remove the fallen order

      Scripture: 2 Peter 3:10 2 Peter 3:13 Hebrews 1:10-12

      • Josh’s eighth claim is that Jesus is heaven’s champion, the one who will wrap creation like a blanket.
      • He quotes Peter’s description of the day of the Lord arriving like a thief, the heavens disappearing with a roar, the elements being destroyed by fire, and earth’s deeds being exposed.
      • Because sin has marked the present creation, the old order will give way to a new heaven and new earth.

    2. 44:38 Waiting for God to dwell with his people is the church’s oil of joy

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:9 Revelation 21:3-5

      • Josh calls the promised future the church’s “oil of joy”: waiting, longing, worshiping, serving, and belonging until the fulfillment arrives.
      • He quotes Revelation 21: God will dwell among his people, wipe every tear, and remove death, mourning, crying, and pain because the old order has passed.
      • The enthroned Lord announces that he is making everything new.

    3. 45:21 The finished work leaves no sin for the believer to recover

      Scripture: Psalm 103:12 John 19:30 1 Corinthians 15:4 Acts 1:3, 9; Hebrews 1:3d

      • Josh tells believers they may search for their sins but will not find them, because Christ removed them as far as east is from west.
      • At the cross Jesus did not manage sin; he purged, bore, canceled, and put it away forever, declaring, “It is finished.”
      • Josh points to Christ’s resurrection on the third day and his ascension after forty days, then returns to the seated Christ as evidence that salvation’s work is done.
      • The completed work does not authorize passivity: “Now, go live for him.” Those who belong to Christ possess peace and joy forever.

    4. 46:17 Unbelievers are invited to release unbelief and come to Jesus

      Scripture: Acts 2:38

      • Josh welcomes those who do not yet trust Jesus but asks why they maintain a tight grip on what is killing them and turn away from the one offering salvation.
      • All divine power to save and all God’s love are found in Jesus; Josh therefore calls them to come, trust, repent, and be baptized.
      • Everyone in Christ is guaranteed an inheritance: sins are gone and the future is secure.

    5. 46:55 God has spoken in his preeminent Son—will the hearer listen?

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:2a

      • Josh welcomes the congregation to Hebrews and returns to the letter’s opening declaration that God has spoken in his Son.
      • Because Christ is preeminent, the unresolved question is not whether he speaks but whether the hearer will listen.
      • Josh begins a prayer of love and thanks for God’s word and for the Father’s love of the Son; the uploaded video ends abruptly before the prayer’s conclusion.
