Overview

  1. 00:12 Be filled with the Spirit

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:21 Ephesians 5:18 Matthew 25

    • Ten weeks into Ephesians, Paul transitions to an extensive layout of God’s blueprint for Christian households, a discussion that continues well into chapter 6. First Josh recalls a key phrase Chris covered last week from the back half of verse 18: “Be filled with the spirit.”
    • The exact instant you surrender to Jesus as Lord and call out to him as Savior, you receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. How people respond varies; many believers still meander around with training wheels and diapers because they haven’t developed godly habits. God gives the gift, but having the Spirit is different from being filled with the Spirit. It takes effort, prayer, submission, and obedience.
    • Jesus makes clear in the parable of the talents that how we use what God gives matters. Jesus will return; we don’t know when, but the call is the same: don’t waste the gift. Use your gifts, talents, resources, and every opportunity to do good and build up the church. Keep watch, don’t cause others to stumble, but don’t be afraid to take risks. Walk in faith, not fear, and be faithful in small things.

  2. 02:28 The overlooked result: submission

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:21

    • We could list the results of being filled with the Spirit all day: joy, love, patience, kindness, gentleness, generosity, humility. But one result doesn’t get much airtime or make the headlines, and Josh argues it is one of the most important: submission. When we are filled with the Spirit, the resulting attitude is mutual submission.

    1. 03:16 A working definition: hypotasso

      • Josh offers to sit down with anyone who objects to his definition and show them how to do a proper word study. He isn’t trying to sell a self-help book; he just wants people to fall in love with the holy book.
      • Paul uses hypotasso, a military word meaning to be under obedience: subordinate, put under, rank beneath, bring under control. This isn’t something done to you; you are placing yourself under control and keeping yourself under rank, out of reverence for Christ. Willing submission is done in fear of God, not fear of man.

    2. 04:51 Outranked by a dummy

      • Josh asks veterans to stand and thanks them; he’ll never forget what his salvation cost or what his freedom cost. Then: have you ever been outranked by a dummy? How many are smarter than their boss?
      • We don’t submit because the person is smarter than us, or even because they’re a good or bad boss. We submit because they hold authority. The military calls it mutiny when the low-ranked no longer wish to be under obedience. Without levels of authority a military is weak, and society and the church weaken the same way. It’s a hot mess.

    3. 06:26 Mutual submission doesn’t erase authority

      Scripture: 1 Peter 5:5 1 Timothy 4:12 Hebrews 13:17

      • This is where the foundation for the Christian household is set. The anti-authority crowd argues that if we submit to one another, there are no higher positions of authority in the church or home. Wrong. That deceitful thinking says, “We won’t submit. You should submit to us.” Christians don’t handle business like this.
      • Peter says the younger should submit to their elders and all should clothe themselves with humility, because God opposes the proud. Peter may not have only age in mind, since Paul tells Timothy not to let them look down on him because of his age; sometimes there are gray-haired babies and real grown-up dark hairs. Hebrews 13 says to have confidence in your leaders and submit to their authority, because they keep watch over you as those who must give an account. Somebody’s answering to somebody.

    4. 07:58 The church as a football team

      • Josh, a bit of a jock in high school, thinks of the church like a football team: players, the quarterback as general on the field, coaches, general managers, owners. In a healthy organization the levels of authority don’t matter because everyone wants what’s best for the team, but if they don’t, there are people who can override.
      • The church should have a team-first attitude: when someone succeeds or is blessed, we all are; when someone struggles, we all do. If one person goes diva, all Terrell Owens on us, we have a mutiny and we’re toast.

    5. 09:06 Don’t let your kids run your home

      • Imagine letting your children run your home; some don’t have to imagine. That’s not the design. Children don’t tell parents what to do or control the situation with temper tantrums. There is an order, a divine blueprint, with authorities.
      • Coming home from a Diamondbacks game, Josh told his kids the best gift he can give his son isn’t a trust or anything financial; it is faithfulness to his wife for 50 years as an example. The marriage is the focal point of the home, not the children.

    6. 10:06 When authorities abuse their position

      • Do some authorities abuse their position? Yes. But that doesn’t permit an attitude of rebellion. Trust God to exact justice in his timing and way. Tell your leaders; godly leaders will help restore you and discipline the person in question. If they don’t, go find a church led by godly men who love Jesus.
      • If submission matters in the church, how much more does it matter in the home?

  3. 10:48 The state of marriage

    • Half of all marriages end in divorce, and almost half of our children will go to bed tonight without saying good night to their father. As Chris mentioned last week, cohabitation is skyrocketing, and people are opting out of marriage.
    • Some say Christian marriages are less likely to end in divorce. Not true: most non-believers just aren’t marrying. Christians feel obligated to marry, but often marry someone just because they say they love Jesus, without inspecting their lives or inviting family to look for fruit or a track record of obedience.

    1. 11:40 Single ladies: look for the Lord’s marks

      • “I love Jesus” isn’t the testimony of a godly man; it’s the testimony of a Sunday school brat. You need a man of God who has taken things out back and killed them because Jesus called him to a holy life. You shouldn’t have to ask on a date whether Jesus is Lord of his life; you should see the Lord’s marks on him.
      • Men of God love Jesus by obeying him, daily putting themselves under obedience, under rank: he is our general and I am his lieutenant, here to serve.
      • A woman of Christ will never be able to fully submit to a man who doesn’t chase Jesus. If he is chasing something else, he will take you away from your first love, and it is so hard to get back. Your life, marriage, kids, and home will never fully be what God designed.

  4. 13:08 Before the wives’ verse

    • Before “the feminist’s favorite Bible verse,” Josh says Paul is speaking to Christian marriages and households, to followers of Jesus. Paul does not suggest, and neither does Josh, that women are inferior to men, or that all women must submit to all men in every situation.
    • There is biblical instruction that a married woman with questions about what is said in church should go home and ask her husband, because the assumption is he will know the answer since he is chasing Jesus. Ladies are up first; in every instruction on biblical marriage in Scripture, the men go last.

  5. 14:25 Wives, submit to your own husbands

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:22

    • The translation can be tricky and much in-depth study goes into it, so here is Josh’s “official commentary” on verse 22: he simply reads it again.
    • If you’re not a Christian, you don’t submit to the Lord, so this doesn’t apply to you. If you are a married Christian woman, it does. But Scripture isn’t saying women are inferior: Josh’s family recently took IQ tests, and he has the lowest IQ in the family, about 122 to his wife’s 140. He’s the idiot, and she still lets him lead. He loves her for that.

    1. 15:52 A cultural shock in Ephesus

      • “But Josh, you haven’t met my husband.” In Paul’s day women couldn’t vote, own land, hold office, testify in court, or get a higher education. Men had many wives and girlfriends, treating life as a playground until taking a young wife for legitimate offspring.
      • Imagine new believers in Ephesus hearing Paul’s letter read and realizing their marriage looks nothing like this. They faced the same question you do: will you revise your thinking now that you’ve heard God’s word, or attempt to revise God’s word to preserve your current thinking? The ball is in your court, and this won’t be the first time Scripture offends you.

    2. 17:27 Two reasons: Christ is head of the church

      Scripture: Ephesians 5:23–24

      • Paul gives two reasons for his command to Christian wives. When a Christian wife submits to Christ as Lord, she will have no issue submitting to her husband, because they will run into each other in their submission to Christ. If both are submitted to Christ as Lord, there will be harmony.

  6. 18:18 Men: headship is not dictatorship

    • When Josh and Kristen bought their first truck, an F-150, the add-on salesman pulled them into a back room. When Josh said he didn’t know what his wife would think, the salesman asked, “Aren’t you the king of your castle?” knowing he was a pastor. Josh told him that’s not how they do things: “We do things together.” He walked out without the add-ons.
    • Yes, men have God-given authority in the home, but why? To bless God, your wife, your kids, your church, and your community. Godly men don’t ask for a blessing because they know they are tasked with being the blessing.
    • Husbands and wives should pray together, be in the word together, teach their children around the word, and serve, sing, worship, and follow Christ together. Without Christ you can have a good marriage, but with Christ it can become a divine blessing to those around you. Ordinary or extraordinary: the difference is submission.

  7. 20:54 Ladies: love your man by respecting him

    • Josh has a few fundamentals for the ladies. One of the best ways to love your man is respect. Men thrive on respect; nearly every time Josh gets angry and lashes out, it is because he feels disrespected. Scripture’s wisdom on the kind of wife to be is great instruction for both the struggling wife and the single man.

    1. 21:29 Better to live in a desert

      Scripture: Proverbs 21:19

      • We all live in a desert here, but assume no air conditioning and no fresh water: we die. Scripture says that between a nagging wife and inevitable death in a desert, it’d be better to suffer in the desert.
      • Solomon prayed for wisdom and God poured it into him. The wisdom of God says nagging is going to kill your husband. Constant worry, fretting, and unrealistic expectations wound your man. Stop picking fights, stop looking for something wrong to gnaw at, and stop contributing to suffering in your home.

    2. 22:56 The dripping of a leaky roof

      Scripture: Proverbs 27:15–16

      • Ancient water torture was simple: water droplets falling on a person’s forehead for hours. Drip, drip, drip. It drives people mad. Women, are you torturing your husband?
      • Nothing is more destructive to a man’s pursuit of God’s calling in the home than trying to lead a woman who tears him down and tries to dominate him. Men thrive when shown respect.

  8. 24:00 Let him lead

    • “Josh, he won’t lead.” There are ways to help him without tearing him down; nagging won’t get the job done. Try a loving nudge: offer an idea, help him, but let him lead. The man is called the head and the woman the helper; things go wrong when the helper becomes a hater. Point out what he does well, build rapport, then throw out an idea.
    • Many women hear that the man should lead and assume they should do nothing and can’t have a vision for the home. That isn’t healthy, and waiting around will make you a hater, not a helper. Call a meeting with paper and pen, develop a vision for your home together for five, ten, twenty years and retirement, pray over it, and ask him to lead you there.
    • We talk about passive men, but passive women exist too. Empower him; don’t harm him. A Christian wife’s careful, thoughtful words can change the trajectory of a home and revolutionize her man.

  9. 26:05 Build a friendship with him

    • Find things you enjoy together, and laugh and have a good time together.
    • Josh’s family loves the pool, but Josh’s wife hardly ever gets in, worried about her hair and the “windchill” in 110 degrees. When she does jump in, it changes the whole backyard; Nolan’s face says, “Mommy just jumped in head first.” It fills Josh’s heart for a month. Monopoly, on the other hand, sends them to counseling, so they avoid it.

  10. 27:43 Husbands display God’s pursuit

    • As we’ll see in the next verse, Christian men need to be Jesus to their wives, not in a domineering stance but a comforting one. The wife learns about God’s love, forgiveness, grace, and mercy in Scripture and experiences it in the home. God pursues her, and the husband displays the pursuit.
    • Headship isn’t chauvinism or sexism; it is humility and servant leadership. Not all men are natural leaders, but the servant takes up the mantle. Learn to lead, men.

    1. 28:29 When your husband doesn’t look like Jesus

      Scripture: Ephesians 5:22 1 Peter 3 1 Peter 3:1–2

      • Women who struggle with Ephesians 5:22 are usually stuck with a man who doesn’t look like Jesus, or even worse, have no man at all. Most of the leading feminist thinkers of the 70s were not married and had no children, yet told people how to be married and raise children. Don’t listen to the fad; go to God’s word.
      • If your husband fails to lead and love, study 1 Peter 3. Peter instructs wives to win him not with insults and raindrops of torture but with their actions. Submit to the Lord and get to work in the home so he sees you.
      • Josh has seen wives in this church win their husbands with inward beauty, then step aside and let their husbands lead even though they had followed Jesus for years. Pray for him day and night and build him up with your words. Women have tremendous power. Godly men are trustworthy, and because they look like Christ, submission comes easily and naturally to the believing wife.

  11. 30:42 Husbands, love your wives

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:25–27

    • That’s Paul’s word to the wives, much shorter than the men’s part. Women learned that men want respect; now men learn that women love to be loved. Paul sets the highest bar: love your wives just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her, to make her holy and present her radiant.
    • Her beauty is not what builds your love; your love is what builds her beauty. Did Jesus love you because of your beauty? No. As you experienced his love, you became a masterpiece.

    1. 32:02 Love her with purpose

      Scripture: Romans 3:23

      • Christ knew what we needed before he decided how best to love us. We needed a Savior; the Lamb of God took our sin and willingly died in our place. Jesus loved us at the place we needed him most.
      • Don’t generally love her or Hallmark-card love her. Love her with purpose: figure out who she is, what she likes and doesn’t, what she needs and doesn’t, and love her in a way she can hear.

    2. 32:44 Homework: two questions

      • Josh dares husbands to ask two questions: What do I do specifically that makes you feel loved? And the big scary one: What do I do that makes you feel unloved? When she tells you what makes her feel loved, listen, then create a plan to love her more like that. When she tells you what makes her feel unloved, listen, own it, and stop it. Be humble.
      • He prays over the homework, since Satan will be in an uproar: that the Lord would protect them as they go home to purify their homes and present their wives holy to him.

    3. 34:04 Every wife is different; adapt

      • Responses will vary; what works in Josh’s home may not work in yours. Some women like time, gifts, touch, encouraging words, or acts of service, and some have what Josh calls “the rotational desire” and like them all. If you’re married to that woman, good luck; he’s praying for you.
      • Just because she felt loved once by one thing doesn’t mean you repackage it to get to the 50th anniversary. She’ll change, you’ll change, kids come, careers change. You have to adapt.

    4. 35:21 Agape: love that gives

      • Paul uses agape: not an emotional or reactionary love but a thoughtful, pre-planned, unchanging, unwavering, self-giving, sacrificial love, so unconditional it can be given to the unlovable and continue in the face of rejection. We give because we love, not love in order to receive. Christ gave himself for the church not because it was deserving but because he wanted to.
      • Men argue back: “She doesn’t know the Lord. She isn’t kind, she keeps record of wrongs, she’s bitter. It was a mistake to marry her.” But Paul destroys that effort to slip expectations: Christ loved not because there was loveliness in his church already; he loved the church in order to make her lovely. Love her.

    5. 36:42 Speak your love

      • Josh thinks of Christ’s love as constant, never in question, because the Bible tells him so. We don’t feel God’s love so much as become confident in it, because we hear and read it again and again. Many men fail to speak their love. Tell her she’s beautiful, tell her she’s a good mom; look at her like Christ does and tell her what he would see.
      • Loving like Christ involves romance too. It’s the small stuff: come home early and cook with her, make her coffee and hand it to her when she walks out of the bedroom, help clean up after dinner so she has energy for “dessert.”

    6. 37:55 Washing with the word

      Scripture: John 17:17

      • Your role as priest in the home begins in your marriage. Speaking God’s promises to your wife matters more than your own words of affirmation. Christ cleanses his church through the ministry of the word, and that ministry needs to happen in your home.
      • Husbands can’t cleanse their wives the way Jesus does, but they can ensure their wives grow spiritually and are bathed in the word so they never dry up. The water Paul talks about isn’t baptism; that water is the word. A Christian husband hates not only his own sin but the sin that seeks to pollute his bride, wanting her cleansed as he has been so that she is radiant when the Lord returns.

  12. 39:03 Love her as your own body

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:28–30

    • Christian homes picture Christ and his church in physical things too. Josh and Kristen aren’t two individuals anymore; they surrendered that when they gave themselves to God and one another. They didn’t settle every issue of one flesh the next morning and had work to do, but they are one: emotionally, spiritually, financially, socially, in every part of life. I hurt, she hurts; I go broke, she goes broke; we rejoice and mourn together.
    • Remember the bathroom scene in Liar Liar where he beats himself up? That is the picture Josh gets from Paul here. Mistreat, hit, shove, or scream at your wife and you are beating yourself up. Cheat on her or look at porn instead of enjoying your bride, and you’re beating yourself up in the bathroom. You’re a fool.
    • The root of most marital issues is sin, and the root of all sin is selfishness. Submission to Christ and one another is God’s solution to the selfishness problem: love her, and by loving her you love yourself. “Trust me, I designed it.” Stop living for yourself.

  13. 41:28 Priest, protector, provider

    Scripture: Philippians 4:19 1 Timothy 5:8

    • Just as God supplies our needs in Christ, the husband seeks to supply all the needs of his wife. That’s the role: priest, protector, provider. Scripture goes so far as to say failing to provide for your family makes you worse than an unbeliever.

    1. 41:50 Does this mean women can’t work?

      Scripture: Proverbs 31

      • No; that’s ridiculous. Maybe it wasn’t necessary when houses cost $40,000, but the average American house is now about $410,000. And to pastors who say the best gift is letting your wife stay home: raise your kids at home, fantastic, but the Proverbs 31 woman is busy running a business, cooking family dinner, and keeping the house in order.
      • Josh married a Proverbs 31 woman. She has always worked and was blessed to work at home, so they could raise the kids at home. That may not be everyone’s situation, and that’s okay. He thanks God for her and tells her she is God’s gift to him, gentle, beautiful, amazing, and his forever.

    2. 43:15 Fifteen seconds on your knees

      • Josh gives husbands 15 seconds to tell their wives how much they appreciate and love them. Optionally: we hit our knees to ask her to marry us, so when was the last time you hit your knees in front of her? No songs, no sonnets, no guitars. Ushers can help anyone who can’t get back up.

  14. 44:22 Leave and cleave

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:31 Genesis 2:24

    • God’s blueprint for marriage didn’t change from Adam to Paul, and it’s the same today: leave mom and dad, unite to your wife, cleave to her, become one.
    • In enriching marriages, Josh and Chris often find a wife, a husband, or tragically both have failed to leave father and mother. A wedding is such an occasion, with invitations, cakes, dresses, and venues, because it is the birthday of a new family with a new headship; all old authorities are cut off. We still love and care for our parents and never abandon them, but they no longer control our lives. Leave and cleave means be cemented together. And Josh goes on record: three cords don’t include mama.

  15. 45:49 A generation of men who lead like Jesus

    • What drives Josh mad is men who plead for reform but won’t reform themselves, who call for national change but refuse personal change. God, give us a generation of men who rise up as servant leaders and lead like Jesus. It’s what the church, our homes, and this country need.
    • The last thing we need is more men in the church predicting Christ’s return. We need men preparing for it: preparing their homes, their church, their communities, and their wives, that she would be radiant. Christians are preparers, not predictors.

  16. 46:56 How do we do it?

    Scripture: John 7:37–38

    • To be filled with the Spirit, you must first possess the Spirit. Jesus said whoever thirsts should come to him and drink, and rivers of living water will flow from within whoever believes in him; he isn’t talking about Gatorade.
    • By faith, right now, submit to Christ, ask him to fill you with the Spirit, and walk in faith. When you find a new joy and a new gentleness, when you become submissive, you will know God heard your cry and answered. Now go wash their feet.

  17. 47:48 A profound mystery: gospel reflection

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:32–33

    • Paul calls it a profound mystery about Christ and the church, then reminds each husband to love his wife as himself and the wife to respect her husband.
    • The gospel is the foundation of any Christian household. God served you, took on flesh, and laid down his life for the church; now go do likewise.
    • Marriage is hard because it isn’t about happiness but holiness. People enter marriage hoping to find perfect love, but God uses marriage to perfect our love. It’s not about finding the right person but becoming the right person, becoming a better servant. Ephesians 5 isn’t about roles; it’s about reflection: gospel reflection through mutual submission. When we follow God’s blueprint we find peace, comfort, and joy; our children and church are blessed, our community notices, and God is glorified.

  18. 49:01 Closing prayer

    Scripture: John 15:13

    • Josh thanks God for teaching him to love through Christ, who laid his life down for him, and asks for help to do the same for his wife, so that their communities, churches, and families radiate from Christ-centered marriages. He pleads for husbands who will go home and ask, “How can I better love you?”
    • He acknowledges the brokenness in the room and prays that those hurting would find their joy in submission directly to God, that he would heal broken hearts so they can sing and see him in life’s tragedies, so the church can thrive and the gospel has victory.
