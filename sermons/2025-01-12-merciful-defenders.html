Overview

  1. 00:11 A city without walls

    Scripture: Proverbs 25:28

    • Josh opens with a proverb for men in chapter 25, verse 28: a man without self-control is like a city broken into and left without walls. He reminds himself of it constantly: who wants to live in a city without walls, or a nation uninterested in protecting her citizens?
    • Throughout history walls have protected people from harm, and they have become more advanced; in 2023, 13–15% of the federal budget, $820 billion, went to defense. Where there is life there is a desire to survive, and this country is worth defending. Josh quotes his favorite president, Abraham Lincoln, at Gettysburg: “that government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth.”
    • The simple rule of life: when you have something precious, you defend it with all your strength. Josh loves that he can sing praises, share God’s word, and pray together without fearing for his life or family, and that the country doesn’t require Crossroads Church to pay taxes. We the people put a government in place that empowers the church to be the church; the First Amendment says Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion or prohibiting the free exercise thereof. Do you realize how much that sentence has changed your life?

    1. 02:52 Spoiled American Christians

      Scripture: Revelation 1–3 Revelation 3:17

      • Compare that with North Korea, where the Kim regime considers Christians a hostile class and possessing Scripture can mean arrest or even execution. The truth is American Christians are spoiled and soft; Josh honestly thinks most would deny their faith in less than ideal circumstances.
      • The church has a history of getting off track. In the first three chapters of Revelation, our Lord, who sees the enemy climbing the walls, writes to Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, and Laodicea. Ephesus perseveres but forgot its first love and is called to remember and repent. Smyrna endures poverty and persecution, and the Lord doesn’t rebuke them; he tells them not to be afraid and remain faithful.
      • The Lord saves his harshest rebukes for those who have the most. The wealthiest church, Laodicea, said “we lack nothing,” and the Lord said, “Actually, you’re poor, blind and naked.” The more we have, the more likely we are to fall into complacency.
      • Every pastor in America wants to think his church is Philadelphia or Smyrna, all commendation and no rebuke, but most American churches are more like Thyatira and Pergamum: churches of tolerance that tolerate compromise, half-truths, and sin. Many are okay with the Lord outside the door as long as their biggest donors are inside. People leave when the church does hard things, but the Lord’s true church under his headship seeks only God’s approval.

  2. 05:48 We need more merciful defenders

    • As Josh works through Jude’s short but fiery letter, he keeps coming back to one thought: the church needs to build up stronger defenders. Why do American Christians seem more concerned with defending their precious freedom than their precious faith? Why not both?
    • God’s word and the gift of eternal life have infinite value, and the message has been entrusted to his people. But many oppose God’s word, and Satan uses them to seek and destroy: they twist it, change it, offer their own gospel, don’t rebuke sin, and look the other way. We don’t need more of that; we need more merciful defenders.

  3. 06:45 Clear orders: the faith once for all entrusted

    Scripture: Jude 3 Revelation 1:1

    • Josh was never a soldier, though military ministry came close, but soldiers have told him one of the hardest parts is unclear orders. We have clear objectives and know exactly what we are defending.
    • He returns to last week’s verse 3: “I felt compelled to write and urge you to contend for the faith that the Lord has once for all entrusted to us, his people.” Once for all: God has given his complete revelation in the Holy Scriptures, and the full counsel of God is available in the Bible.
    • Christians often ask Josh to start a study on “Revelations.” He likes to mess with them, “What’s Revelations?”, until they protest that he knows it’s the last book of the Bible. “Oh, you must mean Revelation”: the revelation from Jesus Christ, which God gave him to show his servants what must soon take place.

    1. 08:41 Defender 101: the warning label

      Scripture: Revelation 22:18–21

      • Defender 101: when someone claims a special revelation from a dream, a vision, or an angel, or more tablets only they can read with “super duper divine tablet goggles,” we laugh, but that is what the Mormons want you to believe, and we can immediately reject everything they say.
      • The faith once for all entrusted came with a warning label. In Revelation 22: if anyone adds anything to the words of this prophecy, God will add to them the plagues described; if anyone takes words away, God will take away their share in the tree of life and the holy city. “Yes, I am coming soon. Amen. Come, Lord Jesus.” The very last words from God to his people remind us to beware of those who add and take away.

  4. 10:04 Know the word to defend it

    • We are called to be defenders, and to defend something you need to know what it is. You don’t need to memorize the measurements in Exodus; Josh just read them in his personal study and lost count of the gold rings. But you need to know the truth, and equally what isn’t true, so alarms go off everywhere in your system when you hear fabrications.
    • Jude reminds us that our doctrine must align with the word of God and our theology must be solid. We need to know who God is and what he teaches.

    1. 10:56 “I just want to love Jesus”

      Scripture: 1 Timothy 4:16

      • Not all Christians seem concerned with this. Part of it is that it requires work; Christians are a little lazy and sloppy in doctrine because they spend more time scrolling Instagram than scrolling the word of God.
      • Josh hears, “I just want to love Jesus,” without concern for what other believers believe; it’s “you do you” applied to faith, and he calls it tragically sloppy. He loves Jesus too and would give his life for him. But what if you are busy loving Jesus and someone around you is busy loving the wrong Jesus?
      • “That’s not my problem.” Yes, it is. Paul tells Timothy to watch his life and doctrine closely and persevere, because then he will save both himself and his hearers. Your doctrine could save your hearers. You don’t have to be a jerk or arrogant about it, but you need to know who God is and what he teaches so you can help others see him more clearly.

  5. 12:43 Grace is an incentive, not a license

    Scripture: Luke 9:23

    • Jude wanted Christians to recognize the dangers of wrong doctrine and bad theology and protect themselves and others. Here he is defending against the idea that Christians can do whatever they want because God’s grace will cover it. Jude, Paul, and many others remind us that grace isn’t a license to sin but an incentive to live godly lives. We don’t make much of his grace by sinning more, but by gradually growing up and out of sin; it isn’t immediate, but it is certain.
    • That is why Jesus says in Luke 9 that whoever wants to be his disciple must deny themselves, take up their cross daily, and follow him. Following Jesus isn’t a one-time event at church camp 20 years ago; it is daily submission to God’s will, knowing that when you fall short you have an advocate, and the next morning you can pick up his will again and follow.

  6. 13:57 What are you waiting for? Baptism

    Scripture: Galatians 2:20 Matthew 28:19–20 John 14:15

    • Josh doesn’t understand those who wait to be baptized, and it has weighed on him in this body. His own testimony: he was baptized at 9, with no pictures, no video, and no memory of it. He would love to jump in the water before his brothers and sisters and say he is all in, but he won’t be baptized twice. His parents took his profession of faith and baptized him as they discipled him, and that is enough: “I’ve been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me.”
    • But some adults here believe and aren’t baptized. If you call this church home and trust Jesus for eternal life but haven’t been baptized, Josh loves you deeply, but you are in disobedience to the one you say you love. Jesus told the apostles to make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and in John 14 he says, “If you love me, keep my commands.”
    • Baptism is a public declaration of faith, a sign of obedience, and a beautiful expression of union with Christ in his death and resurrection. Josh’s challenge: let this be the year. Jesus did not die in public and was not publicly humiliated so you could live in private. Baptisms can be scheduled on the mobile app, and baptism cards by the baptistry and at the desk help people prepare.

  7. 16:53 Jude 17–23

    Scripture: Jude 17–23

    • Picking up where they left off last week, Josh reads Jude 17–23: remember what the apostles foretold, that in the last times there will be scoffers who follow their own ungodly desires, who divide you and do not have the Spirit. But you, dear friends, build yourselves up in your most holy faith, pray in the Holy Spirit, and keep yourselves in God’s love as you wait for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ. Be merciful to those who doubt, save others by snatching them from the fire, and to others show mercy mixed with fear.

  8. 17:44 Remember who gave the word

    • To motivate Christians to defend the truth, Jude first underscores remembering who gave the word. There were many disciples but few apostles; an apostle is one sent with a commission, who witnessed the resurrection, lived and learned with Christ, and was sent to carry the good news to all nations.
    • Whenever something authentic is passed down, a counterfeit rises next to it, and the early church dealt with many false apostles and false gospels. Before they had the written New Testament, the ultimate question about any teaching was: is this what the apostles taught?

    1. 18:57 The church discovered the canon

      • The church didn’t create the canon; it discovered it. If you wonder whether that is valid, read the Gospel of Thomas and then the Gospel of John, and you’ll know quickly.
      • Each book had to be written by an apostle or someone closely associated with one and line up with the core teachings of the faith. It wasn’t an arbitrary decision by a powerful figure or a vote, but agreement among diverse Christian communities over time. Preserving the core teachings for thousands of years across continents and languages is, to Josh, clearly guided by God’s divine providence.

    2. 20:08 Manuscript evidence

      Scripture: John 18:31, 37

      • There are about 250 known manuscripts of Plato, and hardly anyone debates their trustworthiness; there are over 5,000 Greek manuscripts of the New Testament, and everyone wants to debate it.
      • Anyone can look at them through the Center for the Study of New Testament Manuscripts. Josh shows P52, with John 18:31 and 37. We have 66 books by over 40 authors across about 1,500 years, Moses, David, Solomon, Paul, John, Ezra, Nehemiah, Peter, Matthew, from different backgrounds, contributing to one cohesive story.

    3. 21:25 It changed this guy’s life

      • Beyond all that, the truth of God’s word changed Josh’s life, and billions of lives. It helped him see creation, himself, and others through God’s eyes and answered who he is, why he is here, and what he needs. He was stubborn, but God was patient.
      • Consistent exposure to God’s word, fellowship with him, and prayer helped free Josh from multiple addictions and recover from trauma and the loss of his sister. It taught him to be a better husband and father (“I was a jerk”), to forgive people he never thought he would, and to reconcile and share coffee and bread with people from his childhood who mistreated his family in the church.

    4. 22:59 The social impact of the word

      Scripture: Romans 13:13–14 John 17:17

      • Everyone waits for Jesus to reign and rule, and Josh wonders whether he already is through those who submit to his will. Christians clinging to their Bibles led movements to abolish slavery and founded hospitals, orphanages, and rescue organizations, and have led the way in civil rights and peace negotiations. There have been dark times, but the good far outweighs the bad, with impact on art, music, literature, education, missions, and the willingness to stand against tyrants.
      • Augustine of Hippo went from indulgence to faith after reading two verses in Romans 13: let us behave decently, not in carousing and drunkenness, but clothe yourselves with the Lord Jesus Christ and do not think about how to gratify the desires of the sinful nature. John Newton went from slave trader to abolitionist and wrote “Amazing Grace.” Corrie ten Boom survived a Nazi concentration camp and forgave those responsible.
      • God isn’t done: “Sanctify them by your truth.” Murderers find Jesus before lethal injection, and former porn stars teach modesty. The Bible has changed people across history, including Josh. Has it changed yours?

    5. 25:20 Lord, liar, or lunatic

      • The evidence demands we conclude we have the actual words of Jesus, that he existed, and that by his words he claims to be God. Doing nothing with that would be incredibly foolish, and C.S. Lewis boils the decision to three options: Jesus is Lord, liar, or lunatic.
      • Josh doesn’t think he was a liar: Christianity exploded right around his death despite intense persecution. Nor a lunatic: lunatics tend to be self-obsessed, and Jesus was too outwardly focused, ministering to outcasts and healing the sick. That leaves Lord. If he is God in the flesh, “Jehovah in a man’s suit,” then water into wine, the blind seeing, the deaf hearing, the dead raised, and an empty tomb seem like light work.

  9. 27:02 Scoffers who follow their own ungodly desires

    Scripture: Jude 18–19 1 Corinthians 2:14

    • Jude asks us to remember what the apostles said: in the last times there will be scoffers who follow their own ungodly desires, who divide you and do not have the Spirit. The whole New Testament warns against them.
    • “Follow their own ungodly desires” has to be one of the biggest reasons so many deny God’s truth. Josh doesn’t think it is about the evidence; you could show a smart person all of it and it wouldn’t create faith. Many people don’t want God to tell them how to live. “I’ve got major objections to the Bible.” Translation: you don’t like the “shall not” part, or the die-to-self part.
    • That is low-hanging fruit for Satan: give them counterfeit truth, enough to feel good but not enough to save them. You can’t expect holiness or reverence from people who don’t have the Spirit. These mockers were scoffing at the second coming, “Where’s Jesus?”, causing division while living worldly lives. Paul told the Corinthians we can’t expect those without the Spirit to accept the things of the Spirit.

  10. 29:04 Keep yourselves in God’s love

    Scripture: Jude 20–23 Matthew 22:37 Matthew 22:39

    • Look how Jude tells us to respond: build yourselves up in your most holy faith, pray in the Holy Spirit, keep yourselves in God’s love as you wait for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ, be merciful to those who doubt, snatch others from the fire, and show mercy mixed with fear.
    • There are two blocks. Verses 20 and 21 remind Josh of the first and greatest commandment, love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, and mind: keep yourselves in God’s love. Verses 22 and 23 remind him of the second, love your neighbor as yourself: show mercy to others. Jude wrote 30 years after Christ’s ministry, and the cornerstone of Christ’s teaching remains visible.

    1. 30:20 Who’s keeping whom?

      Scripture: Jude 1 Jude 21 1 Peter 1:5

      • In verse 1 Jude addresses those who are called, loved in God the Father, and kept for Jesus Christ. In verse 21 he says, “Keep yourselves in God’s love.” Peter says God’s power shields us until the coming of salvation. So who is keeping whom?
      • There is God’s part and our part. God has been faithful to do his part; our job is to make sure we don’t lose our first love like the church in Ephesus.

    2. 31:13 Vows go both ways

      • Crowd participation: raise your hand if you wrote your own vows (“overachievers”), then if you copied them from the internet. When the baby boomers point out there was no internet, Josh concedes the bad illustration.
      • It doesn’t matter whether you wrote them; what matters is whether you meant it. Healthy relationships are a two-way street. God demonstrated his commitment in the person and work of Jesus, who stayed on the cross despite the arrival of the wrath of God. Now it is your turn to pick up your cross and follow despite the arrival of suffering. Jude isn’t saying you must earn God’s love; you couldn’t if you tried. He is saying stay close, so God can bless you and you can respond with praise.

    3. 33:01 The prodigal son was still a son

      Scripture: John 15:5

      • When the prodigal son was far from home, was he still his father’s son? Of course, though he was in rebellion and the relationship was wounded. But he wasn’t keeping himself where his father could actively show his love; he was disconnected, and his father didn’t know where he was. When he came home and the father ran out, put a ring on his finger, clothed him, and threw a party despite the grumpy older brother, the father could actively show his love.
      • When we are in disobedience, God doesn’t stop loving us and we don’t lose our salvation; we are simply wayward, prodigal, moving away from the love we should move toward. In John 15 Jesus says, “I am the vine; you are the branches… apart from me you can do nothing.” Stay close. Be in the word and be doers of it. Love those around you, but love God first and most, with everything; he loved you first. So keep yourselves in his love.

  11. 34:58 Pray in the Holy Spirit

    Scripture: Jude 20 Ephesians 6:18 Romans 8:26

    • Jude says to pray in the Holy Spirit, and Josh thinks there is a lot of confusion around it. He asks who grew up with the classic bedtime prayer, and someone recites “Now I lay me down to sleep.” Praying “if I die” every night is heavy stuff for little Timmy. Josh and his wife are doing estate planning and are honest with the kids that life doesn’t last forever, but at bedtime they’d rather give thanks for life and celebrate salvation than focus on death.
    • In Ephesians 6 Paul says pray in the Spirit on all occasions with all kinds of prayers and requests, and keep praying for all the Lord’s people. Praying in the Spirit isn’t a special form of prayer like speaking in tongues; whenever you actually pray, it should be in the Spirit. It isn’t a prayer preference; it is proper posture.
    • In Romans 8 Paul says the Spirit helps us in our weakness; we don’t know what to pray for, but the Spirit intercedes for us through wordless groans. Praying in the Spirit could also be called praying with the Spirit, and sometimes it isn’t words but posture.

    1. 37:37 “Lord, please help me today”

      Scripture: Romans 8:15–16

      • This week Josh was a mess: coughing, sleeping terribly on NyQuil, and in a fight with his son on Monday. Tuesday morning he stumbled into his office to start sermon prep, knelt over his little workout bench for maybe five minutes, and all he could muster was, “Lord, please help me today.” He believes the Spirit helped him even acknowledge he needed God’s help.
      • Romans 8 also says the Spirit we received doesn’t make us slaves to fear but brought about our adoption, and by him we cry, “Abba, Father”; the Spirit testifies with our spirit that we are God’s children. The Spirit gives assurance and confidence. Prayer is more than words: emotion, intimacy, thinking on him and his promises, belonging to a Father who loves and saved you.

    2. 39:22 When we pray in strength

      Scripture: James 4:3

      • Our prayers are filled with the Spirit and also guided by him. Josh has also prayed not in weakness but in strength and caught himself praying selfish prayers. James says when you ask you do not receive, because you ask with wrong motives. The Spirit helps us when we are weak and also when we are strong, selfish, and acting like fools.
      • The two things are deeply connected: keep yourself in God’s love by praying in the Spirit, and pray in the Spirit by keeping yourself in God’s love. Trust him, read his word daily, pray alone, with your family and spouse, for his people and lost sons and daughters, for this church, elders, mission, outreach teams, yes the budget, pastoral staff, young families, the older generation battling health issues, and the salvation of those who don’t know him. Pray and keep praying.

  12. 40:46 Be merciful to those who doubt

    Scripture: Jude 22–23

    • When you aren’t praying for them, be merciful to those who doubt, snatch others from the fire, and show mercy mixed with fear. We don’t have to know someone’s heart to speak encouragement to it, or agree with people to build them up.
    • Those who doubt could be believers who are weak in the faith right now, as Josh has been. If you pray in the Spirit, you may realize God is tasking you with building them up. Our brothers and sisters came from lives of sin with influences that kept them from the Lord; instead of peering into their lives and looking down, invite them into yours and pull them up. If you are a mature Christian but actively build up no immature Christians, the only one calling you mature is you, and you aren’t supposed to nickname yourself.
    • Show them how wonderful their salvation is and encourage them back into the word; it’s never too late to turn around. Josh thinks the most vulnerable among us are those hurt in the church, maybe here. Reach out: “I don’t know what happened, I don’t need to know, but I care about you.” This is how we snatch people from the fire: we love them well even if they wronged us or we don’t agree with them.

    1. 43:17 Mercy mixed with fear

      • “Show mercy mixed with fear” means that as we reach down to care for brothers and sisters caught in a trap, we must be careful not to fall in ourselves. Rescue swimmers are taught to subdue someone resisting rescue, or the victim controls the situation and down you go. Josh isn’t telling anyone to put people in choke holds and bring them back to church, but there is a balance: love God’s people and hate sin. We can do both.
      • Not every believer is equipped for these situations. It takes strong knowledge of Scripture, a track record of faithfulness, and an often-forgotten awareness of Satan’s battle strategy. That is why Josh spends so much time dealing with sin in this church: it is easier to teach new believers to avoid bad doctrine than to snatch them out of the fire later.
      • He knows because he barely made it out of the fire himself. If not for loving believers showing him mercy mixed with fear, he wouldn’t be here, and God even used Christians unwilling to show him mercy to help him grow up.

  13. 45:08 The doxology

    Scripture: Jude 24–25

    • Only after he grew up did Josh understand why Christians call the next part a doxology, from the Greek word for glory: Jude ends with a hymn of praise. To him who is able to keep you from stumbling and present you before his glorious presence without fault and with great joy, to the only God our Savior, be glory, majesty, power, and authority, through Jesus Christ our Lord, before all ages, now and forevermore. Amen.
    • There are spots and blemishes all over this room, but on that day God’s people will be blameless, presented in his glorious presence without fault and with great joy. Jude’s goal around AD 65, and Josh’s in 2025, is to remind everyone of the greatness of Jesus Christ. If Josh shows them Jesus every week, there is no way they would forget their first love or find another, because there is no one like him.

  14. 46:49 No one will snatch them out of my hand

    Scripture: John 10:27–28 Romans 8:39

    • Jesus says, “My sheep listen to my voice; I know them, and they follow me. I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; no one will snatch them out of my hand.” Salvation isn’t temporary, as some would teach; once received it can never be lost. If Christ promised to keep Josh and the Spirit helps him keep himself in him, then surely he is his forever.
    • He found us, and nothing can separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord. To the prodigals in the room: Josh loves you, God loves you; remember and repent.

  15. 47:39 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks Jesus for his sacrifice, recalling the garden of Gethsemane and the weight of all of us and the separation our sin causes, and thanks him for drinking the cup. He remembers those who mocked Jesus on the cross, “If you truly are the Son of God, come down,” and that he stayed, because the Son of God didn’t come to save himself; he came to save us.
    • He prays the church would remember who Jesus is like the first time they came to him, and that he was raised so that our sin could never be raised: it is buried and gone forever.
