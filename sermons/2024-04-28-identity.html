Overview

  1. 00:01 Closing the series with your questions

    • This morning looks a little different. Josh says there is no better way to close a series on identity than to answer the questions people actually have, not the ones the pastors think they might have, with a biblical, pastoral response.
    • Every generation is represented on the panel, from the boomers on, which he sees as great health on the teaching team and a picture of the church coming together in unity in Christ. They received more than seven questions but expect to get through only seven.

    1. 01:43 Opening prayer

      • Josh asks the Lord to speak into their hearts and direct them by his word, because no answer man has to offer is worth listening to unless it is rooted in God’s word, and thanks him for Jesus and for saving sinners like him.

  2. 02:23 Question 1: How do I figure out who I really am?

    • The first question: how do I figure out who I really am?

    1. 02:42 Steve: take by faith who God says you are

      • Laying a foundation “from the old guy,” Steve says it doesn’t matter in the end who you think you are; you must take by faith who God says you are. The whole culture is about what I feel, what I think, or what someone is telling me, and that’s why there is so much confusion.

    2. 03:16 What God says your worth is

      Scripture: Matthew 10

      • Another panelist agrees: we forget to look at Scripture. In Matthew 10 God takes care of every creature, clothing and feeding them, and finishes by asking, aren’t you worth so much more? We go by our feelings, not by the truth of what he says; what God says your worth is matters, not how we feel.

    3. 03:51 Made in his image, justified and adopted

      Scripture: 1 Peter 2:9

      • God made mankind uniquely in his image, the imago Dei, giving every human being inherent dignity, value, and worth simply because of what we are. That is why abortion is so abhorrent: that is a person.
      • Beyond that, the New Testament says being justified means we are God’s workmanship, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, coheirs with him, adopted by God. Accepting that brings joy and assurance. We will still struggle with self-worth in an age when everyone’s attention is monetized, but grounding ourselves in who God says we are gives a lasting, unchanging confidence.

    4. 05:00 The pain behind the question

      • A panelist hears pain in the question: someone who feels they have lost their identity. That happens when you put your identity in something that changes, like your work or being a parent, and when it goes away you are left asking, “Who am I really?”

    5. 05:32 “Lead me to that new identity”

      • Josh asks the panel to treat him as that person: lead me to Christ and to that new identity. How do I get there?
      1. 05:44 Admit you have a problem

        Scripture: Ephesians 2

        • The first step to solving a problem is admitting you have one. Paul says in Ephesians 2, “For by grace you have been saved,” but before that he calls us children of wrath. Knowing you are a child of wrath brings you into that grace and into who Christ says you are.
      2. 06:10 At the end of yourself, the start of Jesus

        Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:20

        • You’re in a good place when you ask that question: at the end of yourself you are finally ready to find the start of Jesus. Second Corinthians says we are called to be ambassadors for Christ, but first, be reconciled to God; finding your identity in Christ starts with being reconciled to him and not pushing it off.
      3. 06:33 Often a Christian who lost their way
        • A panelist notes we assume the question comes from an unbeliever, but the series has shown it often comes from a Christian who has lost their way. If God can take a child of wrath and say, “Now you’re mine, my son, adopted, a coheir with Christ,” then if I fall as a believer he can pick me back up. We celebrate his grace and mercy, grace upon grace upon grace.
      4. 07:22 Who are you listening to?
        • The key is who you are listening to. A panelist tells the teens in the room that if their parents limit what they watch, listen to, and carry on their devices, they should thank God for them, however irritating and confining it feels. Those voices create the confusion that leads to asking, “Am I this gender or not?”, and the questions cascade from there.
      5. 08:06 Look at the word of the one who created you
        • If you want to know who you are, look at the word of the one who created you; he tells you who you are. Seek it anywhere but his word and you will find lies, not the truth.
        • In a lighter moment, the panel jokes about a dad who was talking about Taylor Swift that morning (“it’s how you stay young”), and one declares, “Master Steve is a Swiftie.”

  3. 09:01 Question 2: As a believer, how do I balance politics and faith?

    • The second question: as a believer, how do I balance politics and faith? “We’re just going right into that one.”

    1. 09:15 Faith informs politics, not the other way around

      Scripture: Colossians 1:17

      • Every Christian has to remember that faith informs politics, not the other way around, and we have lost the plot a little. Many political philosophies put man and the state in the place of God, and we must reject them. Francis Schaeffer said that if there is no God above the state, then the state is God, a terrifying thought.
      • Take every thought captive and hold it up against God’s revelation. Colossians 1 says Christ is before all things and in him all things hold together, so whatever the result of an election or vote, it is part of God’s sovereign plan to show grace and mercy, and also justice and holiness. Accepting his sovereignty brings peace: even the bad things have their purpose and aren’t meaningless evil.

    2. 10:36 Submit to the governing authorities

      Scripture: 1 Peter 2:13

      • First Peter 2 tells us to submit to the governing authorities, and it isn’t the only place Scripture says so. We don’t like it when we don’t like the government, but this was written when Christians were being killed. Why submit? For the Lord’s sake, so that you are above reproach.
      • God has allowed all authority on earth; the last election didn’t surprise him. If authorities told us to reject Christ, that would be different, but that isn’t what is happening, and we still don’t like to submit.

    3. 11:43 God doesn’t have a candidate

      Scripture: John 18:36

      • Part of the question is that this is an election year. Jesus said, “My kingdom is not of this world.” God doesn’t have a candidate at stake, and he thinks the choice is between two very flawed people. God may use one, since he has always used flawed people, but someone selling a $60 Bible with the Constitution in it is no sign that God is on that side.
      • Keep the main thing the main thing. The church gets caught up in the subtext and the details; on abortion, the point is life. God is the author of life, only he can create it and only he can rightfully end it, not six weeks or fifteen weeks or the other gnarly side issues.
      • God has a plan and is sovereign, and there isn’t much about our nation in the end times. Kings have tried to interrupt God’s plans before and failed every step of the way. “In God we trust, not in man.”

    4. 14:01 A proper placement, not a balance

      • A panelist grew up in a very patriotic household, which was good, but was often confused about who was the higher authority, the president or God. Without anyone intending it, he learned that politics was the hope for a transformed world rather than Jesus. Maybe it is not so much a balance of politics and faith as a proper placement of which is higher.

    5. 14:44 Seek first his kingdom

      Scripture: Matthew 6:33

      • In Matthew 6 Jesus says, “Seek first his kingdom and his righteousness,” and that sentence alone helps him align faith and politics. What are you more passionate about? He has called himself out many times for being passionate about politics but not wanting to serve in the church. His approach to voting: vote righteousness, as best he can.

    6. 15:45 The culture is going the way God said

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:1 2 Peter 3:3 John 18:36

      • Our culture is going down the toilet, and that is the plan. In the last days there will be terrible times; scoffers will come and turn everything upside down. If everything is going “to hell in a handbasket,” that is how God said it would be.
      • We don’t become subservient slaves to it; we vote and work for change as far as we can. But his kingdom is not of this world, so serve the King who is King over all other kings.

    7. 16:40 Josh: check your fear

      • Keep in mind who is watching, and check your fear. Josh’s 18-year-old daughter will vote for the first time this election and is excited and optimistic about the future. He asks older, seasoned believers whether they are infecting others with fear of the future or leading with confidence in the one who will do what he says.

  4. 17:25 Question 3: How can I be one person in all areas of my life?

    • The third question: how can I be one person in all areas of my life?

    1. 17:33 Follow the Good Shepherd’s humility

      Scripture: Philippians 2:3–8

      • Follow the Good Shepherd. Philippians 2:3–8 says Christ counted others more significant than himself, didn’t count equality with God a thing to be grasped, humbled himself, and became obedient to death on a cross. Paul calls us to the same self-sacrificial humility.
      • That consistency comes with time. You will fall and won’t follow perfectly, but as you keep walking with the Lord you look more and more like him.

    2. 18:26 You cannot serve two masters

      Scripture: Matthew 6:24 James

      • In Matthew 6 Jesus says you cannot serve two masters. If you chase anything the way you are supposed to chase God, you have another master, and James calls that double-minded.
      • If he goes to softball on Tuesday and suddenly looks like the guys he plays with because he wants to fit in, he has to ask who he is serving. If it is God, he should look exactly the same there as he does at church.

    3. 19:16 “Unite my heart”

      Scripture: Psalm 86:11

      • A panelist felt this pull as a teacher for 15 years: he spent ten months as a teacher who was also a Christian instead of a Christian who was also a teacher, found himself again over the summer, then watched it fade into the kingdom of his classroom, wearing so many hats he couldn’t clear the doorway.
      • In Psalm 86 David, who meant so many things to so many people, prays, “Unite my heart.” It starts with prayer; you can’t be one person in all areas of life until you ground yourself in Christ.

    4. 20:10 Steve: give yourself a break

      Scripture: Romans 7

      • Steve worries the question has slipped into works-based righteousness: what can I do to be consistent enough to make God smile? The gospel says you are saved by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone, based on the word of God alone.
      • Give yourself a break; he doesn’t think you can be one person in all areas of life. Read the last half of Romans 7, where Paul says the things he doesn’t want to do he ends up doing: “Wretched man that I am, who will rescue me?”
      • The point isn’t to be content with sinfulness. In Christ you have two natures, the sin nature Eli talked about and the nature of your new life, and they will wrestle until Jesus comes. Speaking as the senior, from personal experience, you will never get old enough not to want to do what you shouldn’t.

    5. 21:50 Sanctification is God’s work

      • The missteps happen when we disconnect and forget who is in charge; things go haywire at home when kids forget, and the Lord’s house is no different. Sanctification is God’s work, not yours: you are his handiwork. God is holy, saves you though you are unholy, and makes you holy, and the process looks different in each of us.
      • One panelist was serving the Lord in the church while walking in sin some would call disqualifying. The Lord had called him, but sanctification hadn’t caught up with the calling. He tried and failed; it changed when the Lord took it away, and now it doesn’t even come into his head. Trust him with the process: meet him in his word, be faithful in prayer, worship, and let him deal with the rest.
      1. 23:29 “I’m a hypocrite”
        • Asked whether he thought it was disqualifying, he says at times, yes: he had answers he wasn’t listening to and felt like a hypocrite, convicted because he was preaching a word he wasn’t heeding. The Lord used it to shape his ministry; he cannot say something from the stage he isn’t willing to wrestle with on Tuesday in his office. The warfare happens on Tuesday.
      2. 24:13 Steve: a dirty pipe, not a plugged one
        • Over years of preaching, recognizing his own sin was often Steve’s greatest burden in the pulpit. But the presence of sin is not a disqualifier; the unwillingness to confess it is. God can work through a dirty pipe as long as it isn’t plugged: confess it, lay it before the Lord, and have someone you are accountable to, though even that can drift back into works righteousness.
        • He tells that panelist he knew he was struggling but trusted that God would fix it in his time, because he trusted his heart.

  5. 25:31 Question 4: What is the Christian worldview of race?

    • The fourth question, a big one: what is the Christian worldview of race? One panelist jokes, “I got to go. See you next Sunday.”

    1. 25:43 All nations from one man

      Scripture: Acts 17

      • In Acts 17 Paul reminds us that all nations come from one man, Adam; go back far enough and we share a common ancestor. An us-versus-them mentality about race has no place in the Christian worldview, and the linchpin of Paul’s ministry is that salvation is available to all mankind.
      • We can acknowledge, respect, and learn from one another’s differences, but any racial superiority or hostility, such as the idea of white privilege, is foreign to the biblical worldview; he calls it a Marxist ideal and antichrist, especially in the church. Jesus prayed we would all be united under him.

    2. 27:03 Every tribe and nation

      Scripture: Revelation 5:9

      • A panelist loves remembering that in heaven Jesus ransomed people for God from every tribe, language, people, and nation, a beautiful picture.

    3. 27:14 Josh: no partiality

      Scripture: Acts 10

      • Josh points out that the panel included Scripture references for people to study on their own; they don’t operate from their own wisdom but from God’s. He thinks of Acts 10: no partiality, no favoritism. If God is that way, you should be too.

    4. 27:41 One new man in place of the two

      Scripture: Galatians 3:28 Ephesians 2:14–15

      • Galatians 3 says the same: neither Jew nor Gentile, slave nor free, male nor female; Jesus came to save all. Paul says Jesus came to break down every dividing wall of hostility and create in himself one new man in place of the two.

    5. 28:06 Babel, Cornelius, and Peter on the roof

      Scripture: Acts 10 Acts 10:28

      • The scattering of peoples began with sin, when man tried to build a tower to the heavens and God scattered them by language and location, yet we are still rooted in the same mother and father.
      • At first everyone was Jewish: Jesus, the disciples, the 120 in the upper room, and the 3,000 at Pentecost, so for a short time people assumed you had to become Jewish to follow Jesus. Then, in Acts 10, came Cornelius, a Gentile, which is who most of us are; until this was clarified, we would be out.
      • Peter didn’t want to go to him. On the roof, hungry, he saw a blanket of food unclean under the law and heard, “Rise, Peter, kill and eat,” and refused more than once until he understood the kingdom was bigger than he had known. He told Cornelius it was against their law for a Jew to associate with a Gentile, but God had shown him not to call any man impure or unclean. That opened the door and broke down the dividing wall of hostility.

    6. 30:41 Where’s the fruit?

      • So Christians should see each person as someone God created, loves, and wants a relationship with.
      • A panelist finds it frustrating that Christians can point to the fruit of the Spirit and then tolerate the opposite when politics or race come up: “Whoa, whoa, whoa, where’s the fruit?” He rejects that. If you are walking by the Spirit you will bear fruit, period; if you aren’t bearing fruit, you aren’t walking by the Spirit.
      • Isn’t it crazy how the issues the world makes so complicated turn out to be the easiest questions to answer?

  6. 31:46 Question 5: How do I overcome a father wound?

    • Josh asks a harder one: how do I overcome a father wound?

    1. 32:01 Steve: leverage the wound for the gospel

      • You probably never will in totality, Steve says. Those wounds run deep and will likely stay with you for the rest of your life. But with Christ’s help they can stop haunting you, and you can even leverage them for the sake of the gospel as a testimony of God’s power to change lives. He speaks as one who knew father wounds very deeply and personally.

    2. 33:06 Accept who your heavenly Father says you are

      Scripture: James 1:17

      • Going back to the first question, father wounds can affect how you see God the Father, so you have to understand who he is and how he loves you. James 1 says every good and perfect gift comes from above; he wants wonderful things for you, has adopted you as his son or daughter, and makes you an heir.
      • You have to believe and accept it. You can fight it all day and let past wounds batter the truth, or accept who God says you are, which is who you are. Keep putting his word in constantly; it is like having a great father telling you he loves you and giving you hugs. Are you willing to let that overcome the wounds?

    3. 34:39 A new trend in the family

      • A panelist notes that Steve spoke from his own father wound, but he himself doesn’t have one. God set a new trend in their family through Steve; it echoes to him and from him to his son, and he prays it will echo to his son’s son. God reshaped generations through a man with a heart open to him.

    4. 35:26 Forgiveness and boundaries

      • Forgiveness is the key, but many believers feel guilty unless they remain in the relationship. Forgiveness doesn’t always mean the relationship continues; boundaries are more than appropriate, and he has had to set them with people he loves to protect his heart and the hearts of those he leads.
      • Forgiveness releases your heart from any bitter root and sets boundaries to keep you from being hurt again. But a believer must always be willing to open the door to try again; you can’t shut it all the way.

    5. 36:41 Forgive so your heavenly Father can heal you

      • Forgive the father who hurt you so your heavenly Father can heal you. He is faithful to do it and wants to see your heart move toward forgiveness; when it does, healing can begin. It is scary, but step in.

  7. 37:18 Question 6: What does it mean to be more than conquerors?

    • Speeding up, the panel takes a quick one: what does it mean to be more than conquerors?

    1. 37:26 We didn’t do the conquering

      Scripture: Romans 8

      • In Romans 8, Jesus has already conquered sin and death. We are more than conquerors because we didn’t do any conquering; we accepted what Jesus conquered, said “Yes, please, thank you,” and became heirs and adopted sons and daughters.
      • A panelist pictures the sword scene in Troy, where one champion runs out and wins the fight while the whole army sits back; the army didn’t earn the victory. “Jesus is our Brad Pitt,” which brings down the house.

    2. 38:58 Steve: making the enemy serve

      • Steve shares what John Piper wrote: a conqueror defeats his enemy, but one who is more than a conqueror subjugates him; a conqueror nullifies the enemy’s purpose, but one who is more than a conqueror makes the enemy serve his own purposes and makes the foe his slave.
      • That is what he meant about the father wound: you can transform it into a trophy to the glory, grace, and power of God, who most often uses our greatest weaknesses to do exactly that.

    3. 39:58 Not alone in the darkest valleys

      • Being more than a conqueror means facing life’s trials certain that we are not alone. A mighty Father goes before us, never leaves us, and is for us, so we approach the darkest valleys with confidence that nothing can happen that our loving Father hasn’t permitted for our good.

  8. 40:24 Question 7: How do I become a more dedicated Christian?

    • With time for one more, Josh reads the last question, sent in by a youth student: what is your advice for someone to become a more dedicated Christian?

    1. 40:53 Do your reps

      • Do your reps. Denzel Washington says anything you practice you’ll get good at, so practice imitating Christ, knowing you will fall short and need his grace. Get to know your heavenly Father by spending time with him in his word.
      • Pray a lot and pray messy prayers; God already knows when you’re faking proper, so be real. Surround yourself with godly fellowship, learn to take correction, humble yourself, and serve your family and others.

    2. 41:50 Put it on your calendar

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:16

      • A panelist wasn’t a planned person until he got older, and time with the Lord was what ended up on the chopping block. If he had treated his wife that way while getting to know her, they wouldn’t be married. Until he scheduled it, he would do great for a while and then drop off when life got busy.
      • Second Timothy says all Scripture is God-breathed and useful for training. In training for a sport you are either getting better or getting worse, and the same is true of every relationship. Blocking out 8 to 9 a.m. isn’t a cop-out. The panel jokes about scheduling dinner with a wife who replies, “I’m glad I made it to your schedule,” and steers away from marriage.

    3. 44:27 Memorize God’s word

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:12

      • It isn’t fun or flashy: memorize God’s word. At a college camp a man named Max Barnett urged them to memorize Scripture as if the Bible would disappear and they were the only one who knew it; in some countries missionaries carry whole books like Ephesians in their heads. It isn’t for bragging; it is how God speaks to you when you pray. If you believe God’s word is sharper than a two-edged sword, you will take the time.
      • A panelist recommends the Bible Memory app he was pointed to a couple of years ago; it is a little pricey but has been worth it for his family.
      • Another often overlooked source of growth is pointing others to the Lord. Their questions show whether you are prepared, and that pushes you to be ready, even through serving in kids or youth ministry.

    4. 46:24 A Paul, a Barnabas, and a Timothy

      • During his master’s studies, one panelist found two professors who became lifelong mentors. Later, when the church he served was exploding with growth and he felt unable to handle it, six other pastors who felt the same met with him every year in Nashville for golf and long talks about ministry and life, which kept him strong through tempestuous times.
      • He also learned you need someone you are personally discipling. Howard Hendricks used to say you need a Paul, a Barnabas, and a Timothy: a mentor, a peer, and a disciple.

  9. 48:08 Josh: first, you need an experience with Jesus

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:21

    • Josh closes the panel: before anyone can seek to be a dedicated Christian, they need an experience with Jesus, to hear of his mercy and grace, accept him at his word, and walk in new life.
    • To close out the identity series, he tells who every man on the panel was and is. Measured against the Lord’s law, Josh fails miserably; he is a transgressor and a sinner, and he would gladly share his list over coffee. He grew up in a Christian home and walked away in rebellious years. It was never about belief but obedience: he always believed but didn’t like it, because he knew what it required. The Lord kept softening his heart and brought him back, and he rests in his grace every day.
    • The same grace is available to anyone who puts their faith and trust in him today. The church will baptize anyone ready to follow Jesus, the most amazing decision they will ever make. Then the church moves to communion, set aside for believers to remember that he who had no sin became sin for us so that we might be the righteousness of God.

    1. 50:41 Closing prayer

      • Josh thanks God for every man on the panel, the softness of their hearts, their love for him, and their desire to lead people to his grace and forgiveness, for a calling bigger than themselves, and for the body of believers entrusted to them, asking that they lead well in word and deed.
      • He prays for hearts in the room that haven’t accepted Christ, that they would say, “Lord, take over,” and that everything would change, because the new life the Lord gave him is an amazing life.
