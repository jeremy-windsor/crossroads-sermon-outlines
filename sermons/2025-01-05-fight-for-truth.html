Overview

  1. 01:02 Buy the truth and don’t sell it

    Scripture: Proverbs 23:23 John 17 John 17:17 1 Timothy 3:15 Romans 10:9–11 Proverbs 23

    • In one of Josh’s favorite Proverbs, Solomon says, “Buy the truth and don’t sell it.” One of the most precious resources in the life of a Christian is the truth. God is the God of truth, and he has given us the truth by his Son; in John 17 Jesus prays that the truth would set his people apart: “Father, sanctify them by the truth; your word is truth.”
    • The words of God spoke everything into existence, and the words of God shape us into his likeness. The church is adopted sons and daughters rallying under the banner of God’s truth, “a collection of misfits that are far from okay,” gathered to aim for perfection and celebrate that one day he will come and make everything good again.
    • Paul calls the church God’s household, the church of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth. The God of truth created us, revealed his truth to prophets, and sent his Son full of grace and truth, who died the death we deserved; this is the message of his true church, as Romans 10 says: declare with your mouth that Jesus is Lord, believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, and you will be saved.
    • That is what Solomon means in Proverbs 23: keep buying this over and over. Fill your homes, your hearts, your spouses, and your kids with this resource.

    1. 03:18 Bad investments

      • There are bad investments: Kodak ignored innovation and got left in the dust, and Blockbuster’s board, Josh jokes, must have been extroverts who couldn’t imagine anyone wanting to stay home and stream. Netflix is worth billions and Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy in 2010; the message for those stocks was “sell, sell, sell.”
      • But there is no bad time to buy the truth and no good time to sell it. Get as much as you can; the more you have, the better off you are.

  2. 04:16 Beware of knockoffs: the book of Jude

    Scripture: Jude

    • “But listen carefully: beware of knockoffs.” Over the next two weeks the church will look at the book of Jude. False teachers have risen up against the pillar of truth, the Lord’s church. Jude encourages believers to contend for the faith by fighting for the truth of the gospel, and reminds us that Jesus will protect his church and preserve our faith until he comes.
    • Jude is an English form of the Greek name Judas, a very popular name then. For most of us the name is forever tainted by the kiss of betrayal, but this Jude is one of the good guys.

  3. 05:00 “A servant of Jesus Christ”

    Scripture: Jude 1

    • He introduces himself as “Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ and a brother of James.” We love titles; God doesn’t seem to care.

    1. 05:13 A new take on the Good Samaritan

      • Josh and his staff went to a conference in Texas in November, as they try to every year when finances allow, to connect as a team and work on their hearts. Big conferences always bring an all-star lineup, and Josh admits he finds encouragement in knowing even the most experienced preachers can toss out a dud.
      • One year a pastor promised a completely new take on the Good Samaritan. Josh was wary: Spurgeon didn’t see this angle, but this guy saw what no one else could for 2,000 years. The main point: we need to be more like the innkeeper. Forget the man who put himself in danger to help a stranger; let’s build a church culture on people looking to get paid.

    2. 06:56 Theology or testimony?

      • Josh expects to take heat for this, but these odd new angles usually come from people who seem to love their titles. He isn’t knocking anyone’s education, but he wonders: are you the product of an education system or of God’s transformation system?
      • One speaker had an entire video listing credentials, something like “super duper doctor professor pastor and apostolic leader.” The sermon was polished and well delivered but felt more like a lecture. The next speaker wanted to be introduced only as a servant of Christ, didn’t care if you knew his name, and pointed to nasty stuff in Josh’s heart, reminding him the gospel didn’t just happen when he was 9 years old; it is happening every day.
      • Time after time the guy from an obscure town in Kansas explains the gospel more clearly than the doctor of theology. What is more powerful, the one taught how to teach the gospel or the one who desperately needed it? What is more impressive, your theology or your testimony? That is why Josh loves that Jude simply calls himself a servant of Jesus Christ.

  4. 09:04 A brother of James

    Scripture: Matthew 13

    • Jude is the only New Testament author who identifies himself by a family relationship. James was head of the Jerusalem church and wrote the letter of James, which the church is slated to study in a 12-week series this year.
    • In Matthew 13 we are told James is the son of Mary and Joseph, and since James, unlike Judas, wasn’t a popular name, it is easy to conclude that Jude, like James, was a half brother of Jesus.

  5. 09:41 Called, loved, and kept

    Scripture: Jude 1–2 Romans 9:16 Deuteronomy 29:29

    • We don’t know much about when the letter was written or to which church. Jude addresses it to those who have been called, who are loved in God the Father and kept for Jesus Christ, and says, “Mercy, peace and love be yours in abundance.” You would know if that is you.
    • Salvation begins in the heart of God; it is by his will that we are saved, not our own. Romans 9 says it does not depend on human desire or effort but on God’s mercy.
    • Josh won’t pass up the chance to remind the church that God’s sovereign election will never be fully understood this side of glory: “Stop arguing about election. Elect to get on mission.” Deuteronomy 29 says the secret things belong to the Lord our God, but the things revealed belong to us and to our children forever.
    • Truth is a valuable resource, and so are peace, mercy, and love, made abundantly available to those who trust that they are kept in Jesus.

    1. 11:00 “Nolan, can you pray for me?”

      • Right before writing this sermon, facing a blank page and feeling like he was failing and wasn’t enough, Josh asked his son Nolan to pray for him, though Nolan had been having a rough week at school too.
      • After Nolan prayed, it hit Josh: his son could have the worst day of his life, and what brings him peace is knowing that when he comes home he’ll be fine; his parents can nurse him back. The same is true for Josh: on his worst day he is kept in Jesus. He can run to him with his bruises, fears, and open wounds, and he’ll patch him up and send him out for more.

  6. 12:16 A small book that isn’t insignificant

    Scripture: 2 Peter

    • Josh believes Jude is one of the most undervalued books in the Bible; people tend to think something small is insignificant. Sidebar for the short people: they shine on airplanes. They can’t unload the dishwasher without a ladder, but when the seat belt light turns off they can stand up first, and his wife can stand up straight from the window seat.
    • You may be more familiar with Jude than you think: of its 25 verses, which the church will cover over this two-week series, 19 have parallels in 2 Peter.

  7. 13:44 “Contend for the faith”

    Scripture: Jude 3

    • In verse 3 Jude explains what to expect in his short but fiery letter: although he was eager to write about the salvation we share, he felt compelled to urge them to contend for the faith once for all entrusted to God’s people. He doesn’t put down the topic of salvation because it doesn’t matter; he puts it down to fight the forces of evil that pervert the truth and keep people from salvation.
    • Josh is an encourager and an optimist at heart. He would rather build up the contenders than fight the pretenders, and if he could spend every message encouraging true believers, he would. But the scope of his responsibility is greater: when the enemy is at work, it is his job to stay awake and keep watch. The Christian life isn’t a spiritual vacation; it is a battlefield.

    1. 15:00 Watchmen who don’t fall asleep

      • The night Jesus was arrested, the disciples were tasked with keeping watch and praying, and they fell asleep. Josh is a watchman and overseer, one of 11 active elders, responsible for guarding the truth and protecting the unity of the body. If bullets are zipping over our heads, he isn’t sleeping.

  8. 15:41 Certain individuals have slipped in

    Scripture: Jude 4

    • Jude says certain individuals whose condemnation was written about long ago have secretly slipped in among you. Josh borrows the Tim Allen Santa Clause line that a problem at work is a challenge, but a problem at home is a problem.
    • A Jehovah’s Witness outside the church is a challenge; Josh would trust maybe 70% of the roughly 400 active adult members to talk with one about the deity of Christ. But when Doug in your life group says he doesn’t sin anymore and that if you really had faith you wouldn’t either, now there is a problem in the house. Some of the worst casualties Josh has seen came not from deceptive religions outside but from spiritual pretenders inside.

    1. 16:53 “By their fruit you will recognize them”

      Scripture: Matthew 7:15–20

      • While Jude and Simon were playing Fortnite, Jesus was warning people about this: watch out for false prophets who come in sheep’s clothing but inwardly are ferocious wolves. A good tree cannot bear bad fruit, and a bad tree cannot bear good fruit; by their fruit you will recognize them.
      • Looking for and evaluating fruit isn’t judgmental. When people get uncomfortable with caring believers noticing bad fruit, they try to shut them up by quoting Jesus, “Judge not,” but Josh says misquoting Jesus isn’t cool, because he also says to judge rightly. Bad fruit is bad fruit, and we should speak the truth in love by calling it out, not just noticing it.

    2. 18:12 Grace turned into a license for sin

      Scripture: Jude 4

      • This isn’t a small issue like people stealing towels from the baptistry. In verse 4 Jude calls them ungodly people who pervert the grace of our God into a license for immorality and deny Jesus Christ, our only Sovereign and Lord. They turn grace into a license for sin.
      • Jude’s favorite word is “ungodly”; between Jude and 2 Peter it is used 10 times. They had a standard of godliness, but they weren’t convicted of true godliness by the Spirit of God.
      • The scariest thought for Josh this week: it is possible to clean up your life under your own power. If he is the power that stumbled into an addiction and the power that overcame it, who is to say he won’t stumble back? But if the power of God helped him stop it, it might just be stopped forever.

    3. 19:52 Grace that offends God

      • The idea of grace leading someone to sin should sound foreign and offend a true believer, because it offends God. The precious blood of Jesus was poured out for us; if you were forced to hear Jesus cry out after the nails pierced his hands, you might stop perverting grace.
      • That is how the believer interacts with grace: the Lord teaches them the weight of their sin. It can’t crush us, and we can’t out-sin the grace of God, but we feel anguish as we wrestle with the very sin our Savior was pierced for. It is as if we drove the nails through his hands.

  9. 20:55 Israel: delivered, then destroyed for unbelief

    Scripture: Jude 5

    • Jude, like Peter, gives Old Testament examples of how God deals with people who resist his authority, and he uses the nation of Israel. Verse 5: though you already know this, the Lord delivered his people out of Egypt but later destroyed those who did not believe. Those who reject the truth will always be met with divine judgment.
    • The exodus is a beautiful illustration of God’s love: he hears his people and in his timing gives a way out. In the Passover he says, trust me, obey my commands, I have heard your cries and I’ll send an advocate. Then the most amazing miracle in Scripture: God pulls apart the sea so his people pass on dry land and drops the water on Pharaoh’s army.
    • Brought to the border of the promised land, the people were afraid. Moses, Joshua, and Caleb urged them to walk in by faith, but they refused, and some leaders even floated going back to Egypt, where at least their plates were full. Going against the will and word of God never goes unpunished: everyone 20 and older would die within 40 years. Their unbelief was their undoing.
    • Do you act more like the first wave that refused to go in, or the next wave that believed God’s promises were bigger than their opponents?

    1. 23:20 Who is bringing whom?

      • Be careful what influence you follow, because you shape not just your life or home but the identity of this body. Some so-called Christians might convince you the best option is back where God brought you out of.
      • Maybe the Lord has been chiseling complaining out of your heart and you have learned to build people up and celebrate what God is doing in them. Then a new couple joins your life group, and they’re pretty complaining. What’s the plan: are you bringing them, or are they bringing you?

  10. 24:09 The angels who abandoned their dwelling

    Scripture: Jude 6 Genesis 6

    • Verse 6: the angels who did not keep their positions of authority but abandoned their proper dwelling, he has kept in darkness, bound with everlasting chains for judgment on the great day.
    • There are varying views on these angels, and Josh likes to grab the belt loop of the intellectual types and pull them back: we don’t need to spend 90 hours on Genesis 6 to understand Jude’s point. Scripture makes clear the flood was a response to what man did, not angels. Either way the lesson is the same: the angels rebelled and were punished. Do you think you are any different?

  11. 25:01 Sodom, Gomorrah, and strange flesh

    Scripture: Jude 7 Leviticus 18 Genesis 18–19 1 Corinthians 10:12

    • Jude 7: Sodom, Gomorrah, and the surrounding towns gave themselves up to sexual immorality and perversion and serve as an example of those who suffer the punishment of eternal fire. In a culture with absolute pride in its perversion, Josh doesn’t like the NIV here. The ESV says “unnatural desire,” but the NASB, his favorite translation, says it best: “strange flesh.”
    • These towns didn’t just have a block party that got out of control one weekend; they gave themselves up to a lifestyle of unnatural desires. It is possible “strange flesh” refers not only to homosexuality but to bestiality, which God condemns in Leviticus 18, and Jesus upholds this warning in the New Testament. Genesis 18 and 19 serve as a constant reminder that God does hate sin.
    • Whether the object perverts creation or perverts grace, God will judge it all. Is there really a difference between people today who use grace as a license for sin and the people of Sodom and Gomorrah? How many Christians look down on these towns while having plenty of strange pursuits of their own? What might we see if we looked at yours? Take heed lest you fall.

  12. 27:01 Clean the inside first

    Scripture: Matthew 23:26–28

    • Nolan, 11, came to say good night after his shower and still kind of smelled. Josh had focused so much on teaching him to love Jesus, obey God, and love truth that he forgot to show him how to wash his hair. Nolan is a wonderful person on the inside; sometimes it is what’s on his head that makes him tough to be around.
    • The church’s problem is the opposite. Instead of cleaning up the inside first, we have built programs and ministry strategies for cleaning up the outside: a giving campaign for generosity, a parenting seminar for better parents, recovery programs for addictions. Josh understands the heart behind it, but were you taught to be generous, or shown the one who gave his life so you could live? You can listen to parenting podcasts until Jesus comes, or surrender your life to him, read his word every day, and learn how he guides you.
    • The gentle, approving-of-all, social justice Jesus of Super Bowl commercials says: blind Pharisee, first clean the inside of the cup and dish; woe to you hypocrites, like whitewashed tombs, beautiful outside but full of dead men’s bones. We have taught Christians to walk around with a perm while their hearts are still putrid. You need a new heart.

  13. 29:43 “The Lord rebuke you”

    Scripture: Jude 8–10 Deuteronomy 34:5–6

    • Jude describes the ungodly as blasphemous too: on the strength of their dreams they pollute their bodies, reject authority, and heap abuse on celestial beings. But even the archangel Michael, disputing with the devil about the body of Moses, did not dare to condemn him for slander but said, “The Lord rebuke you,” while these people speak abusively against what they do not understand and are destroyed by what they understand by instinct like unreasoning animals.
    • “The Lord rebuke you”: what a powerful submission to God’s sovereign authority. The ungodly don’t look to God to exact revenge; like animals they want their pound of flesh. But not us.
    • What Jude says about Moses’ body is found nowhere else in Scripture. Everything we know of Moses’ death is in Deuteronomy 34: he died in Moab, the Lord buried him in the valley opposite Beth Peor, and to this day no one knows where his grave is. Josh sees God, aware of Israel’s tendency toward golden calves, sending Michael to hide the body. Satan would want it to lure Israel into worshiping shrines to Moses, sending another generation to die in the wilderness and keeping the line of Jesus from moving along.

  14. 31:53 The way of Cain, Balaam’s error, Korah’s rebellion

    Scripture: Jude 11 Hebrews 11:4 Numbers 16

    • Woe to them: they have taken the way of Cain, rushed for profit into Balaam’s error, and been destroyed in Korah’s rebellion. Jude takes after his big half brother with a woe series of his own, though Josh won’t spend long on verse 11.
    • The Cain method is religion without faith; the way of Abel is faith-filled works. Hebrews 11 says by faith Abel brought God a better offering than Cain; the offering wasn’t better, the approach was. Everything Cain did he did for himself, and he ended up with everything except God.
    • Balaam’s error is similar: using the gifts God has given us to earn material gifts for ourselves.
    • Korah’s story is in Numbers 16, and that is homework. He rose up against Moses and opposed Aaron. Misunderstanding God’s authority is a tragic mistake, and we must not rise up recklessly against government or spiritual leaders. It may seem people are getting away with it now, but hang on: “The Lord rebuke you.” He’s coming soon.

  15. 33:33 Stink fruit

    Scripture: Jude 12–13

    • Pop quiz: how do you know wolves? By their fruit. Jude lists the stink fruit these pretenders yield, dangerous to the church, our families, and the gospel: blemishes at your love feasts, shepherds who feed only themselves, clouds without rain, autumn trees without fruit and uprooted, wild waves foaming up their shame, wandering stars for whom blackest darkness has been reserved forever.
    • Women in the room might picture the blemish at the love feast as a mother-in-law in a black dress at the wedding. Close. The word means a rock washed by the sea, a submerged or hidden rock. These people operate under the surface and thrive in low visibility, because it is easier to spread lies when the captain can’t see you.
    • To avoid them, ask good questions, like how well they know the staff. A relationship with even one staff member or elder is great news, but if no one knows them and they seem to like it that way, exercise caution.

    1. 35:17 Selfish shepherds and the servant leader

      Scripture: Matthew 20:25–28

      • Selfish shepherds cause massive destruction; they only care about themselves and use and abuse people. Many could share a story of encountering one. If that is you, Josh is really sorry; it doesn’t belong in the church, we need to fight it, and he is here if you need to talk.
      • Josh isn’t perfect and will make more mistakes, but his example is Jesus, the Good Shepherd who gave his life for the sheep, and he wants to give his life for this church. In Matthew 20 Jesus lays out the leadership model: the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, but not so with you; whoever wants to be great must be your servant, just as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve and give his life as a ransom for many.
      • Those who take advantage of God’s people should be shaking in their boots. “The Lord rebuke you.” The Lord will handle you.

    2. 37:01 The one light that never goes out

      • Nobody wants clouds without rain or plans a road trip to see dead trees lying sideways; nobody books a cruise after the hurricane warnings. No one wants apostates or false teachers, but they’re here, so we need to contend for the faith.
      • You might hear a beautiful sermon with a new take, bright and radiant like a wandering star, and then it falls into darkness forever. There is only one light that never goes out, the light of the world, Jesus. He isn’t hiding behind clever teaching or years of formal education; he is hiding in plain sight, and if you seek him you will find him.

  16. 38:17 Enoch’s prophecy and the one judge

    Scripture: Jude 14–16

    • Jude quotes Enoch, the seventh from Adam: the Lord is coming with thousands upon thousands of his holy ones to judge everyone and convict all the ungodly of their ungodly acts and defiant words. These people are grumblers and faultfinders who follow their own evil desires, boast, and flatter others for advantage.
    • Jude quotes from an apocryphal book, the Book of Enoch, and some scholars think Enoch refers to the flood. Either way Enoch lived when the ungodly seemed to run unchecked, and Jude, Peter, and all of Scripture teach that the ungodly will face God and be judged.

    1. 39:13 Keep buying the truth; we have an advocate

      Scripture: 1 John 2:1–3

      • In the meantime, keep buying the truth over and over. God will one day execute judgment on all; like the flood destroyed everyone outside the ark, condemnation will fall on all who are apart from Christ. There is one judge, Jesus Christ: no jury, no court of appeals, no higher court. When it’s done, it’s done.
      • But there is one other option: one who offered to represent you on that day. John writes, “My dear children, I write this to you so that you will not sin,” and that is what the fear of God does. “But if anybody does sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ, the Righteous One,” the atoning sacrifice for our sins and the sins of the whole world.
      • We don’t know when he will come, but we have to stay ready and contend for the faith, and until he comes we cry out, “Come, Lord Jesus, come.”

  17. 40:59 A fresh start and a prayer

    • Josh creates space to start the year right. A new year feels fresh, but it doesn’t come close to the feeling of receiving grace and stepping into peace with God through Jesus Christ. He has asked the Lord to make him a more present shepherd, aware of the needs around him, so he and the elders make themselves available to pray with anyone.
    • There is no point moving forward in 2025 without making Jesus Lord and Savior, and those who haven’t been baptized are called to do that. It doesn’t mean all the bad stuff goes away, but it gives hope for what is to come, so we can endure as Jesus did for the joy set before us, an eternity with him.
    • Josh prays, thanking God for his word, which shows the way to salvation and helps the church stay on mission. He asks God to fill the room so those who need grace and mercy would find him, and prays that communion would bless believers clinging to his promises as they remember his blood poured out for their sins and a future where he makes all things right again.
