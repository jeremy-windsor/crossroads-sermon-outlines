Overview

  1. 00:47 The father of all who believe: believe what?

    Scripture: Genesis 15 Genesis 12 Genesis 12–25 Genesis 25:7 Romans 4

    • We continue the series in Genesis 15 and the life of Abraham. His story actually starts with his calling in chapter 12; it spans Genesis 12 to 25, maybe 30 minutes of reading, and Genesis 25 says Abraham lived 175 years.
    • In Romans 4 Paul calls Abraham the father of all who believe. The question that must immediately follow is: believe what? What did Abraham believe, and what do all who come after him believe? Abram believed God would do it, and every true believer since believes God will do it. Do what? What he promises. What did he promise? Now we are asking the right questions.

  2. 01:57 Those he called he also justified

    Scripture: Romans 8:29–30 Romans 8:28 Philippians 1:6 Romans 8:30

    • Paul gives a clear order of operation for God’s plan of redemption in Romans 8: those God foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, and those he predestined he called, those he called he justified, and those he justified he glorified.
    • That is one reason there is no such thing as an arrogant Christian: you didn’t choose God; he chose you. Josh wholeheartedly believes Scripture teaches that a person genuinely saved is eternally saved; you can never be snatched out or lose the new life God gave you through Jesus Christ. Paul preached this everywhere; to the Romans he says that in all things God works for the good of those who love him, who have been called according to his purpose.
    • Ladies, your husband might have unfinished projects all over the house, but God doesn’t leave things unfinished. Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Joshua, Rahab, Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, Mary, Joseph, John: like the Hebrew writer, Josh doesn’t have time to list all who walked by faith. They were certain of what they did not see and confident that he who began a good work in them would carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus.
    • Abraham paved the way by living utterly convinced God would do exactly what he said. He knew that those God calls he also justifies, and those he justifies he also glorifies.

  3. 04:01 Abram’s calling: “Go” and “I will”

    Scripture: Genesis 12:1–3 Hebrews 11:8

    • Remember Abram’s calling in chapter 12: the Lord said, “Go from your country, your people and your father’s household to the land I will show you. I will make you into a great nation and I will bless you... and all peoples on earth will be blessed through you.” Yahweh says “Go”: that is the call. Yahweh says “I will”: that is the promise.
    • If Abram didn’t believe God would do it, he wouldn’t have left his country, his people, and the safety and security of his father’s household. But he was called to separate himself from family and country, and the Hebrew writer says by faith Abram obeyed and went.

    1. 04:55 Obedience isn’t optional

      Scripture: Philippians 2:8 James 2:26

      • Maybe Josh is too critical of the church, but many churches seem to present Christianity as an obedience-optional lifestyle. He loves them enough to say that if obedience is optional for you, your so-called faith isn’t a Christian faith.
      • If you resist the tithe, he can only assume you aren’t convinced God will provide. If you resist forgiving others, you must not be convinced God has forgiven you. If you resist humility, you must not be convinced that God humbled himself, took on flesh, and became obedient even to death on a cross.
      • Obedience is evidence of genuine faith. Ask James: faith without works is dead. Faith apart from obedience is counterfeit faith.

    2. 05:49 Fruits of belief: Lot and the kings

      • We know Abram heard the promise because when Lot’s herders started quarreling with his over whose donkey was whose, Abram said, “Lot, we’re family; let’s not fight,” and gave Lot the better land of Sodom. You start seeing fruits of belief.
      • Later Abram displayed his faith by becoming a soldier, chasing kings from Hebron to Damascus like a military brat. King Melchizedek came out singing praise, and Abram gave him a tenth of everything. When Melchizedek wanted to work out a better deal and give him some stuff, Abram said, in effect, “I don’t need you to bless me, because I believe God will bless me; he said he would. I don’t want you taking his glory.”
      • This is the kind of everyday, bold faith we are called to live as God’s household. This is who we are.

  4. 06:57 Faith isn’t positivity; faith is certainty

    Scripture: Hebrews 11:1

    • Far too many people think of faith as spiritual optimism, holy positivity, a backup plan in case things don’t go the way they think they should, or just thinking about their convictions. For many, faith is something to fall back on, when it is actually something to lean on.
    • Hebrews 11 says faith is being sure of what we hope for and certain of what we do not see. Faith isn’t positivity; faith is certainty, and genuine faith reveals itself in everything.
    • This unshakable faith isn’t raised by religion or cultivated by man; it is given by God and relied on every day of the week, and it determines how we lead our homes, spouses, kids, staff, jobs, companies, and friends. It leads us to take God at his word. Christian faith isn’t just believing God exists or believing in God; it is believing what God says: “He said it, I believe it. I can’t see it; I still believe it.”

  5. 08:23 “The word of the Lord came”

    Scripture: Genesis 15:1 Ephesians 2:20–22

    • God called Abram and said “Go,” and Abram responded in faith; that same faith shone as God justified Abram and credited it to him as righteousness. Abram has just returned from rescuing his crazy nephew Lot, and he has a beautiful encounter with the Lord: after these things the word of the Lord came to Abram in a vision.
    • Before getting to what the Lord says, notice how significant “the word of the Lord came” is; everything we know depends on that phrase, used 221 times in the Old Testament. God reveals himself to a prophet, and the prophet shares it by preaching and writing. We have the Old Testament through prophets and the New Testament through apostles.
    • Paul explains to the church in Ephesus that the church is built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the chief cornerstone; in him the whole building is joined together and rises to become a holy temple in the Lord, and in him you are being built together into a dwelling in which God lives by his Spirit.

    1. 09:47 Churches on the wrong foundation

      • Unfortunately many churches are building on the wrong foundation. They are filling with people who don’t look like Jesus when they come and don’t look like Jesus when they leave. The church is off mission; it let culture do a rebrand, and it failed epically, because churches look more like franchises than fellowships.
      • Satellite campuses are popping up everywhere, and it makes you wonder: are you putting a campus there because you prioritize the gospel and think it isn’t being preached, or because you think you can build your brand? We need to get back to his word.

  6. 10:37 “Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield”

    Scripture: Genesis 15:1 Ephesians 6:10–12 1 Peter 5:8

    • “Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield, your very great reward.” Abram had legendary faith, yet even he needed comfort: “I protected you then, I’ll protect you now. I defended you then, I’ll defend you now. I gave you victory yesterday, and there’s plenty more where that came from.”
    • We aren’t that different from Abram. Ephesians 6 says we are in a war too, and Paul takes a page out of God’s playbook: be strong in the Lord and in his mighty power; put on the full armor of God so you can stand against the devil’s schemes, for our struggle is not against flesh and blood but against the rulers, authorities, powers of this dark world, and spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms.
    • Josh talked with his son about this on Monday: we are literally in a war. He believes in a literal devil, prowling around like a lion looking to devour you. He wants to disrupt your praise, convince you to keep more for yourself because you earned it, keep you from loving your neighbor, give you reasons to be bitter, angry, and unforgiving, keep your addiction and your fear, and keep your children and grandchildren from believing and your marriage from lasting. He is after it all.

    1. 12:24 You have to hold the shield up

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:6 Matthew 22:37–39

      • What does a shield require to be effective? You have to hold it up. If Captain America slid you his shield in battle, you wouldn’t refuse it; you would say thank you, get behind it, and hold it up. Flaming arrows may zip by, but you keep holding it up and preaching to yourself: I know God is good, and I know he rewards those who seek him.
      • Whatever battle you are in, believer, don’t lower your shield. Pray continually, ask him to forgive you, keep forgiving others, remind yourself of his promises, don’t just listen to the word but be doers. Love him with all your heart, soul, and mind, love your neighbor, worship him always, never stop singing, and never stop trusting that God will keep his promises and that no strategy of the enemy can derail his intentions.

    2. 13:42 Check the other side of the shield

      • But we struggle here. The sky can be blacked out with arrows, the enemy coming with everything he has, and you get behind the shield, pray, and know the Lord is with you, and then an arrow gets through and draws blood. Most of us say, “God, where were you? Do you see this? I’m bleeding.”
      • Josh gets it; you are hurting and things are getting crazy. But stop and check the other side of that shield. You don’t even know all the times God has protected you, all the arrows that flew around you and missed. Have you ever thanked him for that? “Thank you, Lord, for protecting me. I don’t even know what you protected me from, and I don’t really want to know, but thank you.” He is our shield.

  7. 15:07 “Your very great reward”

    Scripture: Psalm 37:4

    • Next, he is our very great reward. Abram had just given away a tenth of everything, and God says, “Don’t worry, Abram, I’m your great reward: your fortune, your legacy, your wealth.” Abram turned down the wealth of an earthly king for the reward of an eternal King. Will you?
    • What do you value most? Is God number one, or have you made what God gives number one? Money is nice, influence is awesome, health is great as long as it lasts, wisdom is even better, but if you had to choose between God and his gifts, what would you choose? God gives some great gifts, but he is your very great reward. Don’t delight in the gifts over the giver; Psalm 37 says delight yourself in the Lord, and he will give you the desires of your heart.

  8. 16:17 Doubt isn’t unbelief

    • Josh and his wife don’t tolerate kids talking back. When a command has been spoken in the house and the kids come back with questions, he says, “Just drop them in the suggestion box, kiddo.” “But Dad, there is no suggestion box.” “Exactly. Now go do what I asked.” Great news: God is a way better father than Josh is.
    • The church seems to treat doubt like an infectious disease, as if getting too close might rub off on you: contagious cooties. But there is a big difference between doubt and unbelief. Josh doesn’t think doubt disrupts faith; it has the potential to develop faith: “God, I believe you are the one true God, that you are good and working for my good, but I can’t see it right now. You said you’d do this and I believe you will, but it isn’t happening, and I’m bringing it to you because I’m frustrated, or I’m afraid.”
    • Doubt wants to see God; unbelief wants to dismiss God. Unbelief walks away saying, “God, you’re not good, and you won’t do what you said.” Doubt assumes God will do it but hasn’t yet; unbelief assumes God is a liar and never will. Big difference.

  9. 18:22 Abram shares his doubts: count the stars

    Scripture: Genesis 15:2–3 Genesis 15:4–5

    • God tells Abram, “I’m your very great reward,” and in a beautiful moment Abram shares his doubts in verse 2: “Sovereign Lord, what can you give me since I remain childless and the one who will inherit my estate is Eliezer of Damascus? You have given me no children, so a servant in my household will be my heir.” How can God make him a nation without an heir? How can he know God will actually do what he said?
    • Then the word of the Lord came: “This man will not be your heir, but a son coming from your own body.” He took him outside: “Look up at the heavens and count the stars, if indeed you can count them. So shall your offspring be.” One, two... not literally; it is an illustration.
    • In effect God says: look at the stars; that is how many people depend on my promise. I’m never going to let you down, and do you honestly think I’ll fail all of them? My promises are about the entire world, people from every nation and tongue. I’m working my redemption plan for all of them, preparing a way for Jesus, building a highway for the Messiah. Every star needs a Savior, a shield, a new heart and a new life; that is how many people I love and am sending my Son to die for. Every star has a name, and I know how many hairs are on every head. Don’t be afraid. I said I’d do it. I will.

    1. 20:30 Look up tonight

      • If Abraham’s story doesn’t change how you stargaze, Josh doesn’t know what will. Look up tonight; take a break from the games for five or ten minutes and enjoy God. He knows you, loves you, and made a way for you.

  10. 20:53 Abram believed the Lord

    Scripture: Genesis 15:6 John 1:14

    • What is your response? Abram responded in verse 6: Abram believed the Lord, and he credited it to him as righteousness. The word of the Lord came to Abram, and Abram believed the Lord. This is how it will always work: God speaks, we believe; God promises, we trust.
    • Josh doesn’t know everyone’s name or what they came in with, but he has to ask: if you die tonight, do you know where you will spend eternity? He is certain he is spending eternity in heaven with the Lord. Are you?
    • He knows his future not because he is clairvoyant or knows something special, but because God spoke it and he believes him. The Word became flesh, and Josh took God at his word. Who is your authority on matters of eternal life and death? Who do you trust and listen to? Abram believed the word of the Lord, and that is why Christians carry the Bible like a gun and cling to it: it is the word of God.

    1. 22:40 You can’t do it, so he did it for you

      Scripture: Isaiah 64:6 Genesis 6:5 Ephesians 2:4–5 Hebrews 9:27–28

      • As God came to Abram in a vision, he has come to us through his word, and from Genesis to Revelation it is about Jesus Christ. Jesus came because you needed something other than your own righteousness. You can’t do it, and he knew you couldn’t, so he did it for you.
      • When we present the best possible versions of ourselves, it is nothing but filthy rags. God isn’t impressed. As last week showed, the intentions of your heart are only evil all the time; he knows your heart, motives, and actions. “Fine, God, how do we fix this? What do I need to do?” Nothing. It is about what he does for you: “I’ll do this for you. Just trust me,” just as he told Abram.
      • Because of his great love for us, God, who is rich in mercy, made us alive with Christ even when we were dead in transgressions; it is by grace you have been saved. Josh was dead and now is alive. Jesus died to save him, and God raised him three days later, so the one who died to save him is still alive. Hallelujah.
      • Just as people are destined to die once and after that face judgment, so Christ was sacrificed once to take away the sins of many, and he will appear a second time, not to bear sin, but to bring salvation to those waiting for him. Josh is waiting for him and hanging on his every word.

  11. 24:34 Shout your doubt: Thomas

    Scripture: John 20:25–28

    • That doesn’t mean Josh has no doubts or frustrations; he does. His advice: shout your doubt. Follow the example of the most famous doubter in Scripture, Thomas, in John 20. Maybe he left his iPhone on the nightstand and missed the push notification that Christ had risen, but when the disciples told him, he said, “Unless I see the nail marks in his hands and put my finger where the nails were, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe.”
    • A week later they were in the house again. Many Christians miss this: it was a week later. Sometimes you have to sit with your confessed doubt for a while. We love Amazon and want immediate results, in prayer too, but it doesn’t always happen that way. Buy a prayer journal and write down your prayers; it might be a day, a week, a month, a year, or 10 years. Keep praying and wait.
    • A week later Jesus showed up and said to Thomas, “Put your finger here; see my hands. Reach out your hand and put it into my side. Stop doubting and believe.” Thomas said, “My Lord and my God,” and he received salvation right there.

    1. 26:15 God isn’t impatient with doubters

      • With Abram we see that God isn’t impatient with doubters, so we shouldn’t be either. With Thomas we see that God wants to help us out of our doubt, so we should do the same for others. Your doubt is okay; it just isn’t okay to stay there.

  12. 26:37 Abram doubts himself too

    Scripture: Genesis 15:7–8

    • Abram had doubts about God, and keep reading and you see he had doubts about himself too. God said, “I am the Lord who brought you out of Ur of the Chaldeans to give you this land to take possession of it.” Abram said, “Sovereign Lord, how can I know that I will gain possession of it?” He moves from whether God will come through for him to whether he will come through for God.
    • Either way, in our church, life groups, homes, and everywhere, we must create a safe place for doubts to be shared. If we always beat on people for having doubts, we stunt their faith instead of helping it grow.

  13. 27:30 Cutting a covenant

    Scripture: Genesis 15:9–10 Leviticus 1 Jeremiah 34:18–20

    • God keeps addressing the doubt with something special in verse 9. If God assures those he loves by making promises, we should assure those we love by reminding them of his promises.
    • The Lord said, “Bring me a heifer, a goat and a ram, each three years old, along with a dove and a young pigeon.” Abram, who will ask God anything, doesn’t ask a single question; God gives no instruction, yet Abram knows what to do, so it was probably familiar to him. He cut them in two and arranged the halves opposite each other, but not the birds. Josh’s wife, their little Snow White who loves birds, is probably sighing with relief; Leviticus 1 says we aren’t supposed to separate birds in half. A big win for bird lovers.
    • Why the sacrifices and the cutting? They are about to make a covenant, a contract; it literally means the cutting of a covenant. Today we use DocuSign: write down the agreement, both sign on the dotted line, and if you break your word I can sue you for breach of contract. That is how a written culture does it, but in an oral culture like Abram’s, they acted it out. The creative arts people want to act it out; no, we can’t.
    • They acted out the consequences of breaking your word (if discipline isn’t working at home, you could try this; just saying). In Jeremiah the Lord says that those who violated his covenant, the leaders, officials, priests, and people who walked between the pieces of the calf they cut in two, he will hand over to their enemies, and their dead bodies will become food for the birds and wild animals.
    • So you cut the animals in half, walked between the pieces, and said, “If I don’t keep my word, may I be as these animals; may I be cut to pieces and my body be food for the birds.” It was intense, and people kept their word, unlike today: “I wasn’t in my proper mind when I signed that; I think that says Daffy Duck.”

    1. 31:26 Targeted resistance to God’s covenants

      Scripture: Genesis 15:11 Matthew 19:6 Matthew 5:37

      • Pay attention: birds of prey came down on the carcasses, but Abram drove them away. There is always targeted resistance to God’s covenants. Satan wants to break your marriage covenant in half; you have to fight for it and stay committed.
      • When you decide to give your life to Christ, the birds descend immediately. The devil is always trying to separate what God joined together and separate you from Jesus. Don’t let him. Follow Jesus and keep it simple: all you need to say is yes or no; anything beyond that comes from the evil one. Keep your word.

  14. 32:23 God shows Abram the future

    Scripture: Genesis 15:12 Genesis 15:13–16

    • Abram thinks he is about to walk through the pieces, a big moment of spoken promises between him and God. Instead something even more amazing happens: as the sun was setting, Abram fell into a deep sleep, and a thick and dreadful darkness came over him.
    • Recap why: Abram had doubts because he didn’t have a son yet. So God, the sovereign one, is about to reveal the future: “Let me show you.” Be careful what you ask for. “Know for certain that for 400 years your descendants will be strangers in a country not their own, enslaved and mistreated, but I will punish the nation they serve, and they will come out with great possessions. You will go to your ancestors in peace at a good old age. In the fourth generation your descendants will come back, for the sin of the Amorites has not yet reached its full measure.”
    • Josh doesn’t want to know the future even if he could; it comes with baggage he doesn’t want, and God’s ways are above his. God tells Abram there will be tough years ahead, because he will be patient with the Amorites, still offering them the opportunity to repent even though he knows they won’t, and Abram’s descendants will endure those years as he displays his divine patience for generations.
    • A shield not only protects you from getting hurt; it also keeps your eyes from seeing hard things on the other side you might not understand. Did they get the land after the Exodus? No, not until Joshua. Abram is struggling with not having a son, and God is working a plan that stretches over generations.
    • We get impatient too. We want to see the day of the Lord, and there is nothing wrong with that hope: maranatha, come Lord Jesus. But God says, in effect, “I know you are looking forward to paradise, no more pain, tears, or sadness, but I want to save more people. I was patient and kind with you; I’m going to be patient and kind with the Amorites too. I’m the same God, there is more going on than you can see. Do you trust me?”

  15. 35:49 A smoking firepot and a blazing torch

    Scripture: Genesis 15:17

    • When the sun had set and darkness had fallen, a smoking firepot with a blazing torch appeared and passed between the pieces. The words for smoking firepot and blazing torch are also used when God descends on Mount Sinai; they are words used when God shows up physically. This is a big moment.
    • “Blazing torch” is often used for lightning. We are used to lightning flashing and then gone; this is blazing lightning. Imagine the crackling sound, the sparks, the heat, as it passes between the pieces.
    • God is signing on the dotted line, walking between the pieces and saying, “If I don’t do what I told you I’d do, may I be like these pieces.” He doesn’t say, “Abram, you go first; show me you’re all in, then I’ll commit to you.” He says, “I commit myself to you. Period. Got a pen? No? It’s okay, I’ll use lightning.”

    1. 37:38 God alone passes through

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:13 Psalm 110:4

      • As amazing as that is, there is more. Who passes through? God alone. Hebrews 6 says when God made his promise to Abraham, since there was no one greater for him to swear by, he swore by himself. So God tells Abram this covenant isn’t about your faithfulness; it is about my faithfulness.
      • Israel would have been greatly encouraged by this; it would have carried them through all that suffering: remember the promise. And we can too, because it is true of all God’s promises, including salvation. No matter what anybody says, does, or thinks, God says, “I’m going to send a Messiah; he will shed satisfying blood, sin will be forgiven, and people will be saved.” The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind.
      • So God came down to our level, took on flesh, and became the Christ, fully God and fully man. He lived the life we could never live, died the death we most certainly deserved, and offers new life to all who believe in him.

  16. 39:10 Become a scholar of Christ’s promises

    Scripture: John 6:37 Matthew 4:19 Matthew 11:28 John 14:16–17 John 16:8 John 14:2–3

    • Jesus made promises too. If you are struggling or doubting, become a scholar of Christ’s promises: know them, memorize them, remember them, recall them.
    • John 6: all whom the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never drive away. Those who are his are his forever. Matthew 4: follow me, and I will make you fishers of men. Jesus promises not only life after death but a life of purpose now; you may feel inadequate and afraid like Abram, but Jesus will lead your transformation if you follow him.
    • Matthew 11: come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. Salvation is a gift; you don’t deserve it or earn it; Jesus did it for you, so rest in him.
    • Jesus promises the Holy Spirit: “I will ask the Father, and he will give you another advocate to help you and be with you forever, the Spirit of truth.” The world can’t accept him, but you know him, for he lives with you and will be in you. Jesus later explains that the Comforter will convict the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment, so he will guide us as we walk in new life.
    • And look at this promise: “My Father’s house has plenty of room; if that were not so, would I have told you that I am going there to prepare a place for you? And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come back and take you to be with me.” I will come back.

  17. 41:23 Respond: shout your doubt and come to the table

    Scripture: 1 John 1:9

    • Josh hopes people respond to Jesus today: by singing, giving a tithe, or taking communion. Two chalkboards, front and back, are for shouting your doubt: make known, as Abram did, what you are wrestling with and your frustrations, doubts about God and what he is doing, or about yourself. You don’t grow in faith by burying it or pretending it isn’t there, but by trusting God to work through it.
    • He hasn’t lived that long, but he knows life is so much better when you surrender. Stop fighting a fight that is already over. Write it down, then pray with the person next to you a simple prayer: “Lord, fill us with reminders of your promises.” Leave the thing that frustrates your faith behind; the enemy loves the shadows, so drag your fears, burdens, anxieties, frustrations, and doubts into the light.
    • Then go to the Lord’s table, look at the board, and remember the sacrifice of Jesus: his body and blood remind us God was willing to send his only Son to die on a cross so his covenant of love would be available to everyone who believes.
    • If you decide to follow Jesus, the waters of baptism are ready; you don’t need a T-shirt. The Christian walk isn’t private; secret Christians are counterfeit Christians, and real faith can’t be kept to yourself. Repent and ask God to forgive you; he is faithful and just and will forgive your sins and cleanse you from all unrighteousness. Baptism is a public sign of what is happening in your heart. Don’t wait; tell the world what the Lord has done for you.
