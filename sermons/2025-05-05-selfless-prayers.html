Overview

  1. 00:16 Recap: predestined for praise

    • The church continues its 12-week adventure through Paul’s letter to the Ephesians. Josh encourages everyone to stay locked in; perfect attendance should be the goal, but if you miss a week, catch up on YouTube.
    • Last week’s message, “Predestined for Praise,” saw Paul open his prison letter from Rome by drawing attention to God’s sovereignty over history and his plan of salvation through Jesus, using massive words like chosen, predestined, and adoption. God initiates salvation; he is its author, sustainer, and finisher, so there is no debate about who chose whom.
    • That is the best news ever: if you can’t earn it, you can’t lose it. Salvation isn’t like your car keys or your favorite flask left at church on Sunday. Even if you aren’t faithful, he is, and the moment you believe you are marked with the seal of the precious Holy Spirit. God’s salvation isn’t temporary; it is forever.

  2. 01:39 Predestined to produce praise

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:11

    • When we encounter this unconditional love, mercy, and grace, taken from the orphanage and given a new name, home, family, and heart, all of God’s children are led to the same response: praise. Those loved like this don’t have to exhaust themselves trying to love God; it comes naturally. Just as the apple tree is predestined to produce apples, the believer is predestined to produce praise.
    • We praise for two reasons, the person and the work: who God is and what he has done. In verse 11 Paul says he works out everything in conformity with the purpose of his will. God has a plan and it never fails; whether a good or evil king is on the throne, God always accomplishes his will, and Josh invites a fact check.

  3. 02:56 If God never fails, why pray?

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:15

    • Keep reading, and in verse 15 is one of Paul’s many prison prayers; that is today’s text. The question that always comes up is: if God works out everything in conformity with his will, why pray? Christians tend to fall into fatalism, the belief that all events are predetermined and human actions can’t alter the future, so the outcome is the same no matter what.
    • This is where pausing for a week hurts our understanding. Josh hopes no one reads the Bible 15 verses at a time with a week in between: “Please stop.” Go read the entire book of Ephesians. Paul didn’t write chapters and verses; he wrote a letter, and it is all one thought. Paul has just said God’s plan never fails, the seal is a guarantee, and God chose his children in Christ before he created light, and then he launches into prayer.
    • Paul understands that God isn’t just in control of the ending. He works out everything: beginning, middle, and end, past, present, and future. The potter will take it all, shape it all, and conform it all to his good and perfect will.

    1. 04:46 Is God the author of sin?

      Scripture: Genesis 3

      • Some intellectual types argue that this makes God the author of sin. That is a lie, because from Genesis 3 on, God is responding to sin and working his redemption plan through it. He is the potter and we are the clay, and he can use not only flawed people but even our sinful actions and behaviors to accomplish his purpose, his will, and his glory.

  4. 05:22 God’s method is his people

    • Here is where prayer comes in. God is over it all, but his method for producing his outcomes is his people. He is building his church and gathering his people, and he uses those gathered sheep to find those still lost. We tell them about Jesus, our testimony, and his sacrifice for sin; we teach truth, display grace as best we can, and pray for them to see, know, and love him. God works out everything through his people, and even when we fail, he never will.

  5. 06:06 Two camps: lazy and hyperactive Christians

    • Christians usually lean toward one of two extremes: those who hide at home waiting to be raptured, and those who burn themselves out thinking it all depends on them. Be honest about which you tend toward: the lazy Christian saying, “God’s got this, he doesn’t need me,” or the hyperactive Christian saying, “Don’t worry, God, I got this.”

    1. 06:57 The lazy Christian

      • The lazy Christian is right in a way: God doesn’t need you, but he wants you, and he chose you for a purpose. Satan would love to convince you into complacency. There is nothing wrong with enjoying life, God created rest and it is good, but we must not use God’s sovereignty to justify our complacency. We all need to contribute.

    2. 07:27 The hyperactive Christian

      • Josh has run into more hyperactive Christians: the pastor who refuses to take a break, the staff member who won’t take a sabbatical, the person always asking, “Why don’t we do this? Have we tried that?” They fall in love with the grind, and you can see it on their face; they look like everything depends on them, and you are exhausted just being around them.
      • Beware the church that idolizes results and has no concept of rest: 19 weekend services and staff paid minimum wage. That isn’t a church; it is a corporation masquerading as a church. It usually comes with a preacher who won’t share the pulpit and talks about his teaching gift, when his job is to equip the body to use their gifts. “Put my message on TV in every city. I’ll preach 52 weeks a year. I’m irreplaceable.” These are the people we see fall all the time, and it isn’t a coincidence.
      • The hyperactive Christian misses something important: God can use you when you are energized, and he can use someone else when you need a break.

    3. 09:30 A test, and a next step

      Scripture: Ephesians 1

      • Here is a test his staff won’t like on Monday: take a break. If your first feeling is guilt, your gifting has become a god; if you feel guilty when you rest, your work has become an idol. If you think the kingdom can’t move forward without you, your view of God is too small and your view of yourself far too great. Truly great Christians love to get out of the way and watch God shine through someone else.
      • If you are a lazy Christian, and Josh has been lazy before, sign up to volunteer: get off the couch and into the game. If you are hyperactive, step aside and let someone else get experience. No judgment, just healthy Christian community; grab a pastor or elder and pursue your purpose.
      • In Ephesians 1 we are in the tension between God’s control and our contribution. God wants to use us, so we can’t be lazy, but we can’t be crazy either, because God will get it done without us too.

  6. 11:08 Healthy prayer lives: faith and love

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:15–16

    • To walk this out in a healthy way, we need healthy prayer lives. Josh reads: for this reason, ever since I heard about your faith in the Lord Jesus and your love for all his people, I have not stopped giving thanks for you, remembering you in my prayers.
    • Paul identifies them by two things: their trust in Jesus and their love for one another. Josh can’t think of a better definition of a Christian, and Jesus frames it the same way in the two greatest commandments: love God and love others. Love for all kinds of people without loving God isn’t Christianity, maybe just tolerance; loving God but not people isn’t behaving like a Christian.
    • Do people hear about your faith in Jesus? Do they see your love for his people? Are there rumors about your faith and your love? Nowhere in Scripture are the two separated. And they are just the beginning, which is why Paul keeps praying for them: amazing things happen when the church is filled with prayers for one another.

    1. 12:36 Prayers for others, not ourselves

      • Most of our prayer lives are self-centered; we ask for what we don’t have. Not Paul. He gives thanks for others and makes requests on their behalf. Have you ever felt loved like that through someone’s prayer life? It is a tragedy if you haven’t, and Josh hopes this church can change that.
      • Besides God sending Jesus to die for his sins, Josh has never felt more loved in the church than when someone says, “Josh, I love you. I’m grateful for you, and I’m praying for you.” That is how we love one another.

    2. 13:38 Our lopsided prayers

      • We are instructed to pray for our enemies and for those in authority, whether we voted for them or not. It blows Josh’s mind that there is an entire day dedicated to praying for those in authority, but when is there a day for praying for our enemies? We are lopsided; we do what we want and skip what is hard. We are to pray for those in need, the sick, and those suffering for his name. God’s people are a praying people everywhere they go.

    3. 14:15 Pray for the people within these walls

      • We get so focused on high-level prayers that we forget people have needs within these walls. We bump shoulders but don’t ask or pray for one another. You don’t need names or stories, because God knows. Look at a person and pray from afar: “I don’t know you or what you’re carrying, but I’m saying a prayer for you today.” It doesn’t have to be more than that.
      • People have shown Josh photos of strangers they snapped in the lobby, zoomed in so they wouldn’t forget the face to pray for. A little creepy, but after thinking about it all week, he is okay with it. So he takes a panorama of the congregation, telling anyone who doesn’t want prayer to cover their face, and promising to pray for them anyway.

    4. 16:28 Love for one another is the heartbeat

      • It isn’t complicated. Our love for one another is the heartbeat of this place. Subpar preaching or music in the wrong key, who cares; love for one another is what will mark this church and set it apart.
      • Satan can quickly rob a church of its love. Instead of praying for someone, he tempts you to talk behind their back. Where there should be camaraderie, there is competition; instead of fellowship, fighting; instead of opportunities, oppression. If the church isn’t praying for one another, it will suffocate one another. And there is a big difference between praying out of obligation, duty, or guilt and praying out of genuine love in response to how the Lord loved you.

  7. 17:36 Paul’s prison prayers and four requests

    Scripture: Philippians 1 Colossians 1

    • So how should we pray, and for what? Paul prays from prison several times, in the first chapter of letters like Ephesians, Philippians, and Colossians; reading all his prison prayers would make a good study for a life group, spouse, or kids.
    • Most compelling: Paul never asks to be released. He doesn’t request material things or health. He asks for spiritual sight, wisdom, and Christian character for others. He doesn’t ask God to give them something they don’t have; he prays God would open their hearts to see what they already have.
    • His prayer has four requests: God, help them know you; help them know your call; help them know your riches; help them know your power.

  8. 18:47 Request one: help them know you

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:17

    • Verse 17: I keep asking that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the glorious Father, may give you the Spirit of wisdom and revelation, so that you may know him better. Paul wants them to know God better, and knows they can only do so with the Spirit of wisdom and revelation.
    • Having laid out the huge mystery of God’s salvation and unconditional love in the most amazing sentence of all time, Paul now prays they can see it. Even the great apostle Paul needs God’s help in sharing the gospel. You can give people the truth, but only God can give them sight.

    1. 19:43 Josh’s LASIK story

      • Josh would give up every other sense before sight. Smell, taste, and hearing he could do without, since he doesn’t care about food and doesn’t like listening to people talk; just don’t take sight or touch.
      • His scariest procedure was LASIK. Strapped down like one of Frankenstein’s experiments, he heard the doctor say everything would go dark and wondered whether he had made a mistake and what kind of monster performs this on someone awake. Then the doctor narrated slicing through the cornea and said, “Don’t move,” which is what he thought the straps were for. Twenty minutes of pure anxiety, like the frog in eighth-grade science lab, but now he sees 20/20.

    2. 22:34 Knowing isn’t the same as seeing

      Scripture: Psalm 34:8

      • We don’t want to lose sight because we would miss big moments in the lives of people we love, and spiritual sight is no different. It is one thing to know God loves you and another to see it. Paul agrees with the psalmist that knowing isn’t the same as experiencing: “Taste and see that the Lord is good.” Paul isn’t content with us being taught that the Lord is good; he wants us to experience God’s goodness coming alive in us.

    3. 23:16 The sender and the receiver

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:18 Isaiah 11:2 Matthew 13:13

      • Verse 18: I pray that the eyes of your heart may be enlightened. Remember two facts about enlightenment: the sender and the receiver.
      • The sender is the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of wisdom and revelation. Isaiah describes him: the Spirit of the Lord will rest on him, the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and might, of knowledge and the fear of the Lord. He reveals what man can’t understand on his own, illuminates truth from the word, gives wisdom to apply it and power to live it, and even the motivation to walk in it: the fear of the Lord.
      • The receiver is the believer. Seeing God with the eyes of your heart creates a deep affection for him, a love that drives out cravings for sin and shows up for the people around you. In Matthew 13 Jesus explains why he speaks in parables: though seeing, they do not see; though hearing, they do not hear or understand. You can’t intellectually grind your way to spiritual sight; the Spirit of God must open the eyes of the heart.

    4. 25:02 Rock-solid theology, no passion

      • Josh is outspoken that one of Satan’s most effective tools in the modern church is tradition. Many Christians have rock-solid theology but no passion: eyes open, but no heart. They know the creeds and catechisms, are well spoken, and do and think all the right things, yet something is off.
      • Their paper on God’s love would get them into the top seminaries, but God’s love never moves them to tears. They understand it but have never felt or been overwhelmed by it. That is why churches without prayer look more like Ivy League universities than houses of worship. It isn’t about what you think or do; it is about who you love.

    5. 26:19 “Church, do you know him?”

      • There is no greater knowledge. The atheist says there is no God to know; the agnostic says if there is, we can’t know him. But Paul met God in the flesh, the risen Christ on the road to Damascus, and knows a man can’t understand anything of value without first knowing God. Cram all you want, but if the Spirit hasn’t opened the eyes of your heart, you have nothing of value. “Oh Lord, open our eyes.”
      • That is why Ephesians begins with who God is and what he has done, followed by a prayer that they would know him. Only then does it get into marriage, family, friendship, temptation, sin, and work, because all of those require an understanding of who God is and what he has done.

    6. 27:27 Pray this over your home

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:18

      • The most important thing you can pray over your life, children, spouse, and home is that you would know him: not only intellectually, but the Spirit’s revealed truth penetrating hearts and birthing a deep, life-altering affection. When did you last pray like that for anyone? Men, for your wives; women, for your husbands; parents, for your children.
      • Josh models the prayer: take the guesswork out of my house; we don’t need more philosophy or religion, we need to know you. Convince my children who you are. Lord, you love my wife better than I ever could; teach me to give my life for her, the closest I can get to walking in your footsteps. So that when we see Arizona sunsets and monsoons, we don’t speculate; we know, and our kids and neighbors know. Open the eyes of our hearts, Lord, that we may know the hope to which he has called us.

  9. 29:16 Request two: help them know your call

    Scripture: Ephesians 1

    • The English language has destroyed the word “hope”: I hope she says yes, I hope the kids move out soon, I hope my mother-in-law doesn’t stay an extra week. It feels like a roll of the dice. Biblical hope is certainty that what hasn’t happened will happen.
    • Paul has been explaining in Ephesians 1 that God chose and loved you before creation and will keep you in his love until the story ends. That is our hope: beginning in his love and ending in his love. And if I begin and end in his love, I don’t need to go find another love while I wait for him. Our hope helps us overcome sin, temptation, suffering, and trials. If I see value in what God has for me in his kingdom, I don’t need to chase value down here; if the one true living God accepts me, I don’t need their acceptance.

    1. 30:56 “Doesn’t that bother you?”

      • After Josh shared about some hate mail, one of the nicest ladies in the lobby, who doesn’t know him but listens to him preach, asked, “Josh, doesn’t that bother you?” Sometimes it does. But he reminds himself: why would he want to be accepted by a world that killed Jesus? They didn’t love him, so Josh doesn’t care if they don’t love him.

    2. 31:44 Prayer as the first solution

      • The church today is slow to pray for struggling believers and slow to see prayer as the first solution. When you see a believer struggling, pray over it: open the eyes of our hearts, Lord; help us know you, what you would have for us, and the hope you’ve called us to. That hope will penetrate our lives and motivate purity, obedience, faithfulness, and love, because we are certain of what is coming.

  10. 32:26 Who we pray to

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:17 Romans 5:5 1 John 1:9

    • Before the third request, Josh pauses to honor different backgrounds and address confusion about who we pray to. In verse 17, Paul prays to the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the glorious Father. We believe in one God in three persons, and we pray to the Father, through the Son, by the power of the Holy Spirit. Our Lord taught us to pray to God as Father, so we approach him with confidence, as a son approaches a loving father.
    • The only reason we can talk to God is the work of Jesus on the cross: our sins separated us, and Jesus died and rose to take them away and reconcile us to the Father. The Holy Spirit works this out in us, convicting of sin, righteousness, and judgment, guiding us in truth, and illuminating Scripture so we can know, understand, and obey God. Paul says God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit, and if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness.

    1. 33:58 The power is in the name

      • Anyone can pray. It isn’t the prayer itself that is powerful but the name you put faith in while praying; not what you say or how you say it, but who you admire when you do. “In Jesus’ name” is biblical and good, but what gives a prayer power isn’t the name you sign off with; it is the name you consider when you pick up the phone.

  11. 34:40 Request three: help them know your riches

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:18

    • Paul prays they would know the riches of his glorious inheritance in his people. Read carefully: last week was our inheritance in Christ, but here Paul refers to God’s inheritance in us. He isn’t repeating himself. God looks at his people, his saints, as part of his glorious treasure.
    • If knowing you are God’s greatest treasure doesn’t drive you to worship, nothing will. You are so valuable that Jesus stepped down from heaven, gave up equality with God, humbled himself, took on flesh, and suffered and died willingly so we could have peace with the Father. Paul prays we wouldn’t just know it but feel it and be overwhelmed by it.

    1. 36:08 Six things the Lord hates

      Scripture: Proverbs 6:16–19

      • We are so unworthy, yet God keeps placing worth on us. Josh tries not to talk about God’s love without also talking about what God hates; some ask why he says the Lord hates, but Scripture teaches it and the church teaches the whole counsel of God, not a fluffy version.
      • Proverbs 6:16: six things the Lord hates, seven that are detestable to him: haughty eyes, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, a heart that devises wicked schemes, feet quick to rush into evil, a false witness who pours out lies, and a person who stirs up dissension. Haughty eyes alone, a specific kind of pride, bring us all down; we have all looked down on someone.
      • So how can we behave in ways God hates and still be his treasure? Only one explanation: because of Christ, God now deals with us on the basis of our future, not our past.

    2. 37:46 Gideon, Peter, and who you will be

      Scripture: Judges 6:12

      • Josh always thinks of Gideon. The angel of the Lord said, “The Lord is with you, mighty warrior,” and Gideon, hiding from the Midianites, probably thought he meant the guy behind him. Jesus called Simon a rock, and he responded by being a coward. But Gideon became that mighty warrior, and Peter became the rock the Lord knew he would be. We aren’t defined by what we were but by what we will be when Christ returns.
      • To the young hearts in the room: you are precious to God, and you don’t have to build up that worth on your own; he is already placing it on you. A culture says, “You’re fine. You’re amazing.” No, none of us are, but it doesn’t matter what you were. It matters what he says about you. He loves you and made sacrifices for you. Hang on tight, hold fast, and never forget you aren’t doing it alone.

  12. 39:34 Request four: help them know your power

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:18–23

    • Josh reads the prayer through: the eyes of your heart enlightened to know the hope, the riches, and his incomparably great power for us who believe, the same mighty strength he exerted when he raised Christ from the dead and seated him at his right hand, far above all rule and authority, with all things under his feet, head over everything for the church, which is his body, the fullness of him who fills everything in every way.

    1. 40:20 Resurrection power at work in you

      • The power at work in believers is the same resurrection power that robbed the grave, the power behind the greatest event in human history, that put breath back into lungs with no chance of breathing again, that conquered Satan, sin, and death.
      • So addiction isn’t too much for you to overcome; destructive behavior doesn’t have to define you; the most selfish, obsessive, hateful desires in you can be dealt with. God brought life out of death, so he can breathe life into the deadliest parts of your life. “Lord, help us to know your power.”

    2. 41:33 Savior and sovereign, head of the church

      Scripture: John 19:30 Job 42:2 Ephesians 1:22–23

      • No authority or power in the physical or spiritual world is greater than Jesus. He isn’t only Savior; he is sovereign. All we need is his name, which is above every other name. He is no longer in the grave; he has ascended, God exalted him, and no enemy can overcome him. Seated at the Father’s right hand, he is King of kings and Lord of lords: “You might as well tattoo it on a thigh.”
      • Paul prays believers would see the finality of God’s plan. Now we know what Jesus meant: “It is finished.” May we adopt Job’s mindset: “I know that you can do all things; no purpose of yours can be thwarted.” His power and authority are available to those who believe. Do you believe?
      • Jesus is the head of the church, the boss, and we are connected to him, the fullness of him who fills everything in every way. Josh loves that Jesus fills this church just as God’s presence filled the temple Solomon built, and through the resurrected, ascended, exalted Christ, the power of the Holy Spirit is available to all Christians by faith.

  13. 43:10 How can we doubt?

    • There will be times Christians are tempted to doubt, and Josh has carried heavy doubts. That is why we must pray for one another, so we never forget the power God displayed in faithfully accomplishing salvation through Jesus.
    • How can we doubt when this divine mystery has unraveled before our eyes? How can we feel insecure when we are sealed with a guarantee, forsaken when we are so clearly forgiven, powerless when we have the same power that raised Jesus, or defeated when the power that seated Christ at God’s right hand has seated us there with him? It is time to live lives of power and prayer again. We may not always find resolutions or answers, but we can still love well and always pray with power.

    1. 44:42 Pray like Jesus in his final moments

      Scripture: Luke 23:46

      • It isn’t how long your prayers are. Pray like Jesus did in his final moments: “Father, into your hands I commit my spirit.” Father, I trust you. If that is how Jesus handled death, that is how we will handle life: “Father, I’m yours. No matter what you give, no matter what you take, nothing will take me from you.”
      • People all around us need to see God like this, so we must never stop praying for them, asking God to open the eyes of their hearts.

  14. 45:40 Closing prayer

    • Josh commits his life into the Father’s hands, knowing he has said these words before and sensing that his Christian walk is to keep saying them: my life, marriage, home, children, career, pride, and ego are all yours. He humbles himself, trusting that God will lift him up, and dedicates his life to waiting for that lifting.
    • He has a hope, a certainty that God will do what he said, and that when God wraps up history he will be with him, not against him. He thanks God for a glorious salvation he can count on day after day.
