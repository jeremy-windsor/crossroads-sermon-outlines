Overview

  1. 00:49 Good news and bad news

    Scripture: John 13:34

    • Josh opens with good news and bad news, and at Ken’s insistence starts with the bad. Last year in-person attendance stayed basically flat, about one and a half percent. Worse, less than half of those who attend are in a life group, and less than a third of this body of believers serves in a ministry.
    • The good news: the church’s online presence and influence on social media and YouTube grew by over 10%, getting the truth out to people we don’t even know.
    • What the numbers tell Josh is that the church has more spectators than doers. There is a difference between being nice and being loving; love is an act of sacrificing for another. We aren’t only called to love God: Jesus came with a new command to love one another as he loved us. You cannot call yourself a disciple of Christ if you don’t serve others and actively participate in building up the church.

  2. 02:52 The back door and the problem of maturity

    • There is nuance, and the church should give itself a little grace. The numbers haven’t stayed flat because the front door isn’t being used; new people are coming and neighbors are being invited, frequently. But the back door is overused, people are sneaking out, and it comes out a wash.
    • Josh believes the biggest obstacle to growth this last year has been maturity. It isn’t subpar preaching, loud music, a building that is a shopping mall, or being non-denominational rebels. It is people who join this body with no interest in growing up: infants who divide over secondary issues, who expect paid professionals to carry out all the ministry, who need to be bottle-fed and shown which way to go at every moment.
    • Josh apologizes for swinging, but the message is confrontational. Some of this is outside the church’s control: people who aren’t interested in holy lives avoid biblical teaching, truth takes a back seat to grace when holiness doesn’t matter, and those who don’t love sacrificially love to scurry. Today will force self-evaluation in everyone; he has wrestled with it all week. How can you come once a month, never serve, never give, and call yourself a disciple? He commanded us to love one another as he loved us; if we disobey that command and say we love him, how does it add up?

  3. 05:20 “Go get them”: immaturity and broken unity

    • One way this shows up is when people report that someone has left: they didn’t like this, “You were bald, now you have hair,” whatever it is, and Josh should reach out and bring them back. Why? You had the relationship with them, and you were there through their departure. Go get them.
    • The immaturity of believers is one of the most discouraging parts of ministry. Have you seen a grown-up throw a fit like a child, rolling on the ground because his toy got taken or Daddy spoke too firmly? In high school, if somebody ticked Josh off he stopped talking to them and found other friends. You come to church thinking it won’t be like high school, and then people disagree and go down the street instead of working through it like mature adults, dodging you in the supermarket. “Josh, you should do this.” “No, that’s not us.” “Okay, I’m leaving.” “Okay, bye.”
    • The irony is that the church these immature believers split from will one day be brought under one roof by our Savior and Lord Jesus Christ. If we are going to have unity, why not have it now? From what Josh knows of his king, you will answer for breaking unity 18 times over secondary issues. If he is preaching heresy and a false gospel, split; if he just doesn’t align with your preferences, that is an unacceptable reason. And if you slip out the back door without a word to an elder or Josh, you are in error and will give an account for acting like an infant.
    • The church needs to grow up, and if that means a season of pruning, so be it. The all-talk-no-walk model is not his design for his church. Today not Josh but Paul is coming after our poor attitudes, our lazy efforts, and most of all our immaturity.

  4. 08:26 First doctrine, then duty

    Scripture: Romans 1–11 Romans 12:1

    • Josh warned this day was coming in the series. We have dealt with doctrine: we know who he is, who we are in Christ, what we are called to be, what to believe, and what has been done on our behalf. Now Paul wants us to know our duty and responsibility. Believers have spiritual wealth, not to hoard but to share.
    • The key word in the first three chapters of Ephesians is riches; the key word in the back half is walk. You can tell a lot about someone by how they walk, and Paul makes clear that Christians walk in unity, purity, harmony, and victory.
    • Paul’s pattern is first doctrine, then duty, and the connection point is the word “therefore.” Romans has the same pattern: the first 11 chapters are doctrinal, and in chapter 12 he gets personal and practical: “Therefore, I urge you, brothers and sisters, in view of God’s mercy, to offer your bodies as a living sacrifice.” Based on everything said so far, present your life to God.
    • If you don’t know, you can’t do. This is how the Spirit works: we hear, we are convicted, and we change course. What you believe determines how you behave. We are called by grace, raised from the dead, reconciled to God and each other, and share Christ’s victory over Satan, sin, and death, and we must know these riches to walk according to them. So, church, it is time to get stepping.

  5. 10:46 Verse 1: a prisoner for the Lord

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:1

    • “As a prisoner for the Lord, then, I urge you to live a life worthy of the calling you have received.” First we notice the costly nature of the Christian walk. Paul knows he is a prisoner of Rome, but he makes clear it is for the Lord.
    • This is one of the first areas where we need to grow up. We cry out, “Why did this have to happen to me? What did I do to deserve this?” Imagine adopting the mindset that no matter my circumstances, it is for the Lord. Optimism for a believer who understands the gospel is relentless. Paul saw everything through the gospel: he is a prisoner not because Rome wants him there but because the Lord has him there for a purpose. That is how Paul learned contentment, with a vertical, kingdom perspective instead of an earthly one.

    1. 11:53 Gospel gratitude

      • We don’t walk worthy lives so that God will love us, but because he loves us. The only way to live up to the calling, Josh says to write it down, is two words: gospel gratitude. That is your only shot.
      • That is why new believers are his favorite to be around. Gratitude is easy when you’re wet, coming out of the baptism water. It is harder once you’ve dried off, gone home, and had to make sacrifices because the Holy Spirit is saying, “You don’t need this. Get rid of this.” So Paul explains the attitude that pours out from gospel gratitude: new believers aim for it, and veterans fight to maintain it.

  6. 12:42 Verses 2–3: the graces of a worthy walk

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:2–3

    • Paul writes: “Be completely humble and gentle; be patient, bearing with one another in love. Make every effort to keep the unity of the Spirit through the bond of peace.”

    1. 12:56 Unity is not uniformity

      • Paul uses the body as a picture of Christian unity: each part is different, yet all make up one body and work together. That is why breaks in unity over preferences are so ugly, and Josh doesn’t think the church rebukes them enough. When you reject someone over a non-essential, you reject a part of the body Christ put in place: a preacher for his style, a worship pastor for theirs, a life group leader for their view of the end times, a family over preferences or politics. That pride says you know better than God who belongs where.
      • God knows the needs of this church and this city, and he has brought us together, empowered us, and resourced us to bless others for the advancement of the gospel and the glory of God. Leaving a church for any reason other than its abandoning the gospel is an act of pride, not humility, however you justify slipping out quietly as the high road.
      • Josh says this because he loves the church, not to talk down; he is a fool and has failed to maintain unity many times. But church hopping reveals ignorance of the gospel and isn’t a life worthy of the calling. Get in, get involved, and stay unless he starts teaching a false gospel, and even then, don’t leave; make him leave. If he shows up with purple hair and a rainbow sash calling himself Priestess Josh, drag him out. Unity is not uniformity, but unity does not require heresy.

    2. 15:48 Humility

      Scripture: Isaiah 64:6 Romans 12:3

      • All we need are the graces Paul lists, starting with humility. If you are convinced you have it, you probably don’t. Someone truly humble is haunted by the reality that they might be the worst one, not the best one.
      • You can’t get the gospel and be prideful; they are oil and water. Even a basic understanding of what God has done makes you humble, because your most righteous acts are like filthy rags to God. He saw your hopeless condition and made a way back from the dead through faith in Jesus Christ, when you weren’t even interested. He finished it and drew you to himself. You didn’t see how good God was and run to him; he saw how terrible you were and saved you. There is nothing to boast about.
      • True Christian humility puts God first, everyone else second, and yourself last. How much you give financially makes no difference to Josh if it is joyful and sacrificial, but if you don’t give at all, or not sacrificially, you have some growing up to do. Humility shows up in service and sacrifice.
      • Paul says this to the Romans too, in the Romans 12 parallel: do not think of yourself more highly than you ought, but with sober judgment. There is a balance: we shouldn’t think too highly of ourselves, but not too little either, because God went to great lengths to save and redeem you, and shame has no right to hang around.

    3. 18:28 Gentleness, or meekness

      • Humility always produces gentleness, or meekness, a clear sign of humility; if you are prideful, you can’t be meek. Meekness isn’t weakness; it is power that bends to God’s will. Moses was meek, yet look what God used him to accomplish. Jesus was meek, yet he drove the money changers from the temple. Anyone can claim humility, but meekness is evidence they have it.

    4. 19:05 Patience

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 13:4

      • Patience literally means long-tempered: the ability to endure discomfort without fighting back. It can’t be separated from love; Paul tells the Corinthians, “Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud.”
      • Noah is a wonderful example of long temper and loving patience. God told him to build a ship in the desert, miles from water, and he worked 120 years. Some of us have two months of hardship and fall apart; Noah endured 120 years while people mocked him and called him a fool. He loved them and told them of the coming judgment rather than spitefully withholding it. No one believed him, but he told them.
      • To this day the rainbow is a sign reminding us that God is the ultimate promise keeper. How ironic, Josh says, that the sexual rebellion in our world chose the rainbow, the symbol reminding us that God hates sin, to boast about their sin.
      • We need to shift how we handle this culturally and start hitting our knees for these people. A Jonah mentality toward sinners, “They don’t deserve your mercy, they deserve your wrath, I’m not going over there,” is not a worthy Christian walk. Rebellious people don’t need our campaigns of shame; they need our campaign of the gospel. Go tell them; as Paul will show, that is your job too, not just Josh’s.

    5. 21:32 Forbearance

      • The fourth attitude of those educated in grace is forbearance. In premarital counseling, with a couple who can’t keep their hands off each other, Josh has to tell them, “I know you’re infatuated right now, but if you want this to last, you’re going to have to put in the work.” Paul is talking about a consistent, enduring love, not a hot love: being willing to deal with each other’s junk.
      • When you notice things you disagree with in another, Satan will immediately point them out and try to convince you to use them to break unity. Paul says believers should notice Satan’s play and choose to love them in light of what they don’t like. When you are harsh or impatient with others, you show how out of touch you are with how patient and gentle God has been with you.

    6. 22:52 We keep unity; we don’t create it

      • We are tasked with keeping unity, not creating it. The Holy Spirit creates it. We can notice its beauty, preserve it, protect it, and fight for it, but we don’t create it. And this unity isn’t only in this church or denomination but among all Christians of different races, nationalities, languages, and economic classes.
      • Most of man’s systematic efforts to create unity have failed and only created more permanent separation. History also shows that one church doesn’t work, because large faith institutions grow corrupt over time. Whether many small institutions or one large one, we don’t create unity; our job is to notice, promote, and celebrate it, walking by the Spirit and bringing light to it.
      • Some today say the key to unity is getting rid of certain people: “If we just get rid of the fundamentalists, we don’t need doctrine, we need love,” those Christians who take the word as literal and infallible. Notice the hypocrisy: they are name-calling in order to create unity.
      • Paul didn’t bring up spiritual unity until he spent three chapters unpacking the foundation of our new life and riches in Christ. Unity not built on truth will crumble. Bash us for loving the word of God, but it is the word that reminds us daily not to draw lines between brothers and sisters, call them names, or poke fun at their traditions or lack of tradition. Unity built on tolerance crumbles; unity built on truth lasts.

  7. 25:18 Verses 4–6: truths that lead to oneness

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:4–6

    • Paul lays out the truths that lead to oneness: “There is one body and one Spirit, just as you were called to one hope when you were called; one Lord, one faith, one baptism; one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in all.” Josh goes through them one by one.

    1. 25:41 One body

      Scripture: Galatians 3:28

      • There is only one body of believers, the church, including every believer who has trusted or will one day trust in Christ as Savior and Lord. There is no denominational, geographical, ethnic, or racial body; no Gentile, Jew, male, female, or slave; just Christ’s body, and Paul tasks us with maintaining its unity.
      1. 26:08 No excuse to skip a local church

        Scripture: Galatians 2:20

        • Being one body doesn’t excuse a believer from having a local church home. “I don’t need to be a member of a church; we’re all the church,” says the one who doesn’t give and comes seven times a year so he can stay home and watch football.
        • You can have unity with believers in Asia, and Josh has unity with brothers and sisters in China, but you can’t have community with them. You need a place to worship, serve, and grow. You are born with natural talents and born again with spiritual gifts, and every Christian needs a place to use their gifts to bless God and others.
        • “Josh, you don’t get it, I’m a super introvert.” So is he; people drain him. But it has taken a long time, and he is still a work in progress, and Jesus is teaching him: let them drain you, because Jesus will fill him back up when he rests in him at night and wakes hearing his word. Josh used to make the same excuse, until he realized the reason we like our personal time is that we are the only person we are actually skilled at loving.
        • Jesus commands us to love others, and failing to do so proves we have never experienced God’s love ourselves. Jesus didn’t need anything we had, yet he stepped down and got involved anyway; we drained him of life and he let it happen. That is how Christians live and love: “I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live.” Have at me.

    2. 28:48 One Spirit

      • The Holy Spirit is given to every believer. Believers are called temples of the Holy Spirit, but not as single temples standing apart; more like Lego temples connected together and growing into the holy temple of the Lord. God dwells in us collectively.
      • Just as you gave your wife an engagement ring when you pledged to marry her, cheap or not, the Holy Spirit is given as a pledge guaranteeing that every believer will be at the marriage supper of the Lamb.
      1. 29:34 Longing to go home
        • Are you ready for the day Christ’s bride is presented? Josh longs to go home. Non-believers may think only miserable Christians long for home, but he has an amazing wife, two wonderful kids, a beautiful home, and a ministry the church makes an incredible joy, and he still wants to go home.
        • He is tired of seeing people lose their way, of streets paved in gay pride when he knows streets will be made of gold, of people following every social media influencer with a lot of likes when there is only one worth following. He wants to feast with his Savior and Lord. He wants his kids to see and know the heavenly Father he keeps failing to mimic, his wife to be loved as she deserves instead of being stuck with an idiot like him, and his brothers and sisters to sing together and worship in spirit and truth. He is tired of war, death, disease, fighting, and division, and without the Lord he would be a mess.
        • This morning Josh was blessed by a hymn from the mid-1800s: “Stand up, stand up for Jesus, the strife will not be long; this day the noise of battle, the next the victor’s song.” He wants the church to know and depend on Jesus like that. He has nothing else to offer but a way to Jesus: call on his name today and let his Spirit fill you. There is nothing better than walking with Jesus.

    3. 32:40 One hope

      Scripture: Matthew 5:9

      • What keeps Josh going is one hope: the return of the Lord to take his church home. The Holy Spirit within us is the assurance of this promise. Blessed are the peacemakers, blessed are those who walk by the Spirit, blessed are those who long for the Lord’s return. Come, Lord Jesus.

    4. 33:00 One Lord

      Scripture: Acts 4:12 Galatians 1:9

      • There is but one Lord Jesus Christ, our wonderful Savior. Salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven by which we must be saved. Paul tells the Galatians that if anyone preaches a gospel contrary to the gospel of Christ, let them be accursed.

    5. 33:17 One faith

      • That is why Paul follows with one faith. He isn’t talking about your personal faith followed by repentance but the doctrines that shape our beliefs. Christians can differ in some matters of interpretation and church practice, but all true Christians agree on the faith.

    6. 33:40 One baptism

      Scripture: Ephesians 4:5

      • In verse 5 Paul refers to water baptism. The order of his writing suggests spiritual baptism has already happened, and water baptism is a believer’s response to it: something has happened inside, and the believer has a beautiful one-time opportunity to share it with the church.
      • Water baptism was a huge deal in the early church and still is. But baptism doesn’t save and doesn’t come with a special blessing; it is a believer’s testimony as they identify with Christ and join in unity with his body. One baptism, in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

    7. 34:30 One God and Father

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 6:4 Matthew 28:19

      • Finally, one God and Father. Judaism has always claimed, “The Lord is our God, the Lord is one,” and Christianity is no different; God’s word reveals that his oneness is best seen in three persons. Jesus made this clear when he commissioned the apostles: “Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.”
      • We are children in the same family, set out to love and serve the same Father, and we should be able to walk in unity. Earthly families make compromises to walk in unity at home; God’s heavenly family must make allowances for one another too. Jesus taught us to pray “our Father,” not “my Father,” so you are one of many children. Stop acting like an only child. We must resist living for ourselves and breaking unity, and we must not wander off into false doctrine just to improve the church’s optics.

  8. 35:52 Verses 7–10: unity with variety

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:7–10

    • Paul moves from what we have in common to how Christians look different: unity in the Spirit, but also variety. “But to each one of us grace has been given as Christ apportioned it.” When he ascended on high he took many captives and gave gifts to his people; he who descended is the very one who ascended higher than all the heavens, in order to fill the whole universe.
    • God’s purpose in salvation wasn’t only to save you and give you eternal life but to fill you with himself. The whole point of Christ’s work was that he might fill the whole universe. He wants you to experience him walking with you through life and to be empowered and equipped to love the world as he does. Paul has been praying for us to know the power at work in us, and spiritual gifts are how we grab that power and do good works that glorify God.

    1. 37:05 Spiritual gifts and natural talents

      • There is a difference between spiritual gifts and natural talents. When you were born, God gave you abilities: a great mechanic, athlete, skilled surgeon, or gifted musician. Not everyone is created equal; some are taller, stronger, smarter, faster, or better. Every believer has natural talents, but when born again, they receive at least one spiritual gift.
      • A spiritual gift is a God-given ability to serve God and other Christians so that Christ is glorified and believers are edified, meaning built up and strengthened.

    2. 37:52 How do I know my gift?

      • Two ways, one more efficient than the other. There is a free spiritual gifts assessment on the church’s website; it is helpful but not the best way. The best way is to hang out with other believers on a regular basis with the intent to give instead of receive, and as you do, pray for the Lord to help you discover his gift in you so you can use it effectively for him.
      • There is a warning label: gifts that aren’t used to love and serve others will be used as weapons to destroy others. Christ offers this power through the Spirit to build up the church, not destroy it.

    3. 39:01 The conqueror shares the spoils

      Scripture: Psalm 68

      • Paul paints Jesus as a conqueror who ascended to heaven as champion in a victory parade, and he quotes a victory song David wrote, from Psalm 68. Jesus conquered and returned, sharing the spoils with his followers.

  9. 39:21 Verse 11: four gifts Christ gave

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:11

    • The New Testament has three lists of spiritual gifts, none identical, and there isn’t time to break them all down. Paul’s list in Ephesians names four, placed in the church for a purpose: Christ himself gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, and the pastors and teachers.

    1. 39:47 Apostles: an office and a gift

      • Josh explains the first two because they are the most controversial. There is a difference between an office and a ministry. Many have abused these titles, and many believers have followed false apostles and prophets who aren’t sent by God, make things up, and are prideful and selfish rather than humble and sacrificial. Josh understands the hesitation: people have come up to him in the lobby announcing, “I am a prophet sent by God to warn you.” Of what? That he must stop selling bagels. God’s people need to eat too.
      • Twelve first-century apostles were set apart to know Jesus and be eyewitnesses of his life and ministry, and none of the twelve are with us, so in a sense the office of apostle concluded in the first century. What continues is the gift: a missionary pioneering work or a church planter reaching a city without access to the gospel. The church’s founding pastor had the apostolic gift.
      • We all need to participate in that kind of ministry, but exercising a gift differs from assigning a title. Josh doesn’t have this gift. If someone here discovers it, the elders and Josh would help launch them into a first ministry where the gospel is desperately needed, though Anthem doesn’t need more churches.

    2. 42:03 Prophets

      • We tend to think of prophets as predicting the future, but their primary function is to proclaim the word of God. Early believers didn’t have access to God’s word as we do with digital and physical Bibles, so the gift of prophecy was crucial for laying the foundation we benefit from today.
      • Josh doesn’t believe prophets walk around today receiving ongoing downloads of new revelation from the cloud or the Spirit. But God still offers this gift in the form of faithful, committed men who preach God’s word so that people hear it, respond, repent, and turn back to God.

    3. 42:59 Evangelists

      • Evangelists’ beautiful feet bring the good news, traveling place to place preaching the gospel to win souls. The apostles and prophets laid the foundation; the evangelists built on it.
      • Everybody has a YouTube theology and YouTube discipleship these days, but discipleship happens here in the church, not just on your phone. It isn’t Ray Comfort’s job to evangelize everyone; he has a gift, but not having it doesn’t excuse you from being a witness. Who is sitting here today because you invited them? What about the empty seat next to you: who didn’t get an invite, and what opportunity did you pass on?

    4. 44:06 Pastors and teachers

      • Josh believes this is one gift played out in two ministries. You don’t have to be a pastor to be a teacher, but all pastors or elders must be able to teach. They are responsible to feed and lead the flock by rightly dividing the word of God, which nourishes his sheep and is our protection and provision. We are not in the entertainment business; we are in the shepherding business.

  10. 44:37 Verses 12–16: the body grows up

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:12–16

    • When all these gifts function together in unity, we see why Jesus gave them: to equip his people for works of service, so that the body of Christ may be built up until we all reach unity in the faith and in the knowledge of the Son of God and become mature. Then we will no longer be infants tossed back and forth by every wind of teaching and the cunning of deceitful people; instead, speaking the truth in love, we will grow up into him who is the head, Christ, from whom the whole body, joined and held together by every supporting ligament, grows and builds itself up in love as each part does its work.
    • Doesn’t anyone else want this for this place, for our children, marriages, and homes? It is the closest thing we get down here to being in his presence. Josh is willing to commit to working at it; are you? If we don’t cooperate together, we will never be joined and held together like that.
    • We belong to each other, affect each other, and need each other. However useless you feel, you have a ministry to believers in this place and to unbelievers outside these walls. Who does the real work of ministry here, you or Josh? We do.

    1. 46:54 Equipped to go

      Scripture: Romans 12:1

      • As a leader, Josh’s job is to equip the church to serve, to spur them on to offer their lives as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God, and it is an honor. “Josh, there’s this need over there; you should go chase that down.” Should he, or should you? “But I’ve never done that before.” That is probably exactly why you should. He will be praying for you.

  11. 47:32 Time to grow up

    • Too many of us have sat on the sidelines too long. Josh was guilty of that until the Lord called him to this ministry, which he didn’t want, but the Lord has been with him the whole time. It is time to get in the game; you never know how God can use you until you step in. Climb out of the high chair; it is tall and scary and a long way down, but you have to climb out.
    • Stop complaining and crying when things don’t go your way. Things don’t always go Josh’s way either; the elders don’t always say, “Great idea, Josh.” “But I’m not being fed.” Then feed yourself. Grow up. He isn’t doing airplane with you.

    1. 48:38 Not my church: ours

      • “Josh, I really like your church, but I’m offended by that statement.” Josh is offended by the “your.” This isn’t his church; it is ours, and we serve him. He is our head, our CEO, our leader, and we must submit to him together in unity. Only then will we have joy, purpose, and real effectiveness, and effective doesn’t always mean numbers. Are you growing, being challenged, and doing it for others?
      • The power of this church will be seen in us locking arms, not in Josh. He loves the church, but last year wasn’t good enough; this place is capable of greater impact. Get outside these walls, pray for strangers until they look at us weird, invite them in, and love them like they’ve never been loved. This institution is like nothing else in the world, so let’s start walking like it. Christians don’t thrive alone; by ourselves we tear each other apart. We need people to pour into and people to empty us. Stop wasting his gifts.

  12. 50:27 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays as the church heads into a time to contemplate how they have been challenged. It can be overwhelming to hear the standards set for a believer with riches in Christ, because the bar is high and we feel we don’t measure up. He prays they would put down their work gloves, stop trying to climb the ladder themselves, and simply stop, pray, plead, and invite the Lord in.
    • He asks that they would see things differently: the Christian at the supermarket not walking by the Spirit checks out quickly, but the one led by the Spirit slows down and asks that mom if he can pray for her. He asks the Lord to do whatever he must in their hearts, minds, homes, and lives to make them the caring, loving church he designed.

    1. 51:52 One challenge

      • Over the next minutes there is freedom: tables throughout, prayer walls in the back, benches up front for anyone who wants to fall to their knees, and anyone alone is invited to pray with Josh’s family or join any family circle. His one challenge: ask someone in your family or circle, “How can I pray for you?” and then pray for them, because if we can’t do that here, we won’t start doing it in Safeway, Fry’s, and Costco. He closes in prayer, worshiping, trusting, and thanking God for Jesus.
