  1. 00:36 Fulfilled history after four hundred years of silence

    Scripture: John 14:2–3 Luke 1:1

    • Josh reconnects to last week. Luke investigated the reports, interviewed the eyewitnesses, organized his findings, and gave Theophilus an orderly account so readers could have certainty. Luke’s contribution is historical credibility: Jesus entered real history, lived under real rulers, was crucified on a real cross, left a real tomb, and ascended.
    • Last week’s verse splits into two blocks. Week one established the historical credibility of “things that have been fulfilled among us.” Week two takes up the prophetic significance: these are not just things that happened, but things fulfilled. Things happened just as God had promised.

    1. 02:01 The wrong king, the pagan power, and four hundred silent years

      Scripture: Luke 1:5

      • Luke begins at the back end of one of Israel’s darkest periods. Herod is on the throne and Rome is in control. Herod is not David’s son reigning under God; he is a puppet king placed there by Caesar.
      • Herod was a brilliant builder who kept working on the temple until it was magnificent, but the men beautifying God’s house did not possess a heart surrendered to God. The people were crushed under taxes and bloodshed. Paranoid about losing power, he executed members of his own family, including his wife and three of his sons. Josh’s comparison: Herod makes Pharaoh look like a Sunday school teacher. Impressive from the outside, chaos on the inside, built on Rome’s success and on fear.
      • So God’s people look toward Jerusalem and see the wrong king on the throne, toward Rome and see a pagan power controlling the nation, and toward heaven and see four hundred years of silence. Malachi was the last prophetic voice Israel heard.
      • Josh makes the wait felt. People get uncomfortable when someone leaves them on read for an hour, and some treat four hours as a welfare check. Four hundred years is roughly fifteen generations. Travel back four hundred years from 2026 and the United States does not exist. No electricity, no automobiles, no airplanes, no internet, no phone reporting every tragic event everywhere all at once.

    2. 04:44 The promises stood. The king did not appear.

      Scripture: Genesis 3:15 Genesis 12:3 2 Samuel 7:12–16

      • The promises had not moved. God had promised a savior and a king. The offspring of the woman would crush the serpent. Abraham’s family would bring blessing to the nations. David would have a son whose kingdom would never end.
      • So where was he? Where was the king? Where was this eternal kingdom? Had God forgotten his people? Worse, had God changed his mind? Had God abandoned his promises?

  2. 05:13 Zechariah: a heard prayer, an impossible promise, and a silenced priest

    Scripture: Luke 1:6–7

    • Luke introduces a priest named Zechariah, and the name means the Lord remembers. Josh wonders, speculating, whether a boy named “the Lord remembers” took grief in Hebrew school after four hundred years of silence. A man carrying that name lives while God’s people look at the silence and wonder whether the Lord has forgotten.
    • Luke says both Zechariah and Elizabeth were righteous in the sight of God, observing all the Lord’s commands and decrees blamelessly, but childless because Elizabeth could not conceive, and both were very old. They had tried, wrestled with watching others have families, prayed, and by human standards likely accepted that they would never have a child of their own.

    1. 06:20 The Super Bowl of priesthood

      Scripture: Luke 1:8–10

      • Then one day Zechariah was serving as a priest and his name was selected by lot to enter the holy place and burn incense before God. Not another day in the office. A career opportunity of a lifetime.
      • Josh puts numbers on it. Probably around 18,000 priests in Israel, 24 priestly divisions, so Zechariah’s division of Abijah may have held around 750. The Super Bowl of priesthood. Most priests would serve a lifetime and never be selected to play in the big game. Zechariah won the priestly Powerball.
      • He would enter the holy place alone. Outside, the people would pray. Inside, he would place incense on the altar and the smoke would rise, a visible representation of the prayers of God’s people ascending before him.
      • The tension he carried in: new heights in the priesthood, unanswered prayers in his personal life.

    2. 07:47 Gabriel breaks four hundred years of silence

      Scripture: Luke 1:13–17

      • Gabriel appears and says, do not be afraid, Zechariah. Your prayer has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you are to call him John. He will be a joy and a delight, and many will rejoice because of his birth. He will be great in the sight of the Lord, never take wine or other fermented drink, and be filled with the Holy Spirit even before he is born. He will bring back many of the people of Israel to the Lord their God, and go before the Lord in the spirit and power of Elijah, to turn the hearts of parents to their children and the disobedient to the wisdom of the righteous, to make ready a people prepared for the Lord.
      • What a day in the office. In one announcement, God responded to a family’s pain and broke four hundred years of silence.

    3. 08:47 A sign he did not want

      Scripture: Luke 1:18 Luke 1:20

      • Zechariah struggled to believe. How can I be sure of this? I am an old man and my wife is well along in years. Josh translates: a nice way of saying she is an old lady. Tread lightly, men.
      • Gabriel spends his time in the presence of God and has seen the throne room. He answers: now you will be silent and not able to speak until the day this happens, because you did not believe my words. Zechariah asked for a sign and God gave him one. For the next nine months, every time he tries to speak, he is reminded that God’s saying is his doing.

    4. 09:46 The priest who could not announce the announcement

      Scripture: Luke 1:21–22 Malachi 3:1

      • The irony: Zechariah entered the temple representing the people before God, received the greatest announcement Israel had possibly ever heard, four hundred years of silence broken, the forerunner Malachi talked about coming, the Lord drawing near, and then walked outside unable to say anything.
      • Josh sets the rest aside for another sermon, but says this much. Be careful demanding a sign when God has already spoken. Jesus talks about this, and Josh is not getting into it now. Unbelief in what God said he would do silenced the priest for nine months.

  3. 10:42 Unbelief can silence a witness, but grace still gives the final word

    Scripture: Matthew 16:18

    • A church’s view of God’s word matters. A church that does not believe the word of God, that does not take him at his word, will eventually have nothing of value to offer the world. It might have great music, the best facilities, strong political opinions, and endless complaints against the culture. But if it no longer believes that Jesus saves sinners, that the gospel actually changes lives, that the Spirit works through his word to edify and grow his church, and that Christ is building his church through the Spirit and the word, it will lose its voice.
    • Josh has watched Christians become experts at pointing out the darkness while failing to point anyone to the light. Satan can convince anybody to drift, and the spiritual forces of evil can drag a person off. Believer, do not get comfortable. Christianity is not about your comfort. Christian veterans must keep fighting.

    1. 11:53 Proximity to holy things is not faith

      Scripture: Luke 1:6

      • Zechariah was a priest. Look at the résumé. He played in the big game. He knew the scriptures. He stood in the holy place. He got so close the proximity is hard to imagine. He saw Gabriel with his own eyes, and Gabriel has seen the throne. Yet he still struggled to trust what God said. You can get close. Proximity to holy things does not necessarily mean genuine faith.

    2. 12:30 Disappointment makes hope feel risky

      • Josh refuses to give Zechariah a bad name. Yes, Zechariah struggled with unbelief, and that is not good. But who hasn’t? He was a faithful priest. Luke calls him righteous and blameless, and he is also an old man who has carried disappointment for a lot of years.
      • When you have held disappointment that long, hope starts to feel risky. Opening up to the possibility of something better also opens you up to being hurt. So in that fear, people stop asking, stop believing, and stop hoping, because it is safer not to. Nothing happened before, so why would it happen now? That is where Zechariah is.
      • Josh’s reading: Zechariah believes God will do what he said he would. What Zechariah does not believe is that God will do it through his wife Elizabeth. That is where the issue is.

    3. 14:02 Even if your faith falters, God remains faithful

      Scripture: Luke 1:67–79

      • Even if your faith falters, God remains faithful. Stop fixating on the struggle and start writing some lyrics. There is a song to sing after this.
      • Notice God’s mercy shining through. God disciplined Zechariah. He did not toss him out and find another. Zechariah’s unbelief could place serious limitations on him, but his unbelief could not place limitations on God.
      • Josh tells the church to read this on their own. He does not have time to show it. When God eventually opened Zechariah’s mouth, he immediately used it to praise the Lord. The song is in the last section of chapter 1, starting at verse 67.

    4. 14:59 Two songs, two paths

      Scripture: Luke 1:45

      • Surveying Luke 1, there are two songs, Mary’s and Zechariah’s, and two very different paths to them. There is the path that sings now, and there is the unbelief God must discipline until it sings. Which path are you on?
      • Grace gets the final word in both paths, but blessed is the one who believes and sings.
      • That is the journey of the day. Zechariah received the promise and lost his voice. Mary received the promise, believed the Lord, and found a song. Unbelief left a priest speechless. Faith taught the young woman to sing.

    5. 15:52 The silence of God is not the absence of God

      • The silence of God is not the absence of God. While Israel heard nothing, God was arranging absolutely everything. At just the right time, not early and not too late, God’s promises are about to be born.

  4. 16:14 God remembers what he promised

    Scripture: Luke 1:54–55

    • Josh’s first point: God remembers what he promised. He reads the last two lines of Mary’s song. He has helped his servant Israel, remembering to be merciful to Abraham and his descendants forever, just as he promised our ancestors. God remembered.

    1. 16:38 Remembered does not mean forgotten

      Scripture: Genesis 8:1 Genesis 19:29 Genesis 30:22 Exodus 2:24

      • Anytime scripture says God remembered something, it does not mean he forgot. God does not walk into a room and suddenly wonder why he is there. He does not misplace his phone and find it in his pocket. He does not hop on a flight and realize midway he forgot something and scream “Kevin.” Josh apologizes for the early Christmas-movie reference and notes that Luke 1 has him thinking they could decorate early this year.
      • Omniscience means he knows everything, past, present, and future, perfectly and immediately. Read scripture as if God were actually remembering something he forgot and God has serious memory impairment. Probably amnesia.
      • In Genesis God remembered Noah and the floodwaters began to recede. God remembered Abraham and rescued Lot. God remembered Rachel and opened her womb. God heard Israel’s cries in Egypt, remembered his covenant, and came down to deliver them.
      • So divine remembrance is not recovering lost information. It is God acting in faithfulness on his desired timetable.

    2. 18:15 Mary knew the promise was older than she was

      Scripture: Genesis 3 Romans 5:12 Genesis 3:15 Genesis 12:3

      • Mary understood this. She is young, but she got it. What is happening in her body is the decisive, pre-planned fulfillment of a promise-keeping God, and the promise existed long before she did.
      • When Adam and Eve rebelled, sin entered the world and fractured everything. Their relationship with God was broken and their relationship with one another was damaged. Because Adam represents humanity, through Adam death came to all. Death entered the world because of sin. The serpent appeared to have won.
      • But before Adam and Eve were removed from the garden, God offered hope by making a promise. One day, no dates given, the offspring of the woman would crush the head of the serpent. Josh calls it the protoevangelium, the first gospel, and concedes he probably butchered the Latin.
      • The purpose of Genesis 1 through 3 is to establish two absolute truths about God and his most precious creation. Humanity rebelled, and God promised redemption.

    3. 19:29 The promise gathers detail

      Scripture: Genesis 12:3 2 Samuel 7:12–16 Isaiah 53 Isaiah 7:14 Micah 5:2 Isaiah 61:1 Malachi 4:5–6 Luke 1:13

      • Later God called a man named Abraham and promised that all the peoples on earth would be blessed through him. Abraham would have a family, through that family a nation, and through that nation God would bring blessing to all nations.
      • Generations later, God promised David that one of his sons would sit on an eternal throne. His kingdom would have no end.
      • Then the prophets keep adding detail, including the brutal suffering of God’s Messiah, the rejection he would experience, right down to the location of his birth. The savior would be born of a virgin. He would come from Bethlehem. He would bring good news to the poor. He would bear the sins of his people. And before the king appeared, a messenger would come in the spirit and power of Elijah to prepare the way of the Lord.
      • Then the Old Testament ends. Silence. One year passes, ten years, a hundred, four hundred. God had not forgotten.

    4. 20:36 Fulfillment can be accomplished without words

      Scripture: Luke 1:13 Luke 1:26–27 Luke 1:30–33 Genesis 3:15

      • Fulfillment can be accomplished without words. The whole time, God preserved Abraham’s family and David’s line, governed nations, raised kings up and brought kings down, and directed history toward one precise moment.
      • That moment: Gabriel enters the temple and tells Zechariah that his wife Elizabeth will bear him a son, and he is to call him John.
      • Then Gabriel takes some FaceTime with a young virgin from Nazareth, in the sixth month of Elizabeth’s pregnancy, so she is far along. God sent the angel Gabriel to Nazareth, a town in Galilee, to a virgin pledged to be married to a man named Joseph, a descendant of David.
      • Similar circumstances, and Mary has questions, but she does not ask for a sign. Gabriel says, do not be afraid, Mary. You have found favor with God. You will conceive and give birth to a son, and you are to call him Jesus. He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give him the throne of his father David, and he will reign over Jacob’s descendants forever. His kingdom will never end.

    5. 21:53 Every promise converges on two impossible pregnancies

      Scripture: Genesis 3:15 Genesis 12:3 2 Samuel 7:12–16 Galatians 3:16 Ephesians 2:8 Revelation 5:9 Luke 1:34–37

      • So the offspring of the woman, the blessing of Abraham, the son of David, the king of Israel, the savior of the world, it is all there. Every promise God has made converges on two pregnant women who by human standards have no possible way of conceiving a child.
      • Luke is showing one ancient redemptive promise reaching its fulfillment in the wombs of an old woman and a virgin.
      • Paul would later explain to the Galatians that God’s promise to Abraham ultimately pointed to one particular offspring, Christ. The promise to Abraham was pointing to Jesus all along. One promise, one savior, one people redeemed by grace through faith in Jesus Christ. One king whose kingdom will gather men and women from every tribe, language, people, and nation.
      • Jesus is Israel’s long-awaited Messiah. But he is not Israel’s possession. He is king for all.

    6. 23:07 No word from God will ever fail

      Scripture: Luke 1:34–37 Luke 1:38 2 Corinthians 1:20

      • Look at verse 34. How will this be, Mary asked the angel, since I am a virgin? The angel answered, the Holy Spirit will come on you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. So the holy one to be born will be called the Son of God. Even Elizabeth, your relative, is going to have a child in her old age, and she who was said to be unable to conceive is in her sixth month. For no word from God will ever fail.
      • Mary says, I am the Lord’s servant. May your word to me be fulfilled. Then the angel left her.

  5. 24:00 Faith trusts what God has actually promised

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1:20 Romans 10:13

    • Between Mary’s questions and Mary’s song, Mary just wanted to serve the Lord. The woman who is not a priest says, so be it, God. Whatever you say.
    • If the sermon is going to talk about how God makes amazing promises and answers them, yes, in Jesus, then wisdom says to slow down and discuss what God has not promised.

    1. 24:37 Christians suffer to glory

      • Josh’s eschatology, his view of Christian prosperity, his view of the church and church ministry, and basically everything, centers on what he sees in scripture. Whether you agree with it or not.
      • Christians are not removed from suffering and ushered into glory. Christians suffer to glory. There is a difference. Satan’s plan promises glory but brings suffering, and most people have experienced that, choosing the easy thing or the compelling thing. God’s plan promises suffering that leads to glory.
      • Josh does not build his theology on man-made structures. He does not memorize a systematic theology and claim it. He builds his theology on the unfailing word of God. Not all Christians form their theology this way, and he is not here to pick a fight. He is too tired for that.

    2. 25:31 Preferences are not prophecy

      • Some take their desires, attempt to convert them into divine promises, and then get angry with God when he fails to do the thing he never promised he would do.
      • God did not promise every Christian a spouse. Josh gets the frustration and hopes people meet that person, because marriage can be an amazing blessing. But that is not God’s path for everyone. Singleness has value: a single person can reach people with the gospel that a married person never could, at least not until the kids are raised. Ministering in some remote place would mean taking a family into harm’s way or leaving them.
      • He did not promise that every married couple would have a child. He did not promise that every sick person would receive immediate healing. He did not promise that every dream would come true if we just believe hard enough. He did not promise comfort, uninterrupted success, or complete freedom from suffering.
      • When you attempt to make your preferences into prophecy, you are sitting on a throne that only God sits on. Faith is not confidence that God will eventually do what you want him to do. Faith is confidence that God will accomplish everything he has actually promised in Christ.

    3. 27:19 What he has actually promised

      Scripture: Romans 10:13 John 6:37 Romans 8:1 Romans 8:38–39 2 Corinthians 12:9 Philippians 1:6 1 Thessalonians 4:16 1 Corinthians 15:26 Revelation 21:4

      • So what has he promised? Everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved. No one who comes to Jesus will be sent away. There is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. Nothing can separate us from his love. His grace is sufficient in our weakness. He will complete the work he began in us. The dead in Christ will rise. Sin and death will not have the final word. Jesus will return, every enemy will be defeated, and the last enemy is death, put down for good. Every tear will be wiped away, not dabbed so you cry again, but terminated. There will be no more tears. His people will dwell with him forever.

    4. 28:16 Your plans might fail. His never will.

      Scripture: Luke 1:37 2 Corinthians 1:20

      • Your plans might fail, but his plans never will. Your prophetic timeline is probably wrong. If it is not mine, it is probably wrong. That was a joke, and it did not land. His timeline is always right.
      • Your pain might not leave you, but neither will he. Your disease might get your body, but it cannot touch your soul. Your tears might be flowing right now, but his grace is overflowing.
      • Not one word God has spoken will fail. God remembers every promise, and in Christ each finds its answer. Yes.

  6. 29:02 God confirms what he promised

    Scripture: Luke 1:39 Luke 1:36

    • Josh’s second point: God confirms what he promised. Look at verse 39. At that time Mary got ready and hurried to a town in the hill country of Judea. Gabriel has told Mary that Elizabeth was six months pregnant, so Mary went for a visit. The hurry matters.

    1. 29:25 The list of what Mary does not know

      Scripture: Luke 1:38 John 4:6–7

      • Those with some life experience should notice the list. What Mary does not know is far longer than what she does know. Some are already doing the math. Yet she says, I am your servant. Use me. Josh wants to witness revival like that in today’s church.
      • She understands she is going to be pregnant, but she does not understand what this pregnancy is going to bring. She does not know how Joseph will respond. Josh laughs at a line from Four Christmases, where a man about to go on in a play asks whether Joseph was okay with God getting her pregnant. A terrible line, but Josh gets it. She does not know what everyone in Nazareth will say, think, or do.
      • If the Samaritan woman is sent out to get water at noon, think how the Jewish people will treat a woman pregnant outside of marriage. This is not good. She does not even know what raising the Son of God will require of her. Josh has questions of his own, including whether she can get in trouble for talking back to Jesus, and how that parent-child relationship is going to work. He leaves those over to the side.

    2. 31:02 Faith takes the next step

      Scripture: Luke 9:23

      • That is what faith is. We do not get a detailed layout of what will happen, step by step, when we deny ourselves, take up our cross, and follow Jesus. We do not know what it is going to look like. Faith is taking the next step. Mary’s next step is into Zechariah and Elizabeth’s home.

    3. 31:24 The strangest living room in Judea

      • Walk into that living room. A lot of pregnancy hormones in that space, and a man watching football who, oddly, is not screaming at the TV. An old woman about to deliver, a young woman who has never been with a man, and a mute priest who knows why it is all happening but cannot tell you. You thought your family dinners were weird.

    4. 32:01 The baby leaped

      Scripture: Luke 1:41–45

      • When Elizabeth heard Mary’s greeting, the baby leaped in her womb, and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit. In a loud voice she exclaimed, blessed are you among women, and blessed is the child you will bear. But why am I so favored that the mother of my Lord should come to me? As soon as the sound of your greeting reached my ears, the baby in my womb leaped for joy. Blessed is she who has believed that the Lord would fulfill his promises to her.

    5. 32:39 A holy baby shower

      • Do not skim it. Have you ever seen a really pregnant woman meet another really pregnant woman and become fast friends immediately, going for a side hug because a real hug has obvious issues? From what Josh has been told, and he did not research this, pregnant women comfort one another by presence, because they know they are not alone in their suffering. You connect without saying a word.
      • This is no ordinary baby shower. This is a holy baby shower. The women are connected by blood, but the two babies are connected by the Holy Spirit.
      • Jesus has not been born. He has not preached a sermon, performed a miracle, called a disciple, calmed a storm, walked on water, fed thousands, or raised the dead. No one in that room can even see him. Mary is not even showing yet, and Elizabeth starts worshiping. John cannot preach yet, because he is still on the umbilical cord, so he just starts flipping.
      • Elizabeth calls Jesus Lord before she even knows his name. No one can see him, but everyone is responding to his presence.

  7. 34:16 Open his eyes, Lord

    Scripture: 2 Kings 6:15–17

    • It reminds Josh of one of his favorite moments in the Old Testament. The prophet Elisha was in the city of Dothan when the king of Aram sent an army to capture him. Early one morning Elisha’s servant got up, walked outside, and saw the city surrounded. Horses, chariots, and soldiers everywhere. An army had closed in during the night.
    • The servant ran back and asked what Elisha wanted them to do. Elisha walked outside, saw the same horses, chariots, and soldiers, and was not afraid. Do not be afraid, the prophet answered. Those who are with us are more than those who are with them.
    • Josh imagines the servant starting to count heads so he can prove Elisha wrong. Then Elisha prayed, open his eyes, Lord, so that he may see. The Lord opened the servant’s eyes, and he looked and saw the hills full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha.

    1. 35:19 The army was there the entire time

      Scripture: Matthew 14:30

      • The heavenly army did not arrive when Elisha prayed. They were there the entire time. The servant was afraid because he only saw one part of the reality. He saw the enemy but could not see his allies. He saw what was against him but could not see who was for him. It is like Peter. The waves get too big and you just cannot see anything else.

    2. 35:44 You walked in seeing only the army

      Scripture: Romans 8:38–39

      • In Luke 1 there is no throne, no crown, no army. Mary is not even showing. But John leaps, Elizabeth worships, Mary believes, and Zechariah wants to sing real bad.
      • Some walked in and all they can see is the army surrounding them. The medical diagnosis. The financial pressure. The conflict in the home. The child who is drifting. The marriage that feels cold. The grief that will not leave. The temptation that keeps lingering. The criticism. Everything that is wrong.
      • We are experts at seeing everything that is wrong, everything that hurts us, everything that stands against us, and everything that seems impossible. Those things are real. Christians do not need to pretend the forces against us are not absolutely overwhelming at times. They can be. But it is only half the picture.
      • Josh’s prayer: that God would open their eyes to the greater reality. Your king is reigning. Heaven is not panicking. Nothing that stands against you can separate you from God’s love.

    3. 36:53 King of kings and Lord of lords

      Scripture: Revelation 19:11–16

      • Leading that army is King Jesus, and all of heaven marches behind their warrior king. His name is called Faithful and True. With justice he judges and wages war. His eyes are like blazing fire. The armies of heaven follow him, riding on white horses. He will rule the nations with an iron scepter. He treads the winepress of the fury of the wrath of God Almighty. On his thigh he has this name written: King of kings and Lord of lords.
      • We cannot see his throne, but we know it is there.

    4. 37:31 Why Mary is blessed

      Scripture: Luke 1:42 John 19:25 Matthew 1:23

      • Josh loves what Elizabeth says to Mary. Blessed are you among women. Why, really, is Mary blessed? The word blessed is overused and wrongly used by Christians. We say we are blessed when the biopsy comes back negative, but we were blessed when we went in for it.
      • Mary is not blessed because of her circumstances. She is young. She is pregnant before becoming Joseph’s wife. She has no way of rationally explaining her pregnancy. Her only explanation is that she is carrying the Messiah, which has its own social and cultural implications. She faces public shame and the possibility of losing Joseph. This will not be a run-of-the-mill pregnancy. One day, as a mother, she will kneel at the foot of the cross as her son pours out his blood to save sinners, in his early thirties.
      • Yet Elizabeth calls her blessed. Why? Because despite everything coming her way, what she knows and what she does not know, what once could only be witnessed in the holy place is now growing inside her. He is Immanuel. God with us.

  8. 39:02 God overturns the world through his promised king

    Scripture: Luke 1:48–49 Luke 1:46–55

    • Josh’s third point, and the last thing he wants them to see: God overturns the world through his promised king. His favorite line from Mary’s song. From now on all generations will call me blessed, for the mighty one has done great things for me. Holy is his name. She knows the significance of what God has done.
    • Mary’s song is legendary, not because it is about Mary and not because she is a gifted vocalist, but because of how much Mary wants to magnify the glory of God. That is why her song is known as the Magnificat. Latin for magnifies.

    1. 39:37 Mighty, holy, and merciful

      Scripture: Romans 5:8

      • He is mighty, so there is nothing he cannot do. He is holy, so there is no darkness, no corruption, no injustice, no evil in him. He is merciful, so he looks upon helpless sinners with compassion. God demonstrates his compassion in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.
      • Because God is holy, our sin cannot be ignored. Because God is merciful, he does not leave sinners hopeless. Because God is mighty, he can accomplish for us what we could never accomplish on our own.
      • At the cross, God does not compromise his holiness in order to show mercy. Jesus bears the judgment that holiness requires so that sinners can receive mercy they never deserved.

    2. 40:26 No boast but Jesus

      Scripture: Galatians 6:14

      • That is why none of us can boast in anything but the name of Jesus Christ. No boast.

    3. 40:33 Mary is singing a declaration of war

      Scripture: Matthew 10:34 Luke 1:51–53 Luke 14:11

      • In a few moments Josh’s wife, and he thinks his whole family, will come up and lead a beautiful song. He has been looking forward to it all week. His wife is pretty small. Do not let her size fool you. She packs a punch. She is a beast, a description she probably does not like, but he thinks it is cool.
      • We tend to picture Mary as petite, cute, gentle, and kind. She is probably all those things, and that is fine. But she is singing about the Lord declaring war against human pride. She is singing about God lifting up the humble and humbling the exalted. Mary is singing about thrones coming down. Mom is singing one of her son’s go-to sermons before he preaches it.
      • Jesus said in Luke 14, for all those who exalt themselves will be humbled, and all those who humble themselves will be exalted. The king has come to put everything in its proper place. The proud scattered. He came with a sword. The mighty brought down. The humble lifted up. The meek. The rich going away sad.

    4. 42:10 Let him bring you down first

      Scripture: 2 Peter 3:9 Matthew 10:34

      • Herod may sit on a throne, but he is not king. Caesar may think he is a god, but he is not sovereign. Rome may be the big bully on the block, but Rome does not control history.
      • Before Christians start picturing politicians, celebrities, false teachers, and arrogant people they cannot wait to see God bring down, they should first make sure they have let him bring them down. If you have been humbled by God, you will not want anyone to perish, but all to come to faith. Stop hating your enemy and start praying for them.

    5. 42:45 Pride never thinks the sermon is for it

      • Pride is the most difficult sin to deal with. Everything is connected to it, and everything stems from it. What makes it so difficult, for a pastor trying to grow the church and edify the body, is that proud people never think the sermon is for them. Pastor, I really hope this person is listening. I do too. I am listening. Are you?

    6. 43:26 Control is fear wearing a crown

      • Pride is not always boasting. Sometimes pride is control. I must control my spouse. I must control my children. I think I know best, so I must control the church, the outcome, the narrative.
      • Control is fear wearing a crown. Herod all over again. We become afraid, climb on the throne, and start moving the chess pieces of life in order to protect our kingship.
      • Jesus did not come to watch as you command the game board of life. He came to remove you from the throne, because you never belonged there in the first place. He does not offer helpful suggestions, a ten-point plan on how to improve your kingdom or your kingship. He announces a kingdom that belongs to him. You are welcome in this kingdom as a humbled servant of God.

    7. 44:21 The private world where you can never be wrong

      • Mary sings about scattering the proud. If you think God is done scattering the proud, you are not paying attention. A prideful person creates a special little world where they can never be wrong. In that world their sin is not actually sin, because they can justify it as reasonable.
      • My anger is righteous anger, is it? My criticism is passion and discernment. My control is headship. My gossip is a prayer request. My unforgiveness is wisdom. My stubbornness is conviction.
      • Pride can only admit it is wrong after presenting a detailed explanation of who made that wrong necessary. I said that because you said this. I did that because you did this. I know I have to own some things, but not unless you own that. We construct a reality where we can do whatever we want because of what has been done to us. That is pride.

    8. 45:26 Creatures, not the creator

      • Jesus came to blow this reality up. We are creatures, not the creator. We are sinners, not the savior. We are servants, not the king.

  9. 45:36 What do you magnify?

    Scripture: Luke 1:46–47 2 Peter 3:9

    • So the only song we should be singing should sound a lot like Mary’s. My soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior.
    • As they close, Josh asks what they magnify. If you are not sure, here is how you can know. What do you rejoice in? Your joy will tell you what you magnify.

    1. 46:08 Follow your joy

      • What makes your heart come alive? It drives Josh crazy that some can hear the gospel and barely move, and cannot wait to jet out after service. Where are they going? To a game where they will erupt over a touchdown and lose their minds. Or on a Monday they will celebrate a promotion, a grandchild, or a package arriving. A package arriving gets more excitement out of us than the gospel does.
      • These are good gifts, and that is fine. Enjoy your life. Praise God. But does the gift awaken more joy than the giver? What do you magnify? Follow your joy. It will tell you.

    2. 47:02 Follow your wallet

      • Or, if you get really uncomfortable in here, follow your wallet. Both will lead you to the answer. You want to know what you magnify? Look at where the money is going.

  10. 47:21 Humble yourself and sing your own Magnificat

    Scripture: Luke 14:11 Luke 1:46–47

    • Josh needs to lead them for a second. Bow your head. Close your eyes. Find a quiet place in your heart. If we are going to deal with pride that says, look at this person, look at this person, then let everyone be bowed before the Lord as individuals, as servants of God, looking to identify the things in our own lives we need to deal with. Examine yourself. Specifically, examine where you have allowed unconfessed pride to remain and to fester.
    • This is not something you do every twenty years. We may need to do this several times this week alone. Get used to this position. It is a great position for the believer.
    • If we truly believe that God exalts the humble and humbles the self-exalted, then would we not want to humble ourselves right now? Are you walking humbly before God? As you are bowed before him, answer that.

    1. 48:28 Break the silence

      • Some have not spoken to God in a really long time, and maybe this is the first time in a really long time. He broke four hundred years of silence and came down to our level. Maybe it is time to break the silence now and run to him.

    2. 48:53 Shed it before you sing

      • Over the next few moments Josh lets the room set up. The band comes up. Fellowship with one another in prayer, bowed before the presence of the Lord. They are about to worship, about to come to the Lord’s table, where they will be reminded of the cost of salvation, and about to sing.
      • But if you do that with pride in your heart, it is not worth doing. We need to shed some things. Some have been holding on to bitterness, holding on to things for too long, and need to let it go. Ask the Lord to reveal those things and convict you of them. He does not offer the kind of cleansing where you cleanse, walk out, and need to cleanse again. Let it go. Lay it down.

    3. 49:32 Your Magnificat may look different

      • Some need to sing a song today, and that Magnificat could look very different for each one.
      • Some need to go before the Lord right now and ask him to forgive their pride. Some need to take their bitterness to the throne and say, Lord, would you take this from me? I cannot figure this part out. Can you heal this bitterness in me?
      • Some need to lift up a song, literally. When the music comes on, something about it, maybe the way it is done or the style and it was not like it used to be, some need to praise like they were fourteen again and heard the gospel for the first time. Some forgot how to lift a hand to the throne. Maybe that is their Magnificat today. Maybe it is, I am not ashamed, and I just want to reach for you because I want to know you, walk with you, and need you to cleanse me and renew this in me right now.
      • Some have never put a financial investment into the kingdom of God, and maybe that is their Magnificat today. The song they need to sing is being faithful with what God has blessed them with. Instead of wasting it, invest it in the kingdom. Offer him a gift today.

    4. 51:08 We are your servants

      • Some need to humble themselves today. We all do. So go before the throne in worship, praise, and fellowship. As they hold those items, declare one thing true in their hearts. Lord, whatever happens, we are your servants. In your name we pray. Amen.
