Detailed chronological outline

The sermon begins at 00:41 and continues through the closing prayer at 45:15. Elijah Stanley presents Hebrews 11:1–7 as hope for ordinary believers living between promise and fulfillment. Faith receives the future Christ purchased, trusts unseen realities, rejects prosperity teaching’s attempt to control outcomes, and rests in Jesus when suffering remains. Abel shows worship from a surrendered heart rather than consumer preference; Enoch shows an ordinary daily walk whose reward is God himself; Noah shows century-long obedience before the first raindrop. Their witness calls the church to keep building, gathering, repenting, serving, and worshiping while it waits for Christ to make faith sight.

  1. 00:41 Waiting and the Purpose of Hebrews 11

    • Elijah names waiting as a central difficulty of Christian life and frames Hebrews 11 as hope for ordinary, imperfect people who trust an extraordinary God before promises become visible.

    1. 00:41 So Much of Life Is Spent Waiting

      • Elijah opens with the observation that much of life is spent waiting: for answered prayer, healing, provision, open doors, and suffering to end.
      • Waiting is especially hard in a 2026 culture trained by phones, streaming, and instant answers to expect immediate results.

    2. 01:15 God’s Kingdom Rarely Moves at Our Preferred Speed

      • The Lord often works slowly, giving a promise and then calling his people to wait faithfully.
      • He gives commands that must be obeyed before they are understood and visions of what will be while his people still live with what is.
      • Hebrews 11 meets believers inside that tension.

    3. 01:37 Opening the “Hall of Faith”

      Scripture: Hebrews 11

      • Elijah directs the church to Hebrews 11 and names Abraham, Sarah, Moses, Noah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, and Rahab among its familiar figures.
      • Though commonly called the “Hall of Faith,” the chapter is not merely a display of famous saints.

    4. 02:00 Not a Spiritual Trophy Case

      • Reading Hebrews 11 as a museum of departed saints to admire misses its point and strips away the hope God intends through it.
      • The chapter’s aim is not to showcase extraordinary people but ordinary people trusting an extraordinary God.

    5. 02:16 Imperfect People Who Endured

      • These men and women knew waiting, suffering, weakness, uncertainty, misunderstanding, and even disobedience requiring repentance.
      • Many died before seeing what they had been promised, yet they endured, obeyed, and worshiped.
      • Faith is not confidence that life will unfold exactly as desired; it is trust that God remains faithful even when it does not.

  2. 03:01 Hebrews 11:1: Confidence in the Future and Assurance of the Unseen

    • Hebrews 11:1 defines faith as confidence in God’s promised future and assurance of unseen realities; Christ’s purchase, resurrection, and return make biblical hope certainty rather than wishful thinking.

    1. 03:01 Faith Is More Than Mental Agreement

      • Faith is a God-given conviction that God’s promises are true.
      • Because they are true, believers worship, obey, and endure until promise becomes sight.

    2. 03:14 The Definition Comes Before the Examples

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:1

      • Before displaying faith in people’s lives, Hebrews defines it: “faith is confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see.”
      • Elijah has the congregation read Hebrews 11:1 twice, drawing them into the text before explaining it.
      • Because the verse is so familiar, he warns that people can hear it without actually hearing it.

    3. 04:04 Hypostasis: An Underlying Reality

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:1

      • Elijah explains the Greek word translated “confidence,” *hypostasis*, as an underlying reality, guarantee, or steadiness.
      • Some scholars liken it to a title deed or proof of ownership.

    4. 04:28 Faith Receives Rather Than Creates

      • Faith does not create God’s promises; it receives them.
      • Elijah rejects any idea that faith itself manufactures the future it desires.

    5. 04:34 The Inheritance Illustration

      • An inheritance legally belongs to its heir before the heir moves into the house, accesses the account, or enjoys its benefits.
      • In the same way, Christian faith has a legitimate claim upon what God promised before physically possessing it.
      • The claim rests not on merit or worthiness but on what Christ purchased with his blood.

    6. 05:07 Not a Blank Check for Personal Dreams

      Scripture: 1 Peter 1:3–5

      • Faith is not writing God a blank check for one’s dreams.
      • It presents the “title deed” Christ bought and says that the Father’s promises belong to his people because the Son secured them.
      • Elijah quotes the living hope and imperishable inheritance given through Jesus’ resurrection and guarded by God’s power until salvation is revealed.

    7. 05:46 Biblical Hope Is Confident Expectation

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:1

      • Hebrews 11:1 first gives faith a future orientation: biblical faith is always attached to future hope.
      • Cultural hope expresses uncertainty—“I hope things work out”—but biblical hope is confident expectation rooted in God’s promises.
      • Its certainty rests on the future God has already secured through the person and work of Jesus Christ.

    8. 06:29 Groaning and Waiting Patiently

      Scripture: Romans 8:22–25

      • Elijah quotes Romans 8: creation groans, believers groan while awaiting bodily redemption, and hope concerns what is not yet seen.
      • If believers hope for what they do not yet have, they wait for it patiently.
      • Their confidence rests not in themselves but in the God who promised.

    9. 07:04 Tomorrow’s Promises Shape Today’s Obedience

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1:20

      • Christian hope looks toward Christ’s return, sin’s final defeat, resurrection, the restoration of all things, and eternity in God’s presence.
      • Faith trusts that these promises are “yes and amen” in Christ and brings tomorrow’s promises into today’s obedience.
      • Believers may not know what tomorrow holds, but they know King Jesus, who holds tomorrow and is worthy of worship.

    10. 07:41 Assurance of Unseen Realities

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:1 2 Corinthians 4:18 2 Corinthians 5:7

      • The second half of Hebrews 11:1 moves from the future to the unseen: God, Christ at the Father’s right hand, the heavenly kingdom, and much of the Spirit’s work are not physically visible.
      • Their invisibility does not make them less real; Paul says in 2 Corinthians 4 to fix one’s eyes on the unseen and eternal rather than the seen and temporary.
      • Against materialism’s claim that reality is limited to what can be measured, touched, or observed, Scripture says the most important realities are often unseen.

    11. 08:48 Seeing Reality Through God’s Revelation

      Scripture: John 20:29 Revelation 21:5

      • Assurance is not make-believe; it perceives reality through God’s revelation. Jesus’ words to Thomas bless those who believe without seeing.
      • In suffering, unanswered prayer, and chaos, faith confesses that God is good, working, and faithful because he reigns, Christ’s kingdom advances, and his promises remain certain.
      • Faith bridges promise and fulfillment, enabling faithful life while God’s unseen purposes unfold.
      • Christ himself is the object of hope and assurance: he will return, raise the dead, and make all things new, guaranteed by God’s character, word, and work in believers.

  3. 10:39 What Faith Is Not: Prosperity Teaching and the Sufficiency of Christ

    • Prosperity teaching turns faith into a technique for controlling outcomes, but Scripture presents suffering as a place where Christ’s sufficiency, sanctifying grace, and overcoming victory are known.

    1. 10:39 Counterfeits of Faith

      • Faith is not wishful thinking, optimism, manifesting, positive self-talk, “trusting the process,” or projecting positive energy into the universe.
      • The world treats faith like an internal power, and Elijah says some of that language has entered the church.

    2. 11:08 “Naming and Claiming” Distorts Faith

      • Prosperity teaching presents faith as a force for securing health, wealth, success, or favorable circumstances through confession, declarations, or sufficient belief.
      • Elijah explicitly rejects this teaching and urges the church to reject it too.

    3. 11:48 Trust Through or From Suffering

      • The fundamental error is not merely too much emphasis on material gain; it is a distortion of biblical faith itself.
      • Faith is not a prosperity tool but trust in God’s promises whether he delivers his people through suffering or from it.

    4. 12:06 Christ Is Enough, Whatever Happens

      • Elijah recalls Matt Chandler’s point that Scripture does not promise everything will go right when one follows Christ; it promises that Christ is enough whatever happens.
      • He tells the church plainly that following Christ will not make them wealthy or guarantee continual health.
      • Teaching otherwise ignores Scripture and Christian history.

    5. 12:46 Weakness, Trials, Affliction, and Tribulation

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 12:7–10 James 1:2–3 Psalm 34:19 John 16:33

      • Paul pleaded three times for the thorn to be removed, but received the answer that Christ’s grace is sufficient and his power is perfected in weakness.
      • Elijah concludes that God uses suffering for sanctification: James says trials test faith and produce steadfastness, while Psalm 34 says the righteous have many afflictions and the Lord delivers them.
      • Jesus tells his disciples they will have tribulation in the world but can take heart because he has overcome it.

    6. 14:17 A Suffering Savior Produces Suffering Servants

      Scripture: Acts 7:54–60 2 Timothy 3:12

      • Scripture’s pattern is a suffering Savior producing suffering servants; Elijah invokes Stephen, the apostles, and present-day believers in Nigeria, Gaza, Afghanistan, and China.
      • He quotes 2 Timothy: everyone desiring a godly life in Christ Jesus “will be persecuted”—not might be or probably will be.

    7. 14:49 The Pastoral Damage of Prosperity Teaching

      Scripture: Colossians 1:18

      • Prosperity teaching turns God into a personal cheerleader rather than Lord and makes Christ valuable chiefly for what he gives.
      • It robs sufferers of biblical hope and joy, then blames those who remain sick or poor for allegedly lacking faith.
      • Instead of teaching people to suffer well and recognize God shaping them through trial, it shuns them for not believing hard enough; Elijah says this is not Christianity and apologizes to anyone taught that lie.

    8. 15:49 Jesus Is Enough in Every Outcome

      Scripture: Colossians 1:17

      • Believers may pray for and pursue healing, but whether healing comes or not, Jesus is enough.
      • They may make wise career moves to provide for their families, but whether advancement comes or not, Jesus is enough.
      • Christ is enough regardless of outcome: he is before all things, and in him all things hold together.

  4. 16:40 Hebrews 11:3: Faith, Knowledge, and the Creator’s Word

    • Hebrews 11:3 joins faith and understanding: God’s spoken word explains creation, grounds knowledge, and assures believers that no circumstance or unanswered prayer lies beyond his purpose.

    1. 16:40 “By Faith We Understand”

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:2 Hebrews 11:3

      • Moving to Hebrews 11:3, Elijah reads that by faith believers understand the universe was formed at God’s command, so the seen was not made from the visible.
      • The text says neither “we imagine” nor “we speculate,” but “we understand”; this is the faith for which the ancients were commended.

    2. 16:55 Faith Is Not Intellectual Suicide

      • Atheist objections often pit faith against knowledge, dismissing faith as weak-minded, uneducated, or evidence-free.
      • Elijah argues that Christian faith goes beyond reason without contradicting it and that Christian faith makes human understanding possible.

    3. 18:01 Everyone Has a Creation Story

      Scripture: Genesis 1:1

      • The Christian begins, “In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.”
      • Elijah contrasts this with a secular account in which random evolutionary processes yield a fine-tuned cosmos, laws and patterns, sentient beings, and morality.
      • He argues that the secular story also requires faith rather than occupying a neutral, faithless position.

    4. 18:50 Questions Secularism Cannot Finally Answer

      • Pressing questions about the universe, natural laws, consciousness, and shared morality eventually receive no ultimate answer in the secular account.
      • Elijah mocks morality reduced to changeable “preferences” that can still condemn, shame, and demand obedience without explaining their authority.

    5. 19:34 The Question Is What Faith Explains Reality

      Scripture: Proverbs 1:7

      • Everyone has faith; the issue is whether that faith accounts for the reality people inhabit or must borrow from God while rejecting him.
      • Proverbs 1:7 calls the fear of the Lord the beginning of knowledge; Elijah therefore says reality is finally defined by God’s word.
      • Without God’s speech, humanity is left with shifting opinions and traditions, and with no God above man, man makes himself god.

    6. 20:21 The Creator’s Voice Sustains Those Who Wait

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:3

      • Humanity makes a terrible god, but there is a good God above man and there is a heaven.
      • The voice that raised Christ from the dead is the same voice speaking promises over believers; no circumstance is greater than that voice.
      • If God speaks galaxies into existence, suffering remains within his purposes and unanswered prayer within his reach.
      • The God who creates and sustains by his word is capable; the question is whether his people will trust him while they wait.

  5. 21:12 Hebrews 11:4: Abel, Surrendered Worship, and Consumer Preferences

    • Abel’s offering shows that worship is accepted through faith rather than outward performance, confronting consumer preferences about sermon length, music, service, and unreconciled relationships.

    1. 21:12 Abel’s Better Offering

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:4 Genesis 4:3–8

      • Elijah reads Hebrews 11:4: Abel offered a better sacrifice than Cain, was commended as righteous, and still speaks through faith after death.
      • Recalling Genesis 4, he summarizes that both brothers offered to God, but Abel came by faith while Cain came on his own terms.

    2. 21:52 The Giver’s Heart, Not the Material

      • God did not prefer Abel’s sacrifice because he favors sheep over vegetables; the difference was the giver’s inner disposition.
      • Quoting John Calvin, Elijah says Abel’s sacrifice was preferred because it was sanctified by faith, not because animal fat’s odor pacified God.

    3. 22:24 External Religion Cannot Earn God’s Favor

      Scripture: 1 Samuel 16:6–7

      • Rituals, ceremonies, and outward sacrifices are empty without genuine faith: *sola fide*, faith alone.
      • As Protestants, the church rejects the idea that people earn God’s favor or satisfy divine justice through external works, rituals, or gifts.
      • Samuel’s visit to Jesse illustrates the point: Samuel noticed Eliab’s stature, but God said people see outward appearance while the Lord sees the heart.

    4. 23:24 Cain’s Enduring Temptation: Worship While Retaining Control

      • Cain’s ancient and common sin was wanting to worship God while remaining in control—approaching on his own terms and defining obedience for himself.
      • He wanted God to reward his performance rather than receive his worship.
      • The same temptation survives whenever believers elevate preferences over obedience, especially through a consumer mindset toward church.

    5. 24:11 Consumer Condition One: Keep the Sermon Short

      • Elijah voices the condition, “I’ll engage as long as the sermon is short enough.”
      • If someone can binge a show or scroll social media for hours but cannot attend to God’s word for 45 minutes a week, he says the problem is the person’s attention span, not the sermon’s length.

    6. 24:42 Consumer Condition Two: Sing My Preferred Music

      Scripture: Ephesians 5:19

      • Elijah challenges the condition, “I’ll come into worship as long as you sing this song or play this style.”
      • He and Pastor Josh prayerfully choose a mixture of old and new songs to stir faith, recall God’s promises, and support the church’s teaching in its present season—not to follow popularity or requests.
      • Leaving the gathering because of musical style disrespects the Lord, the prepared worship team, and fellow believers trying to praise.
      • Elijah will care for people loyally, but he will not cater to every worship preference.

    7. 26:56 The Unique Power of the Gathered Body

      • God’s people gather before God’s throne, under God’s word, around God’s table, lifting one voice in praise.
      • There sinners confess, saints sing, the word is proclaimed, and Christ feeds his sheep; leadership therefore protects the gathering from divisive conduct.
      • To belong to the body means singing, receiving the proclaimed word, sharing the Lord’s Supper, serving, and helping embody the church’s mission and vision.

    8. 27:36 Consumer Condition Three: “I Don’t Have Time to Serve”

      • Elijah acknowledges genuinely busy seasons and says the church will not shame people for reduced availability, but the ministries cannot run without participation.
      • Even serving monthly or every six weeks helps; he names needs in the café, camera team, communion team, Crossroads Cares, and the care center.
      • These ministries enact the mission of changing how people see the church rather than leaving it as a slogan.
      • Service is not always convenient, and that is part of the point: being a light requires work and recalls how far Christ went to meet humanity’s need for a Savior.

    9. 29:14 Consumer Condition Four: Worship While Holding Grudges

      Scripture: Matthew 5:23–24

      • Elevating preferences becomes especially damaging when people enter worship bearing grudges against leaders or one another.
      • Elijah admits his own guilt and cites Jesus in Matthew 5: leave the gift at the altar, reconcile with the brother or sister, and then return to offer it.
      • Do not sing of Christ’s grace while carrying bitterness; raised hands do not impress God when hearts remain clenched in unforgiveness.

    10. 30:08 Worship Begins Before the First Song

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:4

      • Abel teaches that worship begins with a surrendered heart long before music starts.
      • It requires repentance, humility, and meeting the Lord on his terms rather than ours.
      • God has not asked people to invent how he should be worshiped; he asks for faith and obedience.

  6. 30:32 Hebrews 11:5–6: Enoch’s Ordinary Walk and God as the Reward

    • Enoch’s walk shows supernatural faith in ordinary days; Hebrews 11:6 locates pleasing obedience in a trusting heart and identifies God himself—not answered prayers or possessions—as faith’s reward.

    1. 30:32 Enoch Was Commended as One Who Pleased God

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:5 Genesis 5:21–24

      • Elijah reads Hebrews 11:5: by faith Enoch was taken from life without experiencing death and was commended beforehand as pleasing God.
      • He then reads Genesis 5:21–24: Enoch lived 365 years, walked faithfully with God for 300 years, and “was no more” because God took him.
      • Elijah declines a detour into debates about the book of Enoch so the church can remain focused.

    2. 31:29 Two Phrases Reveal a Forgotten Truth

      Scripture: Genesis 5:24 Hebrews 11:5

      • Elijah highlights “Enoch walked faithfully with God” and “he was commended as one who pleased God.”
      • Enoch’s commendation rests not on status, reputation, wealth, or works, but on faith and obedience.
      • The account’s power lies in how ordinary that faithfulness appears.

    3. 32:01 One Faithful Step After Another

      • Churches often define faith through mountaintop or rock-bottom moments, but Enoch shows that simply walking with God is a great demonstration of faith.
      • Such a walk proceeds day by day and year by year, one faithful step after another.

    4. 32:23 Most of the Christian Life Is Not a Mountaintop

      • Choosing one’s church and Christian life by pursuit of the next spiritual high rather than faithfulness and obedience is unhealthy and immature.
      • Most Christian life occurs during long workdays, showing up for family, loving a spouse, changing yet another diaper, and tending a child at 2:00 a.m.

    5. 33:00 Supernatural Faith in Ordinary Practices

      • Ordinary faith means repenting quickly, opening the Bible when one does not feel like it, and praying when heaven seems silent.
      • Faith is simultaneously supernatural and profoundly ordinary.
      • Quoting “the great theologian Gandalf the Grey,” Elijah says evil is held back through ordinary people’s small, daily deeds of kindness and love, not only great power.

    6. 33:34 Faith Is the Root; Obedience Is the Fruit

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:6a

      • Enoch’s epitaph is that he pleased God—not through sinlessness or impressiveness, but by walking with God in faith.
      • Hebrews turns his story into a principle for everyone: without faith it is not merely harder but impossible to please God.
      • Before asking what God wants one to do, ask from what kind of heart it will be done; service, giving, singing, preaching, ministry, study, and raised hands can all exist without faith.
      • God wants more than behavior modification: faith is the root and obedience the fruit; reversing them replaces walking with God with performing for him.

    7. 35:32 Believe That God Is

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:6

      • Hebrews 11:6 says whoever comes to God must believe that he exists and rewards those who seek him; Elijah explains the wording as “believe that God is.”
      • Faith settles that God is real, present, and exactly as he has revealed himself: holy, sovereign, faithful, just, merciful, Creator, Redeemer, Deliverer, and King.
      • Many profess God’s existence while few live as if he is actually present.

    8. 36:27 God Himself Is the Reward

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:9, 13, 35–38 Psalm 73:25–26

      • God’s reward is not a guarantee that every prayer will be answered as desired; Hebrews 11 includes people who wandered, suffered, and died waiting.
      • The reward is God himself: the strength of the failing heart and the believer’s portion forever. His people seek his face rather than valuing his gifts above him.
      • Elijah asks whether answered prayers for promotion, health, finances, or marriage would satisfy if God withheld his presence.
      • Faith’s greatest promise is not an easier life but that God will be their God and they will be his people.

  7. 38:15 Hebrews 11:7: Noah’s Century of Obedience in the Waiting

    • Noah builds for roughly a century before seeing rain, showing that faithful waiting consists of ordinary acts of obedience whose place in God’s redemptive purpose may remain hidden.

    1. 38:15 Warned About Things Not Yet Seen

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:7

      • Elijah closes his exposition with Hebrews 11:7: warned about unseen things, Noah built an ark in holy fear to save his family.
      • By faith Noah condemned the world and became heir of the righteousness that accords with faith.

    2. 38:32 A Century Before the First Raindrop

      Scripture: Genesis 6:13–22

      • Familiarity can hide how staggering Noah’s story is: judgment was coming not the next day or week but roughly one hundred years later.
      • Noah worked for about a century without seeing rain and endured neighbors’ ridicule for continuing to build.

    3. 39:01 Every Hammer Swing Became a Sermon

      • Every swing, nail, and board became an act of faith and obedience beneath a perfectly ordinary sky.
      • Each day offered another chance to wonder whether he had misunderstood, yet Noah kept building.
      • He obeyed not because he saw rain but because he trusted the God who promised it.

    4. 39:31 Faith Worships While It Waits

      • The phrase “things not yet seen” returns the sermon to waiting: faith believes God before circumstances catch up and worships while it waits.
      • Elijah names people waiting for healing, a prodigal’s return, direction, justice, or Christ’s return.
      • His encouragement is repeated: “Your waiting is not wasted.”

    5. 40:00 Ordinary Obedience in the Waiting Matters

      • Every tearful prayer, heavy-hearted Sunday gathering, quiet repentance, search of God’s word, and choice of forgiveness over bitterness matters.
      • Noah’s nails seemed painfully ordinary until suddenly they were not.
      • God’s promises arrive on God’s timetable: never early, never late, precisely on time.

    6. 40:44 The Ark and the Covenant Line to Christ

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:7 Genesis 7:23 Colossians 1:27 Revelation 5:9

      • Noah’s ark preserved a family from God’s righteous wrath and maintained the covenant line.
      • Through that line came Abraham, Israel, David, and Christ—the greater David, greater Moses, Son of God, fulfillment of Israel, King of kings, and Lord of lords.
      • Through Christ, the hope of glory, the gospel advances among every tribe, tongue, people, and nation, gathering God’s elect and building his kingdom.

    7. 41:32 God Was Preserving More Than Eight People

      Scripture: 1 Peter 3:20

      • While Noah built, God was preserving the world—the stage upon which redemption would unfold—not merely eight individuals.
      • God is always doing “10,000 things” when his people can barely see one.

  8. 41:52 Do Not Despise the Ordinary; Worship Until Faith Becomes Sight

    • Present faithfulness belongs to a kingdom story larger than one lifetime, so believers keep building, gathering, repenting, and worshiping until Christ returns and faith becomes sight.

    1. 41:52 Today’s Faithfulness Belongs to a Larger Story

      • Believers may never know what hangs on present faithfulness: a watching child, a listening coworker, a neighbor noticing peace, unseen generations, or kingdom fruit they never harvest.
      • Elijah recalls worshiping beside his wife and little girl that morning and realizing the moment was much bigger than himself.
      • God’s kingdom grows quietly, patiently, and steadily—one obedient act, faithful family, and transformed life at a time.

    2. 42:39 The Kingdom Advances Through Ordinary Saints

      • Do not despise ordinary life, waiting, or small acts of faithfulness.
      • Christ’s kingdom has never depended on spectacular people; it advances through ordinary saints who believe Jesus meant what he said.

    3. 42:57 Keep Building Until Faith Becomes Sight

      Scripture: Philippians 2:10–11 Galatians 6:9

      • One day faith will become sight, every promise will be fulfilled, every knee will bow, and every tongue will confess Jesus Christ as Lord.
      • Until then: keep building, worshiping, gathering, repenting, and believing, because the God who raised his Son remains faithful.
      • Elijah applies the call not to grow weary in doing good: at the proper time, a harvest comes if believers do not give up.

    4. 43:32 The Sermon’s Final Call: Worship in the Waiting

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:58 1 Corinthians 16:22 Revelation 22:20

      • The hammer may feel small and the days long, but no act of faith offered to God is in vain.
      • The kingdom is still coming and Christ is still reigning; therefore believers can worship in the waiting.
      • Elijah closes the message, “Maranatha. Come, Lord Jesus,” and turns to prayer.

    5. 43:55 Closing Prayer

      Scripture: Revelation 21:5

      • Elijah thanks the Father for who he is and for the opportunity to participate in kingdom work.
      • He prays for those exhausted by long days, heaven’s apparent silence, and weariness, asking that they find rest and strength in God.
      • Because Jesus has promised to return and make all things new, he asks that the church view the world not as pessimists but as “divine optimists,” trusting the enthroned King of kings and Lord of lords.
      • He entrusts seasons of suffering, doubt, success, and harvest to God’s plan and purpose, ending: “Help us to worship while we wait. It’s in your name. Amen.”
