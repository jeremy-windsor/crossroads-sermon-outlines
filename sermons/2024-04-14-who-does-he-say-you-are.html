Overview

  1. 00:05 Week two of Identity

    • Chris admits that while Eli was talking he was looking around to see who was preaching, before realizing it was him. The church is in its second week of the Identity series, and the fourth week will be a panel. Questions about identity, who am I in Christ, or what someone is struggling with will soon be collected ahead of time, because who you see yourself as and who God sees you as matter, and if that is off, other things will be off too.
    • Last week Josh asked two questions, what does God say about himself and what does God say about you, and focused mostly on the first. Chris will spend most of his time on the second, splitting it in two: how do you believe God sees you, and how does God really see you? If you have the wrong belief about how God sees you, it will have terrible consequences for your identity.

  2. 02:03 A game: what is it?

    • Chris plays a game: he shows a picture, and the church calls out its name or purpose. The first, made by Toyota, draws crickets and a guess of a remote car starter; it is a yarn winder, because Toyota started in the fabric loom business. Next comes a perfume bottle, which someone gets right, and an antique lipstick tube.
    • The last, a glass bulb of liquid with a metal disc floating inside, draws guesses of thermometer, compass, barometer, fishbowl, paperweight, and grenade. It is a glass grenade-style fire extinguisher made between 1870 and 1910, kept on wall brackets for anyone to throw at a fire to put it out or buy time to escape. Chris would love to throw one, until he learned how toxic the liquid inside was.

    1. 05:42 We don’t go to the source

      • Each item was made for a purpose by someone who created it. When we didn’t know what it was for, we made something up that sounded cool, or let someone else tell us, instead of going to the source.
      • We do the same with our identity. We make up, or listen to others tell us, how God sees us, how he feels about us, and what his purpose is for us, but we don’t go to the source, which is God’s word.

  3. 06:37 How do you believe God sees you?

    • Chris asks everyone to ask themselves how they think God sees them. That belief is formed in many places.

    1. 06:50 Our fathers

      Scripture: Matthew 7:11

      • Your relationship, or lack of one, with your father may shape it. An abusive, cold, uncaring, or absent father can make it hard to call God Father or understand what a loving Father means. Matthew 7 says if you, though evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good gifts to those who ask him. If you never received a good gift from your father or parents, that verse seems foreign and distant.
      • If your father was absent, overly critical, demanded perfection, or berated you, your view of God may carry those traits: “If I mess up, God’s going to punish me. He can’t possibly love me because I’m worthless.” When you sin, do you avoid God because you’re afraid he is disappointed in you? If so, stick around; good news is coming.

    2. 08:19 False teaching: God can’t look upon sinners

      • Others have had a false teaching about God burned into their minds by someone they or their parents trusted. Chris has dealt with several, and the most harmful for him was the idea that God couldn’t look upon sin or sinners. If he sinned, even as a follower of Christ, he had to ask forgiveness before God could interact with him; every prayer started with forgiveness. At times he hid from God in shame, believing God must be ashamed of him too. He has talked with others in this body who had the same teaching drilled into them, and he wants to help them walk out of it.
      1. 09:22 God came to Adam and Eve in their sin

        Scripture: Genesis 3:8–11

        • When Adam and Eve sinned, they heard the Lord God walking in the garden and hid. The Lord called, “Where are you?” Adam said he was afraid because he was naked. “Who told you that you were naked? Have you eaten from the tree?”
        • God wasn’t surprised; he knew. He talked to them and then gave them the consequences of their sin. They were in their sin, and he was with them, talking to them.
      2. 10:16 Isaiah in the presence of the Lord

        Scripture: Isaiah 6:1

        • In the year King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord high and exalted, the train of his robe filling the temple. Isaiah says he is ruined, a man of unclean lips whose eyes have seen the Lord, and his guilt is taken away with a live coal from the altar. But he was in the presence of the Lord before that.
      3. 10:49 Forgiveness is fourth in the Lord’s Prayer

        Scripture: Matthew 6:9–12

        • Simpler still, Matthew 6: “Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name, your kingdom come, your will be done… give us today our daily bread, and forgive us our debts.” Jesus is teaching us how to pray.
        • If God couldn’t hear or commune with us while we have any sin, why is forgiveness the fourth item? Logically God wouldn’t hear the first three, and those are pretty important: hallowed be your name, your kingdom come, your will be done, give us our daily bread. Then forgive our sins.

    3. 11:52 Broken relationships

      • That is one example of the dangerous, damaging untruths some have been taught about how God sees us, and there are many more. Few have lived this long without being scarred by broken relationships: rejection, abandonment, abuse, neglect, being unwanted. These seep into our self-perception and alter our identity. Some close off from relationships to spare themselves more hurt; others exhaust themselves chasing approval through perfectionism and codependency.
      • This carries into our relationship with God. How you see God directly relates to how you believe he sees you, so if your understanding of God comes from anywhere but his word, you risk being led astray. He has already told us everything he wanted us to know about himself and about us; all we have to do is read it and believe it.

  4. 13:22 Today’s text: Paul’s prayer in Ephesians 1:15–23

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:15–23

    • Chris reads the passage: for this reason, ever since hearing of their faith in the Lord Jesus and love for all God’s people, Paul has not stopped giving thanks and praying that God would give them the Spirit of wisdom and revelation to know him better, that the eyes of their hearts would be enlightened to know the hope of his calling, the riches of his glorious inheritance, and his incomparably great power, the same mighty strength that raised Christ from the dead and seated him far above all rule and authority, with all things under his feet as head over everything for the church.
    • “For this reason” points back to everything Paul said before it, the one very long Greek sentence Josh described last week. Paul’s thanksgiving for God’s plan of salvation in those first 14 verses overflows into prayer, and Chris wants someone to pray like that for him.

    1. 15:29 Do people hear about our faith and love?

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:15–16 Galatians 6:10

      • Paul is excited by their faith and their love for God’s people, which is what we are called to: as we have opportunity, let us do good to all people, especially the family of believers. Paul isn’t there to see it; he is hearing about it. Word is spreading that they are faithful and loving.
      • That raises a tough question: are people outside these four walls hearing about our faith in Jesus and our love for each other? If so, praise God. If not, why not? That is a whole other sermon, but Chris wants the church to keep it in mind.

    2. 16:44 Knowing God better, to know how he sees us

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:17–19

      • Paul prays the Ephesians would have the Spirit of wisdom and revelation so they may know God better, because when we know God better, we know how he sees us and can begin to understand our identity in Christ.

  5. 17:30 How does God really see you?

    • Here come the answers: how God sees you and what your true identity is if you are in Christ, in three pieces.

    1. 17:40 You are sealed

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:13–14

      • When you heard the gospel and believed, you were marked in him with a seal, the promised Holy Spirit, a deposit guaranteeing our inheritance. When you received Christ and the Holy Spirit, he sealed you.
      • In the ancient world a stamp pressed into clay or wax sealed something important. It served as a trademark, proved goods weren’t tampered with, and proved ownership: a seal indicated ownership, protection, and authenticity. The Holy Spirit is the mark that you belong to God, that he is your protection, that you are truly his, and that one day he will fully complete his work in you.

    2. 19:04 You are adopted

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:5 Galatians 4:4–7

      • Verse 5 from last week: he predestined us for adoption to sonship through Jesus Christ. God has given us the family rights and privileges as if we were born into the family.
      • William Barclay wrote that under Roman law, when adoption was complete, the adopted person had all the rights of a legitimate son in his new family and completely lost all rights in his old one. In the eyes of the law he was a new person, so new that all debts and obligations connected with his previous family were abolished as if they never existed.
      • Paul writes in Galatians 4 that when the set time had fully come, God sent his Son to redeem those under the law, that we might receive adoption to sonship, and sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying “Abba, Father.” You are no longer a slave but God’s child, and since you are his child, God has made you also an heir. From slave to child is a far jump.

    3. 20:43 You are an heir

      Scripture: Galatians 3:29 James 2:5

      • An heir is someone entitled to inherit the rank or property of another. We will not inherit God’s rank; we won’t become God. But we are heirs of the promise made to Abraham: if you belong to Christ, you are Abraham’s seed and heirs according to the promise. That is a pretty good promise: we are God’s chosen people.
      • We are also heirs of the kingdom. James 2: has not God chosen those who are poor in the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom he promised to those who love him? Studying this week how God loves him and what he gives him because he made him his son has blown Chris away. Those in Christ, sealed and adopted, don’t have to worry about what happens after they die; they inherit the kingdom he promised.
      • Are you starting to see how God sees you, and where your identity needs to come from?

    4. 22:44 Co-heirs with Christ

      Scripture: Romans 8:14–17

      • What kind of heirs are we? Co-heirs with Christ himself. Romans 8: those led by the Spirit are children of God; the Spirit we received doesn’t make us slaves to fear but brought about our adoption to sonship, by which we cry “Abba, Father.” If we are children, then we are heirs of God and co-heirs with Christ, if we share in his sufferings in order to share in his glory. Notice the pattern: this is all over Scripture.
      • Chris can’t fathom the worth God has placed on us by making us co-heirs with his Son. His own view of how God sees him is pathetic and small compared to reality.
      1. 24:07 A new birth certificate

        Scripture: Ephesians 1:18–19

        • Attorney David Anderson, who helps people adopt children, notes the parallel between new birth and adoption: one is not born by oneself but by another, so a new relationship is created, a new name given, and a new identity formed. After the judge signs the adoption decree, he files for a new birth certificate listing the actual hospital, doctor, and time of birth, but with a completely new name and new parents. It shows a new birth, a new parent-child relationship, and a new source of identity.
        • Before Christ we had one identity; after Christ we have a new one defined by our new Father, who has made us heirs of the promise, heirs of the kingdom, and co-heirs with Christ. That is why Paul prays that the eyes of your heart may be enlightened to know the hope of his calling and the riches of his glorious inheritance.
        • If you haven’t yet seen the incredible way God sees you, or been challenged to root your identity in being a son or daughter, an heir, and a co-heir with Christ, Chris struggles to know what more he can show you. But there is one more thing that should shake your identity to the core.

    5. 26:20 He sent his Son to die for you

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:18–23 Romans 5:6–8

      • Chris reads Ephesians 1:18–23 again and tells the church to have it memorized before they leave. Here is the third piece of how God sees you: he sent his Son to die for you.
      • Romans 5: at just the right time, while we were still powerless, Christ died for the ungodly. Very rarely will anyone die for a righteous person, though for a good person someone might dare to die. But God demonstrates his own love in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. We hadn’t done a thing for God; we weren’t righteous or good. If that isn’t love, and if it doesn’t inform your identity, Chris doesn’t know what does.
      1. 28:12 What Jesus gave up

        Scripture: Philippians 2:5–7 John 1:10–11 John 1:3 Luke 19:10

        • To understand how much God loves and values you, you have to understand the sacrifice. Philippians 2: Christ Jesus, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage; he made himself nothing, taking the nature of a servant, made in human likeness. He took on the likeness of his own creation because of his love for us.
        • John 1: he was in the world, and though the world was made through him, the world did not recognize him; he came to his own, and his own did not receive him. All things were made through Jesus, yet his own creation rejected him. He gave up the glory of heaven for overwhelming rejection.
        • The Pharisees and teachers of the law wanted to kill him and tried to trip him up and make a fool of him, which never happened, because it is Jesus. He came to seek and save the lost, that we might have life, and faced rejection time and again.
      2. 30:18 The cup and the cross

        Scripture: Luke 22:42–44 Matthew 27:46 Psalm 22:1

        • Jesus knew what he would face. In the garden in Luke 22 he prays, “Father, if you are willing, take this cup from me; yet not my will, but yours be done.” An angel strengthens him, and in anguish his sweat is like drops of blood. He knew the cost of our sin and the horrific pain ahead, and so did the Father, yet his will was for his Son to drink that cup and bear our filthy sin. Jesus took the beatings, endured the humiliation, and ended up on the cross.
        • Matthew 27:46 hits Chris in the gut: about three in the afternoon Jesus cried out in a loud voice, “Eli, Eli,” which means “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?” Taking the punishment for all our sin, he cries out Psalm 22:1 to his Father.
        • God’s love for his children is unconditional and beyond imagining. He loved us so much that he had his Son leave heaven, come to a place that didn’t want him, and die a horrific death while we were still sinners deserving nothing. Our Father wanted to adopt us and spend eternity with us so badly that he sent his own Son to the cross in our place. That is God’s mic drop.

  6. 32:48 Review: how God sees us

    Scripture: Romans 10:9

    • We are sealed: God has given us the Holy Spirit to seal us and ensure our inheritance and eternity with him. God isn’t losing you. When you declared with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believed in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you were saved, and the Spirit sealed you as God’s, under his protection and authority. This comforts Chris more than he can express: he knows who he belongs to.
    • We are adopted, sons and daughters. “Adoption to sonship” is a legal term for the full legal standing of an adopted child. Chris’s research found that under Roman law you could disown your natural-born children but never an adopted child. It doesn’t get more secure: God adopted you because he wants you.
    • We are heirs, inheriting God’s promise and his kingdom, and co-heirs with Christ. And God sent his one and only Son to take on a flesh suit, live a perfect life, and pay the debt we should have paid. Chris has a son, and he can’t imagine sending him through that torture: “God, I don’t know how you love us that much.”

  7. 35:14 The same power that raised Christ

    Scripture: Ephesians 1:19–23 1 Corinthians 6:14

    • At the end of Ephesians 1, that power is the same mighty strength God exerted when he raised Christ from the dead and seated him at his right hand, far above all rule and authority, head over everything for the church.
    • Jesus didn’t die and stay in the grave. If he had, there would be no co-heirs with Christ, no inheritance in God’s kingdom, and no salvation, because he wouldn’t have conquered sin and death. By his power God raised the Lord from the dead, and he will raise us also. No questions.

  8. 36:36 Who have you allowed to tell you who you are?

    • Where does your identity come from? Someone who beat you down, a parent who didn’t know how to love you or wasn’t there, a teacher or someone you trusted who told you unbiblical things, as happened to Chris? If you have let an abuser, a well-meaning but off-base Christian, a Sunday school teacher, a pastor, a friend, even Chris, or anyone but God’s word tell you who you are, your identity isn’t right. Chris couldn’t tell the church about their identity without being in Scripture; God is the one who tells you who you are.
    • Brothers and sisters, if your Father has adopted you, has an inheritance for you, has sealed you, and sent his Son to take your blame and punishment because he wants you in his family, doesn’t that tell you he sees you with infinite worth and value? Hold on to that, don’t let anyone take it from you, and go back to his word if you need to. How he sees you matters, not how anyone else sees you, not even how you see yourself.

  9. 38:44 If you don’t know Christ

    Scripture: Romans 10:9

    • If you don’t know Christ, Chris implores you to see that God loves you and wants you in his family. He made a way, and it isn’t complicated: if you declare with your mouth “Jesus is Lord” and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. Find a pastor or staff member who will walk with you and answer your questions.
    • If you have given your life to Christ but haven’t been baptized, what are you waiting for? The water is warm and there are towels.
    • Being a child of God, bound to Christ, empowers us to live without regret, fear, or shame about who we were, who we are, or who we’re becoming.

  10. 40:08 Closing prayer

    • Chris prays that he needs the words God put in his heart today, and so do others. We get our identity from so many wrong places; he asks God to impress on them their identity in Christ: sealed, adopted, heirs. We woefully misunderstand how important we are to God and how much he loves us. He asks God to change them and help them see themselves as he sees them, because their identity is in Christ, period, and thanks him for the gift of salvation and adoption to sonship.
