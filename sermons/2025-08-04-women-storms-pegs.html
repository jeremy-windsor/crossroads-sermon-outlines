Overview

  1. 01:13 A drama with every ingredient, and a question: why Judges?

    • Today’s text has a lot of ingredients for an incredible drama: a tyrannical king, a female Jewish judge, a male Jewish military leader afraid to get his hands dirty, a Canaanite neighbor whose wife is quite skilled with a hammer, and a Jehovah God who can win wars by simply opening the sky.
    • First Josh addresses a question he was hit with recently: “Josh, why Judges?” It is so violent, dark, and long ago; what does it have to do with us, when we are called to love and preach the gospel? He could spend 30 minutes on it, but gives one reason.

  2. 02:07 The seeker-friendly movement

    • In Josh’s opinion, the seeker-friendly movement is the greatest widespread church tragedy in modern Christianity. In the 80s and 90s influential leaders decided the church should be designed and tailored for the unbeliever: “We want to be a church where the unchurched want to be.” It sounds great and catchy.
    • But unchurched people at least respect believers with convictions. Ditching values Christians have held for thousands of years in the name of tolerance makes you a lot of things, but his church isn’t one of them.
    • Church is for the believer: believers come, hear, receive an education in the gospel, then preach it in the streets, and those who hear come join the church. That is how it has always worked.
    • If you aren’t a believer, you are welcome; you drink the same coffee, and we pray you encounter the risen Christ. But we will never tailor our services to make you comfortable. That doesn’t make us rude; it makes us people of conviction. Our services are built to bring glory to God.

    1. 03:44 Generosity, not tolerance

      • What makes the church a blessing even to non-believers is our generosity, not our tolerance, and this is where our society and Christian culture have gotten twisted.
      • We’ll give you the shirt off our backs, cook you a meal even if the cafe is closed, walk you through the care center for groceries, and likely help with a bill you can’t pay. But don’t ask us to be quiet when we see sin and wickedness. Christians don’t ignore their own sin, at least they’re not supposed to, so don’t expect them to ignore yours.

    2. 04:31 An ancient approach

      • This isn’t a new ministry approach. When Crossroads says it wants to change the way the world sees the church, it has an ancient thing in mind: get back to what they were doing when the Holy Spirit first came.
      • Christians are the reason we have so many hospitals, soup kitchens, and care centers. Charity existed before Jesus, but the Lord’s church, led by the Holy Spirit, made charity cool. Generosity is our outreach, not tolerance.

  3. 05:19 Generosity and conviction toward the homosexual

    • Josh uses homosexuality to show the balance. He will go to great lengths to help and defend a homosexual who is being mistreated: “No, this person has dignity. This is an image bearer. Back off.”
    • If you are living in that sin or struggling with same-sex attraction, come see him; he will take you to lunch today, and you are welcome at his table. What he can never do is tell you your sin doesn’t matter. He pleads: don’t marry your sin. Scripture clearly teaches that homosexuality is a sin and unnatural; this is God’s word, and he is just a messenger.

    1. 06:22 Some sins are harder to deal with

      • Josh calls it ridiculous to say all sins are the same; some are harder to deal with than others. Sometimes you pull off a band-aid and it’s no big deal, and other times other stuff comes with it and you wonder if you caused a worse situation than what it covered.
      • That is what wrestling with sin is like. Josh wrestled with sin a lot and didn’t see victory until the Spirit of God gave him self-control. But just because a sin is acceptable in culture doesn’t mean it becomes permissible for God’s children.

  4. 07:08 This is what tolerance gets you

    • This is why Judges still matters. Josh shows a picture he says should make people angry: a registered sex offender and his partner in a same-sex union, whom the government calls married and the church does not, and between them a helpless child they purchased together so they could play house and pretend what they are doing is natural.
    • He isn’t trying to be mean, just honest: this is what tolerance gets you. Wickedness still exists. We say we’d never go on a hill and sleep with a prostitute by a pole, but this country is becoming a wicked nation before our eyes. “Shame on us,” Josh says; we deserve our own divine hellstorm.
    • To every Christian who has shouted for inclusion, affirmation, tolerance, and sexual liberty, or waved the rainbow flag: this is what you fought for. And the church is doing nothing, holding movie nights about the Lion King instead of calling people to repent.

  5. 08:51 Comfort only through repentance

    • The church wants couples like this to feel at home, but Josh sometimes doesn’t feel at home himself when God’s word is presented; it is a sharp double-edged sword. They won’t feel comfortable because of soft TED talks. The only way they will ever feel comfortable in the Lord’s true church is if they repent and believe in Jesus Christ.

    1. 09:20 How you know you’re in the Lord’s church

      • Anybody can go to church. It isn’t the location, denomination, sacraments, or liturgy. You know you are in the Lord’s church when you feel convicted by God’s word and sing because you have heard it and are responding. In the Lord’s house conviction is a constant; where people only pretend, conviction is subbed out for complacency and comfort.

  6. 10:00 God takes us to school in Judges

    • Judges is vital because the Israelites, like us, were surrounded by wickedness. God wants his people to see what happens when they fail to follow his commands. The lesson: the devil is real, he deceived Eve in the garden, and if you don’t keep watch he will deceive you. Obey me and there’s peace; tolerate wickedness and there’s pain.
    • For the believer today the message hasn’t changed: a lifetime of picking up your cross, following Jesus, and denying yourself is better than a single day apart from God in hell. Jesus came with that message: “I know you think you want that, but what you really want is what I have.”

  7. 10:57 Judges 4: evil again, and Jabin’s oppression

    Scripture: Judges 4 Judges 4:1–3

    • Josh picks up in chapter 4: again the Israelites did evil in the eyes of the Lord (“Get used to it”), now that Ehud was dead. The Lord sold them to Jabin, king of Canaan, whose commander Sisera had 900 iron chariots and cruelly oppressed Israel for 20 years until they cried to the Lord for help.
    • God raises up Jabin to discipline Israel just as they come off 80 years of peace, the longest period of peace recorded in Judges.

  8. 11:34 Matthew 12: the empty house

    Scripture: Matthew 12 Matthew 12:43–45

    • Josh was blown away by this in his quiet time in Matthew 12, where Jesus gives a spiritual explanation of what was happening with Israel through a parable: an evil spirit leaves a person, finds no rest, returns to find the house unoccupied, swept clean, and put in order, and brings seven spirits more wicked than itself. The final condition is worse than the first; that is how it will be with this wicked generation.
    • This is what morality and religion look like without a relationship with Jesus Christ. When our sinful nature isn’t dealt with through the miracle of repentance and trust in Christ, removing sin just makes room for more sin. An empty heart is open to all kinds of evil, which is why God said through Ezekiel that we need a new heart; the Lord must take up residence in our hearts or something else will.
    • Nothing you do apart from Christ to clean up your heart actually matters. External reform and polishing without inward renewal only makes the soul more vulnerable to evil.

  9. 12:58 Judges is a hopeful book

    Scripture: Psalm 51:10

    • Praise God, Judges is a hopeful book, even if it doesn’t look like it on the surface: it teaches that human failure will never stop God’s plans.
    • The historian Josephus says Jabin had an infantry of 300,000, a cavalry of 10,000, and over 3,000 chariots; he was locked and loaded. Israel had no army; they’d be lucky to gather a few farmers on a Saturday with sticks and ox goads. But as last week showed, God doesn’t need an army, just a few good men, or in today’s text, a couple of women.
    • Judges reminds every believer that God hears the cries of his people. Even when we sin, God is still merciful; when you cry out, he will hear, act, and deliver you in his time and for his purpose. So we need to cry out like David, not because of our circumstances, but because something needs to happen in here: “Create in me a pure heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me.”

  10. 14:22 Deborah, prophet and judge

    Scripture: Judges 4:4–5 Isaiah 3:12

    • Look at how God responds to the cries of his people: Deborah, a prophet, the wife of Lapidoth, was leading Israel and held court under the Palm of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel, where the Israelites came to have their disputes decided.
    • Her name means honeybee: she will sting the enemy but is a sweet treat for the people of God. God raising up Deborah was an act of grace but also a slap in the face, because the men were passive. Josh doesn’t want to hear a passive man’s opinion on complementarian views. Israel was a male-dominated society that wanted strong male leadership, and he points to Isaiah 3: “Youths oppress my people, women rule over them.” Putting a woman judge over them was God treating them like little children, which is how they were acting.

    1. 15:28 Prophetesses before and after

      • A prophet speaks for the Lord, sharing the message the Lord gives with the people. Deborah wasn’t the first prophetess or the last: Moses’ sister Miriam, Huldah in the time of King Josiah, Noadiah during the rebuilding of the walls under Nehemiah and Ezra, and in the New Testament Anna and Philip’s four virgin daughters.
      • Deborah had great influence and authority, but thought of herself as the mother of Israel, viewing the Jews as her rebellious children whom she wanted to guide and build up.

  11. 16:22 “The wife of Lapidoth”

    Scripture: Judges 4:4 Ephesians 5

    • Josh loves that verse 4 calls Deborah the wife of Lapidoth. She is the celebrity of the story, and many modern Christians would rewrite it as “Lapidoth, Deborah’s husband,” but it literally reads that Deborah is a woman of Lapidoth. Feminists everywhere are convulsing, but Josh calls it healthy and biblical: she was proud to be her husband’s wife and didn’t need to rise above her position in the home.
    • In Ephesians 5 husbands are commanded to love their wives as Christ loved the church, and wives to respect their husbands. Deborah is extremely talented, smart, and well recognized, but doesn’t let her status in the community confuse her status in the home.
    • Some women are far more gifted than their husbands (“Don’t look at him right now. It’ll make it awkward.”). You may find great success in your career, but don’t let it distract you from your duties in the home; you are called to love and respect your husband and give yourself to the home’s needs and priorities.
    • Some try to use Deborah to advance a feminist agenda, but Josh says they don’t have a leg to stand on. Deborah was gifted by God, as women can be just like men, and being gifted, she still maintained respect for her husband.

  12. 18:37 God’s battle plan at Mount Tabor

    Scripture: Judges 4:6 Judges 4:7

    • In verse 6 Deborah does what prophets do: she sends for Barak and gives him God’s command to take 10,000 men of Naphtali and Zebulun up to Mount Tabor. It feels like Joshua again: here’s your leader, where you’ll go, and what you’ll do. In verse 7 God promises to lure Sisera, his chariots, and troops to the Kishon River and give him into Barak’s hands. Just follow God’s perfect battle plan and he will give you victory.
    • Josh shows a picture of Mount Tabor and asks the church to think like a general: one side arrives first with the high ground but only ox goads; the other has iron chariots but the low ground. “Have you not seen Star Wars? Don’t try it, Anakin. I have the high ground.” Any decent general knows this battle is a cakewalk.

  13. 20:07 Barak: “If you go with me, I will go”

    Scripture: Judges 4:8

    • Look at this brave man’s response: “If you go with me, I will go; but if you don’t go with me, I won’t go.” Talk about a man’s man; put that up against the Independence Day speech.
    • Church pop quiz: when God says go, what do you say? Abraham taught this lesson: if God says go, go, even if you don’t know where. But before we are too hard on Barak, he isn’t the first to hesitate at God’s mission. Josh is one, and so were Moses, Gideon, and Jeremiah. We all know we should obey God’s commands no matter how we feel, but we don’t always do it.

    1. 21:21 Weak man, or humble?

      Scripture: Hebrews 11

      • Many want to punch down on Barak as a weak man. Josh struggles to, because he is listed in Hebrews 11 for a reason. Is his response unbelief or humility? He didn’t say God got the wrong guy; he is willing to go, just not without Deborah. Maybe he was afraid, or maybe he just didn’t want to go anywhere the voice of the Lord was not. His request doesn’t seem to bother Deborah at all.

  14. 22:07 The honor will go to a woman

    Scripture: Judges 4:9 Hebrews 11

    • It does come with a consequence. Deborah, so nice about it, says she will certainly go, but because of the course he is taking, the honor will not be his; the Lord will deliver Sisera into the hands of a woman.
    • Barak is famous for having faith. This isn’t his greatest moment or what he leads with in his testimony, but praise God he is faithful when we are not, and that Hebrews 11 is full of people who stumbled and got up in the name of the Lord.
    • Christians punching down on others doesn’t help. We should follow Deborah’s example: “Let’s go together.” God could have done it all through Barak, but Barak wasn’t willing, so the glory goes to a woman, and as we’ll see, not to Deborah but to another woman God enlists.

  15. 23:24 If you’re unwilling, God will find someone else

    • Josh pauses on a principle: if God intends to do something and you are unwilling, he will find someone else.

    1. 23:35 The perfect guy who wasn’t willing

      • Before ministry, around 2006 or 2007, Josh lived with his dad, who had just planted the church in Anthem School, while going to GCC. He was a punk: bitter, rebellious, angry, a womanizer, addicted to nicotine and pornography. He knew that parable from Jesus all too well; he knew how to clean things up and look acceptable, but he had never trusted Jesus or experienced the miracle of repentance.
      • He was opinionated about everything, especially his dad’s hiring choices in youth ministry. His dad always fired back with a line he’d stored up: “Well, I know the perfect guy for the job, but he’s not willing.” He meant Josh, who answered with a word of eternal torment followed by no.
      • Josh should have said yes. That summer, as an intern taking students to CIY in Colorado, on day three his heart was opened by the Lord to ministry through prayer. He came home with a massive calling, ready to love people and baptize them, and the job was gone. For a while he served that ministry as a volunteer when he knew the Lord wanted him to lead.

    2. 26:14 Don’t ignore a need in God’s work

      Scripture: Judges 5

      • It is dangerous for a child of God to see what God is doing, see a need in his church, and not offer a helping hand. In Deborah’s song in chapter 5 she calls out entire tribes, including Dan, who didn’t rush in to help but sat by the boats on the shore to make a quick escape in case things went south.
      • Watching someone else do the job Josh was convinced God had called him to was so frustrating, but he didn’t need to learn the lesson twice. When the elders approached him in 2020 and said they thought he was God’s next to lead this church, he said, “I’m in. Where do I sign?”

    3. 26:58 Saying no to the blessing

      Scripture: Isaiah 6:8

      • Adopted children of God, learn from his arrogance, pride, and unwillingness. When God wants to enlist you, you may not feel like it, think the timing is wrong, want to make the sacrifices, or think you have the gifts. But God’s work will be done, if not by you then by someone else.
      • And you don’t just say no to the work; you say no to the blessing that comes to those faithful to do it. Josh wanted to baptize students in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, to see genuine heart change rather than faking it till you make it, to watch someone be buried with Christ in the water and rise to new life, born again, overcome by the Spirit. All those blessings were someone else’s, and all he could do was watch and repent: “I’m so sorry I wasn’t ready when you needed me… I’m ready when you need me.”
      • Know the difference between Moses, “Here I am, Lord, but can you send somebody else?”, and Isaiah: “Here I am, Lord. Send me.”

  16. 29:02 Barak goes up, and Deborah with him

    Scripture: Judges 4:10 Hebrews 11

    • Barak summoned Zebulun and Naphtali, 10,000 men went up under his command, and Deborah went with him.
    • If it bothers you that Hebrews 11 mentions Barak and not Deborah, Josh offers three thoughts: don’t be offended so easily; the author of Hebrews is embracing brevity and doesn’t have time to list every figure of faith; and he likely uses Barak knowing anyone familiar with the story already knows Deborah’s great faith.

    1. 29:39 Behind every good man is a great woman

      • Josh’s wife is a gem, like the rubies Solomon talks about, with amazing faith, and God has used her. Marriage is sanctifying; he didn’t realize how much of himself he didn’t like until she pointed it out.
      • We can’t mention a man without considering a woman: John the Baptist and Elizabeth, the sons of thunder and Salome, Timothy and Eunice and Lois, and Jesus and the faith of his mother Mary. Good men don’t exist unless good women make them.

  17. 30:43 Heber the Kenite, double agent

    Scripture: Judges 4:11–13

    • Verse 11 introduces Heber the Kenite, who had left the other Kenites and pitched his tent near Kedesh; when Sisera heard Barak had gone up Mount Tabor, he summoned all his men and 900 iron chariots to the Kishon River. Josh pictures the Return of the Jedi admiral: “It’s a trap!”, then laughs about the GIF debate.
    • Heber’s family descended through Jethro, the priest of Midian and Moses’ father-in-law; Josh pictures the man singing by the campfire in Prince of Egypt. Why Heber separated from God’s people and sided with Jabin and Sisera, Josh can only speculate: climate, business. He was friendly to both sides, the double agent of Judges, so Sisera had no reason to question his report.
    • But the point isn’t Heber or even Sisera. God was working all along; it was time to deliver his people, and he had an inside man.

  18. 32:37 The Lord routs Sisera

    Scripture: Judges 4:14–16 Judges 5

    • Deborah tells Barak, “Go! This is the day the Lord has given Sisera into your hands.” Barak comes down Mount Tabor with 10,000 men, the Lord routs Sisera and his chariots, Sisera flees on foot, and not a man of his army is left.
    • Sisera was overconfident behind his 900 chariots, but no one expected what the Lord had in store. He sent rain and flooded the river, turning the battlefield into a mud pit, which Deborah mentions in her poem in chapter 5, and he sent confusion into the enemy’s minds.
    • The cherry on top: it was the dry season. Baal, the Canaanite god of fertility and storms from last week, was nowhere. Josh hears Deborah and Barak screaming from the summit, “Is your god sleepy today?” Sisera’s chariots are sinking in the mud; time to run.

  19. 34:10 Into Jael’s tent

    Scripture: Judges 4:17 Judges 4:18 Judges 4:19 Judges 4:20

    • Sisera flees to the tent of Jael, wife of Heber the double agent, since Heber’s family had an alliance with Jabin. In verse 18 Jael goes out to meet him: “Come, my lord, come right in. Don’t be afraid.” She covers him with a blanket, and he thinks he has survived. In that culture no one but her husband would enter a wife’s tent, but she rolls out the red carpet, or does she?
    • He asks for water and she gives him milk. Josh jokes about craving lukewarm, fermented, chunky goat’s milk after a hot day. Church pop quiz: what does a baby do after milk? Sleep; milk has tryptophan, which the body uses to produce melatonin.
    • Before he sleeps, Sisera tells her to stand in the doorway and say no if anyone asks whether a man is inside. That is when Josh thinks a light bulb goes off for Jael: married to a double agent, she knows the game. Sisera fleeing means Israel won and the Canaanites lost power; protecting him would put her in trouble, and his pursuers won’t stop. Sisera falls asleep thinking he is in good hands, not knowing Deborah said God promised a woman would take his life.

  20. 36:51 The tent peg

    Scripture: Judges 4:21

    • Verse 21: Jael picks up a tent peg and a hammer, goes quietly to him while he lies fast asleep, and drives the peg through his temple into the ground, and he dies. Josh wonders how Hollywood hasn’t run with this yet; it is a Netflix special waiting to happen.

    1. 37:25 Was Jael a murderer?

      Scripture: Judges 5:24 Judges 5:30

      • Someone is always offended, morally outraged that Jael showed hospitality, broke her promise to guard the door, and killed a defenseless man in cold blood. Josh says calm down. First, no one in that day would walk into another man’s wife’s tent, so she had every reason to believe she was in danger and that Sisera might wake and want his way with her.
      • And Deborah wrote a poem about her: “Most blessed of women be Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, most blessed of tent-dwelling women.” Some are screaming injustice while Deborah is singing her praise, so we should wrestle with it.
      • You are a Christian who has sat under the teaching of Jesus and the apostles; Jael has not. Remember the 20 years Jabin and Sisera brutally mistreated God’s people. If Sisera had recovered and the Canaanites won, hundreds of Jewish girls would have been captured and raped; Deborah’s poem speaks of dividing the spoils, a woman or two for each man. Jael not only helped deliver Israel but protected those women from unspeakable brutality.
      • Heber and Jael played both sides for decades, and suddenly the house of Heber stood next to the people of God. The Lord really can use anyone.

  21. 39:56 God subdues Jabin

    Scripture: Judges 4:22–24

    • Barak arrives in pursuit, and Jael shows him the man he is looking for, lying dead with the tent peg through his temple. That day God subdued Jabin, and Israel’s hand pressed harder and harder against him until they destroyed him.

  22. 40:20 Lessons from the pursuit

    • As we watch Barak pursue Sisera, believers have a few lessons to learn.

    1. 40:26 Keep fighting your sin

      • They had won a decisive victory on the hill, and many believers today would be content to watch the enemy run off and hide in a hole. Wrong. Many Christians rely on Christ’s victory on the hill to atone for their sins but fail to keep fighting their sin. “Jesus saved me from my sin.” Amen, he did. “But why do you keep sinning?”
      • Take a hammer, take a peg, and finish it off. Don’t be content watching the enemy flee and cower. Chase it down and be sure it never gets up again.

    2. 41:16 Take to the streets

      • Many Christians are also content knowing the enemy is dead for themselves, with no intention of telling anyone. The Spirit of the Lord doesn’t produce a private, shy Christian; he grows a public and bold one.
      • Josh’s lesson from Jael: take to the streets, believer. Stop being shy. Pray for that random person in the supermarket, show them Christ crucified, and love on them.

  23. 41:58 From the tent to the empty tomb

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:5 John 19:30

    • “But he was pierced for our transgressions, he was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was on him, and by his wounds we are healed.” Josh asks the church to travel with him from the tent in the Valley of Jezreel to the empty tomb in Jerusalem.
    • Some are living under the oppression of a king called sin, an unholy trinity selling enslavement packaged as freedom. Now the deception is clear and you want to be free, but feel powerless. Like Jael, Josh wants to show you where your powerful enemy is: “Come, look.” That is your sin lying dead, your condemnation with a tent peg driven through its skull.
    • Jesus said it in a word, tetelestai: it is finished. The sins you were enslaved to can be forgiven; run to God in the name of Jesus Christ and trust him alone. Believer, your sin isn’t wandering in the desert, waiting to return when you least expect it. Whatever broke your heart or controlled your life is no more; your sins aren’t wounded or napping, they are gone.
    • When Jesus said tetelestai, he was like a judge handing down a decision for your soul: not guilty. There is no court of appeals; no one can overturn it or accuse you. It’s done.

    1. 43:42 Who then can condemn?

      Scripture: Romans 8:33–39

      • Josh reads Paul’s questions: who will bring any charge against those God has chosen, and who can condemn, when Christ Jesus died, was raised, and intercedes for us? Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? In all these things we are more than conquerors, and nothing in all creation can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.
      • Believer, take heart. Christ has done it. Your sin is dead. You are free.

  24. 44:42 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks Jesus for putting down glory, coming in humility, and standing where he deserved to be; he was under the power of sin, and Jesus took it on himself. The cross reminds him of Christ’s great love and of his call to deny himself, take up his cross, and follow, whatever it means.
    • He prays for anyone trying to fill their lives with things that promise joy, meaning, and purpose: he tried them all, and none came close to what Christ gives, life eternal through faith in Jesus. He thanks God for a church that declares what is true in a culture that demands it shut up.
    • He confesses the years he didn’t give God his life and was afraid to go at it alone with him, and now says, “It’s just me and you. Me and you against the world. Use me however you want, whenever you want, whatever the cost.”
