Overview

  1. 00:05 A red alert

    Scripture: Isaiah 7

    • Imagine all our phones going off with a breaking alert: Amber for an abducted child, Silver for a missing elderly person, Blue for an officer down. Call an invasion a red alert. Imagine learning that 49 states have allied with China to invade Arizona, conquer it, and divide the spoils. The preppers say, “I’ve been preparing for this my whole life,” but most people would be frightened at Americans betraying fellow citizens for gold and a square of dirt.
    • That is basically what happened in Isaiah 7: the northern ten tribes of Israel allied with an enemy to invade Judah, and the people were so gripped with fear they became like shaken trees. God sent Isaiah to help them overcome their fears by putting their faith in God.

  2. 01:58 Unbelief finds reasons to doubt; faith bows in adoration

    • Looking at the first half of the chapter, the golden prophecy, Josh will address fear, faith, fellowship, failure, and fulfillment. It’s not just the faithful who can teach us; the faithless can teach too.
    • The text has been the subject of endless controversy, but the Holy Spirit makes it clear to those ready to receive his testimony. Josh will say it again and again: unbelief will always find a reason to doubt; faith just bows in adoration.
    • His goal for the series isn’t to tackle every objection or skeptic but to build up believers. He wants to see lives changed, which changes marriages, homes, futures, and a church under the headship of the Lord.

  3. 03:33 Don’t neglect prophecy

    • Many Christians don’t give enough time to the prophetic word. A dear brother in the church was concerned Josh had discouraged people from studying prophecy; they talked it through in unity, and Josh goes on record: neglecting prophecy neglects a massive portion of Scripture, robs our souls, and discredits the one who gave his word for our edification and comfort.
    • Where Christians go wrong is tunnel vision on events. Prophecy is valuable because it occupies our minds with a person, the Lord Jesus Christ, who came once to suffer and is coming again to reign, and Isaiah foretells both advents. Prophecy isn’t fortune-telling; it’s a peek into the mind of God at a moment in time.
    • With the monarchy and priesthood failing, God ministered through the prophets, whose job had two bullet points: lay before the people the glories to come through the promised Messiah, and prepare them for his triumphant entry by leading them to repentance from idols to the one true living God. The God who split the sea has been working a master plan all along; pharaohs and kings can’t stop him.

  4. 05:34 Holiday homework: read Isaiah

    • Josh’s hefty holiday homework: read all 66 chapters of Isaiah before Christmas morning. Thirty minutes a day will knock it out; for extra credit, text or email him for a good commentary.
    • Isaiah is like a miniature Bible: 66 books and 66 chapters; the Old Testament’s 39 books and Isaiah’s first 39 chapters on Israel’s past struggles and the promised Messiah; the New Testament’s 27 books and Isaiah’s last 27 chapters on future deliverance. Chapters and verses were added later, but Josh still thinks it’s cool. The theme of Isaiah is the theme of all Scripture: God’s salvation revealed in his blessed Son.

  5. 06:55 Jesus quotes Isaiah: “Today this scripture is fulfilled”

    Scripture: Luke 4:16–21 Isaiah 61 Revelation 19:10 Isaiah 11:2

    • Isaiah is the most quoted prophet in the New Testament, 21 times by first-century authors. The most mind-blowing is Jesus in Luke 4: in the Nazareth synagogue he unrolled the scroll of Isaiah and read, “The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor,” then sat down and said, “Today this scripture is fulfilled in your hearing.”
    • Isaiah 61 was written around 680 BC, and some 713 years later Jesus, known in his hometown as a carpenter’s son, says, “I’m the fulfillment.” If that’s not Christmas card material, Josh doesn’t know what is. The testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy; it all points to Jesus. Isaiah 11 says the Spirit of the Lord will rest on him, the Spirit of wisdom, understanding, counsel, might, knowledge, and the fear of the Lord.

  6. 08:48 The messianic prophet

    Scripture: Isaiah 7 Isaiah 9 Isaiah 53

    • Isaiah is called the messianic prophet because so much is about Jesus: chapter 7 has the Immanuel prophecy, chapter 9 the birth and names of Christ, chapter 53 substitutionary atonement. His name means “salvation of God,” and salvation appears 31 times. Isaiah is the only book to call Satan Lucifer and one of two Old Testament books that describe Satan’s rebellion.
    • Some theories say Isaiah had two or even three authors. Josh doesn’t hold to that and doesn’t care to debate it: Jesus confirms Isaiah’s legitimacy in Luke 4, and if it’s good enough for him, it’s good enough for Josh. Unbelief always finds ways to doubt; faith leads to reverence and adoration.

  7. 10:13 Ahaz and his family tree

    Scripture: Isaiah 7:1 2 Kings 15 2 Kings 16 Exodus 20:3

    • Chapter 7 begins: when Ahaz son of Jotham, the son of Uzziah, was king of Judah, King Rezin of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah king of Israel marched up to fight against Jerusalem but could not overpower it. Judah is in big trouble.
    • Ahaz came from a long line of leaders: his father Jotham a mighty king, one grandfather a high priest, the other an influential king. But this dynasty had a history of bending the rules; his grandfather Uzziah offered incense in the temple, which only priests could do, and Ahaz knew it.
    • Ahaz took the throne at 20 and did not do what was right in the eyes of the Lord; you can read about his reign in 2 Kings 15 and 16. God said, “You shall have no other gods before me,” and Ahaz had gods all over the place. He even burned his children alive in honor of the pagan god Molech. He’s a monster.

    1. 11:56 What culture are you setting at home?

      • Josh isn’t excusing Ahaz, but look at his family tree: if Grandpa and Daddy can bend the rules and don’t take this seriously, why would I? So moms, dads, grandparents, aunts, uncles: what example are you setting? If you let in a little, expect your children to let in more. Hold yourself to the highest standard and let your kids watch.
      • Ahaz is responsible for himself, but the culture of his home was to bend the rules, find a way around them, not bow to them. What’s the culture in your home? Josh is continually surprised by how many Christians don’t even know God’s commands, let alone obey them. Bringing your kid to church once a week won’t turn your family around; only diving into God’s life-giving words and letting your family watch them reshape you will.

    2. 13:29 “She’s watched me fight addiction with a Bible in my hand”

      • On Josh’s birthday a couple of weeks ago, his 18-year-old daughter Madison told him she was proud of him, and why: she had been blessed to watch him fight addiction with a Bible in his hand, watched his faith grow, and seen his life change. He was shocked, because he beats himself up all the time, and God sent his daughter that day to hold him up.
      • He is nothing special; he is up here only because God chose him. Don’t give man credit for God’s craftsmanship. When someone says a pastor is the best thing that ever happened to the church, Josh says, “Hold up. He did that, not this guy.” Not I, but Christ in me. You need more of Christ in you: read of him, memorize what he teaches, obey him, and most of all never stop praying.
      • If Josh could teach his kids one lesson, on his deathbed, it would be: before you try to figure everything out, pray. Afraid, anxious, in trouble, in a trial: pray. Ask God for wisdom, and he will give you peace, strength, comfort, and lead you down the right path.

  8. 15:51 Shaken like trees in the wind

    Scripture: Isaiah 7:2 Isaiah 7:3–6

    • Verse 2: when the house of David heard that Aram had allied with Ephraim, the hearts of Ahaz and his people were shaken as trees of the forest are shaken by the wind. The Hebrew word means to quiver or tremble. The first invasion failed, and they are coming again in greater numbers. Ahaz is called “the house of David” because his response could determine the future of David’s line.
    • The Lord sent Isaiah with his son Shear-Jashub, meaning “a remnant shall return,” to meet Ahaz at the aqueduct of the upper pool. Just because you aren’t looking for the Lord doesn’t mean he can’t come find you.

  9. 16:58 God’s four-point sermon: don’t overreact

    • The message: be careful, keep calm, don’t be afraid, do not lose heart because of these two smoldering stubs of firewood, though Aram, Ephraim, and Remaliah’s son plot to tear Judah apart and set up their own king. God’s point is simple: don’t overreact. Calm down. Breathe.
    • That was a problem for Ahaz, who thought he needed to act fast: why wait on the Lord when he could play clever politician? The temptation was clear: salvation by faith or by works, trusting God to make a way or making a way himself.

    1. 17:59 We have a big Ahaz problem

      • Don’t think no one acts like Ahaz anymore. This is the problem in this country: so-called Christians raised in the church but never raised in Christ, row after row of baby believers raised into habits, not holiness. The key to maturity isn’t more of your solutions, ideas, and traditions, which got you here in the first place; it is filling your heart and mind with his solutions, promises, and ideas.
      • A few threats and a little uncertainty, and we start wheeling and dealing to make a way for ourselves.

  10. 19:10 “If you do not stand firm in your faith, you will not stand at all”

    Scripture: Isaiah 7:7–9

    • Yet this is what the Sovereign Lord says: it will not take place. The head of Aram is Damascus, and the head of Damascus is only Rezin; within 65 years Ephraim will be too shattered to be a people. God is smack-talking these earthly kings. “If you do not stand firm in your faith, you will not stand at all.”
    • God assured Ahaz the schemes would not happen: they are scorched timbers; sit and watch them burn out. “I got this. Don’t overreact.” And that’s exactly what happened: within two years both kings were dead, and within 65 years the northern ten tribes were abolished. God was saying, “Trust me, Ahaz. Put your life in my hands.”

  11. 20:29 Running to another king

    Scripture: 2 Kings 16:8

    • Sadly, Ahaz took the silver and gold from the temple of the Lord and the royal treasuries and sent it as a gift to the king of Assyria. The eternal King offered comfort, peace, and deliverance, and Ahaz ran to an earthly king.
    • Josh can relate more than he cares to admit: he is guilty of finding comfort in the wrong things, and this week looking at Ahaz felt like looking in a mirror. At least Ahaz was open about it. Many Christians look and sound Christian, but the silver and gold meant for the house of the Lord flows into building their own palace. A lot of us have some Ahaz in us that needs to be crucified.

    1. 21:54 Substitutes: powerful people and politics

      Scripture: Psalm 118:8–9

      • Rejecting God’s authority, unbelief drives people to substitutes: religious trinkets, false gods, behavior-modification faith, works, or powerful men. Don’t look to the government or its leaders to meet your needs; look to the Lord. Psalm 118: it is better to take refuge in the Lord than to trust in humans or in princes.
      • Josh was somewhat embarrassed to be a Christian this year as many were so loud about the election, and now that it went their way, where are they? It’s as if they attended church to make sure it talked about what they wanted. “See you in four years.” There’s work to do, and the church needs you on mission. What gets most of your time and energy, the gospel or your politics?

    2. 23:09 Money and career

      Scripture: 1 Timothy 6:17

      • For others it’s money. Churches burn out staff and families with services every day of the week because people stopped going to church to work on Sundays. Did anyone think to find another job, or, as Josh taught his daughter at 16, tell your job you can’t work Sundays because that’s the Lord’s day?
      • Large churches stuff the room with people who abandon the gathering for their careers, let you out on time for the football game, and push a baptism to next week so you keep your idols: “You don’t need to be sanctified, just justified, and then give your tithe.” Said out loud it sounds icky, but that’s the American church.
      • Money won’t solve your problems, and neither will a career advance. Money can buy medicine but not health, a house but not a home, entertainment but not happiness, a bed but not sleep, the good life in Anthem but not eternal life. That’s why Paul told Timothy to command the rich not to be arrogant or hope in uncertain wealth, but to hope in God, who richly provides everything for our enjoyment.

    3. 25:20 Weapons and backup plans

      Scripture: Psalm 20:7

      • Maybe your security is on your belt buckle or in your loaded gun safe, the backup plan and bunker that keep you from shaking when things hit the fan. Nothing wrong with being in the NRA, but if your trust is in the .223 and not the Trinity, you have real problems. Psalm 20: some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we trust in the name of the Lord our God.
      • Look at your life: is an Ahaz alternative creeping up in you? Is anything or anyone more important to you than the Lord?

  12. 26:16 Trust or bust

    Scripture: John 16:33

    • Like Ahaz, God presents you with a choice: stand firm in your faith or not at all. Trust or bust. Scripture is clear we will have trials as believers, and Jesus encourages us not to lose heart, just as God encouraged Ahaz: “Take heart, I have overcome the world.” But there are as many problems, if not more, for those wandering from God.
    • To those who haven’t put their faith in Jesus: you stand where Ahaz stood. What you do with Jesus determines your destiny: faith in him leads to heaven; reject Christ and it leads to hell. It’s the simplest, most honest gospel there is. Stop running to another king this Christmas; run to the King of kings and Lord of lords.

  13. 27:39 “Ask the Lord your God for a sign”

    Scripture: Isaiah 7:10–13

    • Josh loves this part: the Lord told Ahaz, “Ask the Lord your God for a sign, whether in the deepest depths or in the highest heights.” Ahaz said, “I will not ask; I will not put the Lord to the test.” Now the law matters to him and he’s quoting Scripture. Isaiah answered, “Is it not enough to try the patience of humans? Will you try the patience of my God also?”
    • Josh has never understood people who challenge God’s mercy, patience, and kindness. Ask hard questions, fine, but notice God is presenting himself to an idolatrous child murderer who robbed the temple to pay pagan kings, giving this arrogant young king an opportunity to repent: “Ask for a sign, no matter what, and I will prove myself.”
    • What would you ask for? To see the sun go off for a second? Eight feet of snow and a white Christmas in Arizona? Your mother-in-law to disappear for a little bit? Calm down, people. God is not out to destroy you; he wants to rescue even the worst of us. If he is reaching out, don’t push him away; grab his hand, let him lift you out, and never look back.

  14. 30:19 Ahaz’s mind was made up

    Scripture: Deuteronomy 6:16

    • Unplanned: two weeks ago the temptations series ended with Jesus saying, “Do not put the Lord your God to the test.” Ahaz quotes Deuteronomy just like Jesus did and sounds religious, but from Isaiah’s response he isn’t concerned about the law; he is worried about looking like a fool and about his own glory.
    • Evidence cannot create faith, only support it. Ahaz wouldn’t have been testing the Lord, because God offered it. The real problem is that his mind was made up: his trust was already in the king of Assyria. “It’s a done deal, God; I’m all in in a different direction.” How many today make the same mistake, rejecting God’s promise of salvation to trust someone else, themselves, or nothing at all?

  15. 31:50 The Immanuel sign

    Scripture: Isaiah 7:14 Matthew 1:22–23

    • With the house of David running to Assyria, the stage is set for the Immanuel prophecy. A sign was offered and rejected, so God in his sovereignty gives one: “The Lord himself will give you a sign: the virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and will call him Immanuel.”
    • Smarter people have called Isaiah 7 the hardest passage in all of God’s word. It didn’t seem hard to Josh until he read what others said and got more and more confused; with the commentaries he’s confused, but reading the word with the Holy Spirit he’s amazed.
    • Some say it was fulfilled months after Isaiah gave it, but Matthew, 700 years later, quotes it about Christ’s birth: “All this took place to fulfill what the Lord had said through the prophet: the virgin will conceive… and they will call him Immanuel, which means God with us.”

    1. 33:21 Read the text for yourself

      Scripture: Isaiah 7:14–16

      • Others, too smart for their own good, divide verses 14–16 and say 14–15 are exclusively Christ and 16 is Isaiah’s son, two signs. Josh isn’t a theologian, but with great passion he advises: read the text yourself before you read others’ opinions; don’t even take his word for it.
      • Reading verses 14–16 together, it seems obvious the same child is in all three: before the boy knows enough to reject the wrong and choose the right (“sounds kind of like temptation to me”), the land of the two kings you dread will be laid waste, and they were.
      • The most popular view sees an immediate fulfillment for Judah and a future fulfillment in Jesus, and partial fulfillment does occur in prophecy, so Josh appreciates that. But with no training or commentary we can tell the purpose of this text is to set our eyes on Jesus Christ of Nazareth. A virgin conceiving and bearing a son is miraculous; it must be a God thing.

    2. 35:20 Virgin or young woman?

      • Some average Bible scholar might say the Hebrew word also means a young woman, as if he’d put a full-court press on Christians. But the Septuagint, 70 Jewish scholars translating from Hebrew to Greek, all chose a Greek word that can only mean virgin.

  16. 36:00 Immanuel: God with us

    • Josh loves talking about the source of our joy this Christmas: Immanuel. Today names follow trends like kitchen tile; Josh named his son Nolan after Nolan Ryan because it sounded good with the family’s last name, and later learned Nolan Ryan was a Christian, so now they pretend they knew all along.
    • Ancient names had deep meaning: Cain means “acquired” (“guess this is the one we got”), Seth “appointed,” Noah “rest” or “comfort,” Ishmael “God will hear,” Isaac “laughter,” and Jacob “may God protect” but also “overreach,” as if his parents weren’t sure how he’d turn out.
    • Mary didn’t need to Google it; God sent an angel so there would be no confusion. She called her son Immanuel, God with us. If his name doesn’t flood your life with peace, joy, and comfort this season, nothing will. What would you give to join the angels singing on high, “Go tell it on the mountain,” “Hark the herald angels sing, glory to the newborn King”?

    1. 38:35 You are not alone

      • He came down to our level. The uncaused cause of all things caused his own birth; the Everlasting took on flesh; the Almighty humbled himself; the one who made time stepped into it. He had that name as a boy and still has it as King of heaven.
      • Believer, you are not alone. You have no hurt he hasn’t felt, no betrayal he hasn’t experienced. God is with you, will protect you, and will finish what he started in you. Let “Immanuel” be the cry of our hearts this season.

  17. 39:26 Born again

    Scripture: 2 Peter 3:9

    • The virgin birth isn’t the only unusual birth in God’s plan. Jesus came to us through the virgin birth, and Christ brings you back to God by a new birth. God wants everyone born again; Christ died for the whole world, not a select few. Not everyone will trust him, but God desires that all be saved.
    • Trust in Immanuel, in Jesus. The Lord is not slow in keeping his promise as some understand slowness; he is patient, not wanting anyone to perish but everyone to come to repentance. Sinner, wait no more; don’t test his patience another moment. Call on the name of Jesus, receive new birth, and celebrate him forever.

  18. 40:32 Celebrate your Savior’s birth

    Scripture: Ecclesiastes 9:7

    • Josh wishes the church a record-book Christmas: it’s time to throw a feast in Jesus’ name. May homes radiate love, joy, and comfort; give and receive like never before. Solomon says, “Go, eat your food with gladness, and drink your wine with a joyful heart, for God has already approved what you do.” Let Immanuel set up his throne in your heart, and do everything for the glory of God. Merry Christmas.

  19. 41:30 Prayer at the table

    • As the church comes to the table holding the bread and the juice, Josh prays in humility: this season is all about the Lord. He thanks him for coming down to our level, dying in our place, and teaching us to live as a light to others, and prays that any hearts like Ahaz in the room would stop running to other kings and come home.
