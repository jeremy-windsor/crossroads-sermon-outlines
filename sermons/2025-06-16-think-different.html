Overview

  1. 00:08 Honoring Christian fathers

    • Josh welcomes guests and wishes a happy Father’s Day to the men who don’t just fill a spot on the couch but see their role as fathers as part of their Christian duty. May the dedicated Christian fathers go down as heroes, remembered for keeping the faith and acting like men.
    • Don’t let the world reduce a father’s role to provider. Josh honors men who protect the hearts entrusted to them, joyfully embrace their role as priests in their homes, and lift high the name of Jesus; they don’t just talk about Jesus, they walk with him while their kids watch. These men deserve a steak and a beer, if you’re into that kind of thing.
    • He asks who loves Jesus because a man sitting here loved Jesus first, and invites those whose man is no longer with us to pray, thanking the Lord for the men who loved Jesus and taught us to love him too.

  2. 01:55 Ephesians 4:17 and a husband’s guesswork

    • The church has been going through Ephesians and lands today in chapter 4, verse 17. Josh asks the men to be honest: have you ever wrongly assumed what is on your wife’s mind? “Honey, I know what you’re thinking.” No, you don’t.
    • Wouldn’t it be cool to know her smallest thoughts and meet needs she has never shared, like a brain push notification: “Ding. Wife is thinking about how you never wash your coffee mug.” Josh would be out scrubbing his mug, timed so she walks in and catches him doing it.

  3. 03:14 The mind of Christ has been made known

    Scripture: Romans 6:4

    • There is a contrast to appreciate. In marriage a man pursues his wife; in Christianity the believer pursues Christ. To pursue your wife you have to ask what is on her mind, but in Christianity the mind of Christ has been made known. We don’t need to ask; we have been informed so we can put off the old self and put on the new.
    • Last week the church first sang “Made New”: you weren’t meant to be tending a grave. Take off your grave clothes, clothe yourself in Christ, and submerge yourself in the truth found in Jesus. We have been called in Christ, so live up to the calling, and the person next to you has been called too, so walk with them in unity.
    • We have been raised from the dead, so we should walk in purity. We don’t enjoy the things Jesus died for; we hate them, repent, and put them down, and if they won’t let go of us, we kill them before they kill us, trying again and again until Jesus helps us win or we find victory in death. Whether 20 or 80, the goal is newness of life: Paul tells the Romans we were buried with him through baptism into death so that, just as Christ was raised, we too may live a new life. That new life is today’s focus: out with the old, in with the new.

  4. 05:02 Verses 17–25: no longer live as the Gentiles do

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:17 Ephesians 4:18–25

    • “So I tell you this, and insist on it in the Lord, that you must no longer live as the Gentiles do, in the futility of their thinking.” The call on a Christian’s life can be summed up in two words: imitate Christ. There are people who know God and people who don’t, and if you are among the redeemed, Paul insists you act like it.
    • Our efforts to imitate Christ start in our minds; Christians just think differently from unsaved people. What most Christians miss is that God won’t make you think differently; you do that on your own.
    • Josh reads on: they are darkened in their understanding and separated from the life of God because of the ignorance in them due to the hardening of their hearts; having lost all sensitivity, they have given themselves over to sensuality. That is not the way of life you learned when you heard about Christ: put off your old self, be made new in the attitude of your minds, put on the new self created to be like God in true righteousness and holiness, and therefore put off falsehood and speak truthfully, for we are all members of one body.

  5. 06:47 Call to mind your greatest sin

    Scripture: Romans 6:23

    • Rather than listing every sin, Josh asks the church to call to mind the greatest sin in their lives. Not big sins versus little sins, since sin is sin and the wages of sin is death, but the one that should get the most attention, and to stay laser-focused on it.
    • Maybe you are a drunk: not waking up in weird places, but the drink is a daily routine that has taken the place of comforter. Treat this like triage: with a broken leg and a gunshot wound, you don’t throw a cast on in the field; you apply pressure to the gunshot wound. A dirty mouth is a problem you will get to, but hone in on the big one.

    1. 08:02 Lost all sensitivity: the desire and how you meet it

      • Paul mentions people who have lost all sensitivity and given themselves over to sensuality to indulge in every kind of impurity: people enslaved to their desires. This person can’t say no to porn. Some 60% of active churchgoers struggle with pornography, which is why Josh talks about it.
      • What is sometimes taught improperly is that the desire itself isn’t inherently bad; it is how you are meeting it that hardens your heart. Your desire is for your wife, but you satisfy it with a stranger; your desire is for your husband, but you satisfy it with a screen. And the desire isn’t only for your satisfaction but for your spouse; sex is more about giving than receiving.
      • The old attitude says, “There’s a desire; I must feed it now.” The new attitude says, “I know this desire and why I have it. I know what God says. I’m to serve my wife with this; it builds my family, our intimacy, and makes the marriage bed holy. Get behind me, Satan. Help me, Jesus. I’ll wait until my wife and I can satisfy it together.”
      • Maybe that isn’t your thing. Maybe you are a glutton, a liar, a cheater, a thief, covetous, a gossip, or just lazy, going on 40 and still playing World of Warcraft in your mom’s basement. Whatever it is, call it to mind and hang on to it.

  6. 10:07 How you think about your sin

    Scripture: Romans 12:2

    • Now that you have it in mind: is it a big deal or not so big a deal? When it came to mind, did you immediately tell yourself it isn’t a big deal, or were you convinced it was wrong? This is the whole point of today: how you think about your sin matters more than what your sin actually is. Scripture constantly reminds us that how we think informs how we live.
    • Josh has the church finish a verse that isn’t on the screen: “Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by…” Everybody knows it. The problem is that most people fail to change their behavior because they never change how they think about it; they lose it up here, so the battle down here never works.
    • Christians don’t sit around inventing new ways to think about things; we want to know what God thinks. It drives Josh crazy when life groups turn into holy huddles of lukewarm Christians saying, “This is what this means to me.” He cares what God meant and what the Spirit meant to install in us when his prophets and apostles wrote it down.
    • When we learned Christ, our entire outlook changed. Salvation begins with repentance, not “Oh, I’m sorry, God,” but a change of mind. That renewed mind adjusts how a Christian views themselves, the world, and every decision.

  7. 12:08 Futile thinking

    Scripture: Romans 1 Romans 1:21–25 John 14:6

    • Paul says the unsaved person is futile in their thinking: empty thinking with no meaning behind it. If they don’t know God, how can they understand the world he created, themselves whom he created, or those around them?
    • Romans 1 explains how this went down; Josh invites the church to read the whole chapter. He reads from verse 21: although they knew God, they neither glorified him as God nor gave thanks to him, but their thinking became futile and their foolish hearts were darkened; claiming to be wise they became fools, and God gave them over to the sinful desires of their hearts; they exchanged the truth about God for a lie. The assumption of all Scripture is that God exists; there are no apologists in Scripture.
    • Futile thinking leads to nothing. They have innovative ways of thinking that sound really good, but without godly thinking they are meandering in the dark, fools who think they are wise. That is what people look like apart from God, and Satan loves to blindfold them from the truth in Jesus.
    • The unsaved man is dead because of spiritual ignorance; the Christian is alive because of what they learned about Jesus. Truth and life are connected, which is why Jesus says, “I am the way, the truth, and the life.” Life flows in after truth flows through: believe God’s truth and you receive God’s life. Unbelievers are in a terrible position: they have given themselves over to sin and can’t even feel it anymore.

  8. 15:02 Addiction: the way out is back the way you came

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:33

    • The lifestyle Paul describes is easily seen in an addict. At 13, Josh and his buddies were out TP’ing their science teacher’s house at 3:00 a.m. when a friend pulled out a can of Copenhagen stolen from his dad and offered a pinch. Kids: bad company corrupts good character, and Josh is living proof; hang around trash and don’t be surprised when you start to smell like it. He took a pinch, got so high he puked his guts out on the street.
    • He never felt that first-time rush again. Every time he used tobacco he felt it less and less, even as he did it more and more.
    • There is only one way out of an addiction alive: back the way you came. You get to the point where you feel nothing, and when you decide to stop, you feel everything. Nobody aims to stop feeling it; you get there because you thought wrong about something, went the wrong direction, and kept going until it got darker and darker.
    • The New Testament pictures sin as darkness and God’s truth as light. When God shines his light into the darkness, people either stop, repent, and stay in the light to live and grow there, or scatter back into the darkness.

  9. 17:15 Holding your sin and your faith at the same time

    • Paul insists we not live like people who don’t know God, yet we do. With that sin still in mind, have you tried to hold it in one hand and your faith in the other?
    • Here is what we do: pick a church with a soft preacher so we never hear about our sin; have zero Bible study time so we don’t encounter it; avoid accountability and godly men who would call things out; dismiss loving correction as “judging me”; skip life groups because close Christian community might bring things into the light.
    • How many Christians pull from Adam’s playbook in the garden and go into hiding instead of facing it head-on? We suppress the truth because we don’t want to be found out. We know we are sinners; we just don’t want others to know, and sometimes we know they know and don’t want to know they know. Our ego, image, and reputation can’t take the hit.
    • The tragedy is that the longer you live like this, the more distance you create between you and God, and the worse it gets. Be careful: there may come a day you don’t even feel it anymore.

    1. 19:21 Conceal, don’t feel

      • Last Josh checked, “conceal, don’t feel” didn’t work out well for Elsa. If you haven’t seen Frozen by now, that’s on you. He doesn’t mean to speak ill of her fictional parents, who died in a shipwreck, but what kind of parents tell their teenage daughter to conceal her emotions? Imagine Jonathan and Martha Kent raising “conceal, don’t feel Clark”: a troubled teen of steel tearing the planet apart.

  10. 20:12 Burying consequences

    • Another thing we do is bury consequences. We hate thinking our sin could affect others and pretend our struggle comes with no consequences. But there is a simple rule: cause and effect.
    • “Josh, I never wanted this life: two kids, two cars, two jobs, two divorces.” Josh knows life is hard and may not have gone as you wanted, but you only create kids one way; your smart bed didn’t force you against your will, and if it did, you might want to lawyer up. We experience consequences and play the victim card, then look up and ask, “Where’s God in all this?” Where is God in the consequences of your actions? Probably waiting for you to own it, accept the consequences, repent, and stop sinning against yourself, your family, and most of all him.
    • When the human heart is alienated from God, it craves sin. God could be right next to you and you couldn’t see him, because your heart is hardened, your understanding darkened, and your senses deadened by sin.

  11. 21:58 Smallville: the less you resist, the less you notice

    • Josh’s family is watching Smallville, about Superman growing up through puberty. He loved it in high school, Nolan loves it, and Superman borrows from the Bible left and right, which gets conversations going. Sometimes Josh tells Nolan to look away, but he has started looking away too, because his thinking has continued to shift: if Nolan shouldn’t see it, he probably shouldn’t either.
    • Things that used to shock you don’t anymore because you have been exposed to so much; shows you would have turned off years ago, you no longer turn off. That is what this darkening looks like: the less you resist it, the less you notice it. This is futile thinking, ignorance of the truth, and hardness of heart, and it leads to sensuality.

  12. 23:24 Sensuality: a soul that can’t bear discipline

    • “Sensuality” refers to total licentiousness, the absence of moral restraint, especially in sexual sin. One commentator called it “a disposition of the soul incapable of bearing the pain of discipline.”
    • In other words: “I want this.” “But it isn’t good for you.” “Says who? I’ll determine what’s good for me.” “Stop, think about your family, your children, your marriage.” “I don’t care. The heart wants what the heart wants.” But the heart can lead you straight to hell. That is sensuality: no discipline, no self-control, no self-denial.

    1. 24:42 Victim and criminal

      • The Bible doesn’t teach that we are innocent victims led astray. It teaches that we are morally responsible image bearers, made in the image of God, who have gone astray.
      • When you are a victim, you can blame others. When you are a criminal, you can only blame yourself and hope the judge shows mercy. We have all been sinned against, and we can only forgive, because we have sinned too and the Lord forgave us. The believer knows they are both victim and criminal, and the truth that is in Jesus is the only thing that both heals the victim and reforms the criminal.

  13. 25:46 The new self: a new knowledge of God

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:20–24

    • To recap, the problem with the old self is that we were ignorant of the truth, hard of heart, and had lost all sensitivity. The solution is that the new self has a new knowledge of God and a renewed heart. Josh rereads verses 20–24: put off the old self, be made new in the attitude of your minds, and put on the new self created to be like God in true righteousness and holiness.
    • Every believer who has trusted Jesus now possesses the mind of Christ, but again, God won’t make you think differently; you must do that yourself.
    • Paul didn’t say “when you learned about Christ.” People learn about Christ, even mimic him, and aren’t saved. Paul isn’t speaking of intellectual understanding but of a mindset, a worldview, a Christ filter through which we see the world, looking at things through God’s eyes. We fill that desire through a personal relationship with Jesus, knowing him better every day. The unsaved man didn’t know Christ; the believer is increasing in knowledge of Christ daily, and because we believed the truth, we received his life.

  14. 27:40 Truth is in Jesus

    Scripture: John 14:6 John 18:38 Mark 10:18 1 John 5:20

    • Truth is an absolute, objective standard by which reality is measured. If something is true, it is true when I like it and when I don’t, when I feel like it and when I don’t. The world hates this and thinks it makes Christians sound arrogant, as if they have a corner on the market of truth.
    • But it isn’t about us; it is about Jesus. He said, “I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me”: the biggest mic drop in human history. They hated Jesus for saying it and hate Christians for repeating it. Pilate sat there asking, “What is truth?” while truth stared him in the face, and then said he found no wrongdoing in him. Of course not.
    • Jesus told the rich young ruler, “No one is good but God.” According to Jesus, the possibilities are simple: either he isn’t good, or he is God.
    • John explains: the Son of God has come and given us understanding so that we may know him who is true, and we are in him who is true by being in his Son Jesus Christ, the true God and eternal life. The better you know Christ, the better you know the truth. Josh prays the church never grows content just doing what Jesus does, because what we are after is learning to think like Jesus thinks.

  15. 29:59 Moralism versus true gospel change

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:24

    • One of the biggest substitutes for true gospel change is moralism, which teaches that external behavior matters more than internal disposition. It is how we teach kids not to lie: you will get caught and be in big trouble, people will always think you’re a liar, the boy who cried wolf. We discourage behavior by building fear of punishment, rejection, and a tarnished reputation.
    • It works, because we want people to see us positively, but it is ultimately motivated by pride. It makes us tell the truth for our own benefit, but it doesn’t help us love the truth for his benefit.
    • Lying to preserve your image doesn’t sound like verse 24: put on the new self, created to be like God in true righteousness and holiness. The Christian isn’t motivated by self-interest, ego, or reputation, but by a genuine God-made desire for true righteousness and holiness.

    1. 31:48 They perform love

      Scripture: Mark 7:6–7 2 Corinthians 5:17

      • This is what ticked Jesus off about the Pharisees and teachers of the law. In Mark 7 he says, “Isaiah was right when he prophesied about you hypocrites,” calling them actors, pretenders, professional performers: “These people honor me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me.” They performed love.
      • We don’t want to be like that. We think differently: we love truth because God loves truth and we have been made to be like him. If anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come; the old is gone, the new is here. As the mind understands the truth of God’s word, it is gradually transformed by the Spirit, and that renewal leads to a changed life.
      • Physically, you are what you eat; spiritually, you are what you think. So get in the word, Christian, and fill your mind with the truth that is in Jesus.

  16. 33:02 Real Christians sit at the feet of Jesus

    • Some grew up around legalism and have heard that real Christians, the super-Christians, don’t watch R-rated movies, don’t drink, vote Republican, and don’t listen to rap. Josh listens to rap; theologically speaking, he thinks Christian rap is the best Christian stuff out right now.
    • Whatever they told you, here is what you need to know: real, dedicated Christians sit at the feet of Jesus every day. Whatever sin you carry, as you go to the Lord and walk with him, he will work it out in you. Run after Jesus and he will take care of the rest. Trust him and rest.

  17. 34:03 Verses 25–32: out with the old, in with the new

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:25–32

    • Paul says out with the old self, in with the new, so it is time to do some exchanging, with that sin still in mind. Paul lists five things the new creation avoids, with reasons why.
    • Josh reads verses 25–32: put off falsehood and speak truthfully to your neighbor, for we are all members of one body; in your anger do not sin, and don’t let the sun go down while you are still angry or give the devil a foothold; those who have been stealing must work so they have something to share with those in need; let no unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what builds others up; and do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, with whom you were sealed for the day of redemption, but get rid of all bitterness, rage, and anger and be kind and compassionate, forgiving each other just as in Christ God forgave you.

    1. 35:22 One: Christians tell the truth

      Scripture: Revelation 22:15 Acts 5

      • A believer can fall into a lie like any other sin, but there is a difference between a moment of weakness and a life of habitual lies. If your desire is to deceive others, that is strong evidence you don’t know the Lord. “That’s harsh; he might have been a compulsive liar.” Josh understands, but if he hasn’t been renewed in his mind and isn’t thinking differently, his Christian claim might be just another lie.
      • Jesus makes clear that liars won’t inherit the kingdom. In Revelation 22 he says outside are the dogs, those who practice magic arts, the sexually immoral, the murderers, the idolaters, and everyone who loves and practices falsehood. Jesus calls liars dogs.
      • Paul’s instruction is specific to truth-telling in the body of Christ: we can’t build up the church the way we are supposed to if we lie to each other, whether telling lies or living lies. The first lie in the early church came in Acts 5, with Ananias and Sapphira. They died not only because they lied but because their lie harmed the body they had committed to. They wanted to look good for a big pledge, then pocketed the proceeds when they sold, and God made an example of them.
      • We belong to each other in Christ; our actions affect each other, and we share, work together, and meet needs as one. The church can’t function if we are shady with each other. We must speak the truth in love.

    2. 37:58 Two: Christians control their anger

      Scripture: Ephesians 4:27 Proverbs 15:1

      • Christians control their anger, not the other way around. It isn’t a sin to have anger; it is a sin to let anger have you. God’s holy anger is part of his judgment against sin and is good; Jesus turned over the tables in the temple because he was angry at their unrighteousness. But holy anger is hard for us: we feel rightly that something is unjust, then act on it, and emotions tainted by sin ruin everything. We also lack God’s view of the whole timeline; we only see right now, and that causes us to lose control.
      • A woman defended her temper: “I explode and then it’s all over with.” Her friend replied, “So does a shotgun, but look at the damage it leaves behind.” Just because you can’t see the damage doesn’t mean it isn’t there.
      • It is possible to be angry and not sin. But if we do sin, we must make it right as quickly as possible: go directly to the person and stomp it out, because if the fire of anger isn’t put out with loving forgiveness, it can spread and destroy everything the Lord has built around you. That is why Paul says in verse 27, “Do not give the devil a foothold.” Take Solomon’s advice in Proverbs 15: “A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.” In the spirit of speaking the truth in love, some of us would benefit from practicing gentle answers in the mirror.

    3. 40:17 Three: Christians stop stealing

      Scripture: Ephesians 4:28 Exodus 20:15

      • Verse 28 echoes one of the Ten Commandments: “Thou shalt not steal.” When God gave it to Moses, he meant it. Stop stealing.
      • You would expect the application to be “work so you aren’t tempted to steal,” but Paul raises the bar: we work so we can give to others. The default setting of the unsaved is selfishness; the default setting of the believer is generosity. If we steal, we hurt others; so we are commanded to work so we can bless others. A lazy Christian robs himself, others, and God. If you can’t work, the church should care for you; if you can, make sure you are contributing to the life, mission, and ministry of the church.

    4. 41:22 Four: from unwholesome words to wholesome ones

      Scripture: Ephesians 4:29 James 3

      • Time to make some friends: stop cussing. You aren’t good at it; no one is. Whatever celebrity or Instagram pastor you use to justify disgusting language, stop. It isn’t part of the new self. Paul compares it to rotten fruit and spoiled food; foul language is out of character with newness of life. Joking, profanity, dirty stories, and vulgarity are put aside, because as the heart changes, so does our speech.
      • Words don’t have to be four letters to be unwholesome. Our words have power for good or evil, and Christians use them to build others up, not tear them down. Our words should minister grace, drawing others closer to Christ rather than pushing them away. For a refresher on the tongue, read James 3, and join the church this fall for a study of the whole book of James.

    5. 42:59 Five: do not grieve the Holy Spirit

      Scripture: Ephesians 4:30–32

      • “Do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, with whom you were sealed for the day of redemption.” In other words, Christian, don’t neglect what you just heard. The Holy Spirit lives in the Christian, and when your heart is filled with bitterness and anger, the Spirit grieves.
      • What parent wakes up hoping for one of those days when the kids scream, drop-kick each other, and pull each other’s hair out? Conflict in the house is no fun, and neither is conflict in the believer. The Holy Spirit is happiest in an atmosphere of love, joy, and peace, the fruit he was sent to produce in those who obey him.
      • The Holy Spirit can’t leave us; he has sealed us until Christ returns to take us home. We don’t lose our salvation because of sinful attitudes or our struggle with sin, but we can lose the joy of our salvation and miss the fullness of the Spirit’s blessings.

  18. 44:35 Treat others how God treats you

    • It is time to start walking like Jesus: change our thinking and treat others how God treats you. God is patient with you, so be patient with them. God made a way for sinners, so show them his way. God made the first move, so make the first move. God doesn’t dismiss you when you fail, so don’t dismiss them. God forgives you, so forgive them, again and again, for Jesus and his name’s sake.
    • Josh promises that the Holy Spirit in you will jump for joy if you forgive, and you will thrive. So forgive, because in Christ God forgave you.

  19. 45:41 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks the Lord for his word in the first half of this series: his extraordinary promises, generous gifts, the riches and blessings in Christ, grace, mercy, salvation, this church, and the Holy Spirit guiding them in holiness so their lives may be a beacon of hope.
    • As they work this out, the struggle is choosing to walk in ignorance, not listening, continuing to think like the unbeliever, and failing to put on the new self, so that the redeemed walk around looking foolish. He asks the Lord to help them imitate him, make them more like him in their thinking, and from their thinking help them be doers, not because it benefits them but because they love him and his way is better than theirs.
