The Verdict Is In

Overview

  1. 00:33 You can’t handle the truth

    • Josh greets the church and opens with A Few Good Men: the famous line is memorable because it is a legal Hail Mary after months of threatened witnesses and disappearing evidence.
    • A lawyer friend had described how long case prep actually takes—facts, issues, evidence, experts, filings, discovery, the other side’s case. Josh says sermon writing is a lot; this is more.

    1. 02:13 A verdict before the case is heard

      • He imagines walking into court after months of work and hearing the judge ask if the jury has already reached a verdict.
      • That would be outrageous in a human court. When the judge sees all, knows all, and is perfect in righteousness, arguments are not needed because the truth is already known.

  2. 03:09 This is the verdict

    Scripture: John 3:19-21

    • Josh reads John 3:19-21: light has come, but people love darkness because their deeds were evil; evildoers hate the light and will not come for fear of exposure; those who live by the truth come into the light so it may be seen that what they have done has been done in the sight of God.

    1. 03:33 Born Again week three

      Scripture: John 3:1-15 John 3:16

      • Today ends a three-week series titled Born Again. First-time guests can catch weeks one and two on YouTube; he will not recap everything, but he reestablishes the context.
      • John 3 is a thick moment: Jesus’ greatest earthly enemies were Israel’s religious leaders, because he threatened their positions. One Pharisee, Nicodemus, seeks him out.
      • Week one covered most of that conversation. Last week unpacked John 3:16. This last section draws the series into a divine courtroom.
      • Nicodemus comes looking for answers. He has money, power, influence, and reputation, and still a longing for something he does not have.

    2. 05:15 A real heavenly court

      • John’s courtroom language is not hypothetical. Josh calls it a literal divine proceeding across space, time, and history, rooted in biblical covenants.
      • A Supreme Court subpoena would be taken seriously—some would run for Mexico—but you would respond. The heavenly court has already reached a verdict.
      • He has been praying that hearers would not hide, but put faith in Jesus, come to the light, and find peace with the one who made them.

  3. 06:11 Already condemned

    Scripture: Romans 3:23

    • John does not say the verdict will be decided. It is not in deliberation. The verdict is a present reality.
    • That is why the Christian message has not changed for two thousand years: every human being is headed for eternal hell, punished forever for sin. Guilty. All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.
    • Josh says many churches no longer preach condemnation or hell, yet the text still stands.

    1. 07:07 Harsh language is loving

      Scripture: John 3:18 John 3:36

      • Whoever believes in him is not condemned; whoever does not believe stands condemned already because they have not believed in the name of God’s one and only Son.
      • If someone thinks that is merely Josh being harsh, he points to John 3:36: whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever rejects the Son will not see life, for God’s wrath remains on them.
      • Modern cultural Christianity struggles with a wrathful God and asks how a loving God can condemn anyone. Josh says the true message has not changed. Hell is unpleasant; he balances it with heaven—no pain, only peace, joy, purpose, and love forever.

    2. 08:21 How do we get off the wide path

      Scripture: John 14:5-6

      • The question the true church is tasked with answering is how to get off the path to hell and onto the path to heaven.
      • Thomas asked where the Lord was going and how to get there. Jesus answered that they already knew the way: I am the way, the truth, and the life; no one comes to the Father except through me.
      • The church’s answer is by faith in Christ—not by works, not by religion, by believing.

  4. 08:49 By faith, not by works

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:8-9

    • Josh reads Ephesians 2: it is by grace you have been saved through faith—not from yourselves, the gift of God, not by works, so that no one can boast.
    • It is not morality, virtue, kindness, observed days, completed rituals, or religious activity. The only way to live a life pleasing to God, and the only way to escape hell and enter heaven, is putting your life in Jesus’ hands.

    1. 09:43 Faith has always been God’s way

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:4-32 Matthew 5:6 Hebrews 11:6 Genesis 15:5-6 Romans 4-5

      • This is not a New Testament novelty. Next week the church launches a summer series verse by verse through Hebrews, and Josh says they will drill by faith into the fabric of the church.
      • The legends God used to bring the gospel are recorded as living by faith, from Abel to David to the apostles. They believed God’s promises, that he rewards those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him.
      • In Genesis 15 God takes Abram outside, tells him to count the stars, and promises offspring. Abram believed the Lord, and God credited it to him as righteousness. Abraham did not live a perfect life afterward, but he took God at his word.
      • God covered Abraham with his own righteousness, forgiving sin, making him his eternal child, granting everlasting life on the basis of faith. Josh tells hearers to stop entertaining works-based righteousness and to go read Romans 4 and 5.

    2. 12:06 Romans 4 and 5 read aloud

      Scripture: Romans 4:19-25 Romans 5:1-5 Romans 5:6

      • Abraham faced his body as good as dead and Sarah’s dead womb, did not waver regarding the promise, was strengthened in faith, and was fully persuaded that God could do what he promised. That is why it was credited as righteousness—and those words were written for us who believe in him who raised Jesus.
      • Jesus was delivered over for our sins and raised for our justification. Justified through faith, we have peace with God, access into grace, and we boast in hope. Suffering produces perseverance, character, and hope that does not put us to shame, because God’s love has been poured out through the Spirit.
      • At the right time, when we were still powerless, Christ died for the ungodly.

    3. 13:26 Free of charge

      Scripture: Romans 1:17 Habakkuk 2:4 Isaiah 55:1 John 4:13-14 John 6:35 John 6:12-13 Matthew 3:8

      • Paul is not inventing theology. He quotes Habakkuk in Romans 1: in the gospel a righteousness by faith from first to last is revealed; the righteous will live by faith.
      • Isaiah invites the thirsty and those with no money to come, buy, and eat without cost. Josh asks if it is coincidence that Jesus, seven hundred years later, offers living water that satisfies an eternal thirst.
      • You do not need money. You need Jesus. He is the bread of life. Leftover baskets after the four thousand and the five thousand show he does not ask for bread from heaven like Moses; it comes from his own hands.
      • God offers divine satisfaction to condemned sinners free of charge. Jesus picked up the tab at Calvary. Always by faith. Now go produce fruit in keeping with repentance: he has done it; now live for him.

  5. 15:36 Light has come into the world

    Scripture: John 3:19

    • Returning to verse 19, Josh names the verdict as the judgment, the condemnation, the reason people are punished: light has come into the world.

    1. 16:05 What the light is

      Scripture: Genesis 1:3 Matthew 28:19-20 John 1:1-5 Matthew 4:16

      • John uses light and darkness throughout his Gospel. This is not Genesis 1 fluorescent light. One sense of light is instruction Christians can share: teach the nations to obey Jesus’ commands, shine teaching, live in that light.
      • Here the word refers to Jesus himself. Josh reads John 1: the Word was with God and was God; through him all things were made; in him was life, and that life was the light of all people; the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.
      • Matthew says people living in darkness have seen a great light; on those in the land of the shadow of death a light has dawned.
      • You can see the great light and still love darkness instead.

    2. 17:57 Children of darkness hide

      Scripture: John 3:19-20

      • People love darkness instead of light because their deeds were evil. Evildoers hate the light and will not come into it for fear that their deeds will be exposed. Josh says that is human nature: we are children of darkness, and it is against that nature to come to the light, because light means exposure, illumination, humiliation.
      • He tells a preacher’s story: twenty-five letters that said only, All has been exposed. Flee at once. By morning all twenty-five men were gone. If that hit your inbox you would search how fast you could disappear.
      • Even Christians still feel the pull to hide rather than bring everything into the light of Christ.

    3. 19:33 God will not heal what you conceal

      Scripture: 1 John 1:6-10 Psalm 32:3-5

      • Josh points to Courtney’s testimony that morning—arranged, he says, on the same day they are talking about living in the light. Someone had called him brave after he shared last week; he felt like a wimp next to her.
      • The difference with Christ is that the light does not only expose; it heals. God will not heal what you conceal.
      • He reads 1 John: claiming fellowship while walking in darkness is a lie. You cannot walk with Jesus in the darkness; he is in the light. Walking in the light, the blood of Jesus purifies from all sin. Confessed sin is forgiven and purified; claiming sinlessness makes God a liar.
      • David said that when he kept silent his bones wasted away, but when he confessed God forgave the guilt of his sin. Silence rots from the inside; light heals.

    4. 21:21 They love darkness and hate the light

      Scripture: John 3:20

      • Too many people, Christians included, want to be guilt-free in their silence: peace without the war, forgiveness without exposure. Darkness provides cover. Crimes are committed at night.
      • Those who love darkness prefer error to truth, expedience to holiness, what benefits the self.
      • Verse 19 and 20 say two things: they love darkness instead of light, and those who do evil hate the light. Josh confesses that was true of him until God filled him with the Spirit and taught him to walk in the light.

  6. 22:39 I hated the church because of what I wanted to keep doing

    • The sinner hates the gospel because it condemns motives and behaviors and leads to confession, which leads to transformation.
    • Josh hated the church for nearly a decade and blamed that hatred on other people’s behavior—church hurt as the reason he left.

    1. 23:36 Hurt became cover

      Scripture: John 15:4-5

      • The hurt was real at the start. Because he was not in the Word, not worshiping, not in the routines of a believer, not connected to the body or the vine, he convinced himself that others’ wrongs justified his own.
      • Many do not mind Jesus as long as Jesus does not turn the lights on too bright—like a teenager yelling that it is too early. Josh says Jesus is going to shine.

    2. 24:28 Negotiating with the King

      • He walked back into church looking for girls with tobacco in his lip, telling the Lord he was here now and could get to work—then asking God to make an exception for an unbelieving girlfriend if he sat in the front row for three weeks.
      • That relationship was a disaster. He tried the same negotiation with addictions: surely he could serve God and still do this.
      • The more he sat in the Word, taught it, shared it, and lived among God’s people—the more he led people to Jesus—the more his desires changed.

    3. 26:28 Take what you need to take

      • The first time he led someone to the Lord he was still struggling. Everything in him said stay quiet until life was together. The Lord put him there, gave him words, and a student gave his life to Christ.
      • That night in his truck he asked for more of that: whatever you have to do, take what you need to take. The war intensified. He confessed clinging to nicotine for anxiety instead of God’s power. It was not overnight; the Lord freed him from that chain.
      • Openness about the story and the Lord’s work happened at the same time as remaining struggle. Believers make walking in the light normal. Some even dance in it, like David. The only explanation he can give is that Jesus shone in his life. He is never going back.

  7. 28:41 Works carried out in God

    Scripture: John 3:21 John 3:21

    • Those who live by the truth come into the light so it may be seen plainly that what they have done has been done in the sight of God.
    • Josh says he loves the NIV and tries to use it consistently, but it is not good here. The ESV is better: whoever does what is true comes to the light, so that it may be clearly seen that his works have been carried out in God—wrought in God.

    1. 29:16 John 3:1-21 as one thought

      Scripture: John 3:1-21

      • Some Bibles do not print this section in red letters. The majority view, Josh says, is that John 3:1-21 is a continuous thought from Jesus recorded by John. The manuscripts have no quotation marks; interpretation can differ, and that is all right.
      • Whether still in the conversation with Nicodemus or John’s comment on it, it is inspired Scripture preserved for sanctification.

    2. 30:26 Religion tells you; God gives a new heart

      • People confuse works carried out in God with works carried out apart from God. In his addiction, people told him to quit; he even agreed, and it never reached his heart.
      • Then God put it in him to do it. He had not only been struggling with addiction; he had been living in the dark. When God turned the light on, he came into it instead of running.
      • That is why he is not ashamed of the story: what you see is not what he did for God. It is what God did in him. Religion tells you what to do. God gives a new heart so you can do it for him.

    3. 31:42 The Magi’s star preaches light and darkness

      Scripture: John 1:3 Matthew 2:9 Matthew 2:16

      • Josh thinks creation is as disgusted with dead religion as Jesus is. Everything was made through him and for him.
      • The Magi were led by the star to Jesus. When they stepped into Jerusalem—the city that should have known God—the light disappeared. After they heard the king, the star went ahead again until it stopped over the child.
      • The Lord was protecting the child from Herod, the new Pharaoh throwing babies in the river. When they left Jerusalem the light appeared again. Josh hears creation preaching light versus darkness: you can stand in the most religious place on earth and still be in the dark. The star led them out of a city of darkness into Jesus’ presence.
      • Nothing changes until the light of Christ shines. We do not come into the light because we figured it out. The light came for us. When he shines, what used to own you loses its grip. Josh quotes the hymn: the things of earth grow strangely dim in the light of his glory and grace.
      • The greatest evidence of being born again is not a prayer, an aisle, or a thing in a place. It is that you no longer love the darkness you once lived in. That is the fruit.

    4. 34:05 Doers of the truth

      Scripture: John 3:21

      • Jesus says those who live by the truth—the doer of the truth. Josh says the modern church hates this: not those who listen every week, keep a podcast, agree, or give a loud amen, but those who do the truth.
      • Truth here is the opposite of evil. The evildoer acts from falsehood; the Christian acts according to the truth.
      • The sinner believes the lie that God will not punish, that there is no God, no judgment, no hell, and lives like it. The Christian believes God will punish those who reject his Son and reward those who earnestly seek him, that judgment leads into eternity, that hell and heaven are actual places—and more important than streets of gold is the presence of God himself. They live as if those things were true.

  8. 35:47 Shine like stars, not king of the hill

    Scripture: Philippians 2:12-16

    • Paul spurs the Philippians to work out their salvation with fear and trembling—not the old fear, but a fear of disappointing Abba Father. God works in you to will and to act. Do everything without grumbling so you become blameless children in a warped generation, then shine like stars as you hold firmly to the word of life.
    • The church can be that star leading friends and family out of a city of darkness.

    1. 36:42 City on a hill versus king of the hill

      Scripture: John 10:16 John 14:15 Matthew 5:14 Matthew 5:15 Matthew 23:12 Matthew 23:27

      • In Matthew 5 Jesus describes the flock: one flock, one shepherd; those who love him and keep his commandments. You are the light of the world. A city on a hill cannot be hidden.
      • Josh sees a problem: many claiming to be sheep have sharp teeth. They have traded the city on a hill for king of the hill—the childhood snow-pile game of shoving others down.
      • The church stops trying to shine his light and tries to win the fight, the argument, the comparison. When that game takes hold, the lamp goes under a bowl. A city on a hill invites people up; king of the hill shoves people down.
      • Those who exalt themselves will be humbled; those who humble themselves will be exalted. The city Jesus names shines so they see God. King of the hill wants to be the only one standing.
      • Jesus told the Pharisees they could look bright and clean outside and be filled with darkness inside. The church is not called to compete with the world. We are the light of the world.

    2. 39:24 Why a person rejects Christ

      Scripture: John 3:19-20 John 7:7 2 Corinthians 5:20

      • Stop pointing at everyone else’s darkness. Look inward: where are you more concerned about being right than pursuing righteousness, comparing instead of confessing, performing instead of repenting?
      • There is only one reason a person rejects Christ: they love their sin more. They do not want to come near because he shines light on sin, and sinners love sin.
      • It is not that the gospel is complicated, that they have not heard enough sermons, that they cannot find a church that fits, or that the teaching is bad. Sinners hate the light and prefer evil. They hate the truth, the church, Scripture, and Christians.
      • Jesus tells the disciples the world cannot hate them, but it hates him because he testifies that its works are evil. Believers are Christ’s ambassadors, so that burden of being hated fell first on the apostles and now on the church.

  9. 40:55 Born again versus born once

    • The difference is not perfection; it is direction. One runs from the light; the other runs to it.
    • Josh says people are not here for the show or the comfort. Most are here because the light is on. When it comes on, there are no shadows, hiding places, or pretending—only authenticity.
    • If you have something to protect, this is a bad place to be. The light of Christ does not negotiate with darkness. If you are his, his light will reveal it and reject it. That is why some leave: not because the light is untrue, but because it is too bright. They want shade.
    • If you belong to him, you love the light. You start to see what is happening in you is not you—that is God. That is where assurance, confidence, and joy come from: I am not who I was; I did not do this. We come not to hide but to be seen by him and by others, fully known and fully loved.

    1. 43:25 Nicodemus walked into the light

      Scripture: John 19:38-42 John 3:14-15

      • Nicodemus approached Jesus at night, but the light of the world read his soul. That Pharisee had his heart read by the great I Am, and he was never the same. It took time, but the light won.
      • Later, in John 19, he steps into the open carrying the broken body of the one who exposed him. He came in the dark and ended up walking into the light. Josh is convinced we will see Nicodemus in heaven.
      • He heard the good news from Jesus himself: look to the cross and find salvation; look to Jesus and live.

    2. 44:23 Closing prayer

      Scripture: Philippians 2:12 Matthew 5:14

      • Josh thanks the Father for the Word and the Spirit, for conviction that has changed how he thinks about people, himself, and God. Shine on me and change me.
      • He is not the husband he is without God’s help. He asks for courage to walk this out. The true measure of a follower is not what happens in the room but integrity in the closet when nobody is looking.
      • Give courage to honor God when he is the only one around. Work out faith with fear and trembling in those spaces. Make this church a city on a hill: do what you must, take away what you must. Amen.
