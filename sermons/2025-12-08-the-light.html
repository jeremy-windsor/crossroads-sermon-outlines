Overview

  1. 00:09 Advent purpose: know Jesus, then go tell it

    Scripture: Luke 2 1 Corinthians 15:14-19

    • Josh announces three weeks in the first half of John 1, ending with a Christmas Eve look at the nativity in Luke 2. The Advent challenge is simple: go tell it.
    • He trusts the Holy Spirit to create opportunities and wants to equip the church for them. To tell someone about Jesus, believers must know him; to go tell it, they must first have heard it.
    • The incarnation is either fantasy or creation’s decisive event. Christians are either pitiable fools or heralds of the most important news in human history.

  2. 01:50 God explains God through his written word

    Scripture: Romans 1:19-22 Genesis 1:1-5 John 3:3-8 2 Corinthians 5:17 Ephesians 2:18-20

    • Josh says the series is not an apologetic. Scripture assumes the Creator, begins with God speaking light into darkness, and treats suppression of that reality as culpable rather than neutral.
    • Ancient philosophical schools disagreed about the divine but did not deny divine presence. A telephone-game illustration shows why human retelling drifts without the Holy Spirit and the written word.
    • Believers are born again and made new. Ephesians identifies the church’s foundation as the apostles and prophets with Christ as cornerstone—not feelings, wishes, or cultural approval. God explains God.

  3. 07:28 The Emmaus response: opened Scripture, burning hearts, immediate witness

    Scripture: Luke 24:13-24 Luke 24:16 Luke 24:17-18 Luke 24:25-26 Luke 24:27 Deuteronomy 34:1-5; Joshua 1:1-6 Joshua 2:8-21; 6:1-25 Luke 22:20 Revelation 22:20 Luke 24:28-32 Luke 24:33-35 Luke 24:36-43 John 6:1-13 Luke 24:44-49 John 19:30 Romans 6:16-22 2 Corinthians 5:18-20 Exodus 20:1-17

    • Josh follows the disappointed travelers in Luke 24. Jesus restrains their recognition, rebukes their failure to believe Scripture, and explains from Moses and the Prophets what all Scripture says about him.
    • Moses, Joshua, Jericho, and Rahab become a gospel pattern: law can bring people to the promise, but Yeshua brings them in; judgment terrifies the rebellious city while the promised rescue gives Rahab joy.
    • When Jesus opens Scripture, eyes open and hearts burn. The travelers return immediately to testify. Jesus then supplies bodily evidence of resurrection, opens the Law, Prophets, and Psalms, and sends witnesses with promised power.
    • Repentance is not regret or payment. Christ finished the work; repentance is a Spirit-worked reorientation that releases sin to receive forgiveness. The church cannot tell what it has not seen and heard.

  4. 20:16 The life-giving Christ fulfills Scripture and awakens worship

    Scripture: John 1:1-5 John 21:25 John 1:29, 36 John 1:51 John 2:19-21 John 3:3-8 John 3:14-15 Numbers 21:4-9 Romans 6:23 Hebrews 9:22 Ephesians 2:4-5 John 3:15-16 Psalm 47:1-2 Psalm 150:3-5 Revelation 19:1 Zephaniah 3:17

    • The congregation reads John 1:1-5. John presents the divine Word as Creator, life, and unconquered light, selecting signs and scenes so readers recognize the Son of God.
    • Jesus fulfills Old Testament types: Lamb, ladder, temple, new birth, and the lifted serpent. At the cross God turns the curse into the cure—death becomes life for those made alive by grace.
    • Josh refuses to let familiarity shrink eternal life. Psalms, Revelation, and Zephaniah portray loud praise with voices and instruments as a response to deliverance, victory, God’s greatness, and salvation’s joy.

  5. 28:00 The eternal Word is God, personally distinct, and Creator

    Scripture: John 1:1-2 John 14:9 Revelation 22:13 Hebrews 1:1-3 John 1:1-2 John 8:58-59 Genesis 1:2 John 1:1 John 1:1 John 1:1, 14 John 1:1 Isaiah 46:9 John 1:3 Genesis 1:3 Psalm 33:9 Colossians 1:15-17

    • Only eternal God can give eternal life. John says the Word existed before creation, was with God, and was God. Jesus reveals the Father, is Alpha and Omega, and is God’s climactic speech to humanity.
    • Logos spoke to both Jewish and Greek hearers. Jesus’ claim to exist before Abraham confirms John’s testimony and exposes the doctrine of the Trinity: Father, Son, and Spirit are God yet personally distinct.
    • Josh warns against denying Christ’s deity or his personhood, rejects the Jehovah’s Witness rendering of John 1:1, and urges kind but firm gospel witness.
    • All things were made through the Word. God spoke creation into being through Christ, so Jesus is not created; only Jesus can offer eternal life.

  6. 35:25 The Light cannot lose: believe, receive, and come

    Scripture: John 1:4-5 John 14:6 1 John 1:5 John 3:19-21 John 1:5 John 12:46 John 12:36 John 1:10-11 John 14:6 John 1:12 John 1:12

    • Christ does not merely possess or give life; he is life. He enters the darkness of evil, unbelief, sin, and death, yet darkness cannot overcome him.
    • Everyone begins in darkness, but believers become children of light. Josh rejects fear-driven Christianity: the world has always been dark, the Lord still says not to fear, and the light cannot lose.
    • He asks whether listeners know, trust, and have received Jesus. The way was refused, the truth disbelieved, and the life crucified, yet those who receive him are given the right to become God’s children.
    • C. T. Studd’s line presses one lasting decision: only one life will soon pass, and only what is done for Christ will last. Believe in the light and come to Christ today.

  7. 40:16 Prayer for return, opened eyes, and Advent hope

    Scripture: John 1:14 John 14:2-4 John 8:12

    • Josh thanks the Father for Scripture and the incarnation: Christ takes flesh, lives the life sinners could not, dies the death they deserved, and gives a future promise.
    • He clings to Christ’s prepared place and return, asks that Christmas draw the church closer rather than pass as another holiday, and prays that those who have wandered will turn back.
    • Calling Jesus the light of the world, he asks God to open eyes, hearts, and minds so hearers believe, then entrusts the response to God’s will.
