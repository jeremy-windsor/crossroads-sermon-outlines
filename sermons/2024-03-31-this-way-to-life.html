Overview

  1. 00:04 What is your greatest fear?

    • Josh asks the church to think about their greatest fear, the thing they spend the most time worrying about. He has always struggled with the idea that anxiety is a condition: some people are more prone to it, but for most of us anxiety isn’t a disorder; it is a symptom of our fear.
    • According to the National Institute of Mental Health, about 12.5% of US adults will deal with a specific phobia, an anxiety disorder involving excessive and persistent fear of a situation or object. There are social phobias and agoraphobia, the fear of being somewhere escape is hard, which some may feel after being moved into the middle of a row.
    • Few struggle with extremes like that, but everyone is afraid of something. Some fears are rational, born of a terrible experience; others are irrational, just something inside that demands we run the other way.

    1. 02:28 Getting to know each other’s fears

      • Josh polls the room. Public speaking: some would jump the border to Mexico rather than preach, and Josh got a C in high school speech. Heights: a generous member is handing out free trips to the Grand Canyon and the Eiffel Tower (he is kidding). The dentist: a smaller crowd.
      • For Josh it is needles, and he thinks a real doctor should give the dental shot instead of the dentist going at it “like Grandma knitting a stocking.” Snakes: when a security guy crushed a snake outside the church under his boot, Josh knew they had found a biblical church. Flying: after watching Masters of the Air, Josh decided he isn’t a real man. Creepy crawlies: his wife hunts crane flies with her house slippers, and he offers the fun fact that you swallow an average of four live spiders in your sleep each year.

  2. 05:56 The fear of death

    • The truth is we’re all afraid of something. A 2017 Chapman University survey found 21% of Americans are afraid or very afraid of dying, tying death with public speaking as America’s greatest fear. Jerry Seinfeld joked that at a funeral the average person would rather be in the casket than give the eulogy. Since 2017, it isn’t a stretch to think that fear has grown.
    • We fear the pain and suffering of death; we fear not existing; we fear losing control. Some who believe in an afterlife wonder whether they have done enough, and many fear what will become of their families and whether they will see them again.

    1. 07:18 Nothing is more important than the Easter message

      • Josh doubts the room is full of people unfamiliar with the Easter message, but if someone is hearing for the first time that God took on flesh and became the Christ to rescue sinners, praise God. If they choose to accept his words, turn from sin, and live for him, Josh will stop preaching and baptize them right now, because nothing he has to say is more important.

  3. 08:01 Jesus came to address our greatest fear

    Scripture: John 12:49–50 Romans 6:23

    • Jesus makes it clear he carries the very words of God. In John 12 he says he did not speak on his own; the Father who sent him commanded him what to say, and he knows the Father’s command leads to eternal life.
    • The mission of Jesus was to address our greatest fear, not public speaking, but death, and God’s answer to death is eternal life: the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

  4. 08:44 A culture that fears death but not the one who conquered it

    Scripture: Matthew 10:28

    • Our culture fears death but doesn’t fear the one who conquered death. We have lost a healthy fear of God, so much that the president can try to say this day is something different than it has always been, using the name of the Lord in the day of the Lord on his press release.
    • That is what happens when a culture elevates the individual: we have built a fantasy land where everyone is perfect as they are and nobody needs to change, disregarding our history. The culture calls evil good, and if evil is good there is no evil, and you don’t need God. Satan is having a field day in America, even taking ground in churches, and Josh is convinced it is because we fear all the wrong things; Satan uses little fears to distort our view of reality.
    • Jesus teaches about fear in Matthew 10: do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul; rather fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell. If you pictured a little red dude in tights, you’re wrong. Don’t give Satan more power than he has; only God has that power. Satan isn’t even in charge in hell. He’s a ticked-off inmate, not the warden.
    • The fear Jesus teaches isn’t terror; it is reverence, awe, and honor. The believer’s destination is heaven and the unbeliever’s is hell. God’s unlimited, permanent power is far superior to Satan’s limited, temporary power, and only God determines the eternal destiny of souls. That is what Easter is about.

    1. 11:56 Trade little fears for a greater fear of God

      Scripture: Romans 14:10–12 Joshua 24:15

      • Josh’s heart’s desire is to convince the church to trade its little fears for a greater fear of God. We don’t talk about this anymore, too busy tweeting from behind our iPhones, but if you can’t discuss it here, where can you?
      • Paul makes it clear in Romans 14 that we will all stand before God’s judgment seat: every knee will bow, every tongue will confess to God, and each of us will give an account. To those who believe in God: do you give that day enough thought? Does it inform your decisions, priorities, and what you do with the gospel and the gifts God has given you?
      • Josh echoes Joshua: if serving the Lord seems undesirable, choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve, but as for me and my household, we will serve the Lord. Who do you serve? You might as well figure it out today.

    2. 13:15 A couple of Easter sermons changed the world

      Scripture: 1 John 4:14–19

      • The entire world changed because of a couple of Easter sermons. Thousands of Jews became Christians in weeks because someone told them Jesus was executed and is alive again, appearing to over 500 eyewitnesses, and a culture of fear, oppression, and persecution became nearly overnight a culture of faith, hope, and love. That is Josh’s prayer for this country, the president, and every family: that we would turn from sin and return to God in faith.
      • Jesus came to drive out fear. John says the Father has sent his Son to be the Savior of the world; God is love, and love is made complete among us so that we will have confidence on the day of judgment. There is no fear in love, but perfect love drives out fear, because fear has to do with punishment. We love because he first loved us.

    3. 14:53 Gospel preservation over self-preservation

      • Josh’s only explanation for this corporation of love called the church is the resurrection. We must keep preaching and not grow weary of doing good. This building exists because first-century Christians chose gospel preservation over self-preservation.
      • This isn’t just a story; it is history. For thousands of years Christians have said that Jesus Christ, crucified and buried, has been raised, is seated at the right hand of the throne of the universe, and will reign until all things are under his feet. They preached it when it meant losing their jobs, with the church promising to feed them; when it meant they might starve; and when it meant they might die.

  5. 16:07 Everyone says Jesus is a wise teacher, so listen to him

    Scripture: John 14:1–7

    • In his research on world religions, Josh finds that most have tremendous respect for Jesus. Muslims say he was a prophet, a wise teacher sent from God; Hindus call him a holy man and wise teacher; Buddhists say he was enlightened and a wise teacher. Yet they seem uninterested in accepting his teaching.
    • So look at his words the night before his death: do not let your hearts be troubled; trust in God, trust also in me. In my Father’s house there is plenty of room; I am going to prepare a place for you, and I will come back. When Thomas says they don’t know the way, Jesus says, “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.”
    • People don’t like Christians for saying there is only one way to heaven, but all we are doing is quoting Jesus, the one everyone agrees is holy, enlightened, good, wise, and a credible source sent from God.

  6. 18:06 A different empty tomb: Lazarus

    Scripture: John 11:3–4

    • Josh shows another “I am” statement, and in light of the empty tomb, a different empty tomb. Jesus loved Lazarus; they were close, and he loved the family. Lazarus’ sister Mary was the one who poured perfume on Jesus while Judas called it a waste.
    • In John 11 the sisters send word: “Lord, the one you love is sick.” Jesus says this sickness will not end in death; it is for God’s glory, so that God’s Son may be glorified through it. It doesn’t surprise him: the sickness will take Lazarus’ life, but it won’t end that way.

    1. 19:06 God’s timing and God’s love

      Scripture: Isaiah 55:8 John 11:5

      • We struggle with God’s patience and timing; Josh does. But just because God hasn’t shown up yet doesn’t mean he won’t. He may seem to make a habit of showing up late, but he is always on time, working something you can’t see yet. Isaiah 55:8: “My thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways.” Your will is messy; his will is majestic.
      • In case you want to challenge his love because he isn’t showing up when you think he should, John includes the very next verse: Jesus loved Martha and her sister Mary and Lazarus. We make a huge mistake when we evaluate his love based on our opinion of his timing.

    2. 20:20 “For your sake I am glad I was not there”

      Scripture: John 11:14–15 Hebrews 12:2

      • A couple of days pass, and Jesus says he is going to wake Lazarus up. The disciples think he is napping; there were no iPhones, so someone had to shake him awake. Jesus tells them plainly that Lazarus is dead, “and for your sake I am glad I was not there, so that you may believe. Let us go.”
      • Hebrews calls Jesus the author and perfecter of our faith, and here he grows the disciples’ faith. This is why we shouldn’t complain about the Lord’s timing: what if he seems late in order to grow someone else? We always ask what’s in it for me. Believer, are you open to being used for his purposes even at your expense? Jesus was, and that is why we are here today.

    3. 21:29 Martha runs out to meet Jesus

      Scripture: John 11:17 John 11:20–22

      • Lazarus has been in the tomb four days. A Jewish funeral could last up to a week, so the family is near the end of the first stage of grief when Jesus shows up, and the sisters have words for him.
      • Martha goes out to meet him while Mary stays home: “Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. But I know that even now God will give you whatever you ask.” Josh admires her approach; she just wants to talk through her struggle with Jesus.
      1. 22:20 Talk to Jesus about your loss
        • It hit Josh differently this week. He encourages anyone who has lost someone this year to go and talk to Jesus about how they feel; he is the mighty counselor. Don’t just stay home like Mary.
        • Josh has talked to Jesus a lot about his sister, who died in August at 39. He has gone from being extremely angry at the Lord to surrendering entirely. He doesn’t know what the Lord was working in her heart when she passed, and to pretend he knew would be arrogant at best. Let Jesus help you through your loss; he helped Josh, and he can help you.

  7. 23:18 “Your brother will rise again”

    Scripture: John 11:23–24

    • Jesus says, “Your brother will rise again,” and Martha answers, “I know he will rise again in the resurrection at the last day.”

    1. 23:32 The resurrection debate

      Scripture: Isaiah 53:11 Daniel 12:2 John 5:28–29

      • The resurrection was heavily debated in that day. The Pharisees believed there would be a resurrection; the Sadducees didn’t, “and that’s why they’re sad, you see.” Jesus would sometimes start talking about the resurrection between them, let them fight, and slip out the back. You can do the same in the church lobby: say “predestination” between a Calvinist and an Arminian and walk away.
      • Those who believed in a resurrection expected one big resurrection of all Israel at once; a singular resurrection didn’t fit anyone’s beliefs. Isaiah doesn’t use the word, but he speaks of the servant alive after suffering: after he has suffered, he will see the light of life and be satisfied. That is a promised resurrection for God’s servant.
      • There is also a promised resurrection for God’s people. Daniel says multitudes who sleep in the dust will awake, some to everlasting life and others to shame and everlasting contempt. Everyone will rise; who you spend your time with after that depends on what you do with the good news. Jesus confirms Daniel’s vision in John 5: a time is coming when all who are in their graves will hear his voice and come out, those who have done good to live and those who have done evil to be condemned.

    2. 26:06 Lazarus would die again; Jesus never will

      • The Sadducees clearly missed it, maybe skipping one too many synagogues, and even the Pharisees struggled when Jesus raised people. The three resurrections Jesus performed were very different from his own: Lazarus didn’t get a fully glorified body and would die again. Jesus never died again; he is alive and well. Happy Easter.
      • Josh doesn’t know what Christ’s glorified body looked like, holes in him yet eating fish (“does the fish stay in?”), but he can’t wait to see the nail marks himself, have his own glorified body, and celebrate with him.

  8. 27:08 “I am the resurrection and the life”

    Scripture: John 11:25–26 1 Corinthians 15:20–22

    • Martha is right that Lazarus will rise at the last day. She isn’t wrong, just not entirely right. What she doesn’t understand is that Jesus didn’t just come to preach about the resurrection; he came to do it, to be the firstfruits, going first so we could follow.
    • Jesus says, “I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in me will live, even though they die; and whoever lives by believing in me will never die. Do you believe this?” Paul took this gospel to the nations, and Josh brings it today: Christ has indeed been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep; for as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive.

    1. 28:20 You’re here for a reason

      Scripture: Matthew 7:14

      • There are many reasons people are here, but everyone is here for a reason. Maybe you are giving church another shot after being burned and promising never to set foot in one again; Josh made the same promise, and look how that worked out. Maybe Mom and Dad were nagging and you and your siblings decided to give them the victory. Maybe you are burned out, lied to, taken advantage of, scarred by church people, or told so many things you don’t know what to believe.
      • The road to life is narrow, but it isn’t winding or confusing. You don’t need a map; it is simple.

    2. 29:26 Martha’s answer: “Yes, Lord, I believe”

      Scripture: John 11:27

      • Martha says, “Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Messiah, the Son of God, who was to come into the world.” She doesn’t say, “I’ll believe if you do this.” That is what the mockers said at the cross: come down so we can believe. No; stay up there so we can be saved.

    3. 29:58 He is the resurrection, and he is your resurrection

      Scripture: Romans 4:25

      • There are two realities in what Jesus tells Martha: he is the resurrection, and he is your resurrection. Josh imagines people came today because they believe Jesus was raised, but do you believe he was raised for you?
      • It isn’t enough just to believe Jesus was raised. He was delivered over to death for our sins and raised to life for our justification. Death was the penalty for our sins and Jesus paid it, but how can a dead Savior mediate between God and man? He had to be raised to present his righteousness before God for our justification. We have peace with God through Jesus Christ. Happy Easter.

  9. 31:11 Death is an enemy

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:26

    • One of Satan’s ugliest lies is that death isn’t bad, just the natural final stage of life to be embraced. The Bible doesn’t talk about death like that. Death is not your friend or a natural stage; it came as a direct result of the fall and the outbreak of sin. First Corinthians says the last enemy to be destroyed is death.

    1. 32:00 Why Jesus wept

      Scripture: John 11:35

      • Jesus walks up to where they laid Lazarus and is moved to tears. Josh admits he is speculating, but he doesn’t think Jesus cries for a man he is about to raise, or because people around him are sad. He believes Jesus weeps over the presence of an enemy, the same thing he felt in Gethsemane: “I was there for creation; this wasn’t how it was drawn up.”
      • A woman confessed to Josh in the lobby that she feels bad for not wanting to go to funerals. Josh doesn’t either; no one does, because death isn’t natural or the way it was supposed to be.

    2. 33:23 A defeated enemy

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:55–57 Romans 8:18

      • Praise God, through Christ death is not just an enemy; it is a defeated enemy. Paul says, “Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O death, is your sting?… Thanks be to God! He gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.” Paul is talking trash to death at the Super Bowl for your soul, chest painted: “You couldn’t hold him down, so you can’t hold me down, and I’m with him.”
      • For the Star Wars nerds, Josh pictures Obi-Wan before Vader strikes him down: “I will become more powerful than you can imagine.” That is the Christian mindset: death, do your worst. The worst thing that can happen to a Christian is actually amazing, which is why disciples left the Sanhedrin rejoicing that they got to suffer for Christ. Death is just a doorway into resurrection life.
      • The harder you hit me, the stronger he makes me. That is why we suffer with joy, and why Paul boasts in Romans 8 that our present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory that will be revealed in us. This is how a Christian lives: singing at the top of his lungs, smiling ear to ear, serving others and forgetting selfishness, praying for enemies even as they persecute.

  10. 35:46 Two questions

    • Josh closes with a couple of questions.

    1. 35:52 Do you believe this?

      • He died for your sins and was raised for your salvation. Do you believe this? If you believe in him, you will live even though you die. If something changed today and you’re sure, don’t wait: be baptized. Josh is wearing his best clothes, and it is raining, so you’re already wet. Join the family of believers that loves you and give your life to a Savior who loves you.

    2. 36:39 He died for you; are you living for him?

      Scripture: Jeremiah 23:29

      • For believers: he died for you; are you living for him? What are your biggest obstacles? Do you know what the Spirit has gifted and tasked you to do to build up the body, and are you doing it?
      • The Lord says his word is like fire, like a hammer that breaks rocks into pieces. Is he breaking you into pieces and rebuilding you into his image and likeness?

  11. 37:30 “Lazarus, come out!”

    Scripture: John 11:43–44

    • Jesus called in a loud voice, “Lazarus, come out!” and the dead man came out. Josh once needed to hear this too: Jesus is calling out to some, “You have yet to come out.” What do you expect to accomplish in there?
    • If you believe in him, nothing can stop him from accomplishing his purposes in your life: making you holy, building your faith, growing you, giving you joy, teaching you mercy and how to share the gospel. He was raised for you, and the same power that raised him is in you. Believer, are you wielding that power like a weapon and pushing back against the things he hates?

    1. 38:44 Hunt for hidden sin, and get back on mission

      Scripture: Philippians 1:27

      • An idea for the afternoon: after you hunt for eggs, search your life for hidden sin, bring it to light, and kill it. Taunt it or talk trash if you have to, but tolerate it no more.
      • Too many Christians claim Jesus saved them but are unwilling to live for him. Josh invites them back on mission: as citizens of heaven, live in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ, and as the world watches itself fall apart, be there to shine light and share the word of life. Will you join us and help change the way people see the church?

  12. 39:49 Closing prayer

    Scripture: Philippians 2:9–11

    • Josh prays in awe of God’s goodness and his story of redemption in humanity and in Josh personally: that God knows every hair on his head, even though he shaves it; that he didn’t just say he loved Josh’s soul but did what it took to save it, even sending his Son to be humiliated.
    • That isn’t where the story ends: God raised his Son, exalted him, and gave him the name above every name, that every knee should bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord. Josh joins them: “You are Lord. I give you my life. Take it. Use me.” He asks God to use the church, to move it from chattering tongues to feet and hands anxious to wash feet and serve others, and thanks him for the empty tomb.
