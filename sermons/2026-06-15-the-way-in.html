Overview

  1. 00:10 Jesus, the God-man, is the only mediator who can bring humanity to God

    • Josh opens the sixth week in Hebrews by summarizing chapters 1–7 as the answer to who Jesus is and chapters 8–9 as the answer to what Jesus accomplished. He begins with the church’s shared confession of Christ and develops why the mediator must be fully God and fully man.

    1. 00:10 Hebrews 8–9 brings the series to its main point

      Scripture: Hebrews 8:1-9:15 Hebrews 1:1-7:28 Hebrews 8:1-9:28

      • Josh greets the church and sets the day’s stated scope as Hebrews 8 through 9:15—twenty-eight verses that summarize the preceding six weeks and bring the argument to its main point.
      • His compressed map is simple: Hebrews 1–7 answers who Jesus is; Hebrews 8–9 explains what Jesus accomplished.

    2. 01:08 Thomas’s confession belongs to every Christian

      Scripture: John 20:24-29

      • Josh reads the risen Jesus’ encounter with Thomas: Thomas refuses secondhand testimony, Jesus invites him to inspect the wounds and stop doubting, and Thomas answers, ‘My Lord and my God.’
      • Jesus’ blessing of those who believe without seeing turns Thomas’s confession toward every later Christian. Josh says true Christians may differ widely, but they unite in confessing Jesus as fully God and fully man.

    3. 02:03 The mediator had to share God’s nature and humanity’s nature

      Scripture: Galatians 4:4 John 1:1, 14; Hebrews 1:3 Isaiah 7:14; 9:6; Matthew 1:18, 23; Luke 1:35; Romans 1:3 Romans 5:12-19; 1 Corinthians 15:45-49

      • Josh gathers biblical incarnation language: in the fullness of time God sent his Son, the eternal Word and radiance of God’s glory; the Son humbled himself, took flesh, was born of a virgin by the Holy Spirit, came from David’s line, and is Immanuel.
      • He describes Christ’s two perfect natures in one person. Jesus must be fully God to reveal the Father and defeat death, and fully man to obey, suffer, and die on humanity’s behalf.
      • Because humanity broke covenant and incurred its curse, humanity’s debt had to be borne by a man. Josh invokes Paul’s Adam-Christ pattern to explain why Jesus stands in humanity’s place.

    4. 03:02 A bridge across the Grand Canyon pictures Christ’s mediation

      • Josh imagines God and humanity on opposite rims of the Grand Canyon. A merely human bridge never reaches God, and a merely divine bridge never touches humanity.
      • Because Jesus is both God and man, he alone spans the chasm, placing one hand on heaven and one on earth to bring the two together.

    5. 03:31 ‘Catholic’ names the universal church’s apostolic confession

      • Josh calls the God-man confession the confession of the catholic church, then pauses because modern listeners may hear the word only as a denominational label.
      • He explains katholikos as ‘universal’ and says the creedal usage names all true believers who hold the apostolic faith. His history illustration quotes Ignatius of Antioch in AD 107: wherever Jesus Christ is, there is the catholic church.
      • The aside serves his pastoral appeal: Christians should set preferences aside and unite around their common confession so the world sees the church differently.

    6. 04:57 Salvation in Jesus makes knowing and being known by God possible

      Scripture: Acts 4:12 Matthew 22:37-39 Hebrews 4:13

      • Josh calls the church to unite around Acts 4’s confession that salvation is found in no one else and no other saving name has been given under heaven.
      • Resting in God’s salvation creates two relational pillars for obedience: believers can know God—his mind, will, desires, loves, and hates—and can be fully known by him.
      • Being laid bare before God no longer ends in humiliation or concealment; through Jesus it brings peace and can become a means of gospel witness. Josh promises to show how Jesus made this access possible.

  2. 06:22 The seated high priest serves in heaven with a superior ministry

    • Josh opens Hebrews 8: Jesus is the finished-work priest seated at the Father’s right hand, ministering in the true heavenly sanctuary rather than an earthly copy. His person, place, offering, ministry, covenant, and promises are all superior.

    1. 06:22 Hebrews announces its main point: the high priest is seated in the true tabernacle

      Scripture: Hebrews 8:1-2 Hebrews 4:15

      • Josh reads Hebrews 8:1–2 and treats it as a recap: the church has the morally perfect high priest who nevertheless identified with human need, weakness, and temptation.
      • For Hebrews’ original audience, the repeated implication is ‘never go back.’ Josh reduces the reason to one word: Jesus is better.

    2. 07:20 No seats in the earthly tabernacle meant the priests’ work never ended

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:1-4, 11-14 John 1:29

      • Josh notes that the old tabernacle had no chairs, recliners, or couches because a priest’s work was never finished.
      • Every repeated sacrifice preached that the previous sacrifice had not been enough. Against that cycle, John the Baptizer identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God who takes away the world’s sin.

    3. 08:13 Where Jesus sits reveals enthronement, fulfillment, and authority

      Scripture: Psalm 110:1 Psalm 110:4

      • Jesus is not merely resting somewhere; he is seated on heaven’s throne at the Father’s right hand. Josh reads Psalm 110:1 as the Father’s promise to the Son and links enthronement with the priest after Melchizedek’s order.
      • The seat signals that Christ’s sacrificial work is finished and that his priestly ministry proceeds from the highest possible authority.

    4. 08:51 Paul’s prayer displays the exalted Christ serving his church

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:18-23

      • Josh echoes Paul’s prayer that believers’ inward eyes would be enlightened to know their hope, inheritance, and God’s incomparable power.
      • He reads the movement from resurrection to Christ’s seat above every rule, authority, power, dominion, and name, with all things under his feet and Christ appointed as head for the church.
      • The phrase that Christ fills everything in every way shows Josh the ongoing ministry of Jesus through his faithful church. Jesus is better not only because of what he does, but because of where, under whose authority, and for whom he does it.

    5. 10:05 Earthly priests serve a copy; Jesus ministers in the original

      Scripture: Hebrews 8:3-5 Psalm 110:4

      • Hebrews 8:3–5 says every high priest needs an offering, Jesus would not qualify for Levitical priesthood on earth, and earthly priests serve a copy and shadow patterned after what Moses saw on the mountain.
      • Josh returns to Psalm 110:4: David foretold an eternal priest in Melchizedek’s order. Jesus’ descent from Judah barred him from Aaron’s earthly sanctuary, but not from the heavenly sanctuary made without human hands.

    6. 11:09 A superior priest mediates a superior covenant on better promises

      Scripture: Hebrews 8:4-5 Hebrews 8:6-7

      • Aaron’s priests served in the copy; Jesus serves in the original. Josh stacks the comparison: superior priest, superior place, superior ministry.
      • He reads Hebrews 8:6–7 and emphasizes the congregation’s answer: the new covenant is established on ‘better promises.’ That claim raises the next question—what was wrong with the first covenant?

  3. 11:57 The old covenant exposed the people’s fault; God promised a covenant that changes hearts

    • Josh insists God’s law was holy and perfect; the fault lay in sinners whom external command could expose but not transform. Jeremiah’s promise therefore joins forgiveness and inward renewal to the creation of a faithful covenant people.

    1. 11:57 The law was not defective; the people were

      Scripture: Hebrews 8:6-7 Romans 7:12 Romans 3:20 Hebrews 8:7

      • Josh rejects any suggestion that God’s law was the problem. It was holy, righteous, good, and an undamaged reflection of God’s character—nothing could be removed or added to improve it.
      • The law could reveal sin but not remove it, command obedience but not create it, and display righteousness but not make sinners righteous. Hebrews 8:7 therefore locates the need for a second covenant in the first covenant’s inability to bring sinful people into the intended relationship with God.

    2. 13:05 God announced better administration of the same saving grace

      Scripture: Jeremiah 31:31-34 Romans 1:16 Leviticus 18:5

      • Through Jeremiah, God promised a new covenant—not a different plan of salvation, but a better administration of saving grace secured through Jesus, its mediator.
      • Josh says the gospel went first to the Jew and then to the Gentile, and everyone in Christ shares blood-purchased blessings: forgiveness, a new heart, the Spirit, direct access to God, and gifts for blessing God and one another.
      • He contrasts the covenants: the old says ‘obey and live,’ while the new says ‘live because Christ obeyed’; the old displays God’s standard, while the new provides God’s Savior.

    3. 14:18 Reading Hebrews well requires the Old Testament

      Scripture: Jeremiah 31:31-33

      • Josh urges continued study of all Scripture. Hebrews repeatedly travels into Genesis and the Torah because creation, covenant history, and Israel’s law make the prophets and Christ’s ministry intelligible.
      • He reads Jeremiah 31:31–33: God will make a covenant unlike the one Israel broke, put his law in minds and hearts, be their God, and make them his people.

    4. 15:07 The new covenant creates a people, not merely isolated converts

      Scripture: Jeremiah 31:33

      • Josh pauses the reading at ‘I will be their God, and they will be my people.’ The promise creates a new family and a covenant people who learn a different way of relating.
      • He confronts consumer-shaped American Christianity: culture says to stay only while needs are met, but covenant says to remain faithful when relationships become difficult.

    5. 17:02 God’s faithfulness should govern difficult commitments in the church and home

      Scripture: Matthew 18:15-17 Jeremiah 31:32

      • Josh asks what would happen if pastors quit whenever ministry became hard, elders resigned whenever criticized, husbands left whenever marriage frustrated them, or parents walked away whenever children exhausted them.
      • Relationships flourish where covenant is stronger than convenience. Josh applies Jeremiah’s husband-and-bride language: God did not remain Israel’s husband only while Israel was faithful, but pursued an unfaithful people; the church should embody the same steadfastness.
      • His practical request is that people speak with pastors and elders rather than silently disappear from a church. He names departure from the gospel and an unrepentant leader addressed through Matthew 18 as legitimate reasons to leave; preference and consumer dissatisfaction are not.

    6. 19:00 Grace turns church from a product into a covenant family

      Scripture: Jeremiah 31:34

      • When grace takes hold, people stop asking only what a church can provide and start asking whom they can love, serve, forgive, encourage, and remain committed to.
      • The church is not a product to consume. The new covenant not only reconciles people to God but binds them together in unity.
      • Josh resumes Jeremiah 31:34: all will know the Lord, and God will forgive wickedness and remember sins no more.

    7. 20:00 The first promise places God’s law within his people

      Scripture: Jeremiah 31:33 Exodus 20:1-17 Ezekiel 36:26-27

      • Josh counts four better promises in Jeremiah and first dwells on God writing the law in minds and hearts.
      • Under the old covenant, stone tablets stood outside a person and could command, confront, and condemn without changing the heart. Under the new, God plants his truth within.
      • The indwelling Spirit gives a new heart that increasingly loves what God loves, hates what God hates, desires obedience, and has power to say no to sin.

    8. 21:09 Christianity rests on the Spirit’s inward work, not ink alone

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:16 Exodus 31:18; Acts 2:1-4

      • Josh considers whether Christianity would die if every Bible vanished. The loss would be grievous, produce arguments, and complicate questions of authority because the church treasures Scripture as God-breathed and submits to it.
      • Yet the church began in the Spirit’s power. The God who wrote on stone at Sinai now writes truth on hearts, beginning at Pentecost and continuing today.
      • Christians do not only carry a Bible in their hands; they carry its author in their hearts. Christianity is therefore transformation to be lived and experienced, not merely information, a class, or a religion to study.

  4. 22:28 The better promises are God’s initiative, inward transformation, complete forgiveness, and eternal blessing

    • Josh organizes the new covenant around four promises: God acts rather than depending on human vows; God changes desires; God closes the case against forgiven sinners; and Christ’s indestructible life guarantees blessing that cannot fade.

    1. 22:28 What the law demanded, grace provides

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 3:3, 6 Jeremiah 31:33-34 Exodus 19:8; 24:3, 7

      • Josh contrasts the old covenant’s external law with the new covenant gift of the Spirit. Jeremiah’s repeated subject is God: ‘I will’ put, write, be merciful, and remember sins no more.
      • Israel promised at Sinai to do everything the Lord had spoken and then failed. The failure did not expose a bad law but people unable to create the obedience the law commanded.
      • The better covenant is built not on human promises to God but on God’s promises to humanity.

    2. 24:07 God’s ‘I will’ meets Jesus’ ‘It is finished’

      Scripture: Leviticus 18:5 John 19:30 John 19:30

      • Josh sets Jeremiah’s promise and Jesus’ final cross declaration as bookends: God says, ‘I will do it,’ and Jesus says, ‘It is finished.’
      • If salvation rested on Josh’s promises to God, he says he would have lost it years ago. His hope rests entirely on God’s promises to him; this is the first better promise, amazing grace.

    3. 25:01 The second promise is genuine internal transformation

      Scripture: 1 John 2:1

      • God not only forgives sinners but changes them. Instead of writing law only on stone and issuing commands, he gives a new nature that desires obedience.
      • Josh asks listeners to notice changed desires after salvation: former loves may now grieve them, ignored things matter, and overlooked people become visible. The change is incomplete, and anyone who sins still has an advocate in the Lord.
      • Christianity is neither behavior modification nor performance. Genuine heart change makes believers different, and Josh says Jesus is the only explanation.

    4. 26:24 The third promise is complete forgiveness

      Scripture: Hebrews 8:12

      • God does not offer another probationary chance to prove worth. Josh quotes the promise to be merciful and remember sins and lawless deeds no more.
      • This is not divine amnesia; an all-knowing God does not forget. It means he will never hold those sins against his people again: the Judge closed the case, the debt was paid, and Christ’s blood settled the record.
      • Because God no longer holds forgiven sin against believers, they are free to stop holding it against themselves.

    5. 27:22 Remember the Savior’s mercy more than the failure

      Scripture: Psalm 126:3 Psalm 126:3 Psalm 23:4 Psalm 126:3

      • Josh calls fixation on past failure an obstacle even for mature Christians and quotes Psalm 126: ‘The Lord has done great things for us, and we are filled with joy.’
      • Christians often replay pits, valleys, wrong turns, regrets, and shame while scarcely mentioning the mercy that lifted them out; Josh says Satan uses that replay to deceive and discourage believers. The wise believer remembers the Shepherd, the Savior, and the sufficient payment rather than becoming an expert in failure and an amateur in God’s faithfulness.
      • Healing begins by agreeing with God about forgiven sin. Josh calls continued debate with God sheer arrogance—not because sin was small, but because Christ’s grace is greater—and says trusting grace changes a testimony from ‘look how badly I failed’ to joy over what the Lord has done.

    6. 29:52 The fourth promise is eternal blessing grounded in an indestructible life

      Scripture: John 19:34 Hebrews 7:16

      • Everything under the old covenant eventually faded: tabernacle, priesthood, sacrifices, and temple. Josh uses Rome’s spear to picture the destruction of Jerusalem and the temple, the scattering of priests, and the confirmation of Jesus’ death.
      • Rome could not destroy Jesus. The covenant rests not on a building but on the risen Savior, and because he lives forever, it stands forever. Jesus possesses the power of an indestructible life.

    7. 30:48 Hebrews’ many ‘betters’ lead to a better sanctuary

      Scripture: Hebrews 1:1-2, 4; 7:19, 22-28; 8:6; 9:23; 10:34; 11:16, 35, 40; 12:24

      • Josh jokes about earning a B on a college paper cataloging Hebrews’ ‘betters,’ then rapidly names better revelation, superiority to angels, hope, priesthood, covenant, promises, sacrifice, possessions, country, resurrection, provision, and blood.
      • He says his younger self missed the better sanctuary. Hebrews 9 presents the earthly tabernacle as a visual sermon: everything pointed forward and nothing earthly was the final destination.

  5. 31:40 The earthly tabernacle preached restricted access and an unfinished conscience

    • Hebrews 9’s rooms, curtains, priestly checkpoints, and Day of Atonement rites form a visual sermon of distance. Josh uses TSA, the high priest’s preparations, the scapegoat, and a broken-ornament story to make the old sanctuary’s restricted, temporary, external ministry felt.

    1. 31:40 Hebrews 9 introduces the better sanctuary

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:1-15

      • Josh states the chapter’s point: the earthly tabernacle was a visual sermon pointing to heaven’s greater sanctuary.
      • The first covenant’s worship regulations and sanctuary were not the destination but a forward-pointing copy.

    2. 31:59 The tabernacle’s rooms and furnishings mapped degrees of access

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:1-5

      • Josh reads Hebrews 9:1–5: the Holy Place held the lampstand and consecrated bread; behind the second curtain, the Most Holy Place held the incense altar and covenant ark.
      • He names the manna jar, Aaron’s budded staff, covenant tablets, cherubim, and atonement cover, while following Hebrews in declining a detailed treatment of each item.

    3. 32:33 The paroket said ‘closed off,’ like spiritual airport security

      • Josh calls the inner curtain the paroket, meaning ‘closed off,’ and describes it as thick, many-corded, and colorful. Its message was that access to the Holy of Holies was barred.
      • He compares the tabernacle to inconsistent TSA checkpoints: shoes and laptops may be handled differently, travelers can be publicly corrected, and every stage controls who may proceed.
      • In the sanctuary, every curtain and priestly process preached, ‘not yet, not here, not this way, not you.’

    4. 34:27 The high priest prepared for the Day of Atonement under exacting restrictions

      Scripture: Leviticus 16:1-34

      • Josh asks listeners to remember the high priest’s week whenever airport security searches them. He describes isolation from home, protection from uncleanness, repeated washings, heart preparation, and a sleepless night of prayer and Scripture.
      • On Yom Kippur the priest washed, wore unstained white linen, and entered with blood for his own sins. Josh locates the ceremony in Leviticus 16 and says it involved three bulls and three successive entries—first for his own sins, then for the priests, and finally for all Israel—with a fresh full bath and new white linen between entries.
      • The ephod’s tribal stones pictured the people carried on the priest’s heart as he represented them before God. The public watched because their sins hung in the balance.

    5. 36:26 The scapegoat pictured sin carried away

      Scripture: Leviticus 16:7-10, 15-22

      • After the final emergence, two goats were used: one was sacrificed and its blood applied; the other bore blood-marked wool and was driven into the wilderness.
      • Josh presents the departing goat as a picture of the people’s sins being carried away, then asks whether the congregation is exhausted merely hearing the process.

    6. 37:00 Only one man entered God’s room, once a year, and never without blood

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:6-7

      • Josh asks the church to imagine living with one room representing God’s presence while knowing they could never enter it. Ceremonies, arrangements, checkpoints, and cost all reinforced distance.
      • He reads Hebrews 9:6–7: priests regularly entered the outer room, but only the high priest entered the inner room, only once a year, and never without blood for himself and the people’s ignorant sins.

    7. 37:55 An earthly, movable sanctuary could never transform a worshiper’s heart

      • Because the sanctuary was earthly and local, it had to be carried, cleaned, repaired, and moved. Josh lightens the point by imagining a dropped lampstand and recounting how he accidentally spilled Rick’s Christmas ornaments during a Crossroads Cares move.
      • The sanctuary was inaccessible, temporary, and external. Blood on the mercy seat could not change the worshiper’s heart. However carefully built, it remained only a copy.

  6. 39:09 Christ opened the way, cleansed the conscience, and secured eternal redemption

    • The Spirit used the first tabernacle to show that the way in remained closed. Jesus entered the heavenly original with his own blood once for all, clearing guilt, opening God’s presence, and guaranteeing an eternal inheritance.

    1. 39:09 The Holy Spirit showed that the way in had not yet been disclosed

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:8-10

      • Josh identifies Hebrews 9:8 as the reason for the tabernacle discussion. As long as the first tent functioned, the Spirit was showing that entry into the Most Holy Place remained closed.
      • Hebrews calls the arrangement an illustration for the present time: gifts, sacrifices, food rules, drink rules, washings, and external regulations could not clear the worshiper’s conscience and applied only until the new order.

    2. 39:42 All the procedures still left guilt in place

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:12-14

      • After every ritual, the conscience remained uncleared, guilt remained, and the heart remained unchanged.
      • Josh compresses the contrast: man entered the copy with bulls’ and goats’ blood; Jesus entered the original with his own blood.

    3. 40:01 Christ entered the greater tabernacle once for all by his own blood

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:11-12

      • Josh reads Hebrews 9:11–12: Christ came as high priest of the good things now present and passed through the greater, perfect tabernacle outside this creation.
      • He entered the Most Holy Place not with goats’ and calves’ blood but once for all with his own, obtaining eternal redemption. Josh repeatedly contrasts eternal redemption with annual, temporary, or probationary redemption.

    4. 40:47 Christ’s unblemished self-offering cleanses the conscience for service

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:13-15

      • Hebrews 9:13–15 contrasts the outward cleansing achieved by animal blood and heifer ashes with the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself unblemished to God.
      • Christ’s sacrifice cleanses the conscience from death-dealing acts so people may serve the living God. As new-covenant mediator, his ransom frees people from first-covenant sins so the called receive the promised eternal inheritance.

    5. 41:27 The old covenant reached the gate; Jesus brings his people all the way in

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:8-15 Hebrews 9:15

      • Josh restates the contrast: the old covenant displayed holiness and reminded people of sin, but Jesus gives God’s presence and removes sin.
      • The old covenant could not clear guilt; Jesus cleanses completely. Entering heaven with his own blood secured an inheritance no earthly priest could give—eternal, stable, purchased by Christ, and guaranteed by Christ rather than dependent on human performance.

    6. 42:12 The reigning and interceding Jesus guarantees his people’s future

      Scripture: John 6:39 Hebrews 6:19-20 Hebrews 7:25; Romans 8:34 John 14:3

      • Josh tells Christians their future is not hanging by a thread: if they belong to Jesus, he will not lose them. Their future is anchored to heaven’s throne.
      • The Jesus who gave his life now reigns, the one who shed his blood now prays, and the one who entered heaven ahead of his people will bring them there.

    7. 42:40 Everything in the world fades, but Jesus and his covenant remain

      • Hebrews’ repeated answer is that Jesus is better. Health, possessions, accomplishments, and even the deepest earthly relationships fade or last only for a season.
      • Jesus remains; his covenant, promises, salvation, and inheritance remain forever.

  7. 43:31 Stop trying to reopen a finished case; rest, serve, and walk through the torn curtain

    • Josh closes by telling believers to live from Christ’s finished work rather than for a still-pending verdict, then prays for seekers, forgiven Christians, the church’s character, and communion-shaped remembrance of the cross and resurrection.

    1. 43:31 The verdict is rendered, the debt paid, and the curtain torn

      Scripture: John 19:30 Matthew 27:51; Mark 15:38; Luke 23:45 Hebrews 12:2 1 Timothy 2:5

      • If Christ’s inheritance is forever, Christians must stop acting as though God’s favor is still being earned or their courtroom case remains undecided.
      • Josh announces the verdict: ‘It is finished’—tetelestai. The sufficient sacrifice has been offered, the curtain torn beyond repair, and the inheritance secured.
      • Believers should lift their heads and fix their eyes on Jesus: better priest, covenant, sacrifice, sanctuary, and the only mediator between God and humanity.

    2. 44:20 Hebrews does not say ‘be better’; it says Jesus has already done it

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:14

      • The message is not to try harder or move faster to become acceptable. Jesus has already accomplished salvation, so his people can live for him.
      • Because Jesus is better, believers can rest in joy and join the church in serving the living God.

    3. 44:45 Prayer for seekers and for freedom from recovered guilt

      Scripture: Matthew 27:51; Mark 15:38; Luke 23:45

      • Josh prays for both devoted believers and people still exploring, asking God to do unseen ministry, speak to hearts, and open eyes and ears to see Jesus.
      • He repents for trying to retrieve what God washed away and for attempting to reestablish the barrier God tore down. He recommits to walking in Christ-earned freedom with joy, service, and obedience.
      • He asks the Spirit to form a church known for compassion, humility, generosity, and loud, unashamed praise—a people at whom God would smile.

    4. 46:29 Communion prayer pictures Calvary, resurrection, and the coming harvest

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:23-26 Matthew 27:33-50; Mark 15:22-37; Luke 23:33-46; John 19:17-30 1 Corinthians 15:20-23

      • As the church holds the elements representing Christ’s broken body and spilled blood, Josh asks them to picture Calvary—Golgotha, the place of the skull—and Jesus’ blood falling from his beaten body into the soil. He uses blood falling on the skull as a picture that humanity can be brought back to life.
      • He turns that image toward life from death: Christ is the firstfruits, and many will follow. Josh asks the Lord to bring his people wherever he goes and closes, ‘We love you forever. Amen.’
