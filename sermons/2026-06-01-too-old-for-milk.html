Overview

  1. 00:39 Hard Roads, Healing, and Counted Blessings

    • Josh opens by admitting that recent years have felt like one long traumatic injury and uses a hill covered with boulders to picture people moving through unequal amounts of pain.
    • His opening confession moves from bruising hardship to gratitude: Christ continues his work, the church has stood with him, and healing includes intentionally counting God’s gifts.

    1. 00:39 A hill full of boulders

      Scripture: Romans 12:15 Philippians 1:6

      • Some people seem to dodge life’s boulders like Keanu Reeves, while others strike every one; Josh pictures Christian community rejoicing with those who escape injury and mourning with those who arrive bruised, bleeding, and missing teeth.
      • The comedy does not minimize pain. It frames the shared confidence that God will complete what he began whether a person reaches the bottom barely scratched or badly beaten.

    2. 02:39 Pastoral vulnerability and a congregation’s kindness

      • Josh says a hard life inevitably enters pastoral ministry. He has preached angry, lonely, doubtful, and depressed rather than pretending the journey was a cakewalk.
      • He thanks the congregation for standing with him and being kind when he did not deserve it, says his brother Jesus is helping him heal, and accepts his own role in that process by practicing gratitude.

    3. 03:45 Breath, worship, and a common confession

      Scripture: Psalm 150:6

      • Josh counts the church building, the life filling it, breath for praise, and even sad songs sung together as gifts.
      • Different testimonies unite in one confession and one desire to magnify Jesus; Crossroads measures churches by faithfulness rather than mere growth and refuses to exchange God’s word for what is fashionable.

    4. 04:47 Freedom remembered and exercised

      • Following the nation’s Memorial Day remembrance, Josh honors those who died in service and traces the freedom to gather, train children in the Lord, and exercise God-given gifts through sacrifices extending back to the American founding.
      • He connects freedom with responsibility: the church wants to change how wounded people see the church, reject a works-based false gospel, display freedom, and form a recognizably familial community.

    5. 06:00 A serving family gives thanks

      • A first-time visitor sensed the family atmosphere before speaking with anyone. Josh concedes that the family is dysfunctional, then stresses its weekly work to make its home shine with love for God and admiration for Jesus’ sacrifice.
      • He leads a cheer of thanks, prays that opportunities will not be wasted, and tells prospective family members to sign up to meet needs because service is the church’s chosen method for changing perceptions.

  2. 06:58 Hebrews 5: Dull Hearing and the Tragedy of Arrested Growth

    • Josh warns that the text requires heavy spiritual weight training and serious self-examination. Age and church experience do not guarantee maturity, and immature believers harm other believers when they refuse to grow.

    1. 06:58 Maturity is not age, and immaturity is not harmless

      • The sermon’s central issue is maturity. Josh rejects the picture of baby Christians as harmless people with bottles and pacifiers because refusal to grow transfers pain to the rest of the body.
      • He directs the congregation to Hebrews 5:11 and prepares them for a rebuke aimed not at new Christians but at people old enough to know better.

    2. 07:44 The writer pauses before Melchizedek

      Scripture: Hebrews 5:11 Hebrews 7:3–7

      • Josh first reads Hebrews 5:11 in its default wording: much remains to be said, but explanation is hard because the hearers no longer try to understand.
      • The unfinished subject is Melchizedek, the priestly king who will occupy the coming weeks. Josh summarizes his lack of recorded genealogy and life boundaries, Abraham’s tithe and reception of blessing, and his typological resemblance to Jesus as a kingly priest forever.

    3. 08:35 The ESV exposes dull hearing

      Scripture: Hebrews 5:11

      • Josh explicitly names the ESV, says he will use it for the remainder of the message, and repeats Hebrews 5:11 as “hard to explain, since you have become dull of hearing.”
      • He expands the missed opportunity: the writer longs to display deeper historical and cosmic connections, more of Christ’s beauty, and more reason for awe, but cannot begin because the audience has become sluggish.
      • The same word later appears as slothful, sluggish, or lazy. Josh calls them spiritual couch potatoes and says no gifted or passionate communicator can overcome hearers committed to dullness.

    4. 09:45 Milk, solid food, and trained discernment

      Scripture: Hebrews 5:12–14

      • Hebrews 5:12–14 says people who ought to teach still need the elementary principles of God’s word, remain unskilled in righteousness, and require milk rather than solid food.
      • Solid food belongs to the mature, whose discernment has been trained through constant practice to distinguish good from evil.
      • The rebuke concerns arrested development: these believers should be teaching, discipling the next generation, sharing the gospel, and eating solid food, yet they repeatedly return to the bottle.

    5. 10:23 The “you might live on milk” examination

      • Josh confronts veteran churchgoers who have favorite translations, preachers, thermostat opinions, and detailed end-times charts but cannot clearly explain the gospel, forgive biblically, or discern truth from error.
      • His Jeff Foxworthy-style inventory asks whether the Bible opens only on Sunday, interest in Jesus requires a weekly emotional experience, clichés exceed known verses, and only nonconfrontational sermons are enjoyable.
      • Other signs include preferring loyalty over unity, teaching nobody after years in the faith, questioning God’s love in every trial, spotting others’ sin first, praying only as a last resort, confusing depth with argument-winning rather than killing sin, and requiring weekly persuasion to obey.

    6. 12:30 Cute in infancy, tragic in adulthood

      • A child in a high chair is adorable; a middle-aged man in one is tragic. Josh states the principle: what is adorable in infancy becomes deplorable in adulthood.
      • A child putting a LEGO in his nose can be funny, but an elder doing so would prompt a vote. A new convert’s unclean speech can show grace still at work, while the same behavior in a nineteen-year veteran exposes neglected growth.
      • As babies put anything in their mouths, immature believers ingest strange doctrine because they have not trained alongside the Holy Spirit to recognize bad teaching. Josh pictures that training as joining a book club with Jesus and progressing from ten-pound dumbbells to spiritual strength.

    7. 13:59 A trained gospel instinct and the danger of stopping between redemption and rest

      Scripture: Joshua 6:22–25 Psalm 34:8 1 Timothy 4:7–8 Hebrews 5:14 Hebrews 3:16–19 Hebrews 4:1–11

      • Josh says the gospel has become so ingrained that he can trace it through Rahab’s rescue in Joshua 6. He also invokes the prophet commanded to marry an unfaithful woman, but the transcript never verbally identifies that book and the allusion is therefore omitted from the ledger.
      • Having tasted the Lord’s goodness, he rejects distorted teaching like a toddler spitting out broccoli. Paul’s training regimen rejects godless myths and calls for godliness whose value reaches this life and the life to come.
      • Josh returns to Hebrews’ concern with discernment: Moses’ generation failed to enter the promise, and the intended hearers could do the same. Many trust Christ enough to leave Egypt but not enough to enter Canaan, living between redemption and rest like captives rather than sons and daughters.
      • He asks each listener to answer honestly whether he or she lives like a child or a captive.

  3. 15:56 Hebrews 6: Build Beyond the Foundation

    • The writer’s call is not to abandon Jesus or elementary truth but to master the foundation and move deeper into Christ. Josh applies that upward movement to basic obedience, relational unity, worship preferences, and end-times arguments.

    1. 15:56 Leave elementary doctrine and go on to maturity

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:1–3

      • Josh reads Hebrews 6:1–3: move beyond repeatedly laying foundations of repentance, faith, washings, laying on of hands, resurrection, and eternal judgment, proceeding if God permits.
      • He compares the move to advancing from elementary school to university and from addition to derivatives. Growth does not discard the ABCs or basic mathematics; it masters them and builds higher.

    2. 16:40 Deeper into Jesus, never beyond him

      • Josh warns against any supposed maturity that moves beyond Jesus. Christ is the fulfillment toward which everything points and the cornerstone holding the structure together.
      • Believers cannot reach deeper realities while needing constant reminders that church attendance is good or while refusing to get along.

    3. 17:10 Basic obedience before advanced debates

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:1–3 Matthew 18:15–17 Ephesians 4:32

      • The church cannot move deeper while repeatedly debating whether every believer should give sacrificially, or while still learning the forgiveness that should flow from having been forgiven.
      • Josh invokes Matthew 18’s command to go directly to the person involved. Singing before God while nursing a grievance contradicts Christianity’s foundation.
      • He asks why people want mastery of shadows, types, and eschatological systems when they will not practice reconciliation, and why washings, gifts, baptism, wall colors, lights, and haze should keep the church stalled.

    4. 18:18 Preferences, eschatology, and distracting church drama

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:2 Revelation 9:3–10 Revelation 9:1

      • Josh says worship music is a preference: Elijah’s leadership will naturally reflect Elijah’s taste, and Josh raises his hands even when a song is not his favorite because heaven’s King deserves worship.
      • He admits that he sometimes complains too, then urges the church to move beyond drama that distracts from God’s glory and his precious Son.
      • Hebrews even places resurrection and eternal judgment among elementary subjects. Rather than obsessing over when Jesus returns, mature believers should live as if he will return and show the next generation why his first advent mattered.
      • Josh mocks the interpretation of Revelation 9’s locusts as Apache helicopters, then tests the method against the chapter’s fallen star. Christians belong to the welcoming committee, not the planning committee: get people ready, live for Christ’s return, and stop arguing like fourth graders.

    5. 20:32 Build on the foundation instead of sitting on it

      • Hearing weekly sermons and saying amen must become walking in the promise. No builder pours a foundation, installs a screen, and declares the structure complete.
      • Walls are assembled and raised because Christian life is designed for growth, greater impact, unity, and a shared display of God’s love in Jesus’ person and work.
      • Josh asks whether listeners are building or merely sitting on the foundation, then warns that some sedentary Christians may not realize what they are doing.

  4. 21:35 Hebrews 6:4–6: A Severe Warning Within Secure Salvation

    • Josh treats the falling-away passage as a serious hypothetical intended to help believers persevere. He rejects readings that make Christ’s sacrifice repeatable or deny the Spirit-shaped experience described, and he distinguishes a struggling believer from an apostate.

    1. 21:35 The impossible restoration passage

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:4–6

      • Josh reads Hebrews 6:4–6: people once enlightened, tasting the heavenly gift and God’s good word, sharing in the Holy Spirit and powers of the coming age, then falling away cannot be restored again while recrucifying and publicly shaming the Son.
      • He immediately acknowledges the text’s controversy and states that he believes the writer is using a hypothetical example.

    2. 22:22 Growth affects assurance and usefulness, not Christ’s finished salvation

      • Josh’s controlling clarification is that progress or laziness on the foundation does not alter salvation. Lazy Christians love Jesus; their laziness must still be confronted.
      • He has counseled people convinced they committed an unforgivable sin and could never be saved. His pastoral balance is to avoid false assurance for someone not born again while refusing to drive a true believer into despair.
      • The warning must remain serious enough to provoke perseverance without moving the saving bar above or below where Scripture places it.

    3. 23:25 Three rejected readings and the force of parapiptō

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:4–6

      • Josh first rejects the common view that true believers commit apostasy and permanently lose salvation. He notes that apostasia is absent and the text uses parapiptō, which he explains as falling alongside.
      • He also says the lose-salvation reading conflicts with teachers who allow later restoration, since this passage describes restoration as impossible under its stated condition.
      • He rejects the claim that the people were never true believers because they were enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, and shared in the Holy Spirit—not merely in benefits surrounding the Spirit’s work.
      • A third view confines the warning to first-century Hebrew Christians while the new covenant and temple system overlapped. Josh rejects it because the argument to this point concerns Christ’s priesthood, supremacy, and maturity rather than a specific temple circumstance.

    4. 24:08 The clear interprets the obscure

      Scripture: John 5:24 John 10:10

      • Josh states an interpretive rule: obvious passages should interpret obscure ones. He says many texts assure true believers that they cannot finally be lost and points to a homework slide he will not fully read.
      • He verbally names John 5:24 and John 10:10; only those spoken references are retained, while the inaccessible slide’s other references are not guessed. He encourages listeners to photograph the list, read ahead, and do their own work.

    5. 25:53 A once-for-all cross cannot be repeated

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:10–14

      • Josh frames the logic hypothetically: suppose a believer could completely fall away and lose salvation. Restoration would require Christ to be crucified again, which cannot happen.
      • The cross was neither temporary nor partial. Jesus does not die again whenever a believer struggles, stumbles, or wanders; his sacrifice is once for all.
      • Therefore the warning is not designed to make new Christians fear losing salvation next week, but to ignite experienced Christians who have grown lazy and dull.

    6. 26:30 Present contempt must give way to repentance

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:6

      • Josh slows down over verse 6: the text does not say restoration to repentance is absolutely impossible; it connects impossibility with the present action of recrucifying and exposing Jesus to contempt.
      • When that shameful treatment stops, Josh says repentance and renewed fellowship with God can follow.
      • The passage is meant to assure rather than terrorize: proper treatment of Jesus expresses maturity and assurance, while improper treatment shows the need to grow up and leave the bottle.

    7. 27:24 Ways professing believers mistreat Jesus

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:6 John 19:6, 15

      • People treat Jesus casually rather than supremely, want a Savior without receiving him as Lord, or reduce him to information instead of worship.
      • Josh criticizes online Christianity turned into an intellectual competition. Knowing truth without surrender creates hypocrisy—a Pharisee rather than a Christian.
      • Mistreatment symbolically joins Pilate’s crowd chanting for crucifixion and harms the believer. Because the Lord disciplines those he loves, Josh calls the church to repent, turn, stop, and let the offense go.

    8. 28:41 Struggling believers and apostates respond differently

      Scripture: Mark 9:24 Romans 7:15 Matthew 5–7; John 12:4–6; Luke 22:14–23, 47–48

      • A struggling believer asks for help with unbelief, grieves over doing what he hates, and fears dishonoring Jesus. An apostate says enough has been seen, abandons Christ, shrugs at sin, and no longer cares.
      • Judas heard the Sermon on the Mount, witnessed miracles, shared the table, handled money, and still betrayed Jesus with a kiss. Josh calls proximity to holy things without surrender the worst possible place.
      • Spiritual experience by itself therefore cannot prove salvation; the fruit produced under the rain will reveal the field.

  5. 29:58 Hebrews 6:7–10: Rain Reveals Soil and Faithfulness Outweighs Visibility

    • Two fields receive the same rain but yield different crops. Josh applies the picture first to hearts revealed by the gospel and then to mature service that keeps producing without measuring applause, reach, or outcomes.

    1. 29:58 Two fields, two outcomes, and better things

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:7–10

      • Josh reads Hebrews 6:7–10: cultivated land that drinks rain and produces a useful crop receives blessing, while land bearing thorns approaches curse and burning.
      • The writer nevertheless expects better things belonging to salvation because God does not overlook the hearers’ work and love shown by serving the saints.

    2. 30:32 The sower and work tested by fire

      Scripture: Matthew 13:1–23 1 Corinthians 3:13–15

      • The field illustration recalls Jesus’ parable of the sower in Matthew 13, which Josh assigns for further study.
      • It also recalls Paul’s teaching that fire tests Christian work. The captions give “1 Corinthians chapter 3 verse 6,” but the verbally described fire test identifies 1 Corinthians 3:13–15; the ledger preserves that identification with a note.

    3. 30:52 Rain reveals the soil

      • Josh’s first principle is that rain reveals soil. The same rain, gospel, sermon, Bible, church, warning, and offered forgiveness can soften one heart while another hardens.
      • He sees this divergence in ministry: during the Born Again series one visitor came to saving faith while another hearer called him a heretic. The rain was the same; the responses exposed the soils.

    4. 32:16 All believers bear the same kind of fruit

      Scripture: Matthew 7:17 Galatians 5:22–23 Hebrews 6:10

      • Believers do not produce equal quantities of fruit, but they produce the same kind because they share one Father; every good tree bears good fruit.
      • Josh names Galatians’ fruit of the Spirit—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control—then rereads Hebrews 6:10’s assurance that God sees work, love for his name, and service to the saints.

    5. 32:50 A field is evaluated by faithfulness, not awareness

      • A field does not know where its crop goes, choose recipients, measure impact, or stop because nobody congratulates it. It receives rain, remains rooted, and yields what it was designed to yield.
      • Josh calls this sustainable maturity. Against status, reach, likes, subscribers, and auditorium size, perseverance, endurance, and persistence create usefulness to God.
      • A healthy field produces for an audience of one without wondering who notices; it does not know whose table receives the harvest or whose life the crop sustains.

    6. 34:18 Stop counting the harvest and remain useful

      Scripture: John 15:4–5

      • Josh tells exhausted believers that obedience does not count only when outcomes can be measured or seen. God asks faithfulness in the field, not control of the harvest.
      • They may never know what prayers prevented or healed, what kindness softened, what consistency taught a child or spouse, or what one invitation, sermon, coffee, text, hug, conversation, pair of shoes, or act of integrity accomplished twenty years later.
      • The goal is not spectacular performance but consistent usefulness. Mature Christians let God decide where fruit falls and stay too busy abiding in Jesus to obsess over yield, trusting that God sees love, service, and perseverance.

  6. 35:41 Hebrews 6:11–12: Diligence, Assurance, and Imitation

    • The passage ends by joining diligent practice to assurance and telling believers to imitate faithful, patient people. Josh turns that summons into concrete requests for prayer, accountability, leadership, and pastoral care.

    1. 35:41 Show diligence to the end

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:11–12

      • Josh reads Hebrews 6:11–12 and commands the church not merely to believe truth but to live it.
      • Mature faith strengthens assurance: as believers heed the warning and walk with Christ, confidence in their hope grows rather than leaving them awake at night asking whether they are saved.

    2. 36:19 Seek help and imitate patient faith

      • Those who need prayer should ask, those who need accountability should establish it, and those who need honesty should invite it.
      • Josh tells listeners to find someone strong in faith and patiently waiting for Jesus, then learn from and imitate that person.
      • Join a life group; in time, gifted people should stop merely attending and lead. Maturity moves from receiving ministry to helping carry it.

    3. 36:47 Use the elders’ ministry

      • The elders do more than supervise Josh: they encourage the saints, pray over people, and have sworn before God to serve families.
      • The congregation must ask for help so the elders can fulfill that work. Refusing help while remaining stuck is another form of laziness.

  7. 37:06 Held by Christ: Assurance Stronger Than Human Grip

    • Josh closes the teaching by locating perseverance in Jesus’ hold rather than human willpower. Grace restrains sins, Christ keeps names secure, the risen and exalted Savior anchors the soul, and assurance frees believers to fight sin without living as though everything depends on them.

    1. 37:06 Grace restrains darkness believers never see

      • If God left people to themselves even briefly, desires within them could wreck marriages, poison souls, destroy witness, and harden hearts beyond recognition.
      • Josh recalls the film Nuremberg and its closing quotation: to know what people can do, look at what they have done. Great darkness is not safely outside the church; it is present in every person.
      • Believers should thank God not only for forgiven sins but for sins they never had the opportunity to commit because grace restrained them.

    2. 38:12 The testimony is Jesus’ grip, not ours

      Scripture: Romans 8:1 2 Corinthians 12:9

      • A Christian’s story is not how tightly that person held Jesus, but how tightly Jesus held that person.
      • Josh quotes Romans 8:1 and denies that no condemnation results from discipline, wisdom, attendance, willpower, intelligence, or performance. Christ himself holds his people, and without that hold they would fall farther and faster than imagined.
      • The gospel’s glory is that Christ’s grip never weakens, his hand does not slip, he never lets go, and his grace remains sufficient.

    3. 39:14 Names, the book of life, and a Savior who cannot fail

      Scripture: Revelation 3:5 Acts 2:24 Hebrews 7:25

      • Josh recounts an old-saints saying that believers’ names are written on Jesus’ hands. Because the transcript does not verbally identify a canonical passage and the saying does not reproduce one exact text, it is intentionally omitted from the ledger.
      • He then asks who could erase a believer’s name or seize and alter the book of life in heaven. His answer is no one: the Savior would have to fail before a believer could finally be lost.
      • Jesus does not fail, and death could not hold him. Josh therefore urges trust through emotional heights, temptation, suffering, and difficult forgiveness because salvation rests on the risen Christ’s finished work and continuing intercession.

    4. 40:54 The anchor holds in the storm

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:19–20 1 Corinthians 15:17

      • Josh anticipates Hebrews’ declaration that hope is an anchor for the soul. Calm water may hide drift, but wind, waves, and a life taking on water reveal what a person is tied to.
      • If Christ, grace, the cross, or his priesthood could fail, there would be no second anchor, backup Savior, or alternate rescue. If Jesus is insufficient, nobody sees God.
      • Paul’s warning that faith is worthless if Christ has not been raised becomes the reason Christians can rest and produce reverently: the anchor has passed through the veil.

    5. 41:47 The risen Christ receives the highest name

      Scripture: Philippians 2:9–11

      • God raised Jesus, and the one who restores life to the dead can do the same for his people.
      • Josh quotes Philippians 2: God exalted Jesus to the highest place so every knee in heaven, earth, and under the earth will bow and every tongue acknowledge his lordship to the Father’s glory.
      • Believers can therefore breathe, count blessings, fight sin, and pursue holiness without acting as though everything depends on the strength of their own grip; exhausted saints are held by nail-pierced hands.

    6. 42:44 A gospel warning and invitation

      Scripture: John 15:5 Romans 10:9

      • To those far from God, Josh warns that apart from Christ they can do nothing. Calm water can conceal approaching destruction, and wealth, morality, and religion cannot bear the weight of a human soul.
      • He quotes the promise that confessing Jesus as Lord and believing God raised him from the dead brings salvation.
      • The invitation is urgent: run to Jesus while mercy still calls.

  8. 43:22 Closing Prayer: Peaceful Pursuit and Remembered Cost

    • Josh prays against complacency and laziness, thanks God for assurance that permits imperfect attempts, longs for Christ’s return, and approaches communion remembering the cost of freedom and adoption.

    1. 43:22 Help us keep trying without fear

      • Josh asks the Father to prevent complacency and laziness. The text’s assurance that believers cannot be lost creates freedom to fail while continuing to try.
      • He thanks God for removing crushing pressure from Christian production and for giving peace even within the burden of pursuit.

    2. 44:13 Longing for Christ’s return

      • Josh longs for the moment Christ breaks open the sky and reveals himself to the world, and he declares Jesus worthy of it all.

    3. 44:33 The table remembers the price of peace

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:23–26

      • At the table, Josh remembers what peace, freedom, and the movement from slavery to sonship or daughterhood cost.
      • The communion remembrance closes in enduring love and prayer in Jesus’ name: “Amen.”
