Overview

  1. 00:34 Not a cute story

    Scripture: Genesis 5

    • We pick up in Genesis 5. Josh loves looking at the life of Noah, but it blows his mind that this story has been such a success with children. He gets the animals holding hands, or hooves, as they enter the ark two by two, but literally the entire planet dies.
    • This isn’t a cute story; it is a terrifying story about God’s righteous judgment: massive destruction, dead bodies floating around, and Noah’s family stuck in a boat with all those animals for months with no arrival time. Some couldn’t even drive to church without arguing with their family. “Are we there yet?” “No, Shem, and if you ask one more time I’m going to pull this boat over.”
    • Joking aside, these chapters teach us about humanity and our wickedness, and about God’s response to wickedness: his patience, love, mercy, goodness, and compassion. Josh’s prayer through this series is that people would see the goodness of Jesus. The most terrifying moments in history aren’t that terrifying if you have found peace with God through Jesus Christ.

  2. 02:35 Genesis so far

    Scripture: Genesis 1 Genesis 2 Genesis 3 Genesis 4 Genesis 5

    • A quick recap: Genesis 1 is creation and the preparation of the earth, with God the uncaused causer of all things. Genesis 2 is about Adam and Eve. Genesis 3 is about Satan and sin. Then we witness the first murder, between Cain and Abel, in Genesis 4, and it is pretty much downhill from there: the effects of sin and God’s plan for redemption throughout human history.
    • Genesis 5 has 32 verses covering nearly 1,700 years of family history. Josh won’t read Adam’s whole family line, but he wants to point out some things before discussing the flood.

  3. 03:23 Genesis 5: an outline of human history

    Scripture: Genesis 5:1–2

    • Genesis 5 is basically an outline of human history, and its first two verses recap creation in case you missed week one: this is the book of the generations of Adam; when God created man he made him in the likeness of God, male and female, and blessed them.
    • Adam is the first person who ever lived, and we are all his and Eve’s descendants. They sinned, and like a virus it spread; we are all born sinners. We might be cute, but we are wicked little people looking to get ours. Something is seriously wrong with the human condition: we were created in God’s likeness and it was very good, but sin entered, it isn’t good anymore, and we need a way back.

  4. 04:22 And then he died

    Scripture: Genesis 5:5–20

    • From Adam to Noah everyone lives a really long time. Josh jokes with the oldest members in the lobby: only 878 more years and you’ll catch Methuselah.
    • All the days that Adam lived were 930 years, and he died. Seth lived 912 years, and he died. Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared: they lived, and then they died. Enoch lived 365 years, and then he... hold up.

  5. 05:18 Enoch walked with God

    Scripture: Genesis 5:23–24 Romans 3:23 Romans 6:23

    • All the days of Enoch were 365 years. Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him. That’s different. Everyone else has babies in retirement and lives another 800 years, but not Enoch: Enoch walks with God.
    • Watch closely or you’ll miss Enoch’s importance. Everyone wants to talk about God’s judgment, and in Genesis it is hard to miss, but it isn’t just death and destruction. Notice where there is life: there is life when anyone walks with God. Adam and Eve walked with God in the cool of the day; Enoch, Noah, Abraham, David, Isaiah, John, and Paul walked with God. None were perfect, but they walked with God. Do you?
    • We are all born into this fallen world. Romans 3:23: for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. Romans 6:23: for the wages of sin is death. We will all live however many years we live and then die, vegan, fish oil, only carrots, it doesn’t matter. Aren’t you glad you came to church this morning?
    • If you want to conquer death, it isn’t about diet, exercise, supplements, genetics, or choosing not to skydive. The only way to conquer death is to walk with God. Do you know him, spend time with him, consider him day in and day out? Most people know how to make a child; it is another thing entirely to raise one. Checking boxes is one thing; walking with God, letting him lead you and praying for him to change you, is another.

    1. 08:01 Check yourself against the core values

      • To answer whether you are walking faithfully like Enoch, check yourself against the church’s core values. Anyone can line up with them temporarily; the question is whether you desire these things more and more.
      • Be doers: do you have a growing hunger and thirst not just to know his word but to be moved by it and obey it, even if you have to shred some things in your life? Be humble: do you walk around like your stuff doesn’t stink, flexing YouTube knowledge in lobby debates that you aren’t putting into practice, or are you washing feet and serving others?
      • Sing praise: are you anxious to worship him, or looking for ways to avoid it? Josh felt out of whack after missing last week’s corporate worship; he had been worshiping all week, but he missed their voices and the beauty of the saints singing together.
      • Show love, forgive freely, serve others, pray always: is your church family life radiating with these? To be honest, if you can leave the church you call home and not even say bye, it wasn’t home and you weren’t doing church. Are you praying with, forgiving, and serving your brothers and sisters? That is what Enoch’s life looks like.
      • Give generously: are you tithing? Have you ever looked at your paycheck and said, “God, you’re first. You gave me these abilities and opportunities. Take it, multiply it, grow your kingdom; if you bless me, I’ll become more generous, because that’s what you did for me. My wallet isn’t off limits to you”? God doesn’t ask for everything, just a slice. How much do you invest in your kingdom, and how much in his?
      • Suffer well: when hard times come, do you look up and praise him or shake your fist? How we suffer is our testimony to God’s goodness.

    2. 11:46 Like a child longs for his father

      • Josh’s son is a cuddler; physical touch is his language. On Monday he asked, “Daddy, will you cuddle with me?” and Josh about melted. He jumps on every chance to meet that need. Making a child isn’t the hard part; it takes a parent to raise that child.
      • Anyone can play church: pray, serve, give, align yourself. But do you desire these things? Do you know him, walk with him, and is he changing you? Do you long for him like a child longs for his father? That is what Enoch did.
      • Fun fact: only two people in the Bible didn’t die, Enoch and Elijah. They are just gone, and what they have in common is that they walked with God.

  6. 13:10 Methuselah or Enoch: who lived the better life?

    Scripture: Genesis 5:25–27

    • At the back end of the genealogy, all the days of Methuselah were 969 years, and he died: the oldest man in the Bible. Enoch lived 365 years; Methuselah lived 969. Church quiz: who lived the better life?
    • Everyone seems to think the key to a great life is longevity, and there is nothing wrong with a long life, but there is more to it. Josh would rather have 20 years with God than 80 without him.
    • He has seen it play out at baptisms. There is never anything sad about someone being baptized into Christ, but older folks sometimes come out of the water and have to deal with a little shame and sadness, feeling they missed a whole life they could have spent walking with the Lord. There is more value in one year walking with the Lord than a hundred without him. It isn’t just how long or how well you live, but who you live for.

  7. 14:49 Lamech and Noah

    Scripture: Genesis 5:28–31 Genesis 5:32

    • We meet Lamech, Noah’s father, who named his son Noah, saying that out of the ground the Lord has cursed, this one shall bring us relief from our work and the painful toil of our hands. Lamech lived 777 years, and then he died.
    • Verse 32 says Noah was 500 years old before he became a parent. To struggling parents: you aren’t alone; parenting is hard. This dude had the wisdom to wait 500 years before trying to raise a human being; you took what, 25 years? Your brain wasn’t even fully developed. Maybe that’s why you did it.

  8. 15:45 Genesis 6: the wickedness of man and the patience of God

    Scripture: Genesis 6 Genesis 6:1–2 Genesis 6:1–8

    • In nearly 1,700 years of human history in Genesis 5 there is evidence of only one man who walked faithfully with God. In chapter 6, as the world fills with sinners and little grandbaby sinners, we hear about Noah: when man began to multiply, the sons of God saw that the daughters of man were attractive and took as wives any they chose.
    • Some weird stuff gets floated about these first eight verses, but their clear purpose is to expose two timeless truths that aren’t up for debate: the wickedness of the human race and the patience of God. Three points draw debate: who the sons of God and daughters of man are, verse 3, and verse 4.

    1. 16:51 Verse 3: 120 years

      Scripture: Genesis 6:3 Genesis 11 Deuteronomy 34:7 1 Peter 3:20

      • “My Spirit shall not abide in man forever, for he is flesh; his days shall be 120 years.” Some say God is cutting off the human lifespan at 120, and that is possible; it does seem to be the human lifespan. But the genealogy in Genesis 11 mentions that Shem, son of Noah, lived 500 years, and that is two years after the flood, so the first man to die at 120 is actually Moses: the end of Deuteronomy says Moses was 120 years old when he died, yet his eyes weren’t weak nor his strength gone. He still had some left in the tank.
      • Josh thinks God, capable of multitasking, did two things in verse 3: limiting evil with lifespan limits and exercising patience before sending the flood. First Peter tells us of those who were disobedient long ago when God waited patiently in the days of Noah while the ark was being built: that 120 years.
      • God knew he was about to judge sin on earth, yet despite humanity’s wickedness he was still showing patience and offering time to repent. It isn’t that God is slow; he could have sent the flood right then. We are wicked; God is patient.

    2. 18:35 Verse 4: the Nephilim

      Scripture: Genesis 6:4 Genesis 7:23

      • The Nephilim were on the earth in those days, the mighty men of old, the men of renown. Some say they were giants: picture Shaquille O’Neal or The Rock, maybe Goliath’s ancestors. But Josh isn’t sure how they survived the flood, since Genesis 7 says every living thing on the face of the earth was wiped out; only Noah was left, and those with him in the ark.
      • He doesn’t think they were giants like the BFG. They were exactly what it says: men of renown. Everyone knew them and wanted to go to their party: the rich, famous, larger-than-life elite of Noah’s day, popular, successful, powerful, corrupt, and influential.

    3. 20:06 The sons of God and the daughters of man

      Scripture: Matthew 24:38–39

      • There are three views: the sons of God are fallen angels, or royalty, or, the simplest answer and usually the best, men who claimed to walk with God marrying women who wanted nothing to do with God. Ungodly marriages were jacking everything up.
      • Jesus describes the same period in Matthew 24: in the days before the flood people were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, up to the day Noah entered the ark, and they knew nothing about what would happen until the flood came and took them away. That is how it will be at the coming of the Son of Man. Unlike Enoch, they were living it up and not walking with God.

  9. 21:12 Verse 5: only evil continually

    Scripture: Genesis 6:5

    • Wickedness was the theme of daily life, and the Lord noticed: he saw that the wickedness of man was great and that every intention of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.
    • Theologians call this total depravity: everything about us, minds, hearts, emotions, intentions, thoughts, is corrupted by sin. Here is the great confusion: we are made in God’s image, and the greatness of the painter was meant to be seen in the greatness of the painting, but instead of doing great good we do great evil.
    • This isn’t occasional slip-ups, an “oops.” Left to our own devices we choose evil continually. We still have the greatness of his image, but it is stained by sin, all of it. Straight from the lips of God: “I know your heart. It’s wicked, only evil all the time.”

  10. 22:41 God grieved: he knows rejection too

    Scripture: Genesis 6:6 Malachi 3:6

    • God’s response may sound odd if you see him as far off, impersonal, and unrelatable. He isn’t far off; the incarnation of Christ shows he is with us and among us. And God has emotions like you and me, except that he has a handle on them: his anger is righteous, his jealousy is right, his justice is right, his love is unconditional.
    • The Lord regretted that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him to his heart. “Grieved” is the same word Isaiah uses for how an abandoned wife feels. Many know that rejection, and some are in it now. God isn’t some impersonal being on cosmic overwatch; he knows rejection too. He has felt it, endured it, and been patient through it. Don’t give up: the flood waters and the winds obey him, and he will bring you back because he said he would.
    • Some say God’s regret means he changed his mind, but those who know the Lord know he doesn’t change; through Malachi he says, “For I the Lord do not change.” If God regretted us to the point of not wanting humanity to exist, why have Noah build the ark at all? His regret is his pain and sorrow; as Jesus wept over Jerusalem, God wept over the condition of humanity.

  11. 25:13 Justice or mercy

    Scripture: Genesis 6:7–8

    • God responds to sin in one of two ways, justice or mercy, and verses 7 and 8 show both: “I will blot out man whom I have created... for I am sorry that I have made them.” But Noah found favor in the eyes of the Lord.
    • Many picture God as an irrational dictator instead of the patient father he is. In nearly 1,700 years of history, one man is mentioned as walking faithfully with God while generations dove headfirst into corruption and scandal, and nobody else seemed to care.
    • People object, “That’s not fair,” but you only think it unfair if you are a sinner, because sinners don’t like justice unless it is for somebody else. If someone intended only evil toward you all the time and wouldn’t stop unless wiped out, you would want justice. But if you were the wicked one, would you want justice or mercy? We scream for justice until it is aimed at us. God seeks justice even when it is aimed at his Son. There is only one who is good.

  12. 27:18 Why Noah? He found grace

    Scripture: 1 Peter 3:20 Genesis 6:9 Genesis 6:8 Hebrews 13:8

    • But God also responds with mercy. Meet Noah. Spoiler alert: he is going to build a big boat, and God will spare him and his family; Peter tells us eight souls received God’s mercy.
    • Why Noah? Some go to the next verse: he walked with God, he was blameless, he was righteous. But if you have been told God chose Noah as a standout candidate with a spotless resume, you got bad information. There was nothing special about Noah. The idea that God loves good people and hates bad people, so the good get on the boat and the bad had better swim, isn’t taught anywhere in Scripture. There are no good people, just bad people; God was grieved over all of mankind, including Noah.
    • Don’t miss the order of operation: Noah found favor, grace, in the eyes of the Lord. Noah was righteous and faithful because of God’s grace; God’s grace wasn’t a result of Noah’s righteousness. It is the first time we see the Hebrew word for grace in the Bible, hen. Noah wasn’t special; he heard God’s voice and responded.
    • Hebrews 13 says Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever. Nothing has changed: we are saved by grace. You don’t deserve it or earn it, so there is no glory, boasting, pride, trophy, or credit for you. We boast in the cross, in being loved, in his grace. Josh was wicked, God changed him and is still changing him, and the only explanation he has is God.

    1. 30:01 “Are you good?”

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:8

      • Frustrated trying to work through the text with a Jehovah’s Witness, Josh asked her, “Are you good?” She tried to convince him she was because of what she was doing: “I’m on your front porch, so I’m good.” Every world religion tries to make you feel you are good. Christianity says you don’t have a grain of good in you; you are so far from good you wouldn’t let good in if he were knocking on your front door.
      • For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith, and this is not from yourselves; it is the gift of God.

    2. 31:00 Grace upon grace

      Scripture: Romans 8:37

      • Imagine Josh’s surprise that after receiving salvation God keeps hitting him with blessings. For most of his life his relationships were a mess, one toxic relationship after another, and even how he met his wife might make some cringe. Then God went to work on them.
      • He wakes up next to the most beautiful woman in the world, who was somehow convinced by God that he was husband material: grace. Two children who love Jesus: grace. He and his wife have been sad, rejoiced, buried loved ones, lost, and won together: grace. From toxic relationships to a God-honoring marriage, from hating the church to leading her, walking away from addictions even after going into ministry: grace upon grace upon grace.
      • It all started when Jesus said, “Trust me, Josh. I took your sin so you can take my righteousness. I want to walk with you, love you, be your Savior and Lord. I’m going to do all the work, and you’re going to be a co-heir with me.” “Okay, Jesus, I’m down.”
      • To those screaming that God is unfair: isn’t that completely unfair? Christ died for our sins, conquered sin and death, and will reign forever, and Josh gets to reign with him, though he is one of the reasons Jesus endured the cross. Yet he is more than a conqueror through him who loved him.
      • You don’t clean up your life hoping God will love you; you clean it up in response to his love.

  13. 33:30 Grace is like the ark: there was room

    • Noah’s priorities changed immediately when he received God’s grace, and yours will too.
    • God’s grace operates a lot like Noah’s ark: those who walk with him are shielded from the judgment happening around them, and that should affect our behavior. The ark wasn’t just for the animals or the eight souls; it was for anyone who would listen to Noah preach. The idea that it was jam-packed with giraffe necks out the windows and no room is ridiculous. There was room.
    • The church is like a modern-day Noah pointing to Christ as the ark. The only question is who you need to invite in.

  14. 34:25 He has already spoken to you

    • Josh loves reading the communication between the Lord and Noah, and God doesn’t speak directly to Noah until after Noah has received grace. Skeptics tell Josh they’ll trust God when he speaks to them. Why so anxious to wait? God can reach you through a burning bush or blind you while you hike Camelback Mountain, but he has already spoken to you, and he is speaking to you right now.

  15. 35:08 Noah did all that God commanded

    Scripture: Genesis 7–8 Genesis 7:5 John 14:15 1 John 4:19

    • Another mark of someone walking faithfully, seen several times in chapters 7 and 8: Noah did all that God commanded. God said build, and he built; God said collect the animals, and he collected them. Josh’s dog won’t sit when he says sit after months of trying, and Noah has seven days to collect these animals before the rain. Are you kidding?
    • Part of being faithful is saying yes to the task even when you can’t see how it is possible: “God, I’ll do whatever you say, go wherever you lead, say whatever you want. I’d rather fail with you than succeed without you.”
    • Noah did all that the Lord commanded, and nothing has changed in how we follow Christ: God commands, Noah obeys; Christ teaches, we obey. Jesus told his disciples, “If you love me, you’ll keep my commands.” Noah loved the Lord.
    • This desert dweller builds a massive boat without knowing how; God reminds him to put pitch in the cracks. Josh and his son can’t agree on how to build a Lego set, yet Noah and his three sons build God’s ark. Because Noah loved the Lord he obeyed, but don’t forget God loved Noah first. That hasn’t changed: 1 John 4, we love because he first loved us.

  16. 37:35 Your family is your first ministry

    Scripture: Genesis 7 1 Timothy 5:8

    • There is a moment in chapter 7 to see: Noah finishes the build, loads the animals, and boards his family. Pause there: your family is your number one ministry. Paul says he hopes you would stay single to be fully dedicated to the Lord, but if you marry and split your dedication, there is nothing wrong with that; you had better care for that family and lead them well.
    • Scripture pulls no punches. Paul tells the young pastor Timothy that anyone who does not provide for their relatives, and especially their own household, has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever. Do everything in your power to get them in the boat.

  17. 38:29 “The Lord shut him in”

    Scripture: Genesis 7:16 2 Peter 2:5

    • Josh couldn’t shake something in chapter 7 all week: the Lord shut him in. A pastor’s kid who grew up in church, he has heard plenty of lobby talk about God opening doors, and that is a good way to give God credit for what he is doing even when we can’t see it. But we don’t celebrate him enough for the doors he shuts, partly because we don’t see what we are missing.
    • If Josh had married any number of the girls he dated before his wife, his life would be a total wreck. They are probably great people now, but back then so was he a mess. Instead of complaining that your preferred doors aren’t opening, thank him for the ones he shuts.
    • Peter calls Noah a preacher of righteousness. Josh imagines, without claiming to know, that standing behind that big door was bittersweet. Noah would see God’s justice and live to tell about it, but his neighbors, extended family, and school buddies would witness it and not live. Josh pictures him pleading as the door shut: “Stop sinning, repent, walk with me in faith. There’s room for everyone. Just come in. Just trust God.”

  18. 40:43 Don’t let the door shut

    Scripture: Matthew 24:37 2 Peter 3:9 Matthew 24:36

    • Josh pleads: don’t let the door shut before you decide to give your life to Christ. Stop waiting. Jesus promised he would come again and connected it to Noah: as it was in the days of Noah, so it will be at the coming of the Son of Man. The Lord was grieved because his creation rejected him and kept living as they pleased; they did it then and are doing it now, and just as the flood came out of nowhere, so will the sword.
    • The first judgment was by water, and that will never happen again; God’s covenant with Noah is set in the sky, and you might even see it today. He promised. Speaking of wickedness, our culture has taken the symbol of that promise and made it a symbol of pride to boast in sexual immorality.
    • They mock: “Where is he? He said he was coming back. Where’s your Jesus now?” Believers know he isn’t slow and hasn’t forgotten; he is patient, giving every possible opportunity to trust him. He doesn’t want anyone to perish; he wants us saved by his wonderful grace, receiving a righteousness not our own through faith in Christ. He waits because the second judgment will be by fire.
    • Come to Jesus. No one knows the day or the hour the door will shut, but when it does, we would rather you be on the inside. Believe, be baptized, and be saved.

  19. 42:55 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays about the day the door shuts and God puts in motion the finality of his plans. All he can think of is the joy of seeing him and knowing every doubt, rejection, and struggle this side of eternity was worth it. “Come, Lord. Prepare your people; use us.” He is mesmerized by God’s patience, thanks him for being patient so that he could come too, and asks God to lead him to those who need to hear this message.
