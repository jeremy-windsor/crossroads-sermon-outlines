Overview

  1. 00:20 A word to mothers

    Scripture: Psalm 34:18

    • Josh opens on Mother’s Day by acknowledging that a day like this can stir up joy and pain: grief over a loss, longing for something unfulfilled, or a season of loneliness. God sees you; Psalm 34 reminds us the Lord is close to the brokenhearted.
    • The world wants to rob mothers of their God-given purpose of having and raising the next generation, telling young girls the greatest thing they can do is rise to a position normally reserved for a man, when in actuality it is motherhood. Moms and grandmoms are raising the next generation of pastors, preachers, mothers, CEOs, and Christians who will carry the gospel into the workforce.
    • Don’t find your worth in what others say about you as a mom; find it in the immeasurable and unconditional love of Jesus who calls you his own. In his hands nothing is wasted. “Be a daughter first and then let that spring into motherhood.”

  2. 02:34 Ephesians 2 isn’t hard to understand; it’s hard to accept

    Scripture: Ephesians 2

    • In week three of Ephesians, Josh says he has read these 10 verses probably 50 times in the last seven days and isn’t sure he can make them any clearer. “It’s not the reading. It’s not the understanding. It’s the accepting.”
    • When you encounter the truth, you either let it punch you around and surrender to it, or you decide you don’t like it, punch back, toss it aside, and create your own.

    1. 03:33 Why people reject the truth

      Scripture: Romans 1:25

      • Most people don’t reject the truth because they don’t understand it; they reject it because it is inconvenient and they don’t like what it demands. The lie is usually easier on us, with less friction, so many take what Jesus called the wide road.
      • Some reject the truth because they love the world they can see. Some reject it because it might cost them relationships, which Josh sees more within the church than anywhere else. God’s truth demands an admission of guilt, a guilty plea, so many do what Paul mentions in Romans 1: they exchange the truth about God for a lie, because it opposes behaviors they would rather not give up.
      • Do the math: the proper response to the truth is humility, but pride, fear, comfort, bitterness, pleasures, and stubbornness all stand in the way. There are many options for stepping into a lie and few for stepping into the truth. The truth isn’t complicated, but the lies are a mighty web, and Josh has prayed all week that the Lord would help open eyes to this great deception.

    2. 06:01 A lot on the line: surrender or suppress

      Scripture: John 5:28–29 Romans 1:20

      • There is a lot on the line. Jesus says in John 5 that all who are in their graves will hear his voice and come out, some to live and some to be condemned. A spiritual battle is being fought for the hearts, souls, and minds of mankind, and it has eternal implications.
      • Romans 1:20, a memory verse in Josh’s family, says God’s invisible qualities have been clearly seen and understood from what has been made, so that people are without excuse. No excuses: we either surrender to the truth or suppress it, and today each person will face that choice.

  3. 07:04 Don’t forget who you were

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:1–2 Ephesians 1 Ephesians 2:3

    • Josh reads Ephesians 2:1–2: you were dead in your transgressions and sins when you followed the ways of this world and the ruler of the kingdom of the air.
    • Chapter 1 closed by describing the church: Christ is head over everything for the church, we are his body, connected to him and on mission for him. Chapter 2 begins by reminding the church who they were before they heard about Christ. “You’re in Christ now. Praise God. Don’t you dare forget who you were apart from Christ.”
    • Paul says “As for you,” and in verse 3 he says “All of us.” He is talking about you and me.

    1. 07:55 We always think of “them”

      • This is where the lies begin. When we think of nasty, wicked, vile, godless people, we think of them, the people out there. We can’t hear one sentence without suppressing the truth, and we start to sound like the Pharisees: “Thank you, Lord. I’m not like them.”
      • We do it as a nation too, treating other nations and religions as the problem, while those we call bad look at the church and say we started the wars and crusades. Religious people killed Jesus. Religion makes people proud; Jesus makes people humble, and the religious leaders killed him because they didn’t want to be humbled.

    2. 09:13 The one who knows the most must suppress the most

      • Jesus spoke so harshly against the religious elite because they knew the most, which means they had to suppress the most. That is frightening, Josh says, because it means he is the worst person in the room.
      • His calling is to spend all week in the word to bless the church with it, which means he has to ignore the most and go to the greatest lengths to distance himself from the truth when he sins, because he is surrounded by it constantly.

    3. 10:19 Every cleanup makes a new mess

      • We point at those who came before us to prove we are good and they were bad: we are educated and sophisticated and just cleaning up their messes. We have come a long way, but every time we clean up a mess, we seem to make a new one. Josh plays a video his son sent him as a picture of the argument.
      • The great women of old who wanted their voices heard would hardly be proud to see women’s sports forfeited to men. We say you can’t own a human being, and slavery is wrong, but if you get pregnant and don’t want the baby, you can kill it. Religious violence, sexual depravity, broken families, and divorce treated like a high school breakup: every generation points the finger at the next instead of investing in it.

    4. 13:03 No one to point to

      • We love categories: good and bad, religious and irreligious, rich and poor, educated and uneducated, Republican and Democrat. They can help in navigating society, but Scripture is clear that none of them matter. There are bad people and Jesus; there is no one to point to.
      • It is the same approach Josh and Kristen take in marriage counseling: first find where the blame is being placed, because struggling marriages shift blame when what they need is to own it.

  4. 13:48 Dead and disobedient

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:3

    • Josh reads verse 3: all of us also lived among them at one time, gratifying the cravings of our sinful nature, and like the rest we were by nature deserving of wrath. Paul describes the unsaved person as dead and disobedient.
    • We want to fight this: “No, wait, Josh, I’m spiritually alive. If anything, I’m sick or wounded.” But if you don’t have Jesus, you are dead. God speaks and we don’t listen; God moves and we don’t see; God loves and we don’t respond. We are not sinners because we sin; we sin because we are sinners. There are sinners and Jesus, and unless someone has an announcement to make, you are a sinner.

    1. 14:44 Dead in a home dedicated to the gospel

      • Josh was this way for the first 22 years of his life. He grew up under a great preacher and went to church every week, but he went to get girls and have fun, not to hear the preacher. He heard about Jesus almost every week and it didn’t affect him; he knew what he should do and didn’t care. “I was dead in a home dedicated to the gospel.”

    2. 15:17 Not resuscitated but resurrected

      • Being spiritually dead means you can’t understand or appreciate spiritual things. Just as a corpse can’t respond to a pinch, get hungry, or feel pain, an unsaved person’s spiritual faculties aren’t firing and won’t until God gives life.
      • The unbeliever isn’t sick and doesn’t need a hand up. The unbeliever is dead and doesn’t need to be resuscitated; he needs to be resurrected. Dead means dead.

  5. 16:02 Three forces: the world, the devil, and the flesh

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:3 Genesis 2:17 Genesis 3:4

    • Verse 3 shows our death was the result of our disobedience. God said if you eat of the tree you will certainly die; Satan said you will not certainly die. Because the woman believed the lie and the man listened to his wife instead of protecting her, they experienced immediate spiritual death and ultimately physical death.
    • Ever since, mankind has lived in disobedience, encouraged by three forces: the world, the devil, and the flesh.

    1. 16:33 Focusing on just one force

      • Some put all their focus on the world: don’t touch this, don’t use that, no microwave. But even if you remove yourself from the world, you are still left with the devil and the flesh.
      • Some are like the Waterboy’s mama: everything is the devil, football, Nike. “I’m good, but the devil tripped me.”
      • Others think the problem is the flesh, so faith starts to look like WebMD, self-diagnosing and managing symptoms without reaching the deeper issues. It becomes a never-ending game of whack-a-mole, and if you get good at it you become a self-righteous mess the devil will have a field day with.

    2. 17:59 An all-out assault, and you are still there

      • We have major issues when we miss the comprehensive assault of what Josh calls the sin head: the world, the devil, and the flesh. It isn’t an attack from one direction; you are trapped. It isn’t only the world around you or the enemy lying to you; it is you.
      • If you escape everything on the outside, you are still there. A dog behaves like a dog because it has a dog’s nature; swap in a cat’s nature and things get weird quick. You need a new nature. If we fail to see how sinful we are, we will never appreciate how precious our redemption is or know what it means to be saved.

  6. 19:14 Ephesians 2:4–10

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:4–10

    • Josh reads the rest of the passage: because of his great love for us, God who is rich in mercy made us alive with Christ, raised us up and seated us with him, and saved us by grace through faith, not by works, for good works he prepared in advance for us to do.

  7. 19:55 Our riches and our responsibilities

    • When the series began, Josh said to think of Ephesians like a football game. The first half is our riches in Christ, what God has done through the person and work of Jesus; the second half is our responsibilities in Christ: how do I spend, leverage, and invest these riches in my life and in others’ lives?
    • We have been called to good works God prepared in advance, and that calling is directly connected to the riches Jesus accomplished on the cross. You were dead; he made you alive in Christ, so things are going to look way different.

  8. 20:49 By nature deserving of wrath

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:3 Ephesians 2:2–3 Romans 1:18

    • Being alive in Christ isn’t what we deserve: we were by nature deserving of wrath. This phrase upsets most people. The NIV is pretty gentle; the ESV calls those apart from Christ sons of disobedience and children of wrath. And yes, the Greek says wrath.
    • Most churchgoers don’t know what they are saved from. You are saved from God. Romans 1 says the wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against those who suppress the truth by their wickedness. Too many believers sing love songs to Jesus with no idea what they actually deserve.
    • God is holy, righteous, and just, and he is holding back the conclusion of time because he desires all to be saved. Don’t mistake his patience for approval or think he has forgotten. We are uncomfortable with an angry God, but the God who holds the universe cannot look on sin without indignation. “Sinner, listen. God is angry at you.”
    • We are sinners by nature and by choice. God gave you breath and you used it to defy him; we have committed treason and tried to kick him off his throne and sit there ourselves.

    1. 23:03 Are you okay with God being angry at the world?

      • Some don’t like hearing God is angry at them, so Josh plays it out: are you okay with God being angry at the world? A system that values death and not life, where we have to vote for life. A system that rewards cheating over honesty and bails out the big guys. A system that accepts lies and rejects the truth.
      • Of course we are comfortable with that. God can love the world and be angry with it at the same time, just as Josh can love his kids and be angry with them at the same time.

    2. 24:14 Are you okay with God being angry at Satan?

      Scripture: Ephesians 6:12

      • Satan is the father of lies; the prince of the power of the air is a liar. Don’t underestimate him, but don’t overestimate him either: he can’t be everywhere like God, so he has an army of demons upholding his lies.
      • His campaign of lies has drifted into places we never thought we would have to defend. Our government went from printing “In God We Trust” to eliminating the Defense of Marriage Act in 2013, and Josh believes we will look back on 2013 as the year we truly lost this society. Christians were painted as hateful bigots for upholding marriage as the sacred bond between a man, a woman, and God for life.
      • We took the most God-centered human relationship and turned it into a drag show while we watched. Every able-bodied Christian should have been in Washington saying, “Not on our watch,” but we the people lacked backbone, and an entire generation walks around with gender dysphoria because we didn’t want to ruffle feathers.
      • Josh reminds himself our battle isn’t against flesh and blood. He doesn’t blame a politician or a party; Satan is a liar and a thief. Is he comfortable with God being angry at Satan? “You bet I am.”

    3. 26:51 Then why not anger toward you?

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:3 Psalm 51:5

      • If we can rage like this at the world and Satan, how much greater will the wrath of God be? And if we understand his anger at the world and the devil, why is it so hard to understand his anger toward you? We were all by nature deserving of wrath.
      • Satan’s greatest lie is that you deserve God’s love and are entitled to his salvation. The truth is that you deserve his wrath. We are born crooked; the one after God’s heart said he was sinful at birth.
      • Josh and Kristen rarely fight, and when they do it is in the closet, yet their kids bicker. He never sat Madison down to teach her how to make her brother feel small, or showed Nolan how to provoke his sister. They figured it out on their own, because we are born rebels and sinners.

    4. 28:48 We love justice until it’s directed at us

      • If God looked at the world, Satan, and us and said, “Sinners are going to sin,” everyone would scream, “Where’s the justice?” We love justice: someone cuts you off and you slow down beside them hoping they are late for work; someone breaks into your home and you want them taken down; someone takes your spot in line and you hope they get a price check.
      • Most of us are okay with God’s wrath as long as it isn’t directed at us. We scream for justice for them and start kicking and screaming when we get stuck with the tab: “She did it. They did it. The devil made me do it.”

    5. 30:24 We deserve judgment, and that’s where good starts

      Scripture: Proverbs 1:7

      • One thing is clear: we don’t deserve his blessings; we deserve his judgment. It is hard to hear, but it is true, wonderful, and necessary, because everything good starts with a healthy respect for God. The wisdom book says the fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge.

  9. 31:06 No one else to blame

    • Some react, “A loving God would forgive us.” But if God turned his back on our sin and didn’t make it right, he would be unjust. We can’t blame the world, because we made it this way, and if you had made it yourself you probably would have made it worse.

    1. 31:41 You are why your feed looks that way

      • Josh works with many men in addiction because he has recovered from addictions to pornography and nicotine; he knows the sleepless nights and his wife’s tears. Men tell him it is hard to put a lid on lust because their Instagram feed is filled with half-naked women.
      • Your feed is custom fit to your desires; that is how an algorithm works, like looking in a mirror. You are why your feeds are that way. If you were hungry for other things, the algorithm would change. If it won’t, delete your account, or start a new one with self-control, built on Christ and filled with sermons and God’s word. Stop scrolling through these women. You have a wife.

    2. 33:11 Satan only offers what you want

      • We can’t blame Satan either, because he never makes us do anything. He dangles the bait and we bite because it is what we want.
      • To understand the goodness of God, you have to understand the gravity of your sin, and we resist that because it immediately rips our self-righteousness to shreds.

    3. 33:47 How God views sin

      Scripture: Romans 6:23

      • Back to the opening point: when you encounter God’s truth, you will surrender to it or suppress it. The temptation to suppress comes from realizing that if God deals severely with sin, he must deal severely with me. The wages of sin is death.
      • God was so frustrated with sin that he flooded the earth and started over from one righteous man; look at the ark. He dropped a heavenly hailstorm on Sodom and Gomorrah. And it is the same God in the New Testament: Ananias and Sapphira died when they withheld from the church, and Paul warned the Corinthians that some got sick and some died for taking the Lord’s Supper without reverence.

  10. 35:04 Jesus gets us; we don’t get him

    Scripture: Revelation Hebrews 10:31

    • Josh loves that Jesus’ name is on the big screen in the “He Gets Us” Super Bowl campaign, but Jesus gets us just fine. The problem is we don’t get him. The social-justice-warrior, nice-guy, shampoo-model Jesus isn’t biblical. This doesn’t end with Jesus washing your feet or consoling you at a bus stop.
    • Go read Revelation; a 10-year-old can understand it: Jesus wins. He comes as King of Kings and Lord of Lords with a sword from his mouth and a winepress of his fury. He will have his justice; you won’t get the best of him or pull one on him.
    • People say, “Well, God knows my heart,” while defying him. That isn’t comforting; it should scare you, because he knows your deepest and darkest thoughts. Hebrews 10 says it is a dreadful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.

  11. 37:29 Ephesians 2:4: “But because of his great love”

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:4–5

    • Praise God there is a fourth verse: because of his great love for us, God who is rich in mercy made us alive with Christ even when we were dead. It is by grace you have been saved. “Just take a deep breath and relax. You made it.”
    • Attention turns from sinful man to our awesome God, who does four things for sinners: he loved us, raised us, exalted us, and keeps us. We can’t skip the wrath to get to the love; knowing what we deserved makes our understanding of his love so much greater.

    1. 38:37 He loved us: mercy and grace at the cross

      Scripture: 1 John 4:8 Romans 5:8 Hebrews 4:15 Romans 6:23 2 Corinthians 5:21

      • By nature we are sinners; by nature God is love, as 1 John 4 says. God has intrinsic, unchanging attributes such as love and holiness, and relative attributes we see as he relates to creation. His truth becomes faithfulness; his holiness becomes justice; and when he pours his love onto man, it becomes grace and mercy. In mercy he doesn’t give us what we deserve, and in grace he gives us what we don’t deserve.
      • All this is made possible by the death of Jesus. Romans 5: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. He lived the life we couldn’t and died the death we deserved. Hebrews 4 says he was tempted in every way, yet did not sin. The wages of sin is death, but Jesus didn’t sin, so why did he die? 2 Corinthians 5:21: God made him who had no sin to be sin for us.

    2. 40:29 An update on the enemy

      Scripture: Colossians 2:13–15 John 19:30

      • Remember the sin head of the world, the devil, and the flesh? Josh updates believers on their progress: God made you alive with Christ, forgave all your sins, canceled the charge of your legal indebtedness by nailing it to the cross, and disarmed the powers and authorities, triumphing over them by the cross.
      • “I have a new home and a new master and a new nature. I am his.” When Jesus said, “It is finished,” Josh believes him: Christ has done it all, and God’s wrath is satisfied forever and fully.

    3. 41:35 He raised us: resurrection power

      Scripture: John 5:24

      • Jesus raised three people during his ministry: the widow’s son and Jairus’s daughter, to whom he said, “Get up,” and Lazarus, to whom he said, “Come out.” The word of God is power, even resurrection power. In John 5 Jesus says whoever hears his word and believes has crossed over from death to life.

    4. 42:28 He exalted us: seated in the heavenly realms

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:6–7

      • The gospel keeps getting better the more you pause on each part; it drives a sinner to his knees. God raised us up with Christ and seated us with him in the heavenly realms to show the incomparable riches of his grace.
      • The redeemed don’t celebrate in a graveyard; we celebrate in the heavenly realms. Jesus sits next to God, and his bride, his church, is seated there too. We were dead; now we are alive and co-heirs to an inheritance that will never spoil or fade. Lazarus feasted with Jesus the night he was raised; imagine the feast in store for us.

    5. 43:54 Saved by grace, for good works

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:8–10

      • It is by grace through faith, not from yourselves, the gift of God, not by works, so no one can boast; we are his handiwork created for good works. Do good works so God will love us? No. We do good because he loved us. The pressure is off; nothing depends on you. Salvation is a gift, not a reward; it removes chains rather than adding them.
      • When Jesus died, the veil of the temple was torn. Jesus our high priest entered the Holy of Holies on our behalf, and his blood spilled out on the altar. No more sacrifices are needed: one sacrifice, the Lamb of God, and God did it all by grace. The enemy thought he had you, but Jesus said, “You are mine.”

  12. 45:11 To the unbeliever and the skeptic

    • God is patient, but stop testing his patience. If you feel conviction and know you need Jesus, don’t ignore it; the Holy Spirit is cracking you open, as he cracked Josh open too. Come honestly, humbly, and now. Find your way to the cross and unite with him through baptism. He doesn’t need you to bring anything; admit you need a Savior and trust his grace. He has already done the rest.

  13. 45:57 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays that he is overwhelmed and that everything in his life is overwritten by God’s grace and mercy. He thanks the Lord for loving us, stepping down, humbling himself, and willingly taking our place on the cross, and asks that the church would stay connected to him, abide, and bear fruit in his name for his glory forever.
