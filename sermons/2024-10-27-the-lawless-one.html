Overview

  1. 00:14 What is said is not always what is heard

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2

    • Continuing the series in 2 Thessalonians 2, a short chapter of 17 verses, Josh asks if anyone has ever been misquoted to their face: “This is what I heard.” Often people share something encouraging the Lord gave them from his sermon that he doesn’t think he said; usually it is funny and positive.
    • Other times it causes problems: “Josh, you said this and I disagree.” YouTube lets him say “go check tape,” but every word is recorded and archived, so he must say what is true and communicate it clearly. Effective communication is hard; words can be misheard, misinterpreted, and misunderstood.

  2. 02:11 Paul’s problem: confusion about the day of the Lord

    Scripture: 1 Peter 3:15

    • Paul faced this with the Thessalonians. He had been with them, broken bread with them, and sent one letter to comfort them by explaining the day of the Lord, yet some still misunderstood its timing. Told it could be at any moment, some quit their jobs and stopped caring for their families and communities, and intense persecution convinced others the day had come.
    • So Paul “powered up Microsoft Word” to write another letter, because a distracted, confused church is not a healthy church. The rumors encouraged complacency, but the Lord’s day builds urgency. Paul says: hold up, several things must happen before Christ returns. In the meantime stop guessing and staring at the sky; hold fast to the gospel, pray, share the reason for the hope you have as his ambassadors, and don’t be idle.

  3. 04:14 “This is it!” versus “You need Jesus”

    • Two thousand years later, many in the church are still saying, “This is it, it’s happening.” But the message on every believer’s tongue should be: repent and believe. The believer’s purpose is to glorify Jesus by declaring his gospel to the world.

    1. 04:47 The gospel, the only way

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:8 John 14:6

      • Jesus lived the perfect life and died the death you deserved, bearing our sin and satisfying God’s holy wrath against sinners. He was buried, walked out of the tomb three days later in victory over sin and death, and promises that victory to all who love him. It is by grace you have been saved through faith; God offers the gift of eternal life, received only through faith in Jesus.
      • He is the way, the truth, and the life, and it seems it needs to be said: the only way. There may be some truth in other religions, but not the truth you need. Our message to the world is “You need Jesus.”

    2. 05:56 From heaven-gazing to spreading the gospel

      • Tragically, the church has gone from declaring “You need Jesus” to outsiders to declaring “This is it” to insiders. Boldness used to mean telling the Safeway cashier about Jesus; now it means winning the rapture debate in life group. With both letters in hand, we are still distracted by the same things the Thessalonians were.
      • Preparing for his coming doesn’t require being premillennial, postmillennial, or amillennial; nobody cares. It means spreading the gospel, reaching those in need, and building up the church. Many are living for his return when they are supposed to be living for him. Josh has prayed all week this message would move the church from heaven-gazing to spreading the gospel.

  4. 07:30 Reading 2 Thessalonians 2:1–12

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:1–12

    • Josh reads the first twelve verses: don’t become easily unsettled by teaching that the day of the Lord has already come; that day will not come until the rebellion occurs and the man of lawlessness is revealed, who exalts himself over everything called God and sets himself up in God’s temple. The secret power of lawlessness is already at work, but the one who holds it back will do so until taken out of the way.
    • Then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will overthrow with the breath of his mouth. His coming will be by Satan’s working, with signs and wonders that serve the lie, deceiving those who perish because they refused to love the truth. God sends them a powerful delusion so they believe the lie.

  5. 09:23 The Antichrist and the spirit of lawlessness

    Scripture: 1 John 2:18

    • This is Josh’s first time preaching on the Antichrist; the series has challenged him. History has seen evil leaders like Nero and Domitian, Stalin and Hitler, and church history is loaded with false messiahs, cult leaders, and wolves in sheep’s clothing. But one is still to come who will make them all look elementary, the most wicked, deceitful, and powerful man ever.
    • Paul calls him the man of lawlessness, the man doomed to destruction, the lawless one; John calls him the Antichrist. In 1 John: “Dear children, this is the last hour… even now many antichrists have come.” Anyone who opposes the person and work of Christ operates by the spirit of the Antichrist.
    • Between the baby in the manger and the king with a sword, Scripture calls the timeline the last days. John calls the force at work the spirit of antichrist; Paul calls it the secret power of lawlessness. Both agree it is already at work, and near the end there will be a final, great one.

    1. 11:26 Paul’s purpose: peace, confidence, and clarity

      • Don’t lose Paul’s reason for writing: so that Christians would have peace, confidence, and clarity about what is coming. He is building a culture of Christians who don’t “wet themselves” every time chaos comes, a church that doesn’t get anxious, confused, and sweaty when crisis comes.

  6. 12:00 Who is the man of lawlessness? We don’t know

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:3 2 Thessalonians 2:2

    • Verse 3: don’t let anyone deceive you. Verse 2 shows the Thessalonians believed the day of the Lord had come; whether the deception was intentional or someone simply misspoke hardly matters. Paul says the rebellion must come first and the man of lawlessness be revealed.
    • Who is he? Many say a political leader. We don’t know. Paul isn’t telling Christians this so they can play Guess Who until the day of revealing; he is explaining how Satan operates, his blueprint for success.

  7. 13:08 Lawlessness: “Who says that’s wrong?”

    Scripture: Exodus 20:13

    • Paul’s word means without law, anti-law. It isn’t disobedience but outright rebellion. Josh quizzes the church on the sixth commandment: “Thou shalt not murder.” Knowing murder is wrong and doing it anyway is ignoring the law. Lawlessness says, “Who says that’s wrong? I have no issue with this, therefore there is no issue.”

    1. 14:03 “It is murder; I just don’t care”

      • In a 90-minute phone call, a lady from church told Josh that Bill Maher, pressed on abortion, admitted, “It is murder, I just don’t care.” “Mad respect for being truthful, but dang, that’s lawlessness.”
      • Morality used to be rooted in eternal law; over the last hundred years it has become subjective. We went from “Marriage is between a man and a woman because God designed it” to “You think so, but I don’t, so it isn’t wrong.” Josh believes abortion is taking an innocent life, since God said do not murder; now it has become “I don’t feel it’s wrong, so it’s not.” Until the early 1900s, your feelings didn’t decide right and wrong; it had already been decided.

    2. 16:02 An ASU student meets a first-century Thessalonian

      • Picture a gender studies major from ASU talking with a first-century Thessalonian. “What do you mean you’re living your truth? You say you’re a woman, but women can make babies and you can’t. There are natural laws. Why do you have to decide right and wrong for yourself? Don’t natural and civil laws help build healthy citizens?”

    3. 17:09 My Way Island and His Way Island

      • Josh proposes a human experiment no candidate could run on: two island communities, one basing morality on how they feel, “My Way Island,” and one on God’s eternal and holy law, “His Way Island.” In church everyone picks His Way, which wasn’t fair, but My Way Island would be a mess, a nightmare.

    4. 18:05 Understand the scheme, not the man

      • The spirit of lawlessness is at work around us right now, growing and pushing until it is fully revealed. They call it Enlightenment; it feels more like lawlessness. It can manifest as a person, a group, an organization, or an idea, but understanding how it works matters far more than who it works in.

  8. 19:10 Satan wants to be worshiped as God

    Scripture: Genesis 3 2 Thessalonians 2:4 Matthew 24 Daniel 9

    • Satan has been at war with God since he rebelled, wanting not equality but to be above God. He tempted Eve and through her caused Adam to fall. God declared war on Satan in Genesis 3 and promised a Messiah who would crush him. Satan despises Christ and every name but his own and has always wanted to be worshiped as God.
    • Verse 4: he will exalt himself over everything called God and set himself up in God’s temple. Our Lord predicted this in Matthew 24, the abomination that causes desolation, referencing Daniel 9. Without unpacking the prophecy, the world will marvel at this leader who, with Satan’s power, performs signs and wonders and deceives the nations.

  9. 20:36 Where does the rapture fit?

    • Josh pauses for the question many are thinking: where does the rapture fit? It is the wrong question to ask while reading, but a popular one. Many believe a two-stage coming: Christ comes for the church before a seven-year tribulation and later returns in judgment. That pre-tribulation view has been popularized by books and movies of Christians suddenly disappearing.
    • Another view, not less Christian, is post-tribulation, and 2 Thessalonians makes a strong argument for it; this text is one reason Josh holds it, “at least this week.” He loves his pre-trib brothers and sisters and would rather sing and pray with them than debate non-essentials, but he will be faithful to the text and remind the church no one view is more Christian. He looks forward to an epic gathering in the sky as Christ comes in blazing fire with his angels to establish his kingdom, give rest to his people, and judge his enemies.

    1. 22:35 Verse 8: this is the day of the Lord

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:8 Genesis 3 2 Thessalonians 2:3

      • The Thessalonians were troubled because they thought the day of the Lord had come. For pre-tribbers, that day is the second half of the second coming. “My head hurts too.” But verse 8, the Lord Jesus overthrowing the lawless one with the breath of his mouth, is the day of the Lord: not a quiet rapture leaving planes without pilots, but a glorious attack from heaven against the man and power of lawlessness, finishing the war declared in Genesis 3.
      • If the Thessalonians feared they had missed it, why didn’t Paul say, “Didn’t you watch Left Behind? You’re not raptured yet; relax”? Why did he say in verse 3 that the rebellion and the man of lawlessness come first? If we aren’t to be deceived, we had better think these things through.

    2. 23:59 The restrainer

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:6–7 Job 42:2

      • Pre-tribulation views place the man of lawlessness after the rapture. Josh rereads verses 6 and 7 on the one who holds him back. Some say the restrainer is the gospel, the church, or government; Josh believes no human force but God alone can hold Satan back. Nobody operates outside God’s timetable. As Job said, no purpose of yours can be thwarted. The man of lawlessness will appear when God determines.

    3. 24:54 Why would Paul point to someone they’d never see?

      • Fun fact: the pre-trib teaching was developed in 1827 by John Darby, and applying an 1827 framework to first-century Thessalonians gets interesting. Why would Paul prove the day hadn’t come by pointing to a man they would never see? If he held Darby’s view, he could simply say, “We’re still here.”
      • The most natural reading: Paul wants them able to recognize the lawless one, ready not to be deceived and to keep others from being deceived. “I’m in trouble for that one, but it’s okay.”

  10. 26:06 The mystery of iniquity: Satan’s program of sin

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:7

    • “The secret power of lawlessness” is a good translation of the word mysterion: Paul warns of the mystery of iniquity. For decades many have focused more on the man than on the secret power, Satan’s program of sin.
    • Gentile Christians, letting anti-Semitism grow, said he must be a Jew in Jerusalem; most Reformers said the Antichrist was the pope; Catholics had other views; and today nearly every major political leader is called Antichrist on some blog. Far more important than finding a man is recognizing the dark strategy.

    1. 27:15 Counterfeit signs and wonders

      Scripture: Acts 2:22 Matthew 7 2 Thessalonians 2:9

      • Satan will enable his false messiah to display power, signs, and deceitful wonders, imitating Christ. In Acts 2 Peter says Jesus of Nazareth was a man accredited by God by miracles, wonders, and signs. Satan isn’t accredited by God; he has always been an imitator.
      • There are other imitators: false believers, false preachers with a false gospel, churches that think they worship God but worship Satan, with self-righteousness and a false assurance that will prove useless on the day of the Lord. Jesus talks about them in Matthew 7.
      • In Acts, miracles confirmed the authenticity of the message, but miracles alone never prove a man is sent from God; the message must be true and the character must align. John the Baptist was sent from God and never performed a sign. In Pharaoh’s court (go watch The Prince of Egypt), Pharaoh’s yes-men copied Moses in the name of their cheap god Ra, but Moses’ staff ate theirs. God’s miracles lead people to the truth; the Antichrist’s lead people to believe his lies.

    2. 29:52 Overthrown by the breath of his mouth

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:8

      • Believer, don’t worry: Jesus will overthrow the lawless one with the breath of his mouth. This is meant to bring great comfort and peace. Stop wiggling and freaking out. The end is not yet, but when it comes, victory is the Lord’s.

  11. 30:23 The rebellion: a great falling away

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:3 Matthew 24:10–13

    • Verse 3 says rebellion and the revealing of the man of lawlessness come first. Rebellion means to abandon, apostasy: a falling away from the truth by those who once held it.
    • Paul and Jesus say the same thing. Josh reads Matthew 24: many will turn away from the faith, false prophets will deceive many, the love of most will grow cold, but whoever stands firm to the end will be saved. The secret power of lawlessness is already at work and will increase, but those who endure will be saved.

  12. 31:17 They perish because they refused to love the truth

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:10

    • At the end of verse 10, these signs deceive those who are perishing. Why would so many Christians abandon the truth for a lie? Jesus and Paul both say there will be a huge falling away, yet the church is grabbing pitchforks on a manhunt for the Antichrist when it should be teaching people to love the truth. It’s good for you; he adores you; listen, obey, surrender.
    • They perish because they refuse to love the truth and so be saved.

    1. 32:25 Not knowing or believing, but loving the truth

      Scripture: John 14:6 John 3:19–20

      • Josh knows these aren’t easy sermons; he isn’t shoveling prosperity teaching like Osteen or trying to entertain. He wants to tell them what they must know for their souls. This is the only time in the New Testament someone speaks like this, besides Jesus and maybe James.
      • It isn’t merely an issue of knowing or believing, but of loving the truth. Those who perish aren’t lost because they didn’t hear or understand but because they did not love it. Jesus is the way, the truth, and the life, and instead of loving him they delighted in evil. Josh reads John 3: light has come into the world, but people loved darkness instead of light because their deeds were evil.
      • Those who delight in evil choose to love their sin, believe Satan’s lies, and hate the gospel. Like the Pharisees, they believed in him but cared more about man’s approval than God’s.

    2. 34:15 God won’t be one of your favorites

      Scripture: Matthew 10:37–39

      • If Josh could sum up anything useful: God isn’t interested in being on the favorites tab of your iPhone. Only those who put him first are worthy of him. In Matthew 10 Jesus says anyone who loves father, mother, son, or daughter more than him is not worthy of him; whoever loses their life for his sake will find it.
      • The greatest defense against Satan’s program of sin is a deeper desire for God and his truth. If Jesus is truly the treasure of your heart, your love will not grow cold and you will endure to the end.

  13. 35:31 A powerful delusion

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:11–12

    • If you don’t love the truth but delight in evil, Josh warns what comes next: God sends them a powerful delusion so they believe the lie and are condemned. Pharaoh is a constant reminder that God will judicially harden the hearts of those who persist in hardening their hearts against the truth. Pharaoh first hardened his own heart, then God hardened it too. Pharaoh chose the path; God sealed it.

  14. 36:11 God’s program for salvation

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 2:13–17 Ephesians 2:8–9

    • Having unpacked Satan’s program of deception and sin, Paul turns to God’s program for salvation. Josh reads verses 13 through 17: God chose you as firstfruits to be saved through the Spirit’s sanctifying work and belief in the truth; he called you through our gospel; so stand firm and hold fast to the teachings; and may the Lord encourage your hearts and strengthen you in every good deed and word.
    • Paul closes with a “salvation sermon clip”; Josh gets more out of the endings of Paul’s letters than his own sermons. Salvation begins and ends with God: by grace you have been saved through faith, not by works, so no one can boast. You must accept God’s gift; there is no other way sins can be forgiven.

    1. 38:10 Chosen: marvel, don’t debate

      Scripture: John 6:39

      • Paul encouraged them by reminding them God chose them from the beginning. Josh’s heart breaks when all he hears about “chosen” in church is debate. His heart screams: how did he choose me? He knew every step of my rebellion and my twisted affections, and he chose me. “I don’t want to debate you; I want to marvel at the King.”
      • Jesus adds: this is the will of him who sent me, that I shall lose none of those he has given me. If you’re his, you’re his forever, and he’ll raise you up at the last day. God doesn’t fumble, trip at the goal line, miss the pass, or call audibles. His saying is his doing.

    2. 39:25 Someone preached to you; go preach to them

      • God called them through the gospel. Someone preached to you, so go preach to them. The church went from eleven men locked in a room in fear to 2.4 billion Christians, about 31% of the world, all by preaching the gospel.
      • Some propose a rebrand: soften it, take out the sin stuff, maybe reach 50%. “No, stop, walk away.” If you try to change truth, you don’t love truth. Preach it as it is; they need Jesus, and they can only know the truth if you tell them.

    3. 40:30 Stand firm and hold fast

      • This is your purpose. The world would love nothing more than for you to be a Christian without a firm grasp of God’s word. The secret power of lawlessness seeks to tear the Christian from the word while keeping them close enough to think they’re still a Christian.
      • It loves that you show up early, before the countdown, while week after week you delay getting in the water to declare your faith in baptism. What are you waiting for? If you won’t embrace the title “sinner saved by grace” in here, will you out there?

  15. 41:28 Do you love him?

    Scripture: John 14:15 Matthew 5:16 Matthew 5:48 Matthew 6:33 Matthew 22:37–39 Matthew 7:23 2 Corinthians 13:5 John 8:11

    • Being here, on the prayer team, hosting a life group, tithing, singing, or attending every week doesn’t mean you love him. Judas served on Jesus’ team but didn’t love him. Examine yourself right now.
    • Jesus says if you love me you’ll obey me. You’re so close; how can you do nothing with what you’ve heard? He says repent. He says let your light shine; why is yours under a bowl? He says cut it off if it causes you to stumble, and you sweep it under the rug. He says be perfect, and he means it. He says seek God’s kingdom first, and all you seek is your career. He says don’t worry about tomorrow, forgive, lead by serving, love him with all your heart, mind, and soul, love your neighbor, teach others to obey him, deny yourself, give to those who can’t repay. Do this because you love me.
    • None of your actions matter if you don’t love him or haven’t been filled with the Holy Spirit. What will Jesus say face to face: “She’s with me. He’s with me,” or “I never knew you; away from me, you worker of iniquity”? Examine yourselves to see whether you are in the faith. God is patient but won’t hold back forever. Repent, be baptized, go and sin no more.

  16. 44:18 Closing prayer

    Scripture: Psalm 23:4

    • Josh tells the Lord he is feeling fear, not for himself, for God is with him and his rod and staff comfort him, but for those who don’t know God and those who think they know him but don’t love him. He wants to beg them to give their lives to God before it’s too late; he weeps for what he thinks Jesus wept for.
    • He asks God to call out whoever is pretending, do a work only he can do, and drive away sin, shame, and fear so they can stand before him embracing their new title, sinner saved by grace. He thanks Jesus for dying for him, loving him, and choosing him: “Help me. I want to live for you.”
