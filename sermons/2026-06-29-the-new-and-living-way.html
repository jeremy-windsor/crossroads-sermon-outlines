Overview

  1. 00:03 Jesus opens access to God through a new and living way

    • Josh moves from a humorous story about restricted backstage access to the old covenant barrier before God’s presence, then reads Hebrews 10:19–25 and declares that Jesus’ blood has opened the way permanently.

    1. 00:03 A backstage pass produces borrowed confidence

      • Josh recalls attending a Jessica Simpson concert as a high-school freshman, entering an onstage singing contest despite not knowing her songs, winning the crowd’s approval, and receiving unusual backstage access.
      • After the other contestants leave, he meets Simpson’s family and is seated onstage, where she kisses and serenades him; he later jokes that she was a shadow of a better woman, Kristen.
      • The experience briefly transformed his social confidence, but the imposing security guard’s warning made clear that no one entered the protected area without authorization.
      • Josh uses the comic story to introduce the far more serious confidence Hebrews gives ordinary believers to enter God’s presence.

    2. 03:40 The old covenant worshiper faced a real barrier

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:7

      • After noting that the church has been studying Hebrews for nine weeks, Josh connects the security barrier with the tabernacle veil: no ordinary worshiper would presume to enter the Most Holy Place.
      • Even the high priest entered only once each year, and the four-inch-thick curtain marked separation between sinful people and the holy God.
      • Christ bore sin on the cross and opened access to the heavenly sanctuary where God dwells.

    3. 04:56 Hebrews 10:19–25 turns from Christ’s work to three invitations

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:19-25

      • Josh reads Hebrews 10:19–25, beginning with confidence to enter the Most Holy Place by Jesus’ blood and the new and living way through the curtain of his body.
      • The passage grounds its appeals in Jesus’ priesthood and then calls believers to draw near in full assurance, hold unswervingly to hope, and consider how to spur one another toward love and good deeds without abandoning their gatherings.

    4. 05:46 Jesus enters, leaves the door open, and becomes the door

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:19-21 John 10:7,9 Hebrews 8:13 Hebrews 7:16,25 Matthew 27:50-51; Mark 15:37-38; Luke 23:45-46 1 John 4:18

      • Believers enter a place they could never claim for themselves because Jesus has unlocked it. Unlike an old covenant high priest who entered alone, Christ leaves the way open and is himself the door through whom people come to God.
      • Josh asks the church to remember the sentence, “Jesus unlocked the new and living way.” It is new because the old covenant is obsolete in its light, and living because Christ has an indestructible life and always lives to intercede.
      • When Christ’s flesh was torn and his life sacrificed, God tore the temple curtain. That event signified open access for all who believe.
      • Believers may therefore enter boldly rather than fearfully; Josh joins this confidence to the assurance that perfect love drives out fear and identifies Jesus as perfect love.

    5. 06:51 The open way carries a threefold invitation

      • The old way was limited, but the way opened by Christ remains available every moment rather than once a year or once a month.
      • Josh identifies each “let us” in the passage as an invitation and prepares to take the three in sequence.

  2. 07:18 First invitation: draw near with a cleansed conscience

    • Hebrews 10:22 calls believers toward God rather than away from him. Josh locates purity in Christ’s prior cleansing, connects blood and water with faith and baptism, and confronts the shame that tells struggling Christians to hide.

    1. 07:18 Fellowship with God demands purity

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:22

      • Josh rereads Hebrews 10:22: believers draw near sincerely and with full assurance because their hearts have been sprinkled clean from guilt and their bodies washed with pure water.
      • His summary is direct: fellowship with God demands purity.

    2. 07:40 Two opposite errors distort holiness

      Scripture: 1 John 1

      • Josh assigns 1 John 1, with its teaching about light, darkness, sin, and forgiveness, as homework.
      • One error tries to become clean before coming to God and may demand the same self-cleansing from others; the other assumes holiness does not matter because human beings cannot achieve it themselves.
      • The gospel refuses both errors: people do not clean themselves so they may come; they come because Christ’s blood has already cleansed those who believe.

    3. 08:07 First washed in blood, then washed in water

      Scripture: Romans 6:3-5

      • Josh distinguishes Christ’s cleansing blood from the believer’s public profession in baptism: first Christ cleanses, then the believer openly declares trust in him.
      • Baptism publicly identifies the Christian with Christ’s life, crucifixion, death, and resurrection and announces a walk in the new and living way.
      • The nearer a person walks with Jesus, Josh says, the cleaner that person becomes.

    4. 08:44 Priestly ceremonies taught that God is holy and people are not

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:6-10

      • For generations, approaching God involved priestly washing, sacrifice, and sprinkled blood. Every ceremony taught the same reality: God is holy and sinners are not.
      • Josh says this separation helps explain why confession is difficult even for people who readily believe God exists and see evidence of him in creation.

    5. 09:19 Yesterday’s failure makes people hesitate to approach

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:22

      • People carry memories of damaging conversations, relapsed addictions, compromises, regretted decisions, and sinful thoughts, then tell themselves this is not the day to come before God.
      • Josh says Hebrews reverses that instinct: draw near with assurance because cleansing precedes the invitation.
      • Jesus shed the blood, opened the sanctuary, and now calls sinners to come—not because they had a perfect week or defeated every addiction, but because his blood is enough.

    6. 10:29 Confession rests on a faithful promise

      Scripture: 1 John 1:9

      • Josh urges families to memorize 1 John 1:9 and impress it upon children and grandchildren: God faithfully forgives confessed sin and purifies from unrighteousness.
      • No one confesses unless confident that confession will lead to something good. Christian confidence therefore rests in Christ.

    7. 10:55 God receives believers according to Christ’s record

      • Josh calls this a treasure the Holy Spirit must help people discover: God receives believers according to their position in Christ rather than their performance.
      • Aware of his own failures, Josh does not approach the Father dressed in his own record. He comes clothed in Christ’s righteousness, perfection, and acceptance.
      • The Father sees a sinner resting at Jesus’ feet and redeemed by the Son’s blood; that is enough.

    8. 12:03 The completed road was built to be used

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:20 Hebrews 10:11-14,19-22

      • Road construction near Josh’s home supplies the illustration: once a road is complete, residents do not need to ask permission to drive or ride on what was built for their use.
      • Jesus did not construct one more optional route. He consecrated the only way and paved this “king’s interstate” with his blood.
      • Because the veil is torn, the sacrifice finished, and the priest seated, believers should stop hesitating and taking side streets. The invitation is to use the open way and draw near.

    9. 13:09 Shame says hide; Jesus says draw near

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:22

      • Josh believes some hearers need only Hebrews 10:22 because they are not tempted to return to Judaism but are persuaded that they should be further along than they are.
      • He recalls tearful conversations with people who confessed, fell again, and now regard distance from God as safer than approach.
      • Josh names that message as a lie: the devil says to stay away until better, but Jesus says to come near because his blood heals. Shame says hide and that one does not belong; Jesus acknowledges unworthiness while making a place of belonging.
      • The first invitation ends where it began: draw near with confidence.

  3. 14:38 Second invitation: hold unswervingly to hope

    • Josh turns from the vertical call of faith to the forward-facing call of hope. Conversion zeal, a pilot’s heading, God’s flawless promises, and Paul’s faith-hope-love triad all support perseverance.

    1. 14:38 The faithful promiser anchors perseverance

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:23 Hebrews 10:22-23

      • Josh reads the second invitation: hold unswervingly to professed hope because the one who promised is faithful.
      • He classifies drawing near as vertical faith concerning relationship with God and holding fast as forward-facing hope concerning perseverance.

    2. 15:19 Remember the eagerness of first coming to Christ

      Scripture: John 14:16-17; 16:7

      • Josh asks worshipers to remember their excitement when they first came to Christ: no one had to drag them back to church or beg them to serve because they wanted everyone to know.
      • A congregant named Steve, who came to Christ at age 52, says reading John opened his heart when he understood that the Spirit would dwell in believers and that Jesus’ return to heaven was necessary for the Comforter to come.
      • Josh honors Steve’s present prison ministry as fruit of that awakened faith.

    3. 16:19 A one-degree drift becomes a missed destination

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:23

      • After repeating the command to hold unswervingly, Josh asks pilots to pardon his simplified illustration: a one-degree navigation error is easily corrected after leaving Phoenix Sky Harbor but will miss JFK if ignored all the way to New York.
      • Trials will come. Christians must correct drift, keep their heading on Christ, and finish the race.

    4. 17:29 Biblical hope holds promises, not possibilities

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:23 Hebrews 6:18 Hebrews 11:3 2 Corinthians 1:20

      • Josh returns to the reason for hope: “he who promised is faithful.” Christian hope is stronger than optimism, which merely wishes that things will work out.
      • Biblical hope says God has spoken and therefore the outcome is guaranteed. The one who spoke the universe into existence cannot lie, and his speaking accomplishes what he says.
      • Josh invokes Paul’s statement to Corinth that every divine promise is “Yes” in Christ. The captions omit part of his lead-in and closing clause, but the preserved quotation identifies 2 Corinthians 1:20.

    5. 18:32 The three invitations trace faith, hope, and love

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 13:13

      • Josh identifies the literary arrangement he has been building toward with Paul’s familiar line: faith, hope, and love remain, and love is greatest.
      • Drawing near corresponds to faith; holding unswervingly corresponds to hope; considering one another introduces love.

  4. 18:57 Third invitation: consider one another and gather toward love

    • Hebrews 10:24–25 turns attention from what a believer receives to what each believer offers the assembly. Josh develops concrete practices of attentive care, holy provocation, multigenerational example, regular gathering, and shared perseverance toward Christ’s return.

    1. 18:57 Gather, serve, challenge, and encourage together

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:24-25 Hebrews 10:24

      • Josh reads the third invitation: consider how to spur one another toward love and good deeds, keep meeting, encourage one another, and do so increasingly as the day approaches.
      • He summarizes the actions as challenging, gathering, serving, sticking together, and encouraging—love expressed in an assembly.
      • The emphasis is not primarily what the believer gets from the gathering but what the believer can offer: “Let us consider one another.”

    2. 19:40 Church hurt exposes self-considering community

      • Josh invites people wounded by church to raise their hands, using the visible response to reinforce Crossroads’ desire to change the way people see the church.
      • He recounts offering a recently widowed woman a place at his family’s table. Her surprise after a lifetime in the Catholic Church showed him how unusual simple, personal welcome can feel.
      • Josh cannot see and meet every hurt because he is not Jesus. If everyone considers the people around them, however, the body may actually care for everyone.

    3. 21:06 Learn the people in the surrounding rows

      • Josh gives the congregation two months to learn the names and phone numbers of people in three or four nearby rows or sections.
      • When someone has been absent, members should check in, send Scripture and encouragement, say they are praying, and then actually pray.
      • There are no special section leaders in this assignment; every believer takes responsibility.

    4. 22:03 Consider means attentive study without a critical spirit

      • The word “consider” carries more than occasional thought; Josh describes it as attentive study while jokingly ruling out stalking.
      • Attention must not become criticism. Josh says men are responsible to exercise self-control over where they look; rather than gossip about how a woman is dressed, believers can ask whether she has older Christian women encouraging her, knows her God-given dignity, and has anyone praying for her.
      • Some study weaknesses in order to wound. Christians study people so they can protect soft places, help others flourish, pray, love, and welcome them into the family.

    5. 23:50 Replace constant self-consideration with care for others

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:24

      • People are already experts at considering themselves—how they feel, what they need, what others should provide, and what they receive.
      • Hebrews redirects those questions: who is weary, missing, in need of prayer, or ready to be built up?
      • For Crossroads’ mission to become real, members must stop asking only what the church offers and ask what they can offer and whom they can help.

    6. 24:47 Provoke the same intensity toward holy action

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:24 Hebrews 10:24

      • Josh repeats the call to spur one another toward love and good deeds, then explicitly compares the ESV’s “stir up” wording with the NIV’s “spur.”
      • He explains the underlying word with the force of sharp disagreement or provocation: passion that can raise blood pressure should instead be directed toward something holy.
      • Holy provocation asks when someone last helped another person, sought to be a blessing, or met a need. Josh’s application is to make godliness contagious and loving one’s neighbor compelling again.

    7. 25:52 Church is multigenerational formation, not only friendship

      Scripture: Titus 2:2-8

      • Fear of being disliked can make believers unwilling to provoke anyone toward growth, while lobby friendships can obscure the church’s multigenerational purpose.
      • Josh points to the New Testament pattern of older men investing in younger men and older women investing in younger women.
      • Friendships may grow, but the point is to set an example the next generation can follow.

    8. 26:24 Faithful lives teach what forgotten sermons cannot

      Scripture: 1 Timothy 3:15

      • Josh has heard thousands of sermons and remembers few, including some he preached, though preaching still matters because the church is the pillar of truth and God’s word must echo through it.
      • He learned deeply by watching both faithful and unfaithful Christians: durable marriages and adultery, decades of steady service and restless church-hopping, grief-stricken worship and flight at the first trouble, fervent lifelong prayer and lazy prayer, astonishing forgiveness and petty bitterness.
      • A good example can teach better than a stellar sermon because embodied faith lets another generation see obedience.

    9. 28:30 Do not abandon the assembly people need

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:25

      • Josh returns to the apostolic plea not to give up meeting together: people need each believer, and each believer needs people.
      • This tight-knit life is countercultural because even churches can train consumers to evaluate music, sermons, branding, clothing, friends, convenience, and children’s schedules according to personal preference.
      • Hebrews replaces consumer questions with ministry questions: whom did I encourage, and does this gathering equip me to bless others? When someone is absent, something is missing for the whole body.

    10. 29:35 Christianity is a team sport, not solitary golf

      • Josh’s daughter imitates David Attenborough narrating a gazelle separated from the herd and exposed to a predator.
      • The humorous nature-documentary picture makes the communal point: though God saves individuals, he sanctifies and grows them together in fellowship.
      • Christianity is a team sport and a herd mentality, not solitary golf.

    11. 30:37 Gather all the more as faith approaches sight

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:25 2 Corinthians 1:20

      • The nearer believers come to seeing Jesus, the more they need one another. The objective is to cross the finish line together rather than first.
      • Josh restates the three invitations: draw near to God, hold tightly to Jesus as the answer to God’s promises, and keep gathering.
      • One day gatherings by faith will become a gathering by sight. Josh pictures Christ returning, gathering the redeemed around his throne, and his people singing together forever.

  5. 31:26 The fourth warning confronts deliberate rejection and calls sinners toward mercy

    • Josh reads Hebrews 10:26–31 as the book’s strongest warning, distinguishes a stumbling Christian from a hardening heart that rejects Christ, and insists that the warning itself mercifully tells hearers to turn, confess, and run toward Jesus.

    1. 31:26 Hebrews 10:26–31 warns of judgment without another sacrifice

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:26-31

      • Josh identifies five warning passages in Hebrews and introduces the fourth before reading Hebrews 10:26–31.
      • The text warns that deliberate sin after receiving knowledge of the truth leaves no other sacrifice, only fearful judgment; it compares rejection of Moses’ law with trampling the Son, treating covenant blood as unholy, and insulting the Spirit of grace.
      • The reading ends with divine vengeance, judgment of God’s people, and the dreadful prospect of falling into the living God’s hands.

    2. 32:20 The warning addresses a hardening rejecter, not every stumble

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:1-4; 3:7-4:13; 5:11-6:12; 10:26-31

      • Josh attributes the series’ central question to Paul: how will people respond to what God has said about his Son?
      • This warning is not describing a Christian who stumbles but a heart becoming careless, callous, and hardened toward Christ.
      • He links Hebrews’ warning passages as a worsening path: drifting, doubting, growing dull, and finally despising what once brought joy.
      • The warnings also show grace because God places signs at every turn, calling his people back before the danger deepens.

    3. 33:11 Rejecting God’s Lamb leaves no alternate route

      Scripture: John 6:39 Hebrews 10:26 John 3:18 Hebrews 10:27,29

      • Josh is confident Christ will lose none the Father has given him, yet he warns that anyone who rejects God’s Lamb has no other sacrifice or plan B.
      • A person who comes to knowledge of the truth and rejects Jesus stands condemned already; no alternate route can save.
      • Treating Christ’s blood as common and trampling the Son deserves judgment. Josh invokes the lake of fire to communicate the severity awaiting those who remain God’s enemies.

    4. 33:55 The warning itself is a mercy-filled invitation to turn

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:20

      • Josh tells hearers not to miss the hope: a warning reveals mercy by telling sinners not to continue, but to turn around, run to Jesus, confess, and walk in the new and living way.
      • The severe text is therefore not permission to flee Christ; it is a summons to use the way Christ opened.

    5. 34:09 David’s deliberate sin leads from Hebrews to Psalm 51

      Scripture: Psalm 51 2 Samuel 11:2-5; 12:1-13

      • Josh announces that the next series, Renewing Me, will spend six weeks in Psalm 51 beginning August 2.
      • David knew deliberate sin: pursuing Bathsheba was no accident, and he deserved judgment. After a friend confronted and provoked him, he recognized his guilt and threw himself upon God’s mercy.
      • Josh traces the same movement from Hebrews to Psalm 51: stop hiding, confess, trust the blood, and run toward Christ rather than away.

    6. 34:59 The living God’s hands hold both judgment and forgiveness

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:31

      • Josh repeats Hebrews’ image and draws a pastoral contrast: falling into the living God’s hands for judgment is fearful, but falling into those hands for forgiveness is wonderful.
      • The answer to sin is not flight from God’s hands but surrender to his mercy.

  6. 35:12 Remember past endurance, refuse to shrink back, and spend life on what lasts

    • Hebrews 10:32–39 supplies both an example of shared suffering and a final call to confidence. Josh asks whether comfortable believers would endure loss joyfully, then presses the church to invest every entrusted resource in Christ’s kingdom rather than waste life on temporary achievements.

    1. 35:12 Remember early believers who endured public suffering

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:32-33 Hebrews 10:33

      • Josh notes that a chapter commanding encouragement immediately models it by telling believers to remember their early days in the light.
      • He reads Hebrews 10:32–33: they endured severe conflict, public insult and persecution, and stood beside others receiving the same treatment.
      • The word koinonia describes partners or sharers not only in spiritual and material blessings but also in suffering; such partnership belongs to life in Christ’s body.

    2. 36:02 The body suffers and rejoices together

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:26

      • Josh quotes Paul’s explanation to Corinth: when one part suffers, every part suffers; when one part is honored, every part rejoices.
      • He connects sympathy with the literal idea of suffering alongside another and prays that Crossroads becomes this kind of place.

    3. 36:22 They joyfully accepted plunder because lasting possessions awaited

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:34

      • Josh reads Hebrews 10:34: the believers suffered with prisoners and joyfully accepted confiscated property because they knew they possessed something better and lasting.
      • He argues that “plunder” or “seizure” better communicates the wrongful treatment than language that can sound like a neutral confiscation.
      • Their joy was possible because heaven was waiting.

    4. 36:53 Comfort has not trained the church for costly joy

      • The text strikes Josh because he sees weakness and complaint in a church living where following Christ is comparatively easy.
      • Using the alternate-history series For All Mankind and its premise that Russia beat the United States to the moon, he imagines the United States falling to third behind China and Russia and asks how many professing Christians would joyfully accept hardship.
      • He expects the list to be short unless believers heed Hebrews’ call not to throw away confidence.

    5. 38:02 Persevere for the promised reward and refuse to shrink back

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:35-39

      • Josh reads the closing appeal of Hebrews 10: do not discard confidence because it will be richly rewarded; perseverance is necessary to do God’s will and receive what he promised.
      • The coming one will come without delay, the righteous one lives by faith, and the Lord takes no pleasure in the one who shrinks back.
      • The passage identifies the church not with those who retreat into destruction but with those who believe and are saved. Josh asks which group each hearer belongs to.

    6. 38:48 The great Christian tragedy is a wasted life

      Scripture: Philippians 1:21 Hebrews 10:32-39

      • Josh closes by saying the greatest tragedy of Christian life is not failure, suffering, persecution, sin, or death; he quotes Paul’s confession that living is Christ and dying is gain.
      • The tragedy is wasting the life God gave. The original Hebrew Christians could avoid pressure by blending back into Judaism, ceasing public identification with Jesus, and no longer proclaiming his resurrection.
      • Josh says Paul will not let them discard their confidence because life is too valuable to spend on what does not last.

    7. 39:42 Faithfulness to Jesus is the investment that never loses value

      Scripture: Revelation 5:12 Hebrews 10:36-37

      • Promotions are forgotten, trophies gather dust, possessions pass to others, and achievements fade; Jesus does not fade, and faithfulness to him never loses value.
      • Josh wants to spend his years on Christ, the kingdom, and the people who will worship around the throne and sing that the slain Lamb is worthy.
      • The writer’s call for endurance rests on promised reward and Christ’s coming. Josh repeats that the coming one will not delay and urges the church not to shrink, drift, become complacent, embrace comfort, or waste life.

    8. 40:48 Invest every entrusted resource in work that outlives you

      • Practically spending life on Christ means receiving time, talents, gifts, resources, money, breath, and life itself as trusts from God and investing them beyond oneself.
      • Josh names children, community, and church as enduring investments and imagines a serving church where hungry families are fed, single parents receive help, marriages find hope, and people encounter Christ’s compassion before hearing it proclaimed.

    9. 41:36 Healing Drive represents changed stories rather than construction alone

      • In the vision for 41st Drive to become known as Healing Drive, Josh sees more than buildings, streets, bridges, and enjoyable features: he sees changed lives, met needs, and the gospel demonstrated before proclamation.
      • He sees the church becoming what it was meant to be, with God’s people bringing a little heaven to earth.

    10. 42:11 No one before Jesus will wish they invested less

      • Josh insists that no one standing before Jesus will wish for fewer hours of service, less generosity, or less investment in God’s work.
      • In Christ’s presence every sacrifice will appear small and worthwhile, every act of generosity wise, and every resource invested in the calling God gave the church a sound investment.
      • Christ, his kingdom, and his people are worth it; Josh’s final exhortation is, “Christian, don’t waste your life.”

  7. 43:17 Closing prayer for a united, serving church

    • Josh prays that the Spirit will form Crossroads into the deeply connected, selfless, Christ-exalting assembly the sermon has described.

    1. 43:17 Make Christ’s name great together

      Scripture: Luke 10:30-37

      • Josh asks the Father for help building deep fellowship and community in a selfish, discontented world, praying for the Spirit to flood hearts and draw the congregation into one ecclesia—one assembly, purpose, and body.
      • He asks that the church move, speak, serve, and sing together, making Christ’s name greater than every brand, mission, and institutional name.
      • Pointing to the cross displayed above the church’s name on the building, he calls that placement a deliberate proclamation that Christ stands above the church’s brand, name, mission, and purpose.
      • The prayer ends by asking God to make service compelling again and to help the church stop like the Samaritan, meet needs, and change the way people see the church.
