Overview

  1. 00:06 Acts 2 from 30,000 feet

    Scripture: Acts 2 Acts 1

    • Acts 2 is a busy chapter, so Josh gets straight to work. Luke records the birth of the church, what Jesus called in chapter 1 the baptism of the Holy Spirit, the very first message to come out of the Christian church, and finally what that Spirit-filled message ultimately led people toward.
    • There is value in spending a week on each section, but Josh believes the pieces were meant to be read together. The baptism of the Holy Spirit inevitably leads God’s people to something, and his job is to expose that.

    1. 01:17 Faithful to the text, not to a camp

      • Different groups hold varying views on Acts 2, and everyone seems to be trying to make a point. You don’t read Scripture to make a point, and in the effort some truth has been exposed and also some error.
      • Josh isn’t interested in pleasing whatever camp you land in; he wasn’t thinking of you when he put the message together. He wants to be faithful to the text and trust God with what he doesn’t fully understand, not to cause division or hurt feelings, but to understand what happened at Pentecost, how it applies today, and how we should strive to live as his witnesses.

  2. 02:01 Acts 1:8 fulfilled in Acts 2

    Scripture: Acts 1 Acts 1:8

    • Last week covered Acts 1, the last staff meeting, if you will, that Jesus held before he ascended. In chapter 1 the disciples wait for the Holy Spirit; in chapter 2 he comes. In chapter 1 they anticipate the birth of the church; in chapter 2 it is born. In chapter 1 they are told what to do with the Spirit when they receive him; in chapter 2 they receive him.
    • Acts 1:8 is fulfilled in Acts 2, ten days later: you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth. The gift the Father promised comes, and the church is born and begins to testify to God’s amazing work through Jesus Christ.

  3. 03:00 A massive shift in God’s operational strategy

    Scripture: Exodus 25 1 Corinthians 3:16 Ephesians 4:11–13

    • This is a massive shift in what Josh calls God’s operational strategy, which some call his redemptive plan. Before Christ, God’s presence was with the people; now it is in you. In Exodus 25 God told them to build a sanctuary where he would dwell among them. There’s a reason the room is called the auditorium and not the sanctuary: you are all sanctuaries, and this is just a building.
    • Paul tells the Christians in Corinth: don’t you know that you yourselves are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit dwells in your midst? We go from a temple made by man to a temple made by God. Back then they served God out of fear of what he would do if they broke the law; now we serve out of love for Jesus Christ and the power of the Spirit within us. Paul calls it a mystery.
    • Jesus accomplished his work, ascended, and the Father responded to his perfect obedience by sending the Spirit, who was not given until Jesus had been glorified. Christ’s work outlines what God is doing in us: his life is our righteousness by faith, his death brings pardon, his resurrection brings justification, “just as if I’d never sinned,” and his ascension makes way for the Spirit to come and give gifts. Ephesians 4: Christ gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors, and teachers to equip his people until we all reach unity and maturity in Christ.

    1. 05:27 Placed into the body of Christ

      Scripture: Galatians 3:26–28

      • This is a transition of ministry from the unique Son of God to the adopted sons and daughters of God acting on his behalf: the church, the bride of Christ, the household of faith, the family by adoption, a new temple with Christ as its foundation.
      • The baptism of the Holy Spirit is an act of God that places a believer in the body of Christ. As a body has many parts working together, believers come together in unity under Christ’s headship. Jesus was the first example of God among men; his church now exists as many examples, all connected under his headship. Galatians 3: in Christ Jesus you are all children of God through faith; there is neither Jew nor Gentile, slave nor free, male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.

    2. 06:51 Galatians 3:28 is about membership

      Scripture: Galatians 3:28

      • People love to use texts for their agenda, and Galatians 3:28 is a favorite with everything going on in the culture. But Paul isn’t dismantling God’s design for men and women; he is defining who the church includes. He is talking about membership.
      • The church isn’t this building; if it crumbles, they’ll raid your home and meet there without skipping a beat. Nor is it anyone who claims to be the church; there is no creed to memorize and no password at the door. The church is people with a personal relationship with Jesus Christ. A stranger from a church across the country could be Josh’s brother after a few minutes of conversation, because Christ connects them.

    3. 08:07 No Christian is running around without the Spirit

      Scripture: Romans 8:9

      • Some people claim that Christians who don’t do certain things aren’t filled with the Holy Spirit, and that is a dangerous claim. Paul says in Romans 8 that if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, they do not belong to Christ. If you are in Christ, you have the Spirit; if you don’t have the Spirit, you don’t have Christ. God didn’t forget to fill you up.
      • That is why Jesus told them to stay put: Christians don’t do God’s work without God’s power.

  4. 08:48 Pentecost: wind and tongues of fire

    Scripture: Acts 2:1–3

    • Jesus said they would receive power, and ten days later they do. When the day of Pentecost came they were all together, a sound like a violent wind came from heaven and filled the house, and they saw what seemed to be tongues of fire that separated and came to rest on each of them.

    1. 09:10 Josh’s love of fire

      • Josh confesses his family shamed him young for his love of fire. He made gasoline lines in the driveway, lit them, and drove his moped through; he never claimed to be smart. At four, sitting on the counter, his mom told him not to touch the stove because it was hot, and he needed proof: second-degree burns on both hands.
      • He can’t wait for fall, if Arizona cools off, so he can stare at the fire in the backyard. If he were a male Taylor Swift, he would want fire as his special effects every time.

    2. 10:15 God loves fire too

      • Do you know who else loves fire? God. In the Old Testament his special presence showed up in fire: the burning bush before Moses, the pillar of fire in the wilderness, the giving of the law, and his coming to dwell in the temple. Fire is beautiful and terrifying, powerful, mysterious, and thought-provoking.
      • Many people think of fire only as judgment, and the connection is there, as in the lake of fire. It is a display of strength, power, maybe even justice, but it is also necessary for purification. There is a burning bush inside every believer, but unlike the bush Moses saw on Mount Horeb, this fire is actually burning things up.

    3. 11:26 God dwelling in his people

      • God works how he chooses, not how you prefer, or, more uncomfortably, how you were taught he works; some of us were taught wrong. Here the Spirit comes for the first time in a significant way: God taking up residence in his most precious creation. Josh can think of only one other time that happened, the virgin birth, and it led to something incredible.
      • God made his presence known spectacularly: the sound of a violent wind without destruction, what looked like tongues of fire, the whole house filled with his Spirit. There has never been anything like it, because the disciples experience the presence of God around them, the temple, at the same moment they experience his presence within them for the first time: the new temple. There is no word for it other than divine.

  5. 13:00 Envy, and blessed are those who have not seen

    Scripture: John 20:29

    • Josh admits he is a bit envious of those in that house and would have given anything to see God move that way. Then he remembers Jesus’ words to Thomas: because you have seen me, you have believed; blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed.
    • He thinks of the little boy on the tricycle in The Incredibles who saw something amazing in the driveway and, when asked what he’s waiting for, says, “Something amazing, I guess.” Josh has seen the Lord move and is waiting because he knows God is about to do something amazing; he doesn’t know what, when, or how.

    1. 13:59 Don’t force your expectations on God

      • Josh gets the desire to experience amazing things, but he also sees the faithful who haven’t seen the spectacular yet still believe. It is tempting to want God to reveal himself in miraculous ways, but don’t force your expectations on him. Are things happening because you were taught they should, or because God is actually in it?
      • He fears many Christians are so convinced something should happen that they have created cultures and environments that look like movements of God but are actually manipulations of men. We want to see it, so we create it so we can see it. That is from you, not God.

    2. 15:00 A little girl at a revival service

      • Josh’s wife was a victim of this as a young girl. (God plans revival services, not men; he’ll leave it at that.) The children’s pastor taught that every believer can receive the gift of tongues if they believe and ask, then laid hands on the children expecting them to fall, slain in the Spirit. She fell because everyone else did; she didn’t want to be the one left standing.
      • His heart breaks imagining the doubt and insecurity in her mind on the floor: Was I supposed to feel something? Why did God reveal himself to my friend and not me? When is it okay to get up? Does God love me the same? Am I truly saved? The uncertainty it creates is evidence it was not from God.
      • Josh would take that little girl’s hand and tell her what he would tell anyone: he doesn’t know when or how God will reveal himself. For his wife, the Spirit played out not in that moment but years later, as God worked simultaneously in her future husband to prepare both hearts to come together in a marriage that displays God’s unity with his Son. You might not feel what everyone else feels, and that’s okay; keep trusting him. The movement of the Spirit you can’t see is usually the most powerful one.

    3. 17:24 Elijah and the gentle whisper

      Scripture: 1 Kings 19:11–12

      • In 1 Kings 19 Elijah is told to stand on the mountain because the Lord is about to pass by, a drive-by. A great wind shatters the rocks, but the Lord is not in the wind; then an earthquake, but the Lord is not in it; then a fire, but the Lord is not in the fire; and after the fire, a gentle whisper.
      • You can’t put God in a box. If he reveals himself to you spectacularly, praise God and tell Josh the story, but don’t let it go to your head; maybe he couldn’t whisper and needed to shout to get your attention. If he reveals himself in a whisper, don’t be ashamed of the humility of that; maybe he knew you were already looking for him.

  6. 18:34 The Spirit comes because Jesus said he would

    • Too many Christians insist on reproducing Pentecost. The Spirit comes because Jesus said he would send him, not in response to anyone’s spiritual activity, and it is part of a larger plan.

    1. 18:55 Leviticus 23 and the feasts

      Scripture: Leviticus 23 1 Corinthians 15:20

      • Pentecost means fiftieth, because the feast came 50 days after the Feast of Firstfruits; go read Leviticus 23. The feasts there correlate with the work of Jesus Christ: Passover reflects his death as the Lamb of God, Christ our Passover lamb; Firstfruits reflects his resurrection, Christ the firstfruits, as Paul puts it, of those who have fallen asleep; and Pentecost, 50 days later, reflects the formation of his church.
      • At Pentecost the Jews celebrated the giving of the law, which is why they are there in Acts 2; Christians after Acts 2 celebrate the giving of the Holy Spirit to the church. We gather on Sundays because the Lord was raised on the first day of the week, and it is also the day the Spirit was given to the church. That is why they meet every week, so they never forget.

    2. 20:09 Pentecost doesn’t need to be repeated

      • Don’t assume the miracles at Pentecost resulted from the disciples’ ten-day prayer meeting; if so, the Lord would have taught us to pray for that, and he didn’t. When you relapse into sin, you surely don’t pray for Jesus to take on flesh and climb Calvary again; it was a once-for-all event, finished across all time, with past, present, and future sins covered in his blood. The same is true for Pentecost.
      • Be cautious. Instead of putting your expectations on God, wait patiently, never stop listening for his voice, and stop trying to make him fit your mold.

  7. 21:15 Tongues: declaring the wonders of God

    Scripture: Acts 2:4–11

    • All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them. God-fearing Jews from every nation in Jerusalem gathered in bewilderment, because each heard their own language: aren’t these Galileans? Parthians, Medes, Elamites, residents of Mesopotamia, Judea, Cappadocia, Egypt, Rome, Cretans and Arabs: we hear them declaring the wonders of God in our own tongues.

    1. 22:07 The sounds of Pentecost

      Scripture: Romans 10:17 James 3:5–6

      • You can’t read Acts 2 without noticing its sounds: the wind, the tongues, and believers declaring the wonders of God to all nations. The Spirit often does a quiet work inside the Christian, but it never results in a quiet Christian. He has not come to be trapped in our hearts but to be heard throughout the world as the voice of God. This was always the plan: Romans 10 says faith comes from hearing the message, and the message is heard through the word about Christ.
      • Your tongue can be set on fire by heaven or by hell. James says the tongue is a small part of the body that makes great boasts; a great forest is set on fire by a small spark, and the tongue is itself set on fire by hell. The Bible doesn’t speak highly of the tongue, but these tongues in Acts 2 are on mission to declare God’s wonders. Put violent winds together with fire and you get a wildfire: the first time the gospel was preached, it was declared in all languages at once.

    2. 24:00 Unity, not uniformity

      • Those from Mesopotamia didn’t abandon their roots or culture; they became Mesopotamian Christians. Look at how diversity goes when man drives it: the DEI department under man’s control, Josh says, stinks. Pentecost is what happens when the Lord leads the DEI department. The Spirit doesn’t eject the Mesopotamian from the person; he adds Christ to the Mesopotamian. The gospel takes the whole thing, roots and all, and replants it in Christ.
      • So some Christians won’t look or worship like us, and that’s okay; God loves variety. Some won’t take communion every week; the church hasn’t unlocked any secrets, it just decided years ago to make it available weekly. The Spirit isn’t destroying diversity but building it, so let it humble you.
      • If you like dancing, dance; raise your hands, stand, sit and pray, or come kneel and bow down. This is home. If the service moved to Uganda or the underground church in China, it would look vastly different, and there is room for that. Unity does not mean uniformity. This unstoppable movement is global and will look global; God isn’t dismantling cultures but building the character of the people within them.

  8. 27:04 “They’ve had too much wine”

    Scripture: Acts 2:12–13

    • Amazed and perplexed, they asked what this meant, but some made fun of them, as there are always some: they’ve had too much wine.
    • Josh, “just a little Baptist boy with no clue,” looked up the phrase “drunk in the Spirit,” which always confused him. He thinks of self-control when he thinks of the Spirit, because that is what Scripture teaches, and from experience he thinks of no control at all when he thinks of drunkenness. Nowhere does the Bible say to be drunk in the Spirit, and it doesn’t speak highly of being drunk at all.
    • Yet some leaders tell God’s people to cast off all restraint, which is not biblical, and point to slurred speech and falling to the ground as proof of the Spirit’s work. Why would the very one who gives self-control lead us to behave like drunks? Being filled with the Spirit isn’t an emotionally charged experience, a divine buzz, or a heavenly high; it is faithful, sober, reverent submission to the God of glory.

  9. 28:53 The first Christian sermon

    • Josh wasn’t sure how to handle this part until he sensed the Lord telling him not to overcomplicate it, just read it. He won’t assume everyone has read the first Christian sermon ever preached, so he reads it, long as it is.
    • A few notes: Peter didn’t preach in tongues; he probably spoke Aramaic. It is a message from a Jew to Jews on a Jewish holy day, about the resurrection of the Jewish Messiah their nation had crucified. It has three points: what happened (the Spirit came), how it happened (Jesus is alive), and why it happened (to save sinners).

    1. 29:51 Not drunk: this is what Joel spoke of

      Scripture: Acts 2:14–21

      • Peter stands with the Eleven: these people are not drunk as you suppose; it’s only nine in the morning. He starts with a joke, as a great sermon should. Orthodox Jews didn’t eat or drink before 9:00 a.m. on a holy day; this was before Jimmy Buffett and Alan Jackson let everybody know it’s five o’clock somewhere.
      • In verse 16 Peter says this is what the prophet Joel spoke of: in the last days I will pour out my Spirit on all people; sons and daughters will prophesy, young men see visions, old men dream dreams; and everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.
      • Peter isn’t saying Joel was predicting Pentecost itself; he is showing that the Spirit Joel spoke about is here now. Jaws would have dropped: Jesus had finished the plan of salvation, the work of redemption was done, and only one thing was left, sharing the good news with the whole world, beginning with Israel.

    2. 31:36 Jesus of Nazareth, raised and exalted

      Scripture: Acts 2:22–35

      • Verse 22, the big chunk: Jesus of Nazareth was accredited by God through miracles, wonders, and signs; he was handed over by God’s deliberate plan and foreknowledge, and you, with the help of wicked men, put him to death on the cross, but God raised him from the dead, because it was impossible for death to keep its hold on him.
      • Peter quotes David: I saw the Lord always before me; you will not abandon me to the realm of the dead or let your Holy One see decay. David died and his tomb is still here, so David isn’t talking about David; as a prophet he saw ahead and spoke of the Messiah’s resurrection. God raised this Jesus, and we are all witnesses. Exalted to God’s right hand, he received the promised Holy Spirit and poured out what you now see and hear. David did not ascend, yet he said, “The Lord said to my Lord, sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet.”

    3. 33:34 What the skeptic should notice

      Scripture: Psalms

      • Josh could listen to Peter preach all day. For skeptics, and for believers from an apologetic standpoint: Peter spends no time convincing anyone that Jesus was a real person. They all knew it; the historical evidence is tremendous, it wasn’t debated then, and it really isn’t debated now. What they didn’t know was that God raised him and gave him the name above all names.
      • David wrote 73 psalms; go read them, and now you know the motivation behind what he wrote: he saw what was to come and got a sneak peek at Christ’s raised body. About 1,500 years separate the patriarch David from the doubter Thomas, yet both saw the raised body of Christ.

    4. 35:05 Can the witnesses be trusted?

      • Peter offers eyewitnesses as evidence that Jesus was alive, and Paul would later say there were over 500. Can we trust their testimony? Josh thinks so. First, the disciples didn’t believe Jesus would be raised; they had to be convinced, and what changes your mind is strong evidence to the contrary. They saw something that turned them on a dime.
      • Second, they had nothing to gain by preaching a lie: no wealth or health, but opposition, prison, torture, and death. A lunatic won’t take that to his death. Thousands believed, then tens of thousands, then millions, and the message is still accepted today. The witnesses can be trusted.

    5. 36:14 If Jesus is dead, he can’t send the Spirit

      Scripture: John 16:7

      • When Josh presents the good news, he goes to the cross to show where the sinless substitute died for the world. Peter, because of his audience, points to Calvary as the place where Israel killed her own Messiah. He is confident because he knows the truth everyone else is still catching up on: Jesus is alive.
      • John was there when Jesus said it is for your good that he goes away, because unless he goes the Advocate will not come, but if he goes he will send him. If Jesus is dead, he can’t send the Spirit. Jesus is alive and did what he said, so Peter preaches with absolute confidence.

  10. 37:07 Cut to the heart: repent and be baptized

    Scripture: Acts 2:36–41

    • Now that the truth has cut deep, it is time to let Jesus bandage it. Verse 36: God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Messiah. Cut to the heart, they ask, “Brothers, what shall we do?” Peter replies: repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit; the promise is for you, your children, and all who are far off. About 3,000 were added that day.

    1. 38:07 Baptism: an outward expression of an inward reality

      Scripture: Acts 10 Hebrews 11

      • Some twist these verses to teach that you must be baptized to be saved, or to receive the Holy Spirit. Josh says Luke destroys both in Acts. Baptism is an outward expression of an inward reality, a public declaration of how Jesus has changed your life, sharing an intimate faith with the body so they are built up and invited to celebrate what God is doing. If baptism were required for salvation, Peter would bring it up in his other letters and teaching, and he doesn’t.
      • The idea that you can’t receive the Spirit without baptism is easier to dismantle: read Acts 10 and tell it to the people in Cornelius’s home, who received the Spirit before they were baptized. Stop putting your expectations on God; his way, his timing. Get out of the way and pray.
      • We aren’t baptized to receive the Spirit or to be saved; we are baptized because we are saved and want to obey, and because Jesus’ baptism so pleased God that the skies opened, the Spirit came down like a dove, and he declared, “This is my Son.” As his adopted sons and daughters, all we want is to please him. If baptism were required for salvation, none of the people listed in Hebrews 11 would be saved either.

  11. 40:17 Four devotions that built a kingdom culture

    Scripture: Acts 2:42–47

    • This is why Josh didn’t break the chapter into bite-sized pieces: the Spirit’s movement called people from all nations to be one under Christ wherever they returned, and they devoted themselves to four disciplines that developed a kingdom culture.
    • Acts 2:42: they devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching, to fellowship, to the breaking of bread, and to prayer. They were filled with awe, had everything in common, sold property and possessions for anyone in need, met daily in the temple courts, broke bread in their homes with glad and sincere hearts, and enjoyed the favor of all people, and the Lord added to their number daily those who were being saved.

    1. 41:16 Devoted to the apostles’ teaching

      • The central point of the apostles’ teaching was that salvation is a gift from God: you didn’t make it happen; Jesus did it for you. That is why you can go through all the toil of life with a smile: you hold a gift that compares to no other.
      • The world says everything you need is within you. Christianity says nothing you have matters if you don’t have Jesus, because something is wrong with all our hearts. You aren’t unhappy because you married an idiot or your boss is a jerk; something in you needs to be transformed and your mind renewed, by devoting yourself daily to God’s word and to relationship with Jesus.
      • Growing Christians are students of the word. If you don’t like to read, buy headphones and get an audio Bible or a C. S. Lewis audiobook. Talk to people who know more than you; if they are jerks about it, Josh will knock them down a peg. Memorize Scripture, read with your family, hang it on your walls and doorframes, sign your kids up for Kids Truth on Wednesday nights, and join a life group, as Chris said.

    2. 43:37 Devoted to each other

      • They also devoted themselves to each other. “Josh, I can’t find a good group.” Then you’re off the hook; go home and watch Netflix. No: start your own. “I don’t get a lot out of it.” Life groups weren’t launched so you could get a lot out of them, but so you could pour a lot out; you empty yourself and are filled in the emptying.
      • The early church responded to Christ’s sacrifice by sacrificing for each other; it was contagious, which is why it was unstoppable. What better way to love God than to love your neighbor? “I love Jesus, I just can’t stand his people” is a cute line, but there are no excuses: God didn’t ask you to like your neighbor; he said to love him. Jesus didn’t wait for you to be lovable; he loved you first. Go and do the same.

    3. 45:13 Sacrificial giving, not socialism

      • They sold property and possessions for anyone in need and put it all on the line for each other. That is why the church talks about giving as sacrifice, not tithes, numbers, and percentages.
      • Some numbskull always says Jesus was a socialist. No: this was voluntary, no one told them to do it; it was temporary, and yes didn’t always mean yes when people tried to take advantage; and it was motivated by love, not tyranny. Their desires changed, and they had to do it because they knew Jesus did it for them.

    4. 46:07 Love more attractive than miracles

      • Many think the movement was unstoppable because of the apostles’ miracles. Josh argues that even more attractive than the miraculous was the way the church loved each other and served the Lord. That is why Crossroads emphasizes Crossroads Cares, the public needs list, and the body responding to the community’s needs.
      • He wants to be an Acts 2 church, so full of the Spirit that when the doors open, love, affection, and sacrifice burst out and hit the community; a church that boldly proclaims the good news to the nations and devotes itself to God, the apostles’ teaching, and one another.

  12. 47:11 Have you called on the name of the Lord?

    Scripture: Luke 11:13

    • Before all that, one question: have you called on the name of the Lord? None of it matters unless you first call on his name for the forgiveness of your sins. Be baptized and show everyone the faith God has given you. Do you trust Jesus to save you? No question matters more, because anyone can do all these things; this behavioral nonsense means nothing without faith and will not please God without faith.
    • Jesus says in Luke 11: if you, though evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him. So let’s ask the Lord for what we need.

  13. 48:15 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks God for the picture in Acts 2 of a spark that started in a house and blew up into a global movement declaring his goodness, mercy, and gospel. He asks God to give them everything they need, not to be seen or perceived a certain way, but to be filled with him, because what is in us isn’t worth being filled with: less of us, more of you. They have seen what happens when people get out of the way and let God move. “Make us that kind of place, Lord. We surrender to you; we lay it all down.”
