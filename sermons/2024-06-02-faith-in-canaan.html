Overview

  1. 00:07 Joshua 2: a detour before the crossing

    Scripture: Joshua 2 Genesis 12 Joshua 1 Joshua 3

    • Josh asks the church to turn to Joshua 2. The first five books of the Bible are about the law, and the church covered Genesis earlier this year. Joshua begins a long line of books on the history of Israel: they are free, but they haven’t taken the land God promised Abraham back in Genesis 12.
    • After a massive leadership transition, Moses has died and Joshua is filling his shoes; it is time to cross the Jordan and grab the promise. You finish chapter 1 thinking, “Time to cross, let’s go,” but that is chapter 3, which Pastor Steve will cover next week. Instead chapter 2 gives the story of a sketchy lady from Jericho named Rahab.

  2. 01:31 A prostitute in the Hall of Faith

    Scripture: Hebrews 11 Joshua 2:1 James 2

    • When Josh thinks of women in the Bible, he thinks of godly women who have their stuff together, like Mary, who hears from the angel that she will have a baby and essentially says, “Here to serve God, let’s do this,” or Sarah, first lady of the Hebrew race, who gets an honorable mention for her faith in Hebrews 11.
    • Only two women are named in Hebrews 11, and the other is Rahab. Verse 1 tells us Joshua secretly sent two spies to look over the land, especially Jericho, and they stayed in the house of a prostitute named Rahab. Whatever you call her, harlot or hooker, we know what she did: she made a career selling herself to men.
    • How can Hebrews 11 mention these two women in the same breath? James 2 mentions Rahab alongside Abraham, and Levi the tax collector puts her in the genealogy of Jesus with Jacob, David, Solomon, and Josiah. How does this prostitute keep making headlines in God’s redemptive plan? That is what Josh wants to show.

  3. 04:06 From pervert to preacher

    • Josh gets real: without the Rahab story and others like it, he isn’t sure he would be convinced God could use him in the church. People from his past would have less than flattering names for him too. If he met them now, he would own his actions and ask forgiveness, but he wouldn’t have much to say except that he is different, and the only thing he can point to is Jesus.
    • Jesus carried him from pervert to preacher, from greed to generosity, from failure to father, and turned a lost boy into a loving husband. It’s a miracle. In college he could barely take care of himself; he used one towel an entire semester before washing it. Now Jesus leads him as he cares for hundreds of hearts and souls. He boasts in his transformation because it is God’s work, not his.

    1. 06:10 A skeptic asks how he knows

      • After church, a skeptic thanked Josh for his courage in discussing his journey with addiction and shared his own transformation. The difference was that Josh gave credit to God and the man took the credit himself. He asked, “People outside the faith have transformations too. How do you know yours is from God?”
      • Josh told him the NIH cited in 2019 that 74% of addiction treatment is rooted in some sort of spirituality within the church, so conquering addiction without faith isn’t common. And if someone did, he would ask what they replaced it with. When the man finally told him, Josh looked him in the eye: “I didn’t replace my addiction with anything. It was just gone. That’s how I know God did it for me.” It isn’t everyone’s story, but it was his.

    2. 07:43 Where sin increased, grace increased more

      Scripture: Romans 5:20–21

      • Nothing brings Josh more joy than seeing people turn from sin and testify that God helped them, and the church has countless such stories. Rahab went from prostitute to the ancestral line of the Messiah and the Hall of Faith.
      • Romans 5 came to his mind: where sin increased, grace increased all the more. You can’t out-sin the cross of Christ or earn a title Jesus can’t obliterate. His favorite coffee-mug phrase: God can and does use messed-up people to build his kingdom on earth.

    3. 09:01 No GPA to maintain

      • Christians don’t have a grade point average to maintain before God. Failing one class and sitting at a dismal 2.3 doesn’t mean you can’t be accepted or used; God can take your rotten performance and use it for his glory.
      • God isn’t pleased by your performance, reputation, or abilities; he is pleased by your faith. Faith, not denomination, education, or affiliation, was the most important thing about Rahab, and it is the most important thing about you.

  4. 09:50 The king of Jericho hears about the spies

    Scripture: Joshua 2:2–3

    • The king of Jericho is told Israelites have come to spy out the land and sends word to Rahab to bring them out. Israel’s spycraft was a bit unpolished; if the people you are spying on know you are there, that’s typically bad.
    • The king is nervous. He doesn’t want an invasion, he knows millions of people are just east of the Jordan, and many in Jericho would have heard of their escape from Egypt. He is trying to hold the city together and maintain control, and he is watching it slip away.

  5. 10:56 Hidden under the flax

    Scripture: Joshua 2:4–6 Joshua 12 Joshua 2:15

    • Rahab hides the two men, tells the king’s men they left at dusk when the gate was closing, and sends them off to chase them, while the spies lie hidden under stalks of flax on her roof.
    • Jericho was one of many major cities in Canaan ruled by a king; the list of those kings is in chapter 12 for some light reading. It covered about nine acres, with archaeological evidence of double walls about 15 feet apart, and Rahab’s house was built into the outer wall, as verse 15 says.

  6. 11:59 Yes, Rahab lied

    • With stories like this we get hung up on the wrong details, so Josh deals with it: yes, Rahab lied, and yes, lying is a sin. We get it. And believers who say her lie is okay are hypocrites.
    • God didn’t need Rahab to lie to protect the spies; if she had told the truth, he would have found another way. God can work his will through lies, and he can forgive her on the basis of her faith. God punishes sin, but God saves sinners who believe.

    1. 13:05 Hospitality and a hard place

      • Hospitality was paramount in ancient cultures, and laws put all the pressure on a host to protect and care for anyone who entered the house. Her position wasn’t black and white: follow the social laws or the moral laws?

    2. 13:34 Don’t expect new believers to be caught up

      Scripture: Luke 6:36

      • Rahab grew up worshiping pagan gods under leaders who made laws to benefit themselves, not under leaders who kept the law on their lips. Expecting a non-believer to convert and immediately act like a mature believer is ridiculous.
      • Josh has baptized people and gone out to eat with them while their hair is still wet, and they are dropping bombs in the lobby. The church prays, shows patience, and trusts God to work on their hearts, as he did with Josh over more than a decade. Someone following Jesus five minutes won’t look like someone following him fifteen years; expecting it is higher expectations than Jesus had. Spend more time in the red letters: Jesus says be merciful as your Father is merciful, and that was about our enemies, so how much more for a brother or sister in Christ?
      • Rahab acted like any pagan in the city would, and you probably would too if cornered; and if you would have told the truth, to God be the glory, not you. It drives Josh mad when Christians reach the other end of holiness and act like they got there on their own. That is pure pride and causes others to stumble. Be humble is a command, not a suggestion; it is evidence you are in the kingdom. Spend less time on the speck in theirs and more on the plank in yours.

    3. 16:08 Celebrate faith more than failures

      Scripture: James 2:25

      • Take a page out of James’ playbook. He saw her actions not as a chance to rebuke her but as proof she was a true believer: was not even Rahab the prostitute considered righteous for what she did when she gave lodging to the spies?
      • If you get nothing else, remember this: we must spend way more time celebrating the faith of others than calling out their failures. What gets more of your attention, Rahab’s title as a prostitute or her work as a refuge to the spies, her lie or the fruit of her faith? You might not agree with her approach, but you have to admire her faith.

  7. 17:16 Justified by faith

    Scripture: Joshua 2:7 Hebrews 11:31

    • The Jericho soldiers set out after the spies on the road to the fords of the Jordan, and the gate is shut behind them. Hebrews points out that Rahab was justified through her faith: by faith the prostitute Rahab, because she welcomed the spies, was not killed with the disobedient. She lied, but she was justified by her faith, and the evidence is her effort to spare the spies and, as we’ll see, her whole family.

    1. 18:00 Better mediocre with Christ

      • God is funny sometimes: the paranoid king’s best soldiers are locked outside the walls, while God’s mediocre spies, who couldn’t get into the city without being spotted, are safe inside. Better to be mediocre with Christ than excellent without him. Josh chuckles as men make their plans and God’s providence shines through.

  8. 18:37 “The Lord your God is God”

    Scripture: Joshua 2:8–11

    • Hidden behind bundles of flax that grew about four feet high, the spies hear Rahab before they lie down: she knows the Lord has given them the land; everyone is melting in fear because they heard how the Lord dried up the Red Sea and what Israel did to Sihon and Og; for the Lord your God is God in heaven above and on the earth below.

    1. 19:28 Rahab’s truth matters more than her lie

      • Far more important than Rahab’s lie is Rahab’s truth. News flash: there is a believing heart in Canaan. The Red Sea was forty years ago, so the city had known about Israel for forty years; while Israel wandered, their enemy was shaking in fear. Some people live like that now, in an atmosphere of paranoia.
      • Rahab had to make a decision of allegiance: hand them over or hide them, serve the king of Jericho or the king of the cosmos. No one is saved by fear. Fear can help us get to faith, but it can also overwhelm you until you melt in it.
      • People in Jericho aren’t that different from people in North Phoenix. Many wouldn’t argue God’s existence and understand intellectually that there is a case for him, but they have never entered a relationship with him through Jesus. Rahab made her decision and chose the God of heaven.

    2. 21:27 What would your decisions tell us?

      • If we looked at your life with a fine-toothed comb, what would your decisions tell us? Not your decision to be here today, but Monday afternoon when you are fed up with your boss, Tuesday night when you are exhausted heading home, or when your adult children act like adult children. Are you following the God of heaven or the lowercase gods of this world?
      • Saving faith isn’t a weekend-warrior lifestyle. Genuine faith doesn’t just believe God is real; it believes God is our refuge. Faith isn’t holding a set of right beliefs; celebrating someone’s strong theology is basically saying they are smart. Faith is clinging to hope in our most desperate hour: “Lord, I’ve got nothing else; you’ve got my life.” The question isn’t whether your theology is strong but whether, when you suffered, you blamed God or rejoiced that you are his child and a co-heir with Christ, clung to fear or drew near.

  9. 23:32 Verse 12: the source of fear becomes the source of mercy

    Scripture: Joshua 2:12–13

    • Rahab includes herself among those melting in fear, but in verse 12 she realizes the source of her fear can be the source of mercy. She asks the spies to swear by the Lord to show kindness to her family, spare her father, mother, brothers, sisters, and all who belong to them, and save them from death.
    • In effect she says, “I understand your God is the one true God, and he has guaranteed you victory. I want in. When you march on Jericho, please spare my family.” She isn’t taking out an insurance policy in case something happens; she believes it is happening and aims to cut a deal with God. She believes God is good, fair, wonderful, and worthy of her sacrifice and worship.
    • Isn’t it incredible how much we learn about faith from a prostitute? Some might be offended to come to church and get taken to school by a prostitute.

    1. 25:28 Faith that seeks God’s reward

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:6 James 2:14

      • Hebrews 11 makes it clear that without faith it is impossible to please God, because anyone who comes to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him. To cut a deal with God, Rahab had to believe he would reward her kindness. Seeking rewards from God pleases God.
      • James 2 asks what good it is to claim faith without deeds; can such faith save? James, Rahab, Paul, John, and Peter all say the same thing: what you do reveals who you are. Saving faith is best seen by works, not words; share your faith by showing it. A dead faith turns the spies over to the Jericho FBI and tries to protect its own family by getting in good with the king. The question is which king you are getting in good with.

    2. 26:59 “God just hasn’t come through for me”

      • Many in the church are nervous when a prostitute is our example of faith, but we shouldn’t be. After Josh shares his journey with addiction, people tell him they are happy God showed up for him, but he just hasn’t come through for them. That mindset frustrates him: God gave you eternal life through Jesus Christ. He hasn’t taken this away yet? So what? Maybe it isn’t time yet; maybe he never will. When the spies knock, will you lay your life in his hands or take matters into your own?

    3. 28:01 Bring it to him with open hands

      • God didn’t reach down and grab Josh’s addiction one morning. At first he didn’t think he needed to be saved from it or that it would get in the way. It took years of the Spirit convicting him. He didn’t make a fancy plan with medications on the nightstand; he said, “God, you need to take this now. Carry me through the sleepless nights. Don’t let me become a crabby husband or an awful father. These withdrawals are killing me, but I don’t want this anymore because I know you don’t want it for me. Take it.” And he did.
      • Many are waiting for God to reach in and grab things from their lives, but that isn’t how it works. Bring it to him with open hands, not clamped ones, lay it at his feet, and he will either take it or he won’t. If he does, praise God, and don’t closet that testimony: God doesn’t sanctify you for your silence; he saves us for our song.
      • If he doesn’t take it, praise God; he has something for you. He isn’t confirming that what you’re doing is okay, but you can still do kingdom work with some mud on your boots. Keep working it out with him, ask him for help, keep talking to him, waiting on him, and being around people who love him. Keep the faith.

  10. 30:11 Faith without seeing

    • We know Rahab had faith because we saw it at work. She believed God could change her circumstances and her family’s circumstances.
    • The Israelites camped east of the Jordan were eyewitnesses to the Red Sea; they saw Moses. Rahab, a long way off on foot, only heard about it, and even heard it as the sea drying up. She isn’t an eyewitness, yet she believes so much that she commits treason against her earthly king, hoping her heavenly king will protect her family. Rahab never made it about Rahab. That is faith that works.

  11. 31:44 “Our lives for your lives”

    Scripture: Joshua 2:14 1 Corinthians 15:57

    • The spies assure her, “Our lives for your lives,” promising to treat her kindly and faithfully when the Lord gives them the land. They pledge their lives not to break their word. That seems to be a go-to play in God’s playbook: Jesus gave us the same assurance, my life for yours. Debt-free himself, he took our unbearable debt load and gave us a way to live debt-free again. Jesus paid it all; sin had left a crimson stain, but he washed us white as snow. Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.

    1. 32:46 Don’t keep Jesus on the down low

      Scripture: Romans 10:14

      • The spies demanded that Rahab keep their little covenant hush-hush, but the Lord hasn’t asked you to keep your relationship with him on the down low. As the world grows more hostile, the temptation will be to bunker up, close the windows, and keep a low profile. Fight the urge, because Jesus wants everyone to know he has paid the price of redemption.
      • Keeping Jesus a secret might spare you hate mail or nasty Facebook comments, but what about the souls around you? Even Rahab had to hear before she could believe. Paul says in Romans 10: how can they believe in the one they have not heard of, and how can they hear without someone preaching to them? God’s people put faith in his promises and then speak them.

    2. 33:58 A promise to hold on to

      Scripture: John 14:18–21

      • Josh shares a promise he recalls often, John 14:18: “I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you,” reading through Jesus’ words that whoever has his commands and keeps them loves him and will be loved by the Father. He is holding on to that promise like no other, and that is all Rahab is doing: holding on to a promise.

  12. 34:57 The scarlet cord

    Scripture: Joshua 2:15–21

    • Rahab lets the spies down by a rope through her window in the city wall and tells them to hide in the hills three days. They tell her the oath won’t bind them unless she ties the scarlet cord in the window and brings her whole family into her house; anyone who goes into the street is responsible for their own blood. “Agreed,” she says, and she ties the scarlet cord in her window.

    1. 35:58 Visible signs of God’s covenants

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:26 Hebrews 9:22 1 John 1:9

      • In biblical covenants God often assigns a visual reminder: circumcision for Abraham, the book and the people sprinkled with blood at Sinai, the rainbow for Noah, and the bread and the cup Jesus gave his disciples, which the church took earlier in the service. Paul says whenever you eat this bread and drink this cup, you proclaim the Lord’s death until he comes.
      • The scarlet rope would mark Rahab’s house as under the protection of Israel’s army when they took the city. It may have seemed insignificant to the neighbors, but not when the invasion began. Its color reminds us of blood. It was a sign of her faith, which led to the salvation of her family, much like the blood of the Passover lamb: every home marked with blood was spared that night.
      • There isn’t just a scarlet thread in Rahab’s window; there is a scarlet thread running through Scripture from beginning to end, a stream of blood pointing to Christ’s sacrifice on the cross. Hebrews 9 says the law requires nearly everything be cleansed with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness. Two thousand years ago on a hill in Israel, Jesus shed his blood for every sin that ever was and ever would be, and if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness.

    2. 38:16 Hang the red rope in your window

      • If this is Josh’s last day, don’t cry for him: he is heaven-bound, covered by Christ’s blood, and he has been a priest to his family. His family hangs that red rope in their window; they love Jesus and aren’t afraid to say so. Never underestimate how your faith can impact those around you: Rahab’s whole family and their families were saved, a generation.
      • Josh carried this all week: he can’t think of anything worse for a believer to endure than an unbelieving family member. If they don’t hang the scarlet rope now, keep praying and never lose hope. It is hard to share faith with family, but keep talking to them about Jesus even if it ruins your little Thanksgiving festival; don’t be quiet just to preserve the peace.
      • He admires how Rahab loved her family and prays loved ones would see our love for Jesus and his love for us and come running to him. The world needs more homes with a scarlet rope in the window, and it won’t happen unless we are faithful to speak Jesus to them.

  13. 40:22 The Prince of Egypt and the fathers in the homes

    Scripture: Exodus 12:30

    • The Prince of Egypt came out on December 16, 1998, when Josh was 13, and he watched it over and over. He wept at the midnight Passover scene. He wasn’t angry at God; he knew why it was necessary. He was frustrated with the fathers in the homes: why didn’t you do it, why did you ignore the warnings and the signs, why didn’t you listen to Moses or follow your neighbor’s lead?
    • Those emotions still hit him when he reads Exodus 12: there was loud wailing in Egypt, for there was not a house without someone dead. It became a theme for his ministry.

  14. 41:57 Rededicate your home to the Lord

    Scripture: Joshua 24:15

    • It isn’t too late to cover your life and your home in the blood of Jesus. You can start fresh, but you have to walk in faith and hang the scarlet rope: “I’m a sinner in need of a Savior. This house needs God’s grace. I fell short of his standard, but I trust God’s grace is enough for me.” Josh holds fast every day to a simple faith that on the final day God will see Christ’s blood on him, pass over him, and welcome him as one of his own.
    • His challenge to those tasked with spiritual leadership in their homes: rededicate your home to the Lord today, even if it looks different than the first time and you don’t control everything you once did. Throw out the clutter, find the red ribbon you buried and hang it back up, read the word together, teach your children who Jesus is and how much you need him, encourage your kids daily even if they don’t want it, and love your spouse like Jesus does.
    • If Jesus were visiting your home after church, would you rush home to clean things up? Clean it up now, confess to the Lord, recommit, and decide today: as for me and my household, we will serve the Lord.

  15. 44:05 Closing prayer

    Scripture: John 14:18

    • Josh praises God as good and more precious than anything he has experienced, more than he deserves, and thanks him for his promise not to leave us as orphans and to be with us always, in struggle and in celebration.
    • On a theme of conquest, courage, and boldness, he is glad to stand behind God rather than in front of him opposing him, because the day will come when God’s conquest meets evil and those who sought their own desires. He doesn’t know when, but the victory is God’s, and God says we share in it.
