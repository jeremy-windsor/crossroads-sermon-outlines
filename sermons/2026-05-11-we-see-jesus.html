Overview

  1. 00:32 Pay the most careful attention so that salvation does not drift away

    • Josh opens Hebrews 2 by carrying forward Hebrews 1, explains the warning through the image of an unsecured boat and a slipping ring, and applies the danger of neglect to ordinary Christian habits before contrasting the law’s just punishment with the greatness of salvation in Christ.

    1. 00:32 Hebrews 1 leads directly into the warning of Hebrews 2

      Scripture: Ephesians 6:17 Hebrews 2 Hebrews 1:1 Hebrews 2:1

      • After joking that he nearly left without preaching, Josh tells hearers with a “sword of the Spirit” to turn to Hebrews 2 and says its opening picture is meant to be seen whether or not someone considers himself a visual learner.
      • He recalls Hebrews 1:1: God spoke to the ancestors through the prophets in many times and ways. Even with the Old Testament’s pictures, types, shadows, and near-savior figures pointing toward Christ, the eternally significant message can still be ignored, dismissed, or forgotten.
      • Hebrews 2:1 therefore commands the church to pay the most careful attention to what it has heard so that it does not drift away.

    2. 01:40 Pararreō pictures tragic negligence, not an accidental detour

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:1

      • Josh explains the Greek word pararreō through uses that intensify the translation “drift away.” One picture is a valuable boat left unsecured to wander aimlessly; another is a ring allowed to slip from a finger.
      • A new speedboat would be protected like a firstborn, and a wedding ring is worn as a reminder of promises. Josh asks whether believers devote the same energy and passion to protecting faith and treats Hebrews 2 as a relentless call to self-examination.

    3. 02:51 The warning addresses believers under attack and points them back to their anchor

      Scripture: 1 Peter 5:8 1 Timothy 6:12 John 16:13 Hebrews 6:19

      • The warning can benefit skeptics, but Josh says it is directed to believers: a great truth has been heard, yet without the most careful attention surrounding currents can carry it away.
      • First-century Jewish Christians deeply honored the Old Testament, but even they were warned about dismissing God’s greatest word to humanity, the good news of Christ.
      • Peter’s picture of the devil prowling like a lion reveals an enemy intent on stealing the precious gospel possession. Josh warns that baptism and public profession do not end the attack; Satan celebrates when a public Christian lives a weak faith.
      • The Holy Spirit equips believers to fight the good fight and guides them into truth. Anticipating Hebrews 6, Josh says God’s promises are a secure anchor: when drift occurs, the anchor has not moved; the believer has allowed slack through irresponsibility, laziness, or neglect.

    4. 04:44 Neglected habits produce drift even inside the gathering

      • Josh calls neglect a greater source of trouble in a believer’s life than any other failure and names its ordinary forms: neglecting personal Scripture habits, prayer, worship, the gathering, preaching and teaching, service, spiritual gifts, tithing, and support for the local church.
      • Simply attending does not fulfill the gathering’s purpose. Some remain in the lobby, some hear teaching but never serve, and some check a box without entering the church’s mission. The cumulative result is drift.
      • The author’s discomfort can almost be felt: many things receive careful attention, but the salvation proclaimed by God through his Son deserves the most careful attention.

    5. 06:03 The greater salvation makes neglect inescapably serious

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:1-3a Romans 5:1

      • Josh returns to Hebrews 2:1–3a: if the message mediated through angels was binding and each violation and disobedience received just punishment, no one can expect to escape after ignoring so great a salvation.
      • Addressing Hebrews with the law, the author reminds them that breaking God’s law carries justice. Josh contrasts that judgment with the salvation message: sins are removed, believers have peace with God through the Lord Jesus Christ, and healing has come. His instruction is to hold tight.

    6. 06:50 Deuteronomy models careful attention through every part of life

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 6:1-9 Deuteronomy 28

      • Josh reads Deuteronomy 6:1–9 in context: Israel must learn and obey God’s commands, fear the Lord across generations, love him with heart, soul, and strength, keep his words on their hearts, teach them to children, discuss them through daily rhythms, and place visible reminders around the home.
      • He sees the same demand for most careful attention. He summarizes the law’s blessings and curses as a Father leading children toward enjoyable life with him: obedience is blessed and disregard brings punishment.
      • For Mother’s Day, Josh asks children to thank the mother, aunt, or grandmother who has corrected them, turning discipline into a humorous expression of gratitude.

    7. 09:21 Blessing at the end does not promise an easy process

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 28

      • Josh rejects prosperity teaching: following Jesus does not guarantee present wealth, beauty, success, or freedom from struggle. Struggling and non-struggling people alike love Jesus.
      • A cake illustration separates unpleasant ingredients from the finished result. Flour, raw eggs, yeast, and extract are undesirable alone, but together they become cake; likewise, the Christian may endure hard ingredients now while awaiting final blessing.
      • Because Scripture promises eternal praise, crowns, and rewards, Josh calls hearers to protect gospel truth at least as fiercely as they protect a prized car or other possession.

    8. 11:51 Violation and disobedience expose sins of commission and omission

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:2-3a Leviticus 19:18; Matthew 22:39; Mark 12:31 Matthew 5:44; Luke 6:28 2 Samuel 11:1-27 Jonah 1:1-3; 4:2 Genesis 3:1-6 Numbers 20:7-12 Luke 12:48

      • Returning to Hebrews 2:2–3a, Josh says “violation” or “transgression” concerns sins of commission, while “disobedience” suggests sins of omission. Sin includes both doing what God forbids and failing to do what God commands.
      • Avoiding robbery does not fulfill the command to love one’s neighbor, and refusing to curse an enemy is not the same as praying for that enemy. Omission can therefore be as troubling as commission.
      • A rapid church quiz illustrates the categories: David’s adultery with Bathsheba is commission; Jonah’s flight from Nineveh is active commission rooted in resistance to mercy; Adam’s passivity while Eve is deceived is omission; and Moses striking the rock instead of speaking to it is commission.
      • Grace does not eliminate the Lord’s discipline. Jesus’ warning that much is required from those given much leads Josh to summarize: commission does what God hates, omission fails to do what God loves, and Hebrews 2 says both are judged. Neglect is especially dangerous because it can destroy while a person feels safe.

  2. 14:35 God confirms the salvation message and gives gifts that build the church

    • Josh follows Hebrews 2:3–4 from the Lord’s announcement through apostolic witnesses, signs, wonders, miracles, and gifts of the Spirit; he then turns the passage into a concrete call to discover and use Spirit-given gifts rather than neglect the body.

    1. 14:35 The Lord’s message was confirmed by witnesses and apostolic signs

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:3-4 Acts 2:1-4 Acts 2:43 1 Corinthians 15:3-6 Matthew 27:62-66; 28:1-7 Acts 1:3, 9 Romans 8:34

      • Hebrews 2:3–4 says the salvation first announced by the Lord was confirmed by those who heard him, while God testified through signs, wonders, miracles, and gifts distributed by the Holy Spirit according to his will.
      • Building on the prior week’s claim that Jesus is God’s prophetic voice, Josh points to Acts 2: the Spirit-filled apostles emerged with signs and wonders, and everyone was filled with awe at what God performed through them.
      • Josh lays out the apostolic timeline: Jesus died, rose, appeared to more than five hundred witnesses, and ascended after forty days. The sealed tomb and Roman guard did not prevent resurrection, and the witnessed ascension placed him at God’s right hand, reigning and interceding.
      • Because Jesus was no longer physically present, the signs authenticated the apostles’ message as God’s. Josh says the church now has the completed word brought through those apostles, and God bears witness through his Spirit using that word.

    2. 16:19 The Spirit testifies, distributes gifts, and governs them by Scripture

      Scripture: Romans 8:16 1 Corinthians 12 Ephesians 4

      • Romans 8 says the Spirit himself testifies with believers’ spirits that they are God’s children. The same Spirit gives gifts for ministry in the church.
      • Josh assigns 1 Corinthians 12 and Ephesians 4 as homework because there is not time to read both. He emphasizes that spiritual gifts are real, given according to God’s will, governed by Scripture, and intended for the church’s growth rather than the individual’s exaltation.
      • Believers do not choose their gifts. Their responsibility is to discover and use what the Spirit has distributed.

    3. 17:31 A gift left undiscovered or unused becomes another form of drift

      • Josh says that if believers do not even know their gifts, he is failing them.
      • Josh compares physical birth certificates with the absence of a “new life certificate” listing spiritual gifts. Discovery requires action, and most believers likely possess several gifts.
      • He urges hearers to take the church’s available gifts assessment and then ask an elder or pastor for help interpreting and deploying the result. He names prophecy, service, teaching, encouragement, giving, leadership, mercy, wisdom, discernment, faith, healing, miracles, tongues, helps, and administration.
      • The participation numbers trouble him: only two of ten who begin the assessment finish, and only half of those find a use in the body. Even allowing other ways to discover gifts, Josh says that if the assessment were the only route, only forty people in the church would be using their gifts; the pattern pictures widespread drift.
      • The modern church drifts not only when people skip Sunday but when they refuse daily church life and participation. Families that do not teach children to value the body deprive others of God’s gifts. The Spirit gives, Scripture governs, and the church is built; gifts serve and strengthen salvation rather than distracting from it.

    4. 20:56 The chapter break hides a deliberate return from the drifting boat to the captain

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:1-4

      • Josh notes that chapter and verse numbers are useful but uninspired additions, first appearing in this familiar format in the 1560 Geneva Bible.
      • Hebrews 2:1–4 completes the argument established in chapter 1 rather than beginning an unrelated unit. The author then connects the visual of a drifting boat to the reminder of the church’s great captain.

  3. 21:55 Humanity lost dominion, but the church sees Jesus crowned

    • Josh reads Hebrews 2:5–13, explains its use of Psalm 8 and Genesis, contrasts failed humanity with the exalted Son, and presents “but we do see Jesus” as God’s answer to the dilemma created by sin.

    1. 21:55 Hebrews 2:5–13 moves from lost rule to the suffering and crowned Son

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:5-13

      • Josh reads that the world to come is not subjected to angels. Humanity was made a little lower than the angels, crowned with glory and honor, and given everything underfoot, yet the present world does not display that full subjection.
      • The decisive conjunction follows: “But we do see Jesus,” temporarily made lower than angels and now crowned because he tasted death for everyone by God’s grace.
      • The reading continues through the pioneer of salvation made perfect through suffering, the one who sanctifies sharing one family with the sanctified, and Jesus’ unashamed identification with brothers and sisters.

    2. 23:13 The glorified Son—not angels—holds authority over the world to come

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:5-13

      • The author resumes the prior discussion of Christ’s superiority after pausing to warn against drift. Josh explains that Jewish readers rightly associated angels with mediation of God’s word, but some also regarded angels as administrators of the present world and rulers of the world to come.
      • Hebrews pushes back: future honor and authority belong to the glorified Son and the members of his family, not to angels.

    3. 24:15 Psalm 8 marvels at the glory and dominion God gave humanity

      Scripture: Psalm 8:4-6 Genesis 1:26-28

      • Josh identifies Psalm 8 as the author’s source and adds it to the week’s homework. David marvels that God would share power and glory with mere mortals.
      • At creation, God gave the first man and woman dominion. Humanity was made lower than angels in rank yet received a privilege never promised to angels: rule in the world to come.
      • Josh repeats Psalm 8’s description of humanity crowned with glory and honor with everything placed underfoot, then asks the church to remember when that condition was visible.

    4. 25:16 Genesis 2 and a hungry lion show how far dominion has fallen

      Scripture: Genesis 2:19-23

      • In Genesis 2, animals came to Adam for names and the woman was recognized as flesh of his flesh. Josh contrasts that original authority with trying to name a lion face-to-face on safari.
      • The comic conclusion—that the lion will eat the person—makes the loss tangible. Humanity was made to wear a crown and exercise dominion, yet now cannot control the creatures or even itself.

    5. 26:13 “But we do see Jesus” is God’s answer to Adam’s failure

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:8b-10 Romans 5:12-19

      • Josh identifies “but we do see Jesus” as the message-title phrase and the most beautiful conjunction in Scripture. Jesus became human to suffer and die for human sin and restore the dominion lost through sin.
      • Using federal-headship language, Josh says Adam represented humanity and lost dominion, while Jesus regained it and now has everything beneath his feet. Where Adam’s line lost the crown and became enslaved to sin, believers enjoy restored dominion through the captain of salvation.
      • The application returns to the opening image: anyone who wants to avoid drift must let the captain steer.

    6. 27:06 Revelation’s doxology teaches the church to marvel at the incarnate and exalted Christ

      Scripture: Revelation 1:5-6 Hebrews 2:9-10 John 20:24-29 Acts 7:55-60 Hebrews 2:9

      • Josh reads Revelation 1:5–6: Jesus is the faithful witness, firstborn from the dead, ruler of earthly kings, the one who loves and frees his people by his blood, and the one who makes them a kingdom and priests. Glory and power belong to him forever.
      • Grace moved Jesus to become human and taste death for sinners. Josh tells the church to marvel: “We see Jesus.”
      • Believers do not see with physical eyes as Thomas did, but spiritually as Stephen saw the Son of Man beside God while dying. They see Christ in creation, incarnation, temporary humiliation, sacrificial death, and exaltation.
      • Jesus’ humiliation was for a little while, but the peace he gives is eternal. He tasted death for his people and is now crowned with glory and honor. Where humanity failed, Christ succeeded, entering ruin to return his people to their crown.

  4. 28:45 The pioneer turns suffering into glory and calls the sanctified his family

    • Josh centers Hebrews 2:10–11 on the pioneer made perfect through suffering, clarifies that “perfect” describes Christ’s completed saving position rather than moral improvement, and declares that the crowned Son is not ashamed of those he sanctifies.

    1. 28:45 The pioneer’s path reveals that following Jesus means suffering before glory

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:10 Matthew 16:24-25; Mark 8:34-35; Luke 9:23-24

      • Hebrews 2:10 brings the hearer into the picture: God fittingly made the pioneer or captain of salvation perfect through suffering while bringing many sons and daughters to glory.
      • Josh asks whether people who say they want to follow Jesus know where he leads. Satan offers immediate glory that ends in suffering; Jesus calls people to deny themselves and turns present suffering into glory.
      • Jesus suffered on earth and received glory in heaven. Those who follow accept the same destiny, share his glory by trusting him for salvation, and become his family. Such grace is too precious to drift from.

    2. 30:00 “Made perfect” describes completed qualification, not correction of sin

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:10

      • Because people may use Hebrews 2:10 to dispute Jesus’ identity, Josh clarifies that Christ was sinless. “Perfect” concerns position rather than conduct.
      • Only by taking flesh, suffering, dying, and rising could Jesus complete his qualification as Savior and high priest. Incarnation, crucifixion, and resurrection belong together in the Jesus pattern of suffering leading to glory.

    3. 30:42 Those who see the crowned Son are seen and welcomed as his siblings

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:11

      • Josh returns to Hebrews 2:11 and the message title: by a “rule of line of sight,” those who see Jesus are seen by him.
      • Because God’s Spirit is at work in the sanctified, Jesus is not ashamed to call them brothers and sisters. Their own lives do not yet show everything under their feet, but everything is under his.
      • If believers are in the crowned Christ, their story is moving from dust to glory.

  5. 31:27 By sharing flesh and blood, Jesus breaks death’s power over his covenant people

    • Josh expounds Hebrews 2:14–16 as the death of death: the incarnate Son disarms Satan’s accusing weapon, frees people enslaved by fear, gives resurrection victory, and helps Abraham’s descendants—the people of faith.

    1. 31:27 Only an incarnate Christ could save humans and defeat the fear of death

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:14-16

      • Josh reads Hebrews 2:14–16: because the children share flesh and blood, Jesus shared their humanity so his death could break the devil’s power of death and free lifelong slaves to death’s fear; he helps Abraham’s descendants, not angels.
      • He calls this blessing “the death of death.” Angels cannot die, and Jesus did not come to save angels. To save humans he had to become human; only the incarnate Christ could defeat Satan, sin, and death through death.

    2. 32:24 Satan remains active but has been disarmed of accusation

      Scripture: John 8:44 Revelation 12:10 Colossians 1:13; 1 Peter 2:9

      • Satan is alive and busy, but Jesus broke his power and disarmed him. Josh identifies accusation through sin and death as the weapon.
      • Jesus called Satan a murderer and liar. As the author of sin, Satan exploited death’s fear to control people and build a dominion of darkness.
      • Those who trust Jesus have been delivered once for all from that dominion into Christ’s kingdom of light. The death, burial, and resurrection give them victory over accusation.

    3. 33:22 Resurrection victory lets believers mock death and stand firm

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:55-58

      • Josh quotes 1 Corinthians 15:55–58 as a spiritual mocking of evil: “Where, O death, is your victory?” and “Where, O death, is your sting?”
      • Death’s sting is sin and sin’s power is the law, but God gives victory through the Lord Jesus Christ. Therefore brothers and sisters must stand firm, refuse drift, give themselves fully to the Lord’s work, and know that their labor is not vain.

    4. 34:00 Abraham’s descendants are the faith-defined people Christ intentionally redeems

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:16 Galatians 3:7, 29 Ephesians 1:4-7

      • Josh returns to Hebrews 2:16: the Son did not come to help angels but Abraham’s descendants.
      • He identifies those descendants as people who have taken refuge in God and received peace through Jesus—the covenant people marked by faith.
      • Josh calls the redemption intentional and targeted before the foundation of the world: Christ came to save his people.

  6. 34:32 The merciful and faithful high priest understands temptation and runs to the cry

    • Josh reads Hebrews 2:17–18, traces Christ’s lived human experience and substitutionary atonement, then uses the speaker-named Amplified rendering to show that the tempted Savior actively helps his tempted people.

    1. 34:32 Jesus became like his siblings in every way to atone and help

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:17-18

      • Hebrews 2:17–18 says Jesus had to become like his brothers and sisters in every way so that he could serve God as a merciful, faithful high priest, make atonement for their sins, and help those being tempted because he himself suffered temptation.
      • Josh says the church has a Savior who understands rather than a distant observer. Angels cannot understand mortal weakness as Jesus can.
      • He connects that care to Crossroads Cares: because Jesus cared for his people, the church cares for others.
      • Jesus knew infancy, childhood, adolescence, adult responsibility, fatigue, hunger, thirst, loneliness, betrayal, hatred, rejection, lies, false accusation, family misunderstanding, physical anguish, and death. He entered the human experience to serve as high priest.

    2. 36:10 Mercy toward people and faithfulness toward God meet in the perfect mediator

      Scripture: John 19:30

      • Jesus is merciful toward weak people and faithful toward holy God: compassionate without excusing sin, trustworthy while standing, dining, speaking, and sympathizing with those he saves.
      • Many priests fail, but Jesus never will. He made the necessary sacrifice and declared “tetelestai”—finished—on the cross.
      • Josh describes atonement as divine wrath turned away through substitution. Jesus became like his people, stood and felt and wept with them, and paid for them.

    3. 37:16 The Savior still helps: run toward him in temptation and confession

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:18 Hebrews 1:14 John 8:11 1 John 1:9; 2:1

      • Christian drift grows when people forget that Jesus is still helping. Because temptation never conquered him, he is both qualified and willing to help.
      • Josh explicitly opens the Amplified Bible and reads Hebrews 2:18: the one who suffered temptation, testing, and trial is able immediately to “run to the cry of” those exposed to the same suffering.
      • Angels can minister, but only Jesus runs to the cry and wipes tears. The high priest does not merely say “try harder”; he says to lean on and call on him.
      • Josh quotes Jesus’ words, “Neither do I condemn you. Go and sin no more,” and urges hearers not to hide when tempted but to run to Christ for grace.
      • If a believer sins, Jesus is also an advocate before God, and sincere confession receives forgiveness. Help is available both in resisting temptation and after failure.

  7. 39:01 Seeing the present, reigning Jesus changes daily obedience

    • Josh applies the sermon’s title to distraction, bitterness, money, family relationships, holiness, forgiveness, and patience: faith sees not a distant historical teacher but the crucified, enthroned Lord who remains with and helps his people.

    1. 39:01 Refocus when waves pull the eyes away from Christ

      Scripture: Matthew 14:28-31

      • Josh asks everyone to pause and refocus. Like Peter watching the waves, believers take their eyes from Christ when life grows noisy and distractions multiply.
      • They cannot afford to look away, because seeing Jesus—not merely as a distant historical figure but as a living, reigning, spiritually present Christ—changes everything.

    2. 39:35 The crucified Son is enthroned, interceding, helping, and always present

      Scripture: Romans 8:34 Matthew 28:20 John 8:2-11

      • The crucified one is now enthroned at God’s right hand, ruling his church, interceding for his people, and running to their cry by the Spirit. He is a present Lord who sees, knows, and helps.
      • Josh quotes Jesus’ promise in Matthew 28, “I’m with you always,” then asks whether hearers live as though that promise were true.
      • If Jesus stood visibly beside them, would bitterness take root, obedience become optional, sinners be treated callously, resources be spent entirely on self, or believers remain worldly, anxious, selfish, and prideful? Josh invokes the Christ who silenced hypocritical accusers and gave grace to a sinner.

    3. 41:50 A clear sight of the wounded Christ kills sin and reforms relationships

      Scripture: Matthew 27:26; Mark 15:15; John 19:1 Romans 8:29; 12:2 Hebrews 2:17-18

      • Josh asks who could domineer over a wife, abandon children, hate brothers, or ignore another’s need while looking at Christ wounded by Roman flogging.
      • The more believers see Jesus by faith, the more sin dies. Seeing the reigning King would produce ready obedience, steadier holiness, better self-denial, conformity to Christ rather than the world, free forgiveness, and patient treatment of people still being sanctified.

    4. 42:51 Hebrews testifies in the present tense: “We see Jesus”

      Scripture: Hebrews 2:9 1 Corinthians 16:22; Revelation 22:20 Hebrews 2:9

      • Josh says the passage does not merely claim that believers can see Jesus, have seen him, or will see him, though he affirms those truths. He voices the church’s future hope with “Maranatha” and “Come, Lord Jesus.”
      • The text’s actual testimony is present: “We see Jesus.” Josh repeats that this belongs to the church and God’s family now.

  8. 43:28 Communion becomes the family’s united confession: “We see Jesus”

    • Josh leads believers to the Lord’s Table, gathers the sermon’s pictures of Christ into one confession, calls attention to the body and blood that purchased salvation, quotes Paul’s life-and-death confidence, and begins a prayer that continues until the uploaded video ends.

    1. 43:28 The Lord’s Table belongs to believers who confess Christ together

      Scripture: Matthew 28:20 Hebrews 4:15 Hebrews 2:9, 14-15

      • Josh invites believers to prepare the communion elements and asks those who are not in Christ—not yet owning him as Lord and Savior—to pass, because the family meal belongs to believers.
      • Holding bread and juice together, the church makes one confession: “We see Jesus.” The elements direct attention to the one who left glory, took flesh, was tempted as humans are without sin, tasted death, and broke the accuser’s power.
      • Though Christ ascended, his promise of presence remains trustworthy. Against ongoing sin, conflict, death, and trial, the church’s two-thousand-year testimony remains unchanged.

    2. 45:01 The church sees Christ from creation through incarnation, suffering, and glory

      Scripture: Genesis 1:3; John 1:1-5 Luke 1:39-45 Hebrews 2:9-11

      • Josh sees Jesus at the beginning, before created things were visible, speaking light and life. He then pictures the extraordinary divine infant entering Elizabeth’s presence in Mary’s womb.
      • At Mary’s greeting, John leaped in Elizabeth’s womb—an early sign that the Savior had come.
      • The confession gathers Hebrews 2 again: the church sees the suffering Savior, the Christ crowned with glory, and the brother unashamed to call his people family.

    3. 45:51 The broken body and poured-out blood make death gain

      Scripture: Matthew 26:26-28; 1 Corinthians 11:23-26 Philippians 1:21

      • At the Lord’s Table, Josh says salvation did not come cheaply: Christ’s body was broken and his blood poured out for forgiveness. He left glory and entered human suffering so his people could enter his glory.
      • After repeatedly asking whether the church sees Jesus, Josh quotes Paul: “For to me, to live is Christ and to die is gain.” Life and death can both be received this way because believers see Jesus.

    4. 46:32 The closing prayer thanks God for revealing Christ from the beginning

      Scripture: Genesis 1:3, 26-28; 2:8-25

      • Josh begins the closing prayer by thanking the Father for self-revelation. He looks back to the light of creation, God’s majesty and goodness, and humanity’s brief enjoyment of dominion in the garden as a glimpse of what could be.
      • The uploaded video ends at 47:00 while the prayer is still in progress; no completion beyond the verified captions is supplied here.
