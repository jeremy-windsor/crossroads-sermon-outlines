Overview

  1. 00:16 The final part of Unto Us

    Scripture: Isaiah 53

    • Josh asks everyone to turn to Isaiah 53 for the final part of the Christmas series Unto Us, which has looked at messianic prophecies in Isaiah. Today’s section, the suffering servant passage, is one of his favorites.
    • Josh is about to celebrate 14 years serving the church, and Crossroads turns 20 next month. Its vision is to serve the needs of Anthem, Phoenix, and the world; it has met thousands of needs in Jesus’ name, and it wants to be marked by faith, hope, and love, known as insanely loving and kind, Christians who don’t just talk a big game but show the world the gospel has changed them from the inside out.

  2. 01:36 Spectators instead of servants

    • The American church is filled with spectators when it should be filled with servants, and not even good spectators; at least Chiefs fans show up every week. A Scottish preacher said, “You Americans, your gospel is 3,000 miles wide and only one inch deep,” and he’s right.
    • The church is so focused on making room for people that it has forgotten the people in the room. If you compromise the gospel to win people, you win them to a false gospel, which means you’ve won no one.
    • The greatest challenge isn’t declining attendance, finances, or megachurch models planting flags across the city. It is showing American Christians who think they are Christians but aren’t that they need the true gospel and eternal life. Satan has a tight grip here: how do you convince people who have everything that they still need something?

  3. 03:24 This is eternal life

    Scripture: John 17:1–5 John 17:3

    • Because we have the good life, we have stopped thinking about eternal life. Our 80 or so years, if we’re blessed, are a blip on the timeline of eternity. Josh shows a line, red for this life and white for eternity: which do you spend most of your time thinking about, worrying about, planning for? If we never talk, think, or teach about eternal life, how do we know whether we have it?
    • Jesus defines eternal life in John 17, the high priestly prayer: “Now this is eternal life: that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom you have sent.” From the depths of his heart, Josh wants young and old to know him. Everything under the tree is temporary, but in the next few minutes something can happen that lasts forever.

    1. 05:46 What Josh wants for his kids

      • Josh doesn’t stress about giving his kids a wonderful Christmas or plan extravagant vacations so they’ll remember him as a good dad. His heart’s greatest desire is that they become servants of God. He can teach them to look, act, and seem Christian, but only they can choose to give their lives to God. He doesn’t care if they are rich or poor, go to Harvard or GCC; he just wants Madison and Nolan to give their hearts to Jesus.
      • When Madison climbed into the baptistry at 12 (she’s 18 now), it was the happiest moment of his life, and he prays for his son to do the same: “None of y’all better come hand me towels; we’re worshiping wet.”
      • Every Christmas parents wonder what to get children who already have everything. More socks? We have to teach them that despite appearances, if they don’t have Jesus, they have nothing. Josh’s desire is simply to deepen the church’s understanding of Jesus and show them the only true God and the one he sent.

  4. 07:50 A portrait of the suffering servant

    Scripture: Isaiah 53

    • Isaiah 53 paints a portrait of Jesus you’ll never forget. The Holy Spirit guided Isaiah’s pen to paint the suffering servant 700 years before he was born in Bethlehem: his life and ministry, crucifixion, death, burial, resurrection, and exaltation.

  5. 08:17 “To whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?”

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:1 Psalm 8:3 John 12:37–40

    • Verse 1: who has believed our message, and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed? Psalm 8 describes creation as the work of God’s fingers; in Exodus he delivered Israel with a strong hand; but to save lost sinners he stretched out his mighty arm. God isn’t shy about demonstrating his power and love, yet some always refuse to believe.
    • People ask whether it discourages Josh to preach and not see lives change. His answer is always no. He wants everyone to believe and grow, but belief has never always come from preaching. John 12 says that even after Jesus performed so many signs, they still would not believe, to fulfill Isaiah’s words, and that God blinded their eyes and hardened their hearts.

    1. 09:55 Hardened hearts

      Scripture: John 8:12

      • With hardened hearts we like to point the finger at God when we should look at ourselves. If you persistently harden your heart toward God and the one he sent, you may find he has hardened it: “This is what you want. Take it. I’m not going to force myself on you.” Pharaoh hardened his own heart seven times before God first hardened it.
      • Josh fears many in the room have heard about Jesus and done nothing with it. He doesn’t know many of them by name, but he has prayed for them all week, and his hope is that this Christmas they would call on the Lord for a new heart. He gave Josh one and can do it again. Jesus says in John 8, “I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness, but will have the light of life.”

  6. 11:15 No beauty to attract us

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:2

    • Verse 2 gives a glimpse of his appearance: he grew up like a tender shoot, a root out of dry ground, with no beauty or majesty to attract us, nothing in his appearance that we should desire him.
    • Josh brings out a friend he calls “Vidal Sassoon Jesus”: veneers, no tongue scum, no chest hair, beautiful flowing hair. “I can’t take my eyes off him.” Sorry, but this ain’t Jesus.

    1. 12:18 A culture obsessed with appearance

      • Josh can’t think of a better message for the nation, especially for children and teenagers, because our culture is obsessed with appearance, success, wealth, prestige, and influence. He thinks much of the mental health crisis comes from pursuing these things, and there are only two outcomes: obtain everything and realize it doesn’t matter, or chase it and never get it. Either way, welcome to an identity crisis.
      • Look at how miserable some of the most influential people are. Arnold Schwarzenegger seems to have it all; Josh’s Christmas gift to the church is to Google how he feels about dying. He has the most notorious movie line, “I’ll be back,” and he is furious to realize he won’t be, because everything in the red is meaningless and all he has earned will be gone.

    2. 14:00 Jesus knew exactly who he was

      Scripture: James 4:10

      • Jesus didn’t need beauty or majesty because he knew exactly who he was: where he came from, why he was here, and where he would go when he finished the job. With that confidence you can serve and elevate others, because you aren’t busy proving yourself.
      • Believers can have the same confidence. James 4: humble yourselves before the Lord and he will lift you up. That isn’t a call to salvation; humility is learned and relearned almost every day. And can’t we expect to be treated the same as our Master, if not worse?

  7. 14:54 Despised and rejected

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:3 Hebrews 4:15

    • Verse 3 shows how he was treated: despised and rejected, a man of suffering familiar with pain, one from whom people hide their faces. “Despised” means regarded with utter contempt; “rejected” means lacking or transient. The best example today might be a homeless person; Jesus lived like one. The word for pain means both emotional and physical.
    • Hebrews reminds the church that we have a high priest able to empathize with our weaknesses, tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin. He understands being human, even a human of low status; he came for those of low status and tasks his church with their continued care.

    1. 15:57 The powerful always reject humility

      Scripture: John 1:11 John 8:11 Romans 6:1–2

      • The powerful always reject humility; they treated God’s servant like a slave. John 1 says he came to his own, and his own did not receive him, because he wasn’t focused on what mattered to them: wealth, reputation, position, authority, and freedom. It isn’t different today. “I don’t go to church because all they want is my money”? Then isn’t it safe to say all you care about is your money?
      • Worse, the church has noticed people don’t want to give up their idols and has stopped preaching holiness. Anyone brave enough to say “go and sin no more” is labeled judgy or a legalist: “We’re not under law, we’re under grace.” So should we keep sinning that grace may abound? By no means.

  8. 17:27 Ten reasons Jesus was despised

    • Josh offers ten reasons, going fast because he is only on verse 3 and it’s Christmas Eve.

    1. 17:40 1–4: authority, sinners, the spotlight, sin

      Scripture: Matthew 28:18 John 7:7

      • First, his authority. People don’t want to answer to anyone. Jesus says all power is given to him in heaven and on earth, and man says, “Cool, but I like pretending I have the power.”
      • Second, he hung out with sinners: Zacchaeus, Matthew, the woman caught in adultery, and more. That is a powerful statement of God’s love and forgiveness: no one is beyond redemption, and repentance, faith, and love matter far more than appearance or prestige.
      • Third, he stole their spotlight. They were threatened: what if people stop coming to the temple and go listen to him?
      • Fourth, he preached against sin. It isn’t the Jesus of the Super Bowl commercials, but yes, he ate with sinners and washed feet, and his message was the same: go and sin no more. In John 7 Jesus says the world hates him because he testifies that its works are evil. Repentance isn’t a nice “my bad, God”; it is turning away from the lifestyle: “I don’t want it. I want you.” People don’t like it when you point out their selfishness and wrongdoing.

    2. 19:39 5: he called God his Father

      Scripture: John 5:18 Colossians 1:15–20 John 10:30

      • John 5 says they tried all the more to kill him because he was even calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God. Be careful what you call Christian and what you call the church; false Christians and false churches masquerade as the real deal. Test everything.
      • Jehovah’s Witnesses call themselves Christians but aren’t, Josh says, because they don’t believe Jesus is God, teaching he was created and subordinate, and wrote their own Bible version to prove it. The true church builds on the apostles’ teaching that the Son is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation, which doesn’t mean he was created. Keep reading: in him all things were created, he is before all things, in him all things hold together, and God was pleased to have all his fullness dwell in him. Jesus is God.
      • Mormons believe Jesus is the Son of God and Savior, and you could talk to one in the park a long time before realizing something is off, but they don’t believe he is co-equal and co-eternal with the Father. The fastest-growing religion in the country can be deconstructed with one sentence from John 10: “I and the Father are one.”

    3. 22:02 6–10: the only way, self-denial, hatred of God, compassion, no reason

      Scripture: John 14:6 Matthew 6:24 John 15:23 Psalm 14:1 John 15:25

      • Sixth, he said he was the only way: John 14, “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.” Seventh, he taught self-denial: no servant can serve two masters. Creation or Creator, idol or God, money or God: what’s it going to be?
      • Eighth, they already hated God. John 15: whoever hates me hates my Father as well. Some hate him so much they deny he exists; how can you hate something that doesn’t exist? David said it best in Psalm 14: the fool says in his heart, “There is no God.”
      • Ninth, his compassion: he healed the sick and gave sight to the blind no matter what day it was, and they screamed about traditions. Thank goodness the church has come a long way. No, we still break unity over Christmas trees and Easter bunnies; that sermon is tabled.
      • Tenth, for no reason at all. John 15: this is to fulfill what is written in their law, “They hated me without reason.”

  9. 23:46 Pierced for our transgressions

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:4–12 Revelation 5:12 Philippians 2:10–11

    • All this hate, animosity, and rejection, and still Josh wants to show them Jesus. He reads on: surely he took up our pain and bore our suffering; he was pierced for our transgressions and crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was on him, and by his wounds we are healed. We all, like sheep, have gone astray, and the Lord laid on him the iniquity of us all. Led like a lamb to the slaughter, silent; cut off for the transgression of my people; yet it was the Lord’s will to crush him. After he has suffered he will see the light of life; my righteous servant will justify many, for he bore the sin of many and made intercession for the transgressors.
    • That is why a baby in a manger was worthy of the gifts of kings, why he is still worthy of your gifts 2,000 years later, why the angels around the throne say, “Worthy is the Lamb who was slain,” and why every knee will bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord. He didn’t die because of anything he had done but because of what we had done.

    1. 26:06 Genesis 3 set right

      Scripture: Genesis 3 Luke 23:43

      • “Wounded” means pierced through. Everything that went wrong in Genesis 3, Christ set right in the year 33. His hands were pierced; Eve grabbed the fruit with her hand. His feet were pierced; Adam and Eve ran and hid. His side was pierced; Eve came from Adam’s rib. He wore a crown of thorns; God cursed the ground to produce thorns and thistles, and Jesus took the curse on his head. At a tree we declared war on God, and on a tree Jesus brought us peace. We forfeited paradise to be like God, and God became like us to reopen its doors. Merry Christmas.
      • That door is open to the worst of us: Jesus told the criminal crucified next to him, “Truly I tell you, today you will be with me in paradise.”

  10. 27:15 Transgression and iniquity

    • It is never easy convincing people they have gone astray. Many define good as being better than someone worse. Imagine on trial for murder, your whole defense is that the guy in the next courtroom is worse. If your lawyer comes up with that, stop hiring discount lawyers; Discount Tire is great, but nobody loves discount lawyers.
    • Isaiah speaks of transgression and iniquity. A transgression is a single sin; iniquity is a lifestyle of sin. Transgressions come from you; iniquity is part of you. We are sinners by choice and by nature, and Christ takes care of both: by his wounds we are healed.
    • If God only dealt with our transgressions and did nothing to fix our hearts, we would fall into the same cycle again and again forever. Forgiveness with no change in us changes nothing. Isaiah 53 promises both will be healed in the person and work of Jesus. Jehovah Rapha, the Lord who heals, is a good doctor: he won’t just treat your symptoms; he wants to cure your disease.

  11. 29:15 Silent at an illegal trial

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:7 Isaiah 53:10 John 18:11

    • Verse 7: oppressed and afflicted, he did not open his mouth; like a lamb to the slaughter and a sheep silent before its shearers. In our courts, if anything in a trial was illegal, mishandled evidence or a tampered jury or witness, a judge can declare a mistrial. Everything about Christ’s trial was illegal, yet Jesus said nothing. Don’t you want to know why?
    • The answer is in verse 10: it was the Lord’s will to crush him and cause him to suffer. Christ’s illegal trial was heaven’s plan. Peter was slicing off ears to stop the unfair treatment, and the Lord said, “Put your sword away! Shall I not drink the cup the Father has given me?”

    1. 30:36 God’s plan all along

      Scripture: Isaiah 53:11 Matthew 1:21

      • Verse 11: my righteous servant will justify many, and he will bear their iniquities. Wicked men nailed Jesus to a cross, but his death was not the religious elite’s plan, not Caiaphas’s plan, not the Roman government’s plan; it was God’s plan all along. Jesus was God’s sacrificial lamb for the sins of the world.
      • “She will give birth to a son, and you are to give him the name Jesus, because he will save his people from their sins.” That is the only message that matters. He will do it; he doesn’t know how to fail. Merry Christmas.

  12. 31:34 Every good Christmas message sounds like Easter

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:10–11 Philippians 2:9–11

    • Isaiah speaks not only of Christ’s death and burial but of his resurrection and exaltation: he will see his offspring and prolong his days; after he has suffered he will see the light of life. He did not remain dead. Every good Christmas message sounds like Easter. Those who saw him face to face testified the tomb was empty; the authorities tried to cover it up and then to kill his followers, but the news spread too far and too fast.
    • “I will give him a portion among the great, and he will divide the spoils with the strong” sounds like Paul in Philippians 2 because that is where Paul got it: God exalted him to the highest place and gave him the name above every name, that every knee should bow and every tongue acknowledge that Jesus Christ is Lord. His victory is our victory; he divides the spoils with those who love him.

  13. 32:55 “He will see his offspring”

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:10 2 Corinthians 5:21

    • “Josh, Jesus didn’t have children.” True, and it was a great shame for a Jew to die childless, but Jesus didn’t die childless. He gave birth to a spiritual family: we are adopted sons and daughters of God through Jesus Christ, and he is not ashamed to call us brothers and sisters. We are co-heirs with an inheritance that will never spoil or fade, bought at a price, standing confident in his righteousness, not our own. Paul said it best: God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.

  14. 34:00 Propitiation

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:12 Psalm 32:1–2

    • A fun word from Bible school: propitiation. God is angry at sin because it offends his holiness and violates his law. In his holiness he must judge sinners; in his love he desires to forgive them. He can’t ignore sin or look the other way without contradicting his nature and his law. How does God solve this? Isaiah 53:12: he was numbered with the transgressors. The law’s demands have been satisfied, and God can graciously forgive all who call on the name of Jesus. It’s done.
    • Psalm 32: blessed are those whose transgressions are forgiven, whose sins are covered, whom the Lord does not count against them.

  15. 35:13 Candlelight: light breaking into darkness

    • Soon the room will fill with candlelight, one of Josh’s favorite moments of the year: a reminder of light breaking into darkness, of the faith of those around us, and that our hope is a living hope. As you sing, look around as the flame lights the faces of those near you and find joy that the love of our Savior lives in your neighbor’s heart. Reflect on God’s mercy and rejoice that your sins are no longer counted against you.

    1. 36:14 Break the silence

      Scripture: Psalm 32:3–4 Psalm 32:5

      • Some are struggling, and the weight of unconfessed sin is heavy. When did you last confess your sins to the Lord: a week, a month, years? David struggled, and so has Josh: “When I kept silent, my bones wasted away through my groaning all day long… my strength was sapped.” If there is silence between you and God, break it this Christmas. Go to the Lord, then lift your candle; don’t be afraid.
      • Look how it turned out for David: “I acknowledged my sin to you and did not cover up my iniquity. I said, ‘I will confess my transgressions to the Lord,’ and you forgave the guilt of my sin.” When you lay your fears, burdens, sins, and anxieties at Jesus’ feet and rest in his grace, you are freed to love, sing, rejoice, serve, and teach. As the flames transform the room, God’s forgiveness will transform you. We are no longer defined by our failures, past, addictions, or family history, but by his love and mercy.

    2. 37:58 Go tell the world

      • If the light of life has transformed you, show the world. Being a light isn’t just being nice and not yelling at people who cut you off on the freeway; it is sharing God’s grace with the whole world and pointing to a living, loving God who forgives and redeems through his Son. Let your candle remind you: you are deeply loved, and if you confess your sins, God will forgive you. Go tell the world what the Lord has done for you, and whether they accept or reject your message, when he returns your inheritance is forever with him in paradise. Rejoice in the Lord and be glad. Merry Christmas.
