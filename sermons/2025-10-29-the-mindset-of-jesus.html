Overview

  1. 00:06 A tough question: do we struggle with selfishness?

    • Chris opens by asking whether the church is ready for a tough question, then greets them first. He usually gets to sit out there and sing and listen with them; this morning he’ll be up front, but they are still worshiping together.
    • The question: how many of us struggle with selfishness? He raises both hands. Sometimes he thinks he’s doing okay, doing the dishes or taking out the garbage for his wife, and then realizes he did it just so he wouldn’t have to deal with her frustration and the uncomfortable “How many times did I ask you?” conversation.
    • To the ladies, after a quick “that came out wrong,” he says every marriage is different, but maybe dinner is ready, the laundry is done, or the diaper is changed so you don’t have to hear him complain that he’s hungry, naked, or dying from the smell.
    • So why do we do the things we do? To avoid upsetting or disappointing someone? To feel good about ourselves? Do we sign up for Crossroads Cares, serve in a ministry, or give because we don’t want to feel guilty or disappoint leadership? The question to hold on to this morning: what is my mindset?

  2. 03:08 Philippians 2:1–4

    Scripture: Philippians 2:1–11 Philippians 2:1–4

    • Chris turns to Philippians 2:1–11 and reads verses 1–4: if you have any encouragement from being united with Christ, any comfort from his love, any common sharing in the Spirit, any tenderness and compassion, then make my joy complete by being like-minded. Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit; in humility value others above yourselves.

  3. 03:44 What is the “therefore” there for?

    Scripture: Philippians 1:27 Philippians 2:1

    • There’s a ton to unpack, but first, what is the “therefore” there for? He knows they’ve heard it 10,000 times, but it matters. Back in chapter 1, verse 27: whatever happens, conduct yourselves in a manner worthy of the gospel of Christ, standing firm in the one Spirit, striving together as one for the faith of the gospel.
    • Two things he can’t say better than Scripture: conduct yourselves worthy of the gospel, and stand firm in the one Spirit, striving together as one. Then back to “Therefore, if you have any encouragement from being united with Christ.” Do you see the connection? Standing firm and striving together, therefore, and now we’re into Philippians 2.

  4. 05:02 Four “if” statements

    • Paul gives four “if” statements to get us in the right place for what is coming.

    1. 05:06 If any encouragement from being united with Christ

      Scripture: John 17:20–21 1 Corinthians 1:10

      • Aren’t we called to be united? Jesus prayed for it in John 17: that all who believe through their message may be one, just as the Father is in him and he in the Father, so that the world may believe. In 1 Corinthians 1:10 Paul appeals that there be no divisions, but that they be perfectly united in mind and thought.
      • No wonder unity is written throughout the New Testament: our unity in Christ is a major factor in evangelizing the world. If we aren’t united, why would the world want anything to do with Jesus? We’d look just like the world: fractured, broken, and selfish. Nobody wants that.

    2. 06:27 If any comfort from his love

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1:3–4 Psalm 119:76

      • 2 Corinthians 1:3–4 praises the Father of compassion and the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our troubles so we can comfort others. Psalm 119: “May your unfailing love be my comfort.”
      • Jesus’ love is like the blanket you wrap yourself in on a cold day, or in Phoenix a maybe-kind-of-getting-cold day, with a cup of hot chocolate, knowing everything’s going to be okay. No matter what life brings, Jesus’ love comforts us through all of it.

    3. 07:31 If any common sharing in the Spirit

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 12:13 John 7:37–39

      • Chris reads that we were all baptized by one Spirit so as to form one body, and John 7:37, where Jesus says anyone who is thirsty should come to him and drink, and rivers of living water will flow from within them. By this he meant the Spirit, not yet given because Jesus had not yet been glorified.
      • Well, guess what? Jesus has now been glorified and the Spirit has been given. If we have put our faith in Christ, the same Spirit lives inside each one of us. We have a common sharing of that Spirit. We’re together.

    4. 08:34 If any tenderness and compassion

      Scripture: Colossians 3:12 Galatians 5:22–23

      • Colossians 3:12 tells God’s chosen people to clothe themselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience, and Galatians 5:22 lists the fruit of the Spirit: love, joy, peace, forbearance, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

  5. 09:02 Rhetorical ifs, then “make my joy complete”

    Scripture: Philippians 2:2

    • Do you see the progression? These are almost rhetorical ifs. Of course we are encouraged by being united with Christ; having Christ brings love, and being loved is comforting; Christ gives us the Holy Spirit, which every believer shares; and the Spirit brings tenderness and compassion we couldn’t have before Christ.
    • Paul is essentially saying, “If you have these things… but because you’re in Christ, I know you do.” Chris treats it as an “if” word game: “Well, if… no, I have that. I got all of them. Okay, Paul, what now?” Paul tricks us into affirming the four ifs so he can hit us with the “then”: make my joy complete by being like-minded, having the same love, being one in spirit and of one mind.
    • In two verses Paul wraps up all his letters to the churches, because Paul writes some long sentences; Chris can’t even get punctuation right. Paul calls churches to be one in spirit, to deal with those who break unity, and to call them to repentance so unity can be restored. Every letter dealing with junk in a church aims to restore a believer or rid the church of sin and false teaching, so it can focus on sharing Christ, loving one another, and glorifying God.

  6. 11:36 If you have this, then just do it

    • With four ifs and a then, Paul is saying: if you have this, just do it. Paul beat Nike to it by a few thousand years. United in Christ? Be like-minded. Have his love? Have the same love for each other. Share the same Spirit? Be one in spirit and mind, and the Spirit will give you tenderness and compassion. Come on.
    • The old Just Do It commercials got Chris hyped, maybe because he was younger or loves sports, and Paul has the Philippians just as excited and ready to go.

  7. 12:37 How do we do it? Verses 3–4

    Scripture: Philippians 2:3–4

    • But Paul is no dummy; he knows the next question once they come down from the excitement: how do I do what you just said? Verses 3–4: do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit; in humility value others above yourselves, not looking to your own interests but each of you to the interests of others.
    • Some stopped listening; the wind got knocked out of their sails: “Say that again?” Chris tells those who have been on mission with him that the Bible is the inerrant word of God and we follow it, but there are hard parts he is tempted to erase: “That’s too hard, God.” We don’t, but this is one of those places.

  8. 13:54 A house of mirrors

    • Remember the opening question about selfishness? Chris does struggle with it, and these verses are like stepping into a house of mirrors: everywhere he turns, he sees his own junk reflected back, all the ways he puts his own interests ahead of God and other people, including people he loves.

    1. 14:29 Selfish ambition and vain conceit defined

      Scripture: Proverbs 16:18 Proverbs 11:2 1 John 2:16

      • Selfish ambition has two sides: pursuing my own glory instead of God’s, and being concerned with my own interests and welfare over the good of others. Chris focuses on the second, because Paul is talking about how brothers and sisters in Christ treat each other.
      • Vain conceit is pretending we are better, stronger, and more capable than others: actions motivated by selfishness, pride, and one-upmanship. Proverbs is all over pride: pride goes before destruction, and when pride comes, then comes disgrace, but with humility comes wisdom. He could spend all morning there, but goes to 1 John 2: the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life come not from the Father but from the world.

    2. 15:55 Humbled at the men’s retreat

      • Chris got humbled at the men’s retreat. Last year he and his partner played hours of cornhole and never lost, so this year everyone was out for his blood, especially after he joked that only rain could keep him from being grand champion again. Then they moved cornhole inside, and his body had to live up to what his mouth said.
      • He lost about half his games, and his partner carried him through many of the wins. He realized how upset he was with himself and how much pride had snuck in. Instead of enjoying fellowship with the men, he was letting selfish ambition and vain conceit in, and God convicted him.
      • Some will say it’s just a game and he’s competitive. True, but he can compete without letting pride and conceit in. And if he lets them in over something as meaningless as cornhole, how likely is he to let them in over matters of much greater importance?

  9. 18:16 Humbly value others above yourselves

    Scripture: Romans 12:10 1 Peter 5:5 Philippians 2:4 1 Corinthians 10:24

    • We must humbly value others above ourselves. Romans 12:10: be devoted to one another in love; honor one another above yourselves. 1 Peter 5:5: clothe yourselves with humility toward one another, because God opposes the proud but shows favor to the humble.
    • This is not easy; look at all the Scriptures on pride and humility. It has to be a day-to-day, moment-by-moment thing. Paul gives the final piece of the how-to in verse 4: not looking to your own interests but to the interests of others. 1 Corinthians 10:24 says it a touch differently: no one should seek their own good, but the good of others.
    • If we struggle to follow this, it comes down to what is good for the other person. Who gains from what I’m doing? Who loses from the decisions I’m making? Is this going to be great for me or for someone else?

  10. 20:10 Look exactly the opposite of the world

    • It has to be constant, because it is so easy to slip into selfish ambition and vain conceit. Our flesh screams at us to be selfish, prideful, arrogant, and conceited, and without Christ that is just who we are, with no way to defeat it. But united with Christ and with the Holy Spirit living in us, we can be different and live lives that turn the world upside down.
    • The world says live your truth (Chris doesn’t even know what that means), get what’s yours, climb over people. The Bible says no: as brothers and sisters, love one another so that you put each other above yourselves, look to each other’s interests, and are united, like-minded, and one in spirit. In other words, look exactly the opposite of the world.
    • Show them that following Jesus looks different, that the values, expectations, and love are different because it is God’s love. Like the old song says, though Chris won’t sing it because Josh sings and they’d all leave: they will know that we are Christians by our love.

  11. 22:06 Philippians 2:5–8: the same mindset as Christ Jesus

    Scripture: Philippians 2:5–8

    • As if they haven’t covered enough that’s hard, Chris reads verses 5–8: in your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus, who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage, but made himself nothing, took the nature of a servant, and humbled himself by becoming obedient to death, even death on a cross.
    • Our relationships as believers are supposed to have the same mindset as Jesus. Jesus is God, yet he did not use that for his own advantage and made himself nothing.

  12. 22:57 The Creator who chose to become his creation

    Scripture: Genesis 1:1

    • Have you stopped to think about what this says? Chris pictures Jesus in heaven with the Father and the Spirit, the Trinity in perfect fellowship, angels all around, the glory of God filling the place. Then God “decides” to create (God isn’t in time, but Chris’s brain needs a start and a stop), as we see in Genesis 1: “In the beginning, God created.”
    • He creates time, matter, space, stars, planets, living things, and humans: a perfect creation. He didn’t need to; he wanted to, knowing his creation would reject him, knowing he would need a plan of salvation, and knowing God the Son would choose to leave heaven and become his creation to save them.

    1. 24:20 Jesus was the Creator

      Scripture: John 1:1–3

      • Jesus was the Creator; all things were made through him. Chris reads John 1:1–3: in the beginning was the Word, the Word was with God, and the Word was God; through him all things were made. Jesus was right there in creation speaking things into existence. The dude was creative.

    2. 24:50 Is the Word Jesus? Is Jesus God?

      Scripture: John 1:14–18

      • Some doubt the passage refers to Jesus, so Chris keeps reading John 1:14–18: the Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us, full of grace and truth. John the Baptist said, “He who comes after me has surpassed me because he was before me.” Do that math in your head. No one has ever seen God but the one and only Son, who is himself God.
      • That clears up two things. Is the Word Jesus? It says the Word became flesh and then talks all about Jesus. And to those who say Jesus never claimed to be God or that Scripture doesn’t say so: it says no one has seen God but the Son, who is himself God. Doesn’t get much easier than that.

  13. 26:31 All that glory in a flesh suit

    • So God the Son, who created all things and knew his creation would reject him, chose to leave God’s side and lower himself to nothing, becoming a servant and taking on human likeness, flesh and bones and everything that comes with being us, minus sin.
    • Chris can’t fathom what heaven is like; the greatest thing he can imagine isn’t even close. Jesus left that and stuck all that glory in a flesh suit. He recalls the first Men in Black, where a huge, powerful alien bug hides inside an “Edgar suit,” while insisting he isn’t comparing Jesus to a cockroach, so please don’t send emails. It is a terrible example, but what human example could compare?
    • The God of the universe became a helpless baby who needed a mom and dad to meet his every need, diaper changes included. He submitted to the earthly parents he created, or he would have broken one of the Ten Commandments. He washed feet. He was poor. He gave up heaven and didn’t even get the riches of earth, just a carpenter’s life. He gave up everything to show us how to live, and then he died so we could live.

  14. 29:01 Do you know Jesus?

    Scripture: John 10:10

    • Do you know Jesus? Have you given your life to him? He gave up so much to live the life we couldn’t, perfectly following the law and living by God’s righteousness, to pay a debt for our sin that we never could.
    • If you haven’t made Jesus Lord and Savior, reach out to a staff member, an elder, or the believer next to you, and let them tell you what Jesus has done in their life and how much he means to them. Jesus came that we might have life and have it to the fullest.

  15. 29:50 Not just what he gave up, but what he did

    Scripture: Philippians 2:8 John 10:17–18

    • It isn’t just what Jesus gave up to show up in his own creation, but what he did. He lived a perfect life, showed us how to live, and loved, healed, and cared for people. But what he did was go to the cross, allowing himself to be tortured, beaten beyond recognition, humiliated, pierced, and hung there. Verse 8: obedient to death, even death on a cross, with an exclamation point after “cross.” That’s how horrible it was, and how amazing it is for us.
    • As John 10:18 shows, he laid his life down willingly: “No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord.” Don’t question Jesus’ love for you. He went to the cross knowing every sin you would ever commit, before and after you became a believer, and climbed up on that cross anyway. That’s how much he loves you.

  16. 31:41 Do you have the mindset of Jesus?

    Scripture: Philippians 2:5

    • Back to verse 5: in your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus. Chris asks one more tough question, of them and of himself: do you have the mindset of Jesus? Remembering Paul is talking about relationships with other believers, he breaks it into three: Do you value others above yourself? Do you look to the interests of others before your own? Have you become a servant to your brothers and sisters in Christ?
    • On the last one: do you view church with a consumer mindset, “What do I get out of it?”, or with the mindset of Jesus, “What can I do? How can I serve?” There is a huge difference. Which one are you?

  17. 33:02 In marriage

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:25

    • To make it practical, start with marriage; the application is easy to see, if not easy to do. Are you in your marriage for what you can get, or to serve your spouse? Men, we are called to love our wives as Christ loved the church, and we just saw how much he loves his people. Do you even come close? Chris wishes he could say he does, but he falls very short and needs the mindset of Jesus in his marriage.
    • Wives, who do so much for their husbands: are you looking for what your husband can give you, or focused on what he doesn’t give you? To both men and women: you can only control the actions of one person, and that’s you. We are each called to have the mindset of Jesus regardless of what our spouse does.

  18. 34:44 In the church: why are you here?

    • Done picking on married people, since he is one, Chris asks: why are you here? To love and serve one another, worship God together, raise up the next generation, and spur one another on? Or for what you can get: the kids’ programming, the music, the preaching, the breakfast and coffee? Those aren’t bad, but if they are the only reasons, your mindset needs to change.
    • If we all had the mindset of Jesus, kids and youth programs wouldn’t be short of volunteers, and they wouldn’t worry about adding students on Wednesday nights for lack of adults. He wonders why no men volunteer with Kids for Truth, though he just learned two signed up. These kids are the next generation who will carry the gospel forward; the children’s and youth directors should not know what to do with all the volunteers.
    • Crossroads Cares needs the care center staffed and more coordinators. It is the church’s outreach to Anthem, Phoenix, and the world, often to lost souls, giving a real opportunity to share the gospel, and it also helps our own spiritual family and lightens the heavy load many volunteers carry. Gifts land in different places, from the engaged team to the office, facilities, and worship and tech, but use your gifts.
    • It doesn’t stop with serving through the church. In every interaction, a conversation, an event, a meal, or coffee, we still need the mindset of Jesus, valuing the other person above ourselves and looking to their interests.

  19. 38:03 No on-off switch

    • The mindset of Jesus doesn’t change or waver; there is no on-off switch. It is just who Jesus is, and it should be who we are too.
    • Chris is praying hard for himself and for the church, and asks them to pray that God gives them the mindset of Jesus and helps them see fellow believers through his eyes. If we see each other the way Jesus does, our actions will change, and we will treat and love each other differently.

  20. 38:49 The payoff: Philippians 2:9–11

    Scripture: Philippians 2:9–11 John 1:11 James 1:12

    • The final part of the passage is the payoff. Philippians 2:9–11: therefore, here we go again, because of all Jesus did, God exalted him to the highest place and gave him the name above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow and every tongue acknowledge that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.
    • Jesus laid it all down, and God raised him up, not just up but to the highest place. He came to that which was his own and they did not receive him, yet in the end every tongue, believer or not, will acknowledge who he is.
    • And because of what Jesus did, James 1:12 says the one who perseveres under trial will receive the crown of life the Lord has promised to those who love him. So lay it down, have the mindset of Jesus, be a servant, think of others more highly than yourself, and in the end receive the crown of life and be with Jesus. Chris can’t wait for that day.

  21. 41:00 Closing prayer

    • Chris prays, as he has all week and will for a long time, that God gives them the mindset of Jesus, who laid down his life and became a servant because he cared for us more than his own life. To have that mindset means telling brothers and sisters, “I care more about your interests than about climbing a ladder or getting what I want.”
    • He asks God for that difficult mindset, so that they love one another well and bring incredible unity, and so that the lost will see it and say, “I want that. What is that?” Then they get to tell them all about Jesus and what he did for them, because our unity is a witness to the world.
