Overview

  1. 00:10 Wrapping up Unstoppable: God’s not done yet

    Scripture: Acts 28

    • The series wraps up in Acts 28. The book of Acts ends with Paul in Rome, but please don’t mistake that for the conclusion of the Great Commission. God’s not done yet: his mission to share his plan of salvation continues through his church today, through us.

  2. 00:46 Paul and Barnabas were relentless

    • Luke dedicates more than half of Acts to Paul’s ministry. Paul and Barnabas sail to Cyprus and cross the whole island on foot preaching in the synagogues, sail north to Perga, hike to Galatia, turn to the Gentiles when the Jews reject the message, and are driven out of Antioch. They just keep going.
    • Josh was convicted this week by their tenacity. It is easy to get shut down and back into silence, and modern Christians are guilty of it; honestly, we are soft. We will say hard things in here where we get amens, but at any resistance out there we zip it up and snuggle into Christian circles where the worst thing anyone will say is “He’s a Calvinist.” We have mastered echo chambers where everything gets applause.
    • The culture is demanding a shift to a more Pauline ministry approach: speak where it’s hard to speak, where people don’t invite you to speak. And when you preach, don’t sweat the outcome. Some will reject it, some will accept it; that’s between them and God. Your job is to share it.

    1. 02:36 Shake the dust off your feet

      Scripture: Matthew 10:14

      • Jesus openly taught this in Matthew 10: if anyone will not welcome you or listen, shake the dust off your feet when you leave. It’s okay to move on. Josh thinks the church has lost influence because Christians have a broken idea that they must close every sale, and when they can’t, they get discouraged and stop selling. Don’t you remember that without the Lord’s help you would not have believed either?
      • Preach to them, pray for them, and be willing to part ways. You may have planted a seed that won’t sprout for months, or it already has and they got baptized last Easter at some church you’ve never heard of. Maybe the Jehovah’s Witness who stopped knocking isn’t avoiding you because you smell funny; maybe your faithful words are sinking in. Josh loves Paul and Barnabas’ confidence in the Holy Spirit to keep working after they head out.

  3. 04:03 Sometimes church breakups are good

    Scripture: Matthew 6:8

    • Later Paul and Barnabas have a disagreement and part ways before the second missionary journey. This took Josh a long time to learn. He used to be devastated when someone left the church, taking weeks to get over it, whether they wrote ten-page letters about how horrible he is or simply stopped coming without saying goodbye.
    • When you lift up the mission of the church in prayer, we always pray for God to bring people to help. We never think to pray for the right people to leave. But the Lord knows what you need before you ask; he’ll clear it out.
    • Crossroads set out to build the most giving, selfless, over-the-top loving and generous church on the planet; it’s part of Josh’s daily prayers. God brought some of the most generous, loving, selfless, kind people he has ever met, and he also removed some of the least generous, most selfish, most toxic. When people leave, the finance team checks the impact as good stewardship, and about 95 percent of the time they find church folks eager to supply the church with their complaining but not their currency.

    1. 06:33 Breaking up for multiplication

      • Bottom line, sometimes church breakups are good. Not everyone will fit the mission here, and there’s no shame in that. Paul takes Timothy and Silas one way, Barnabas and Mark go the other, and they all go with the gospel. Sometimes God breaks something up for the purpose of multiplication.

  4. 07:00 Apollos: don’t teach if you can’t be taught

    • From Philippi to prison, to Thessalonica, Berea, Corinth, and Ephesus, the gospel spreads like wildfire, and here we meet Apollos. Josh couldn’t do Acts without him: don’t step into a position to teach if you’re not willing to be taught. Being willing to say something doesn’t make it worth hearing.
    • Apollos was preaching an almost gospel, like the sermon where you keep waiting for the gospel and then it’s communion. Priscilla and Aquila sorted it out with him so he could be more effective. Notice they didn’t write him a long email ripping his theology to shreds. They invited him into their home.

    1. 08:22 Invite me over

      • Those tasked with teaching will mess up. Josh will trip on his words or say something you disagree with. Please don’t use your life group to shred him or be divisive with your besties in the lobby. Invite him over; he loves steak and potatoes, a super simple guy. Let’s sit in your home, love on each other, and talk it through.
      • There’s way too much “I didn’t like what the pastor said, so I’m going down the street.” You weren’t intended to go down the street; you were intended to work it out here. There are healthy ways to leave a church, but we have to work on more things together.

  5. 09:19 Third journey, and the unnamed heroes

    • Paul’s third journey revisits many places, with an extended stay in Ephesus, and travels the southwest coast of Asia Minor. Despite the risk, he sails back to Jerusalem, gets mixed reviews, and is eventually arrested and put on trial.
    • It’s easy to read the last 15 chapters of Acts and forget that God used many people to spread the gospel and launch his church. Paul is a warrior for Christ, but he’d be the first to tell you about the unnamed heroes, his disciples and their disciples. The church is built on unnamed Christian heroes: the lame beggar in chapter 3 whom God used so Peter could preach to thousands; Stephen and Philip, who got things done so the apostles could preach; Cornelius, who showed Peter the gospel is for all people; James at the Jerusalem Council; women like Priscilla and Lydia. There was work for everyone.

    1. 11:03 Are you okay being an unnamed Christian?

      • We all have different roles. Without Chris as executive pastor, Josh wouldn’t get the better part of two days to pray over and prepare a message. Without Steve, Hunter, and Holly shepherding the church’s sons and daughters, he wouldn’t have time to sit with moms and dads working through marriages. Andrea’s and Elijah’s jobs are too big without volunteers serving communion, pointing cameras, running slides, and welcoming people. If your job is making bagels, praise God: we are partners in the gospel, and your hospitality makes guests feel at home. God can use one small act to accomplish big things.
      • This hits Josh too. It is important to be content to serve where God has us. This is the church God tasked him with shepherding, and he is responsible for their souls. He isn’t building a platform or a brand; he’ll let God determine his influence. Are you content being a page-eight Christian, or would you prefer the headlines?

  6. 12:45 Paul appeals to Caesar: a legal journey

    Scripture: Acts 25:11 Acts 25 Acts 26:32 Philippians 1:21

    • Paul faces trial on charges that aren’t credible. Sound familiar? Remember Pontius Pilate. Paul’s defense: if the Jews’ claims about him are false, no one has the right to hand him over to them, and he appeals to Caesar. If you wonder how Christianity went from Jerusalem to Rome, it starts in this moment in chapter 25. King Agrippa goes on record telling Festus that this man could have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar.
    • Of all Paul’s journeys Rome was the most important, but it wasn’t a mission journey. No charting the route, gathering supplies, or raising funds. Paul had been in custody close to two years when Festus took over from Felix, and the second Festus reached his case, Paul appealed. It was a legal journey. Paul surely didn’t think this was how he’d get to Rome, but the Lord gave him an opportunity and he took it.
    • Appealing to Caesar was a first-century habeas corpus, everyone getting a day in court, except your fate is in Caesar’s hands, and Caesar was Nero, a ticking time bomb who might lop off your head to burn off frustration. Paul figures he’s already in Roman custody, so he might as well be in custody in Rome before Caesar. It’s Holy Spirit bold, “to live is Christ, to die is gain” bold.

  7. 14:56 Lives that make people scratch their heads

    • What stands out to Josh in Acts is that believers’ actions should occasionally have people scratching their heads. Why are you so patient with that person? Why do you keep forgiving them? Why are you giving so much money away? Why do you spend Wednesday evenings with teenagers? Why are you still smiling after all you’ve lost? Cancer is destroying you and you’re singing and preaching to nurses.
    • Sadly our lives don’t always look different. The average American gives 2 percent to charity; the average Christian gives about 2.5 percent to the church. Shouldn’t the margin in generosity between a believer and a non-believer be larger than half a percent? The church was established to accomplish the Great Commission, and the average believer spends more on entertainment than on advancing God’s kingdom. That’s a tragedy.

  8. 16:32 Faith in the storm

    Scripture: Mark 4:40 Psalm 103:2–5

    • Roman officials put Paul and 275 others on a boat to Rome; a hurricane leaves them lost at sea for nearly a month. One thing after another, yet Paul never doubts God’s sovereignty; he finds opportunity and stays an optimist no matter what.
    • Satan peddles a lie among Christians that God should protect Christians from the storm. It sounds right because God is our protector, but didn’t Jesus rebuke the disciples for waking him during the storm: “Do you still have no faith?” God allows storms so people can see what faith looks like in a storm.
    • The world needs to see the difference between suffering and suffering with eternal hope: a cancer patient and a cancer patient with eternal hope, a grieving parent at a child’s grave and a grieving parent with eternal hope. That’s how you make the world scratch its head: in the storm you say, “Praise the Lord, my soul,” and refuse to forget all his benefits, who forgives your sins, heals your diseases, redeems your life from the pit, and renews your youth like the eagle’s.

    1. 18:16 When I am weak, then I am strong

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 12:8–10

      • Your testimony is most effective when your life is a shipwreck. Don’t waste it. Paul pleaded three times for the Lord to take his pain away, and the Lord said, “My grace is sufficient for you, my power is made perfect in weakness.” Paul responded that he would boast all the more gladly about his weaknesses and delight in insults, hardships, persecutions, and difficulties, for when he is weak, then he is strong.
      • We feel so bad for Paul, imprisoned and shipwrecked, and meanwhile he’s delighting in it. Josh wants to be more like that; he struggles not to rely on his own strength and wants to rely on God more.

  9. 19:25 Malta: unusual kindness

    Scripture: Acts 28:1–2

    • This brings us to the island of Malta, a fitting close to the series. Once safely ashore, the islanders showed them unusual kindness, building a fire and welcoming them all because it was cold and raining. As the weather cools, Josh invites the church to gather around the campfire on Malta and notice several things.

    1. 19:52 You never know how God will respond to kindness

      Scripture: Luke 6:38

      • First, the islanders showed unusual kindness. Why unusual? They had no idea they were hosting an apostle, that Luke would write it down and the church would celebrate their hospitality for ages, or that Paul would heal the chief official’s father and everyone sick on the island. You never really know how God intends to respond to your acts of kindness.
      • Jesus told his disciples, “Give, and it will be given to you. A good measure, pressed down, shaken together and running over… for with the measure you use, it will be measured to you.” Looking to receive blessings? Become a blessing to someone else.

    2. 21:07 Hospitality is a requirement

      Scripture: Titus 1:8 Romans 12:13 1 Peter 4:9 Hebrews 13:2

      • A Christian can try to empty themselves but can never run out; that is by design. Christian hospitality isn’t a recommendation; it’s a requirement. Elders must be hospitable, love what is good, and be self-controlled, upright, holy, and disciplined, and if someone calling himself an elder here isn’t, let the church know as soon as possible. But it isn’t only for leaders.
      • You may not have the gift of hospitality (the spiritual gifts assessment is on the website), but practice it anyway. Romans 12: share with the Lord’s people who are in need; practice hospitality. Being bad at it isn’t an excuse to do less; it’s a reason to practice more.
      • Peter gives the attitude: offer hospitality to one another without grumbling. And not just to the Lord’s people: Hebrews 13 says don’t forget to show hospitality to strangers, for by doing so some have shown hospitality to angels without knowing it. Some say Paul wrote Hebrews and some say not; this would be a strong argument for it, because it sounds like the Malta islanders.

    3. 23:17 The law written on their hearts

      Scripture: Romans 2:14–15 Romans 2:1–3 Romans 2:11–12

      • A timeless truth here: the moral law is written on the islanders’ hearts. In Romans 2 Paul says that when Gentiles who do not have the law do by nature what the law requires, they show its requirements are written on their hearts, their consciences bearing witness. Nobody taught the islanders this was good; they just knew it. God revealed himself to them, which is one reason Paul says people are without excuse: you who pass judgment on someone else condemn yourself, because you do the same things.
      • This answers a question many believers wrestle with: how does God deal with unreached people who haven’t heard the gospel? Josh believes God can justly condemn those who haven’t heard because they fail to keep the moral standards they impose on others. Romans 2:11: God does not show favoritism; all who sin apart from the law will perish apart from the law, and all who sin under the law will be judged by the law. What made Malta’s kindness unusual was their limited knowledge of God and his law.
      • So it is strange to Josh when Christians are overly concerned for unreached people groups. He isn’t saying have no concern, but the greatest concern isn’t the unreached group; it’s the reached group that turned its back on God.

    4. 25:50 The greatest mission field is next door

      • In the documentary What Is a Woman?, Matt Walsh visits dozens of the nation’s “finest” educators with PhDs who can’t define a woman. Then he visits an uneducated tribal group in South Africa in front of a straw hut, explains the gender ideology the nation is falling in love with, tells the chief men can get pregnant now, and the chief’s council can hardly contain their laughter: you drove all these miles for a joke?
      • God doesn’t need a lesson on justice from us. At least those tribes notice creation and the created order, acknowledge a natural and an unnatural way, and try to live the natural way. They are far better off than a nation that turned its back on God and decided it can choose its gender based on how it feels in the morning. The person who has never heard the gospel is far better off than the person who rejects it.
      • Josh wants the American church to drop the idea that global missions are always on some remote island. We’re losing this country. The greatest mission field is the house next door. Do we need to send a missionary to your neighbor, or can we just send you?

  10. 28:16 Verse 3: Paul gathers brushwood

    Scripture: Acts 28:3

    • We’re two verses into chapter 28 and 25 minutes in, so they won’t finish the chapter, but the Lord loves to use small stuff to do amazing things. Verse 3: Paul gathered a pile of brushwood.

    1. 28:37 Don’t let headline pastors tarnish the humble ones

      • Josh has to stop here. Against his better judgment he subscribed to Ministry Watch, as Chris suggested, and it’s one depressing story after another about pastors failing morally. Don’t let a few grossly misguided pastors ruin your view of the whole bunch.
      • For every abusive pastor, hundreds wake up looking forward to putting others first. For every pastor caught embezzling, thousands let God rule even their checking account. For every pastor bragging about weekend attendance, thousands worry they walked through the lobby too quickly and missed someone who needed encouragement or prayer. For every pastor who dresses like Beyoncé, thousands get their hands dirty. For every pastor who cheats on his wife, thousands work faithfully alongside their wives, many of whom also work full time, to help couples hold on to their commitments. Don’t let the headline pastors tarnish how you view the humble ones.

    2. 30:23 If Paul led a pastors’ conference

      Scripture: Mark 10:45 John 13:3–5

      • What would a pastors’ conference led by Paul look like? Josh thinks Paul would push back hard on the modern idea that a pastor needs a podcast, snug jeans, and a nice haircut. Paul would say you’ve got to serve. He doesn’t expect anyone to serve him; he isn’t above collecting kindling.
      • Humility isn’t a preferred trait for Christians; it’s a requirement, for even the Son of Man did not come to be served but to serve and give his life as a ransom for many. If you were at the top of the org chart over all humanity, what would you do first? Jesus, knowing the Father had put all things under his power, got up from the meal, took off his outer clothing, and smacked Judas in the face. No, it doesn’t say that. He wrapped a towel around his waist and washed his disciples’ feet.
      • Jesus washed their feet; later his blood would wash away their sins; later still Peter would die preaching this message. If you hear anything today, hear this: the world needs more men of the word like Paul and Peter, and more women of the word. Get in the word, know your Savior, and speak of him to anyone who will listen.

  11. 32:30 The viper, and a sense of justice

    Scripture: Acts 28:3–6

    • A viper driven out by the heat fastened on Paul’s hand, and the islanders said he must be a murderer whom the goddess Justice would not allow to live. Paul shook the snake into the fire and suffered no ill effects, and after waiting a long time they changed their minds and said he was a god.
    • Some argue this didn’t happen, that Luke embellished it to make Paul look untouchable, like your high school football stories before your coach says you rode the bench. Josh calls that ridiculous given Luke’s credibility as an author and historian. Their argument rests on Malta having no poisonous snakes today, but that doesn’t mean it had none 2,000 years ago; the snake could have come off the ship, and species go extinct as civilizations grow all the time. Don’t get distracted.
    • This again proves all humanity has a sense of justice, a law written on our hearts. They figured he was a murderer and justice would be served. They clearly had a sense of right and wrong; they just had the wrong name for it.

    1. 34:22 Stay alert

      • Josh can relate to Paul: you survived a hurricane but can’t pick up firewood without something going wrong. You endured something you didn’t think you’d make it through, and before you can dry off, rest up, or charge the batteries, there’s a sneak attack. Satan is ruthless; he won’t give you time to grab a towel. He runs at you when you’re exhausted. Stay alert, Christian. Always stay alert.

  12. 35:04 Publius, healing, and persuading about Jesus

    Scripture: Acts 14 Acts 28:7–10 Acts 28:23

    • This isn’t the first time Paul was mistaken for a god; it happened in Acts 14 too, and usually that means it’s time to preach. Publius, the island’s chief official, welcomed them for three days; Paul prayed for his father, sick with fever and dysentery, laid hands on him, and healed him, and the rest of the sick on the island came and were cured. They honored them and supplied what they needed to sail.
    • Luke doesn’t mention Paul preaching on Malta, but Josh finds it impossible to believe he didn’t. A couple of months later in Rome, Paul witnessed from morning till evening about the kingdom of God, trying to persuade them about Jesus from the law and the prophets. Everywhere he went he tried to persuade people; some believed and some didn’t.
    • To the evangelists: even the greatest evangelists often watch someone walk away. The lesson from the best is that no matter what people say, call us, or how they treat us, we keep sharing, keep loving, go out of our way to show hospitality, and keep showing them Christ.

  13. 36:36 The gospel: no one is good

    Scripture: Romans 14:12 Psalm 14 Isaiah 64:6

    • Josh hopes God would use him today to persuade anyone who needs it. With his whole heart he believes the Bible is breathed out by God and everything we need for instruction on the way to eternal life is on its pages.
    • Romans 14 says we will all give an account of ourselves to God. That is scary, especially when Paul quotes Psalm 14: there is no one righteous, not even one; no one who understands, no one who seeks God; all have turned away and become worthless; no one does good, not even one. This isn’t the warm, fuzzy message some think it should be.
    • Your first instinct is to try to be good: “You watch, I’m going to be good.” But Scripture says all of us have become like one who is unclean, and all our righteous acts are like filthy rags. Even your effort to be good is bad. You’re bad at being good. We all are.

    1. 38:04 One who was good at being good

      Scripture: 1 Peter 2:22 Isaiah 64:6 Romans 6:23

      • But there is one who was good at being good. Peter says of Jesus, “He committed no sin, and no deceit was found in his mouth.” The skeptic asks how one sinless man 2,000 years ago helps with my sin. One word, quiz next week: propitiation, the act of making something right; a more familiar word is atonement.
      • Remember Isaiah 64: we have all become like one who is unclean. Jesus aims to clean up the mess. Romans 6 says the wages of sin is death, so the wages of no sin is life. Through Adam’s sin came death; Jesus brings life. How is that good news for me and not just for Jesus?

    2. 39:17 The great exchange

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:21 1 Peter 2:24

      • Theologians call it the great exchange, the climax of the Christian message, the doctrine of substitution. 2 Corinthians 5:21: God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.
      • Jesus earned righteousness; we earned God’s wrath. In an unfair trade initiated by God’s grace and mercy alone, Jesus took on God’s wrath and satisfied it completely on the cross, and we receive righteousness through him: forgiveness forever. As Peter declares, he himself bore our sins in his body on the cross, so that we might die to sins and live for righteousness; by his wounds you have been healed.

    3. 40:10 He called his shot

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:17 John 10:17–18

      • That isn’t the end of the story, or sin would get the final word. Paul says if Christ had not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in your sins; if Jesus were still dead, sin dealt with him. But Christ has been raised and sin is defeated. His resurrection declares that sin no longer has the final word; Jesus does. He proves he is God in the flesh, the God-man, the only one with authority to raise himself, and he didn’t just do it, he called it.
      • Christians are complacent in their celebrations. Sports fans freak out when an athlete calls the shot, like Jordan over Ehlo or Barry Bonds pointing and sending it 400 feet. It’s time to start hooting and hollering for Jesus, because he called his death and resurrection and did it. John 10: “No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord. I have authority to lay it down and authority to take it up again.” Only God can make claims like this.

    4. 42:01 What will you do with it?

      Scripture: Matthew 25:46 Romans 10:9 Ephesians 2:8–9

      • God is presenting himself to you; what will you do with it? Jesus says in Matthew 25 that some will go away to eternal punishment, but the righteous to eternal life. From the bottom of his heart, Josh wants them to experience eternal life.
      • If you declare with your mouth, “Jesus is Lord,” and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. It is by grace you have been saved through faith, not by works, so no one can boast. Church membership, baptism, tithing, life group, being a good neighbor, taking communion, keeping the Ten Commandments, living by the Sermon on the Mount: none of it has power to save. The only thing that has any part in salvation is faith in what Jesus Christ has done. Around here, we boast in the name of the Lord.

  14. 43:16 A word to a fearful church before the election

    • As the ushers pass the communion elements, Josh wants to be pastoral. He senses a spirit of fear in the body of Christ. A new series, The Invisible Enemy, is about to begin, and he is disturbed and discouraged by what he sees in Christian circles as elections come around. He rebukes that spirit of fear 100 percent.
    • As a Christian, it is your civic duty to vote: register and go vote. Inspect who you vote for, why, and what they stand for, and vote righteousness as best you can, though that is hard to discern. Above all, pray: get on your knees for your country, your nation, voters, and your neighbor, and plead with God to soften your heart.
    • There’s nothing to be afraid of. The future is written: Christ reigns, and no matter who has authority until then, he comes and takes it all. He is King, Lord, and Savior. Go into these months with confidence and faith in the one who did it and is coming to wrap it all up. He loves them too much to let them live in fear: stop, look up, and remember who you serve.

  15. 45:47 Come to the Lord and be baptized

    • For those hearing the gospel for the first time, for whom forgiveness forever sounds pretty good: come to the Lord today. Be baptized as a first step of obedience and say before family, friends, and church, “I want to follow Jesus, not just as Savior but as Lord.” The shirts and the water are ready, or schedule it so family can be there. Too many people are waiting. What are you waiting for? Now is the time.

  16. 46:32 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks the Father for sending his Son to save his soul, for the ability to live in challenging times as many believers before lived in worse ones while holding fast to the gospel and the hope that comes through Christ, and for every morning he wakes knowing God loves him, cares for him, and has him.
