Overview

  1. 00:13 Don’t build your faith on someone else’s encounter

    • Josh begins by highlighting his many deficiencies. Some come wondering what great thing the Lord put on Josh’s heart this week; others, what he will put on their own. Please don’t build your faith on someone else’s encounter with God’s word or grow content watching someone else be led by the Spirit. Learn to walk by the Spirit on your own.

    1. 00:59 The least impressive man behind a pulpit

      • Josh reminds the church, maybe monthly, probably weekly, that he is likely the least impressive man to stand behind a pulpit for miles. He isn’t the most intelligent or educated; he failed ninth grade English, a class about his native language, and was on academic probation his first year at university.
      • He has read many books on faith, family, and marriage, but runs out of words fast when asked to explain them; he isn’t gifted to riff on topics. He has been frustrated with God and struggled with envy of people who remember everything they study. Turning 40 this year, he is learning to appreciate his deficiencies and come to terms with how God made him.

    2. 02:56 Never far from the major theme

      Scripture: Colossians 1:27

      • Josh wants the church to know what to expect: he will never venture far from the major theme of his life. His love transformed Josh’s life and his grace gave him power to overcome sin, and when he gets far from it, he falls on his face.
      • Every week he labors over 5,000 words rooted in God’s word, and he is at his best close to the Scriptures. Others have a gift for building a beautiful illustration and tying it in at the end; that isn’t what you will get here. Far from the commands and promises of Jesus, we are simply sharing human wisdom. The power and wisdom of God is found when his church preaches Christ, and when Josh veers off he hears a whisper: “Christ in you, the hope of glory. That’s what they need to see.”

    3. 05:04 Nothing to offer but a clear picture of Christ

      • If he were asked to stand up for 40 minutes and couldn’t talk about Jesus, they would stare at each other and maybe talk about the Pacers for five minutes. He has to keep coming to Christ to understand who he is, what is going on around him, and how to respond. Just when he thinks he has it nailed, the Spirit stirs another conviction: “Look at this rotten thing in you. Let me work on you.”
      • What you will get here is a less than impressive man trying to keep up with Jesus, with a fire burning in him, wanting you to know the one who sparked it.

  2. 06:36 Four words: he is our peace

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:11–22

    • Today’s text contains four words every believer must know about Jesus: he is our peace. Josh reads the 11 verses first, Ephesians 2:11–22, planning to come back through them slower: those who were far away have been brought near by the blood of Christ, the dividing wall of hostility destroyed, one new humanity created, and Jew and Gentile built together into a dwelling in which God lives by his Spirit.

  3. 08:31 What the “therefore” is there for

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:1 Ephesians 2:4–5 Ephesians 2:11

    • Whenever you come across “therefore,” ask what it is there for. Paul has just finished discussing the salvation of sinners from last week: you were dead in your transgressions and sins, but because of his great love God made us alive with Christ. It is by grace you have been saved.
    • Chapter 2 opens with a general discussion of salvation, but in verse 11 Paul pivots: God’s gift of salvation begins in the heart, but it pours out from there and makes waves socially and culturally.

  4. 09:24 An odd intro: circumcision

    • To the modern reader this seems an odd intro. We argue about immigration, sexuality, morality, and citizenship, but when was the last time you got into it with a coworker over circumcision?

    1. 09:53 Family night

      • Every Friday is family night in Josh’s home: no devices, just eye contact, competition, and connection, sometimes anger followed by a counseling session. If you don’t have designated family time, especially young families, set it now and don’t change it.
      • Last week’s family night was kicked off by Nolan asking, “Mommy, what’s circumcision?” She did such a good job that Josh jokes he invited her up to take everyone through it again.

  5. 10:44 Deep animosity between Jew and Gentile

    • In Paul’s day circumcision was a big issue, used like a racial slur today: “There’s one of those uncircumcised ones. What a dog.” After Peter preached at Pentecost, the first churches were mainly Jewish, but Paul’s missionary journeys reached Gentiles, and most converts in the Ephesian church were Gentiles.
    • For centuries the circumcised Jews had looked down on the uncircumcised Gentiles with an attitude God never intended. They called Gentiles dogs and unclean, wouldn’t do business with them, and viewed them as beasts unworthy to be mentioned with the seed of Israel.
    • The Gentiles returned the hatred, fueled by religious exclusivity, cultural separation, and economic tension. Jews refused to worship Roman gods or the emperor, the most severe edicts were passed against them, and Caligula publicly called them the most detestable of races. Tacitus tells us Gentiles accused Jews of child sacrifice; there is no evidence of it, but it didn’t need to be true, only effective. Jews were seen as antisocial because they held firm to their nationality and didn’t mingle, which always led to conflict.

  6. 13:34 “Without hope and without God”

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:12

    • This is the setting Paul wants the Ephesian Gentiles to remember: at that time you were separate from Christ, excluded from citizenship in Israel, foreigners to the covenants of the promise, without hope and without God in the world. That last phrase hits hard.

    1. 14:05 Remember who you were

      • The humbling act of remembering who you were apart from Christ should never be forgotten; the church needs more of it. A believer must always be able to say, “I was one way, now I’m this way, and the only explanation I have is Jesus.”
      • Want to kill your effectiveness for the kingdom? Claim his work in your life as your own. Consider your witness dead immediately.

    2. 14:44 Driving school: observe everything, admire nothing

      • Josh’s driving instructor, maybe a former Marine, taught the mirrors this way: “Observe everything, admire nothing.” Take it all in, but don’t stare. Don’t live staring in the rearview mirror or fixate on the past, but if you never glance at it, all kinds of problems will sneak up on you.

    3. 15:26 Without Christ: there is only one way

      • These Gentiles worshiped the goddess Diana before they met Christ, and according to Paul they were without hope and without God. That is a huge problem for anyone who claims all religious paths lead to God. Josh thinks Paul would be ripping coexist stickers off bumpers, though he tells the church not to do that.
      • Paul knows anyone, Jew or Gentile, separate from Christ will be condemned. There is only one way, which is why the first Christians were called the Way and persecuted so harshly for saying everyone else’s gods were made up. Heaven is exclusive: the only way to be approved by God and given eternal life is to be washed in the blood of Jesus.

    4. 16:28 Without citizenship, covenants, hope, and God

      Scripture: Acts 17

      • They were without citizenship. God called the Jews and built them into a nation with his laws, blessings, and promises; a Gentile could enter as a proselyte but wasn’t born into it.
      • They were without covenants: Abraham, Moses, David; God made no covenants with Gentile nations. They were aliens and strangers, and the Jews never let them forget it, praying on street corners, “Thank you that I’m a Jew and not a Gentile.”
      • They were without hope, looking to the stars, human wisdom, and Diana’s favor while religion and philosophy fell short of their biggest questions.
      • Most devastating, they were without God. In Athens, which you can read about in Acts 17, there were more gods than men, yet they were without God because they didn’t know the one true living God.

    5. 18:20 “Not to us”: Psalm 115

      Scripture: Psalm 115:1–8

      • Josh asks whether anyone remembers the song “Not to us, but to your name be the glory,” and says he only discovered this week that it comes from Psalm 115. He reads it: our God is in heaven and does whatever pleases him, but idols of silver and gold have mouths but cannot speak, eyes but cannot see, and those who make them will be like them.

    6. 19:15 A light that became like the nations

      • God called the Jews, beginning with Abraham, to reveal himself as the one true living God to the world. He gave them his perfect law, and through them offered the world a Savior. Israel was to be a light to the Gentiles so they might be saved; instead they became like the nations.
      • Let that warn the church today: the more the church looks like the world, the less it has to offer the world.

  7. 19:54 But now: brought near by his blood

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:13–18

    • Even when we aren’t faithful, he is. Josh rereads what God did for the Gentiles: but now in Christ Jesus you who were far away have been brought near by the blood of Christ, for he himself is our peace, who has destroyed the dividing wall of hostility and made one new humanity, reconciling both to God through the cross.

    1. 20:48 Shalom

      Scripture: Isaiah 9:6 John 16:33

      • Christ’s humility tore down all hostility. Because of Christ we have peace with God and with one another; we are marked and lavished in peace. Isaiah calls Jesus the Prince of Peace.
      • The Hebrews used a beautiful word, shalom, describing the world after God makes it new, when he removes sin, death, and Satan forever: no war, murder, death, suffering, lies, shame, tears, or sin, just God’s endless love and his trusting people at his feet.
      • In John 16 Jesus tells his disciples that in him they may have peace; in this world they will have trouble, but take heart, he has overcome the world. Josh hears “take heart” as a command from his Lord, and when we obey it, we have shalom.

    2. 22:20 Reconciled to God and to each other

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:13

      • Sin separates us from God and from one another, but the gospel has reconciled us to him and to each other forever. There was a difference between Jews and Gentiles so that God’s plan of salvation might be accomplished; now that it is, there is no more difference. Christ’s work of reconciliation erased those differences forever.

    3. 22:54 A hard lesson for the early church

      Scripture: Acts 10

      • This was a really difficult lesson for the early church, and some circles are still learning it. For centuries Jews and Gentiles differed in religion, dress, tradition, diet, and laws. Up to Acts 10, when Peter was sent to the Gentiles, the early church had no issues.
      1. 23:20 Looking for the perfect church?
        • If you are hopping churches looking for the perfect one, keep searching; Crossroads is far from it. If you want a country-club vibe with top-notch bathrooms, this won’t be a good place for you. The church has a care center open on Sunday mornings, inviting desperate people in desperate situations, the homeless and the downtrodden, because that is what Jesus tasked us to do. You will see some messy stuff here.
        • The first church in Jerusalem might be the closest thing we’ve got to perfect, so pack up the DeLorean and head back.

    4. 24:30 The high school lunchroom all over again

      • When Gentiles started receiving everything they had been without, citizenship, Christ, hope, and God, problems came up. Jewish Christians got in Peter’s face about eating with Gentiles, Peter worried they would, and Paul got in his face for worrying. It was the high school lunchroom all over again: “Is it okay if I sit here?” “Seat’s taken.”
      • Judaizers asked whether a Gentile must become a Jew to become a Christian, and a council in Jerusalem said no: Jews and Gentiles are saved the same way, by faith in Jesus Christ.

    5. 25:30 The law and the dividing wall

      Scripture: Acts 21

      • The largest point of contention was the law. Jews followed dietary laws and Gentiles didn’t; if Josh went without bacon for a long time and someone else got to eat it and still got Christ, he would be upset too. The same went for ceremonial laws.
      • There was also a wall in the temple separating the Court of the Gentiles from the rest. Think of a baseball game where Gentiles had to sit in the nosebleed section, and don’t you dare sneak down to an empty seat by the dugout in the third inning. Archaeologists found an inscription from Herod’s temple: no foreigner may enter the barricade around the sanctuary, and anyone caught will have himself to blame for his ensuing death.
      • Not even a Jew could bring a Gentile into the restricted part. Paul was accused of this in Acts 21 and would have been stoned to death if Roman soldiers hadn’t stepped in. For Jews and Gentiles to be reconciled, this wall had to be destroyed forever.

    6. 27:37 Jesus tore down the wall

      Scripture: Romans 10:12–13

      • This is what Jesus did on the cross, at the cost of his blood. When he died, the veil of the temple was torn, and we have access to God; but Paul says the wall of separation was torn down too. We can praise God with our enemies. We went from enemies of God to friends of God, and now our enemies worship with us.
      • By fulfilling the demands of the law in his righteous life and bearing its curse in his sacrificial death, Jesus completely destroyed the barrier between Jew and Gentile. Paul tells the Romans there is no difference between Jew and Gentile; the same Lord richly blesses all who call on him. We are one in Christ.

    7. 28:47 No more hostility

      • A converted Jew, a true believer, will love the Gentile and long to see them saved; a genuine, mature Gentile believer longs for the seed of Israel and rejoices when even one comes to faith. No more hostility, division, or hate. It has to stop: nothing is more unchristian than for a Christian to despise a Jew. His Spirit in us won’t lead us to laugh, speak harshly, or act with hostility.
      • Remember the King of Kings was a Jew: Jesus, whom we hold up as God in the flesh, descended from Abraham, of the tribe of Judah, a Hebrew of the Hebrews. This one new humanity must be marked by love; he purchased it with his blood.

    8. 30:30 Romans 11: the mystery that stirred Josh to worship

      Scripture: Romans 11:25–36

      • Josh reads Romans 11 because it stirred his heart to worship this week: Paul doesn’t want them ignorant of the mystery, so they won’t think themselves superior. Israel has experienced a hardening in part until the full number of the Gentiles has come in, and so all Israel will be saved. God has bound everyone over to disobedience so he may have mercy on them all. “Oh, the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God. … To him be the glory forever. Amen.”

  8. 32:10 He is our peace, and he makes peace

    • If we have learned anything in the first two chapters of Ephesians, it is that Christ came to make dead men alive and enemies into friends. The Prince of Peace has done it. Through him we have peace with God and one another. Many have tried to achieve peace in our lifetime and failed, but not Jesus; he doesn’t know how to fail. We must uphold and protect the oneness he bought for us.

    1. 32:55 Our own fight here and now

      • We might not relate to a first-century street fight over circumcision, but we have our own fight here and now. We can’t let cultural divisions seep into our oneness; having differences is fine, but allowing division isn’t. “Jesus makes peace, but his church makes war. What has happened?”
      • Josh isn’t a pacifist and believes in just war, but he sees no room in Scripture ever for disunity and division in the church. Our culture is vicious and it is seeping in: men versus women, black versus white, young versus old, Democrat versus Republican, Calvinist versus Arminian, dispensationalist versus covenant.
      • You can’t hate someone because they are different. Christians are commanded to love God first and love others, and a violation of the second is a violation of the first. You aren’t given room to break off from people you disagree with or don’t like.

  9. 35:13 He preached peace, and his children make peace

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:19 Luke 2:14 John 14:27 Matthew 5:9

    • Before verse 19, Josh gives this its due. Jesus is our peace, and he also made peace. The Father entrusted judgment to Jesus, and he could have come to declare war, but in his grace he came preaching peace; his presence alone preaches peace. The heavenly announcement of his birth was glory to God in the highest and on earth peace to those on whom his favor rests.
    • No matter your background, past, what you’ve done, where you’re from, or what you look or smell like, we all have equal access to God’s peace through Christ.
    • Jesus tells his disciples, “Peace I leave with you; my peace I give you,” and children of God can make peace like him. In the Sermon on the Mount in Matthew 5 he says, “Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called children of God.”
    • We have something the world wants but can’t find. Everyone keeps promising peace and no one delivers, while true believers have peace in chaos. The Spirit produces fruit including love, joy, and peace. We preach peace because Christ is peace, and reconciliation is complete.

  10. 37:21 Kingdom, family, and temple

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:19–22

    • Josh reads verses 19–22: no longer foreigners and strangers, but fellow citizens with God’s people and members of his household, built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets with Christ Jesus as chief cornerstone, rising to become a holy temple. Now that the two groups are one, Paul shows what it looks like with three metaphors: God’s kingdom, God’s family, and God’s temple.

    1. 38:03 United in God’s kingdom

      Scripture: Philippians 3:20

      • Whether once far away or nearby, believers became one nation in Jesus Christ, fellow citizens of God’s kingdom, and our citizenship is in heaven.
      1. 38:21 Proud American, but God first
        • Josh is very proud to be an American and loves this country, the greatest nation on earth, which he says became great because it was in God we trusted when it was founded. He has watched it fall to sinfulness as we have forgotten him, and he prays for revival all the time. But no matter how much he loves being an American, his primary devotion is to God’s kingdom. God first.
      2. 39:11 Every nation before the throne

        Scripture: Revelation 7:9–10

        • God’s kingdom has many nations. In Revelation 7, John sees a great multitude no one could count from every nation, tribe, people, and language before the throne and the Lamb, crying out, “Salvation belongs to our God.”
        • That is what made it possible for Josh to travel to Russia, meet random college students on mission for Christ, and have more in common with them than with most Americans he met in college. God knows what he is doing.
      3. 40:08 From the table of nations to Acts

        Scripture: Genesis 10 Acts 10 Acts 8–10 Acts 8 Acts 9 Acts 10

        • There is a beautiful connection between Genesis and Acts. Genesis 10, the table of nations, tracks the spread of the nations from the sons of Noah, and in Acts 10 God sends Peter to preach to the Gentiles. In Acts 8, 9, and 10, Noah’s families are united in Christ: a descendant of Ham, the Ethiopian treasurer, is saved; a descendant of Shem, Saul of Tarsus, becomes Paul the apostle; and descendants of Japheth, the Gentiles in the household of the Roman soldier Cornelius, are reconciled to Christ.
        • Sin brought division; Christ brought unity. All believers, regardless of nationality, belong to his holy nation.

    2. 41:08 United in God’s family

      • The second metaphor, probably Josh’s favorite, is a household. Through faith we enter God’s family under the headship of our heavenly Father, a family that lives in two places, believers on earth and those who have died in heaven, and one day those realities will collide.
      • Earthly experiences can distort eternal truths; for some, the word “family” starts the nightmares, and families can be messy. But Paul isn’t describing a nightmare family; he is describing a legendary one with a divine nature that thinks differently: God accepts this person, so how can I not? I will love them and serve them, because Jesus thought they were worth dying for.
      • It is the only place in the world where you can walk up to a complete stranger, bare your heart, and they will take it on and walk it out with you.

    3. 42:54 United in God’s temple

      Scripture: Matthew 16:18 John 1:14

      • In his final metaphor, which Josh now says may be his favorite, Paul says we are united in God’s temple. Ephesus had two main temples: the temple of Artemis, one of the seven wonders of the ancient world, where pagans worshiped their sleepy gods with feet that can’t walk, which Gentile readers would picture; and Herod’s temple, which Jewish readers would picture.
      • Paul gives directions to neither. Both would be destroyed, but there is a new construction project underway, and the builder has gone on record that it will last forever. Matthew 16:18: on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not overcome it.
      • In Genesis God walked with his people; in Exodus he dwelt in the tabernacle, then in the temple, until Israel sinned and his glory departed. Next his dwelling was the body of Christ: the Word became flesh and tabernacled among them, and man nailed him to a cross. Today, through his Spirit, God dwells in the church. We don’t go to a temple or need a holy magic place; God meets his people wherever they gather in his name and dwells in hearts that trust Jesus. So whose house are we doing church in next week?

  11. 45:12 What God has done through the first two chapters

    • After the first two chapters of Ephesians, Josh can’t help but praise God and marvel at his grace: through Christ he raised us from the dead, seated us on the throne, reconciled us to God and one another, and built all believers together like stones into his new dwelling place, where he lives by his Spirit.

    1. 45:55 If you still feel like a foreigner

      Scripture: Romans 3:22–24

      • Some still come to church feeling like foreigners and strangers. No matter how far you are from him, because of the cross, aliens become citizens, strangers become family, enemies become friends, idolaters become temples of the one true God, the hopeless inherit his promises, and those far off are brought near. Not even death can overcome what Christ has done.
      • Josh pleads: stop, turn to Christ by faith, and trust him. Romans 3 says righteousness is given through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe; there is no difference between Jew and Gentile, for all have sinned, and all are justified freely by his grace. Call on the name of Jesus and he will save you.

    2. 47:19 Do you have beautiful feet?

      Scripture: Matthew 5:9 Isaiah 52:7

      • Some know the message well and have been reconciled, but have grown lazy. Who have you told? When did someone last trust Jesus because you told them to? Are you spreading this message of peace wherever you go? Blessed are the peacemakers.
      • Isaiah 52 says, “How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of those who bring good news, who proclaim peace.” Do you have beautiful feet, church?

  12. 48:23 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays for beautiful feet, courage, wisdom, and strength. We don’t study the mystery of salvation to puff ourselves up, but to walk up on the mountain and proclaim before everyone that he is Lord, Savior, and God, who took on flesh, willingly suffered and died for sin, and was raised again; the tomb is empty.
    • Before closing, he challenges the church to sit in the quiet of their hearts and consider the Lord. The world is fighting for your attention, but right now the Lord demands it. “Remember him and never ever forget what he’s done.”
