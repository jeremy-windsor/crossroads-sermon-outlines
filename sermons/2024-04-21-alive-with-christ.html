Overview

  1. 00:07 Week three: Ephesians and our identity

    • Josh welcomes the church to week three of the series on identity. There is no more valuable resource on identity than Paul’s letter to the church in Ephesus; its first two chapters answer who we were, why we needed to be saved, how we were saved, and what we were saved to.
    • Two weeks ago he asked people to share how God is moving in their lives. The response was so large he started a document called “God is moving,” and he will share more at the family meeting in a couple of weeks. He, the pastors, and the elders are praying for each person who responded and want them to be known here.
    • Ephesians also explains the blessings we receive through Christ. Jesus made it clear this life isn’t going to be easy, but when we keep our eyes on the spiritual blessings we have in Christ, the challenges of this life become light and momentary.

  2. 01:37 The greatest commandment: a head thing or a heart thing?

    Scripture: Matthew 22:36–40 Deuteronomy 6 Numbers 15

    • A lawyer asks Jesus which is the greatest commandment, and Jesus answers: love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, and mind, and love your neighbor as yourself; all the law and the prophets hang on these two.
    • Jesus quotes the Shema, the best-known passage in Judaism, which every faithful Jew recited twice a day. But reciting something might check the box of “with all your mind,” and that’s about it: a head thing, not a heart thing. Many believers today do the same; they sound like Jesus, but the heart isn’t involved.
    • Your pursuit of God is extremely shallow if you don’t first understand your true identity in Christ. Christ stirs our affection for God and transforms how we see God, ourselves, and others. Without that, you might perform it or pretend it, but it will get exhausting; unless your identity changes, you’ll never find joy in it.

  3. 03:21 Three ways God reveals himself

    Scripture: Ephesians 2 Romans 1

    • Before Ephesians 2, Josh wants to remind the church of their true identity. He can lay out eternal truths from the creator of the universe, but he can’t make anyone accept them; as Romans 1 makes clear, our instinct is to suppress them.
    • Paul teaches that everyone is without excuse: God has so clearly revealed himself that all men know he exists.

    1. 04:05 Creation

      • Just look around at the world, how it works and what is in it, and we can learn an incredible amount about God.
      • The atheist cracks Josh up: their selling point is that you only have to believe in one miracle, just without a miracle worker. Christians believe in many miracles, but at least we believe there is a God who performs them.

    2. 04:53 Conscience

      Scripture: Romans 1

      • We witness God around us and within us through our conscience, a little invisible compass that helps us tell right from wrong. God’s law wasn’t only handed down to Moses on two tablets; it is evident in his most precious creation.
      • Some are better at ignoring that compass than others; for anyone confused about that, Josh points back to Romans 1, where some became fools and their hearts were darkened.

    3. 05:34 His word

      • Finally, and Josh’s favorite, God reveals himself in his word, which has somehow been preserved for thousands of years. God ensured the truth was written down according to his will and maintained it through the years.
      • As we read and hear the word, teaching, and preaching, we come to know who God is, who we are, and most importantly what God requires of his most precious creation.

  4. 06:14 God is awesome, and he is holy

    Scripture: Leviticus 19:2 Matthew 5:48 1 Peter 1:15–16

    • The word “awesome” comes from the phrase “true awe,” so it should really be reserved for describing God.
    • God is holy. Leviticus 19 says, “Be holy because I, the Lord your God, am holy.” To the smarty-pants Bible scholars who object that we aren’t under the law: true, but his law still matters, and grace isn’t to be turned into lawlessness. Holiness mattered to God then and it matters now.
    • Jesus urges the believer in Matthew 5 to be perfect as the heavenly Father is perfect, and Peter writes, “Just as he who called you is holy, so be holy in all you do,” quoting Leviticus. As image bearers and adopted children, we are called to obey his commands and be holy, perfect, and merciful as he is.
    • That is where people get hung up, because we know being holy will require some serious rearranging in our lives.

  5. 07:50 Self-evaluation and three-bubble anxiety

    • Josh hopes the church will see that self-evaluation is an often undervalued but massively important Christian discipline in the pursuit of holiness.
    • While his wife was away on business in Colorado, he texted her: how does she think he is doing at leading their home to love, obey, and fear God, and teaching her and the kids the commands of God? Then he stared at the three bubbles. “Three bubble anxiety”: it’s a real thing.
    • He called himself out: does he wait for God to speak like he waits for his wife to text? He has a bad tendency to make requests of God and go about business as usual. When was the last time you waited on God?
    • Knowing it isn’t always popular, Josh says God gave Adam the command, not Eve, for a reason; men are called to teach their homes the commands of God and to display a life dedicated to godliness. To rearrange your life, you may need to start by repenting to your family. One of the most liberating things you can show your family is that Daddy and Mommy need grace too.

    1. 09:58 “Except for when we play Rocket League”

      • Driving to Tucson, Josh asked his family whether they see the same man on stage at church that they see at home. Madison said yes, no doubt; his wife said yes, no doubt; his son Nolan said, “Well, except for when we play Rocket League,” soccer with cars on an Xbox.
      • Nolan was right. Josh is extremely competitive and hates losing, so the family removed the game from the Xbox because it was “de-sanctifying.”
      • For many Christians the tool of self-evaluation is buried way too deep in the toolbox; we don’t ask others how we’re doing.

  6. 11:20 Secure in Christ

    Scripture: Romans 8:1 Galatians 2:20

    • It always comes back to identity: we don’t ask because we aren’t secure. Josh is secure in Christ, so he can admit losing his temper learning to play soccer with a car.
    • He has been justified, and God looks at him as if he had never sinned; there is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. He is a new creation, has received God’s righteousness, has unity with Christ and all believers, is adopted, washed, and forgiven, and has authority to proclaim Jesus King of kings and Lord of lords.
    • The same Spirit that raised Christ from the dead is alive in him, giving power to say no to sin and walk away from temptation, to approach God in prayer with confidence, and to replace fear with power, love, and discipline. God calls us to holiness and then gives us exactly what we need to pursue it.
    • He walks by faith in the one who loved him and gave himself for him. But he didn’t always walk like this; he was very different before.

  7. 12:42 “You were dead in your transgressions and sins”

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:1

    • Who is Paul talking to here? A believer. Paul gives a quick recap of what the believer was before, and every believer can fill in that blank: “You were…”
    • Josh wants the church to hear that our identity doesn’t have to be determined by what we were. Depending on past sins, that will be harder for some to embrace, because the stain of sin can linger and follow you around; some may never completely separate from it. Nevertheless, his grace is sufficient.

    1. 13:47 “It’s not you, it’s me”

      • When a relationship goes bad, we assume the other person is the problem. Josh asks who has ever heard, “It’s not you, it’s me,” and hates to break it to them: it was actually you.
      • In marriage counseling with his wife, no one comes in saying, “I’m a mess and I need to change.” It is always the other person. The first piece of counsel hits like a ton of bricks: if you want to improve your marriage, first let them see you’re willing to improve yourself.
      • God doesn’t play games or sugarcoat it. He says, in effect, “I’m not the problem. You’re the problem.”

    2. 15:24 Sin is a condition of the heart

      • Sin isn’t just something we do wrong; it is a condition of our hearts. We don’t become bad because we do bad things; we do bad things because we’re bad. We don’t become greedy because we steal; we steal because we are by nature greedy. We lie because we are born liars, flawed broken vessels.
      • Josh never sat his daughter Madison down to teach her to lie; she figured it out because she is a liar by nature, and she is really bad at it, for which he praises God. His advice to parents: pray your kid gets caught, or pray they’re really bad at lying.

    3. 16:52 Not choking on sin, but dead in it

      • Don’t skim over this: you were dead in your sin. Wrestling with that will only build up your praise for the one who saved you, because there was nothing you could do.
      • Many Christians prefer to think they were choking on their sins: that they could still signal for help or force it out, self-medicate, or “Home Depot it” by doing a bunch of good things to cancel out the bad. No, you weren’t choking on sin; you were spiritually dead and could do nothing but be dead.
      • Part of a believer’s responsibility is never to forget entirely what we were. It doesn’t have to define us, but if you forget who you were, how can you praise God for who you are now? And if you never tell others what God has done in your life, how can the church be built up?

  8. 18:12 Following the ways of this world

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:1–3

    • Josh reads verses 1–3: dead in transgressions and sins, following the ways of this world and the ruler of the kingdom of the air, which is Satan, gratifying the cravings of our sinful nature, and by nature deserving of wrath.
    • What are the ways of this world? John Piper says money, sex, and power are the three towering gods of this age, an unholy trinity. Our world is obsessed with getting as much of them as possible and values and seeks them more than God.

    1. 19:21 Money: make it serve the master

      Scripture: Matthew 6:24

      • Money can either glorify God or become your god. It isn’t bad in itself; our view and use of it make it bad. Follow Jesus himself for five minutes, not someone on YouTube who follows Jesus, and you’ll know why any faithful preacher won’t hesitate to talk about money. If Josh were church shopping and money never came up in three months, he would be out of there.
      • No one can serve two masters; you cannot serve both God and money. The way to keep money from becoming your master is to make your money serve the master. The Lord doesn’t command giving because he needs your money; he commands it because the last thing you need is a master besides him.

    2. 20:40 Sex: a gift turned into a product

      • Sex is the most beautiful, intimate, and private way to connect with your spouse, a gift from God to enrich and deepen marriage, and Satan has turned it into a product to be sold.

    3. 20:53 “They cease to be devils when they cease to be gods”

      • Whether money, sex, power, or influence, C. S. Lewis said it best: these things cease to be devils when they cease to be gods. When they aren’t idols, they can be used to serve God and glorify his name.
      • Be careful, believer: the second you won’t use what God has given you to glorify him, that very moment it has become your god.

  9. 21:36 By nature children of wrath

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:3

    • Whether we rebelled or simply failed, we did not love God with all our hearts or our neighbor as ourselves. Some won’t like it, but Josh grabs the ESV for the title we earned, because it is especially dark there: “We were by nature children of wrath, like the rest of mankind.”
    • That is the answer to the blank from before: we were children of wrath, by nature flawed and broken.

    1. 22:14 Flawed musicians holding amazing instruments

      • We have beautiful instruments meant to play music to the one true, living God, but the musician isn’t cutting it. Josh asks a music lover, Kelly, for her favorite instrument; she says guitar, and he assures her it will stop being her favorite once he plays it. Hand him Kenny G’s saxophone and it still won’t sound like Kenny G.
      • It is more than bad music: we started playing for ourselves and not for him, exploiting his creation for our selfish purposes, placing our glory above his, and gratifying the cravings of our sinful nature. That is what children of wrath do: they satisfy cravings.

    2. 23:51 Fasting: proving cravings don’t control you

      • Another tool buried too deep in the believer’s toolbox is fasting. When was the last time you said no to yourself just to prove that thing had no control over you? God’s Spirit gives discipline and the power to say no to temptation; are you wielding it like a samurai?
      • Turn off social media and grab a Bible: stop scrolling, start flipping. Josh enjoys a good glass of wine, but if you can’t put it down, put it down. Skip a meal and sign up to pray for an hour instead. Having just heard from someone who doesn’t know where their next meal will come from, he urges people to embrace hunger pains as a reminder of how blessed they are and find worship in an empty stomach.
      • God’s children will be marked by self-control; maybe the best thing you can do is prove to yourself you don’t have any. We made gods out of God’s gifts and earned the title children of wrath. But hang in there: the title you earned doesn’t compare to the title he earned.

  10. 25:39 Why can’t God just look the other way?

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:3

    • Skeptics ask why an all-powerful God can’t just forgive. Our culture screams for justice but has no idea what justice looks like. For God to turn his back on our sin and not make it right would be unjust.
    • If someone stole your gold bars, you would press charges; if someone cut you off on your way to prayer meeting, you would honk, because you love justice. When we do it we call it justice; when God does it we call it unjust. We accept justice legally, defending the righteous and condemning the guilty, but not spiritually.
    • We are guilty, deserve to be punished, and have no one to point the finger at. We were by nature deserving of wrath.

  11. 27:06 The gospel is good news because the bad news is really bad

    Scripture: John 8:32 Proverbs 1:7 Romans 6:23 Romans 5:12

    • You may not like hearing that you deserve God’s wrath, but learn to love those words because they are the truth, and Jesus says in John 8 that the truth will set you free.
    • God’s wrath is his perfectly just response to sin; Josh put it this week as “his holy impulse towards evil.” No one wants to talk about it, yet Proverbs says the fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge, and we aren’t likely to fear God by his kindness. If you don’t understand who God is fully, you will understand nothing else.
    • We need to hear how severely he will punish sin: the wages of sin is death, two deaths; death came through sin and spread to all men because all sin.
    • God’s wrath isn’t just an Old Testament tactic like the flood or Sodom and Gomorrah. The Lord does not change, and in the book of Acts, Ananias and Sapphira lied to Peter about the tithe and both died.

    1. 28:48 Memorize Nahum

      Scripture: Nahum 1:2–3

      • More Christians need to memorize Nahum, and this pastor is saying it: the Lord is a jealous and avenging God, filled with wrath against his enemies; he is slow to anger but great in power and will not leave the guilty unpunished. That is who God is.
      • Don’t confuse God’s righteous anger with the sinful anger you have experienced from people around you. God doesn’t have mood swings or fly off the handle; he is slow to anger, great in power, and will punish the guilty.

  12. 29:40 “But God”

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:4–5 Genesis 3 Romans 5:8 Hebrews 4:15 2 Corinthians 5:21 Colossians 2:13–15 Romans 8:1

    • Verse 4 holds the two most amazing words in the universe: “But God.” Because of his great love for us, God, who is rich in mercy, made us alive with Christ even when we were dead in transgressions; it is by grace you have been saved. Hallelujah.
    • Isn’t it strange how much we struggle to admit our rebellion and our title, children of wrath, yet how quickly we come around to God’s love? This is why so many Christians have sleepy worship and prayer lives: either we don’t care to admit how helpless we were, or we believe culture’s lie that we are so lovable that God’s great love doesn’t seem so great.
    • You can’t understand how great his love is until you understand how far he went to save you, from Genesis 3 to Calvary to this room. While we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Our disobedience took place around a tree, and God completed our salvation on a tree. He took on flesh, a high priest tempted in every way yet without sin, the blemish-free lamb and final sacrifice.
    • God made him who had no sin to be sin for us. Jesus didn’t die for his sin; he died for ours. In Colossians 2, God made you alive with Christ, canceled the charge of our legal indebtedness, nailed it to the cross, and disarmed the powers and authorities. There is now no condemnation; Christ paid your debt in full, past, present, and future.

    1. 32:30 Who you were no longer matters to God

      • Who you were no longer matters. It might matter to them, but it doesn’t matter to God. Jesus paid it all; sin had left a crimson stain, he washed it white as snow. It is by grace you have been saved.

    2. 33:05 “I don’t need to change; I need Jesus”

      • Aren’t you tired of trying to be good all the time? Josh tried, and it was exhausting, and he was really bad at it; the harder he tried, the more epically he failed. “This time, Josh, you’re going to get it right. This is the last time I’ll put that down.” All he found was failure and frustration.
      • He doesn’t know how or why, but one day he stopped trying to change and started seeking him, and realized Jesus did it for him because he had no chance. “I don’t need to change. I need Jesus.” Then God started working on him, he began to resemble the one who saved him, and God is still working that out in him to this day.

  13. 34:10 Already seated with Christ

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:6–7

    • God raised us up with Christ and seated us with him in the heavenly realms, so that in the coming ages he might show the incomparable riches of his grace. This blows Josh’s mind: Christ was raised about 2,000 years ago and ascended about 40 days later. Now read it again.
    • According to God, if you’re in Christ, you are already seated with Christ in the heavenly realms. When Jesus said it is finished, he meant it; salvation has been accomplished. You don’t need to fix yourself; you need Jesus. Only he can make you holy.
    • Walk with Jesus and the world gets to witness the song of the redeemed. That is the church’s purpose: who better to show his grace to the world than those who have experienced it?

    1. 35:38 A shout of praise from the redeemed

      Scripture: Romans 1:16

      • Worship can look a lot of different ways, so Josh asks everyone among the redeemed to raise a shout of praise and fill the place with the song of the redeemed. He deserves it all and is worthy of our hearts and souls. “I will not be quiet. I’m not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God that saves sinners like me.”

  14. 36:29 Saved by grace through faith, created for good works

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:8–9 Ephesians 2:10

    • It is by grace you have been saved through faith, not by works, so that no one can boast. Faith is trusting God completely, more than believing in God: taking God at his every word and placing your life in his hands.
    • We are God’s handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works prepared in advance. Salvation is by faith, but it always produces good works. That is more loving kindness from God: how tragic for someone to think they have saving faith when they don’t.
    • When you’re saved, God remodels your heart and gives you new desires; if your desires don’t change, you’re not saved. It isn’t overnight, but if you today looks the same as you a decade ago, that looks more like your handiwork than his. Just like Josh, you need Jesus.
    • Believe, give him your heart, open his word, read it, memorize it, and trust him with your life, and the good works will show up. Apart from faith your work is meaningless; walking by faith, your works impact eternity. Good works respond to God’s good gifts by offering your time, talents, money, and entire life with open hands: “God, it’s all yours.” That’s who we are.

  15. 38:25 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks God for his word and his faithfulness to tell the full story of who we are, who we were, and who he wants to shape us to be, and that apart from him we can do nothing.
    • He prays that the church would abide in Christ instead of running after foolish pursuits and doctrines, preach the gospel to themselves on good days and bad, walk in this world as foreigners in a way that resembles him, change the city for good, lead people to Christ in workplaces and homes, and see cracked marriages rescued, broken relationships reconciled, and unity rooted in Christ.
