Overview

  1. 00:20 A standalone message on God’s promises

    • Steve says preaching this message feels a bit strange for two reasons. He is used to preparing whole series for the youth, or taking off, flying, or landing the plane as part of the Sunday teaching team, but this is a standalone message, so he is responsible for the open, the close, and everything in between.
    • Second, the church has just finished a study that took them into heavy and dark topics: how low people, even God’s people, can go when they wander into selfish desires, let sin reign unchecked, and live without a king. After Judges, talking about God’s promises might feel like sunshine after a storm or rainbows after a flood.
    • From 15 years in the high school math classroom, he likes to tell you what he’s going to tell you, then tell you, then tell you what he told you. The short of it: while human beings often break their promises, God never does, and every promise he has made is fully trustworthy and completely fulfilled in Jesus Christ.
    • For the notetakers, there are four sections, each with a central human character, each telling us something about God, and each wrapping up with a truth about his promises.

  2. 02:39 Part one: the God who covenants

    • The church just sang “Same God,” a youth favorite from summer camp, and it is easy to sing without noticing the lyrics: “I’m calling on the God of Jacob, whose love endures through generations. I know that you will keep your covenant.”
    • We can’t get to God’s promises without exploring covenants. A biblical covenant is a binding agreement initiated by God in which he makes promises and sets conditions for relationship with his people. He sets the terms, provides the promises, and, unlike human covenants, guarantees the fulfillment himself. A covenant is essentially a housing for one of God’s promises.
    • God covenanted with Noah never to flood the earth again, marked by a rainbow: a covenant of preservation. With Moses he declared Israel his treasured possession, a kingdom of priests and a holy nation if they obeyed: a covenant of law showing God’s holiness, Israel’s sin, and their need for a savior. With David he promised a descendant would reign forever: a covenant of kingship pointing to Jesus, the Son of David, whose kingdom will never end.

    1. 04:44 Abram and the smoking firepot

      Scripture: Genesis 15 Genesis 15:2–11 Genesis 15:12–16 Genesis 15:17–18 Genesis 15:13

      • Some of the more astute are thinking, “Steve, you missed one: what about Abraham?” God had already promised to bless Abram and make him a great nation, but God’s timeline was slower than Abram wanted, and he began to question the promise. Steve turns to Genesis 15 in three parts.
      • He reads Abram’s complaint that he remains childless, God’s command to count the stars, “So shall your offspring be,” Abram believing the Lord and it being credited to him as righteousness, and Abram asking how he can know, followed by God’s instruction to bring a heifer, goat, ram, dove, and pigeon, which Abram cuts in two.
      • This is how two men in that culture entered a covenant: they divided animals and walked through them, declaring, “Let it be done to me as was done to this animal if I should not keep my part.” It was a blood oath.
      • Picking up in verse 12, God tells the sleeping Abram his descendants will be strangers and slaves for 400 years before coming out with great possessions. Then the most important part: a smoking firepot and a blazing torch pass between the pieces, and the Lord makes a covenant with Abram.
      • Who walked through the pieces? Not Abraham. Only God. That is God saying, “I am taking full responsibility for this covenant. Even if you fail, I will bear the cost. The promise rests on me alone.” That is why Judges isn’t the last book in the Bible: even when the people were faithless, God remained faithful.
      • For extra study, the firepot represents what God indicated in verse 13, the dark, oppressive bondage of captivity, and the torch signifies that God would still be with them, a light in the darkness.

    2. 08:56 He swore by himself

      Scripture: Hebrews 6:13–15

      • This covenant does not depend on Abraham or his descendants’ ability to keep it. Hebrews 6:13–15 summarizes it: since there was no one greater to swear by, God swore by himself, and after waiting patiently Abraham received what was promised.
      • Centuries later, when humanity failed the covenant again and again, God himself bore the penalty, just as he promised: on the cross Christ became the one cut off for our covenant unfaithfulness. All of this points to Jesus.
      • Truth number one: God’s promises depend on him, not us.

  3. 10:29 Part two: the God who confirms

    • As humans we’ve all made promises we didn’t keep, not necessarily from malice; sometimes we plain forgot. But God doesn’t make promises and forget them, or sit back hoping they come true someday. He confirms them constantly as he sees them to completion.

    1. 11:01 Jeremiah’s almond branch and boiling pot

      Scripture: Jeremiah 1:11–13

      • God is calling Jeremiah, a very young man, maybe 15 to 17, to be his voice and prophet. Steve reads Jeremiah 1:11: Jeremiah sees the branch of an almond tree, and the Lord says, “You have seen correctly, for I am watching to see that my word is fulfilled.” Then he sees a boiling pot tilting from the north.
      • Are we making plant-based milk here? Not quite. In Jerusalem the almond tree was the earliest to bloom, in February, a sign winter was ending, so biblically it represents anticipation, a promise of what is to come. The Hebrew word for almond is nearly identical to the word for watching, and God plays on it: he is watching over his word with care, like a shepherd who won’t let a single sheep wander or be lost.
      • Jeremiah needs that hope, because the boiling pot represents Babylon, the northern enemy that will take Judah captive. God’s will comes to pass, not just promises of blessing but promises of consequences and judgment. Like the almond blossoms, the people could anticipate good even in the mess.

    2. 13:34 No promise of life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness

      Scripture: John 16:33

      • That is hard for us to process in the West. There is no verse in the Bible that promises life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness; Steve checked thoroughly. But God has promised eternal life, liberty from sin, and joy in the pursuit of Jesus.
      • God never promised life would be easy; he guarantees the opposite: you will have trouble. But he promised we never have to go it alone: “I will be with you. Take heart. I’ve overcome the world.”
      • Truth two: God never promised comfort, but he did promise his presence, and we can rejoice in that.

  4. 14:28 Part three: the God who cannot lie

    • Our covenant-making God has the power to confirm and keep every promise and to work out all things, good or bad, and he keeps his promises even when man fails to do the same.

    1. 14:49 Paul’s broken promise to Corinth

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1 1 Corinthians 16:5 2 Corinthians 2:1 2 Corinthians 1:17

      • We all know the sting of broken promises, and that is where 2 Corinthians 1 meets us. Paul had to explain why he broke a promise. His critics accused his character and thought him fickle and unreliable, because in 1 Corinthians 16 he had written, “I will come to you when I pass through Macedonia,” and he didn’t make that trip.
      • The Corinthians were used to manipulative, calculating ministers and assumed Paul was another shady preacher out for gain. But his first visit had been so difficult that he says in 2 Corinthians 2:1, “I made up my mind not to make another painful visit to you.” In verse 17 he asks, “When I was planning this, did I do it lightly?” He had the best intentions.
      • The good news is that we now have 2 Corinthians, with its teaching on comfort and affliction, strength and weakness, and servant leadership. If Paul had forced a return visit and taught in person, he would have had no reason to write it.

    2. 17:00 “But you promised”

      • Has there been a time you had to go back on a promise? We scale our promises and how acceptable it is to break them: “Sorry, honey, we’ll go to the park tomorrow.” “But you promised.” “Sorry I broke curfew, Dad.” “We’re not going to make it home for Christmas.” “I just don’t think we can make this marriage work anymore.” “But you promised.”
      • We make big promises only to break them, and Paul had done the same, though for good reasons. His fear wasn’t what they thought of Paul but what they thought of the gospel. Though his plans changed, he wanted them to see the foundation of what he taught about Jesus was still firmly rooted.

    3. 18:19 Something God cannot do

      Scripture: Titus 1:2 Hebrews 6:18 2 Corinthians 1:18–19

      • In Sunday school Steve sang, “My God is so big, so strong, and so mighty, there’s nothing my God cannot do.” As a smart little kid he figured there had to be something God cannot do, and after weeks with a dusty old concordance he found it: Titus 1:2 and Hebrews 6:18 both say God cannot lie.
      • It is impossible for God to lie; he is always truthful. Our yes is sometimes yes and sometimes no, but God’s yes is always yes. Steve reads 2 Corinthians 1:18–19: as surely as God is faithful, our message is not yes and no, for the Son of God, Jesus Christ, was not yes and no, but in him it has always been yes.

    4. 19:44 As surely as God is faithful: Josiah

      Scripture: Isaiah 41:10

      • Do you know God is faithful? When did you last look back and recount his faithfulness in big and little things, in easy and hard times, when joy came fast and when it had to be waited for?
      • Steve’s family is approaching ten years since his middle son Josiah, at 8 months old, contracted bacterial meningitis, probably from a fungus at a pumpkin patch that October. Doctors at Phoenix Children’s said he was hours from permanent brain damage or death. Steve spent hours alone in the hospital chapel reading and praying, while hundreds prayed, many from this church.
      • His faith was challenged, and God’s promises held him. He still has Isaiah 41:10 highlighted from that time: “So do not fear, for I am with you… I will strengthen you and help you.” Unlike many promises directed to others, this one is personal because of the “I” and the “you.”
      • He couldn’t find a verse saying, “Stephen, your baby is going to be okay.” That promise doesn’t exist. What he found was the promise that no matter what comes, “I am with you.” He credits nobody but God that Josiah is healthy today at 10, and his faith grew ten times stronger. Even when outcomes go opposite to what we want, God is not diminished and has not broken a single promise. Even when the outcomes are unwelcome and daunting, God is faithful.

    5. 23:11 What is your Ebenezer?

      Scripture: 1 Samuel 7

      • In 1 Samuel 7 the Philistines return the ark, the people turn their hearts back to God, and God gives them victory. Samuel sets up a stone and calls it Ebenezer, which translates as “Up until now the Lord has helped us.” God was faithful even when his people had not always been.
      • What is your Ebenezer, the physical reminder of God’s faithfulness in your past? For Abraham it may have been that torch; for Jeremiah, the almond blossoms bursting every season no matter what king sat under their shade. For Steve’s family it is the October chill and pumpkins in the grocery store, reminding them of God’s faithfulness to their sick little boy.
      • Truth three: God’s promises and past faithfulness are evidence of his future grace. In a world of change and uncertainty, remembering how God moved in the past should give you faith he will move in the future.

  5. 25:01 Part four: the God who completes

    • Faithfulness creates faith, which creates faithfulness, which creates faith, and that momentum builds as we walk with Christ. Back in 2 Corinthians, Paul argues that the message of Jesus is not yes and no; it is only yes. The hope of the gospel is never a no or a maybe.

    1. 25:32 The Philippian jailer: you will be saved

      Scripture: Acts 16 Acts 16:30–31

      • Steve thinks Paul may have had in mind his declaration to the jailer in Philippi in Acts 16. Trembling after the earthquake opened the cells, the jailer asks in verse 30, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?” The answer isn’t, “I’ve got a book for sale; how much time you got?” It is short and simple: “Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ and you will be saved.” No ifs, buts, or maybes. Jesus is yes.

    2. 26:20 Jesus makes the promises yes

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1:20 2 Corinthians 1:20

      • Jesus is not only the yes; he is the one who makes it yes. Steve reads 2 Corinthians 1:20: no matter how many promises God has made, they are yes in Christ. He loves the ESV: “For all the promises of God find their yes in him.” It is as if the promises had been waiting and searching and finally resolve with the appearance of Christ.

    3. 27:02 More than 8,800 promises

      • How many promises has God made? Steve didn’t count them himself, but research tells him there are over 8,800 in the Bible, about 7,700 in the Old Testament before Jesus is on the scene. Unlike Paul or us, God has never had to explain why he didn’t keep one, because he has kept them all and will keep them all.
      • He jokes that they’ll now go through each one. But reading God’s word daily, you will come across many promises every day. The Bible is more than narrative, history, or poetry; it is a record of God’s character expressed through his promises, some for comfort, guidance, or warning, all trustworthy because they come from the mouth of God.
      • He reads a few and asks which one the Holy Spirit is calling each person’s attention to: I will be with you. I will protect you. I will keep my word. I will be your peace. I will always answer you. I will be your strength. I will fight for you. I will provide for you. I will prepare a place for you. I will come again. I will always love you. These aren’t greeting-card messages; they are God’s promises, and they are all yes in Jesus.

    4. 29:16 Promises made more sure

      Scripture: Matthew 1 2 Peter 1:19

      • Imagine having all the Old Testament promises with no Jesus. We might call the book good and well written, but not living, active, or life-changing. We would be asking, “God, if you are who you say you are, where are you?” But the cries of a baby in Matthew 1 pierced that silence. We have Jesus.
      • 2 Peter 1:19 says we have the words of the prophets made more sure: fully affirmed and completely reliable, because we have Jesus, the Word himself, a lamp shining in a dark place until the day dawns. God’s promises were always there but dimly lit until Jesus, the light of the world, came to fulfill each one.
      • That is why we end every prayer “in Jesus’ name, amen.” They aren’t magical words; we acknowledge Jesus is our only access to the Father because of the finished work of the cross. We come not in our righteousness, since we have none, but in his. There is power in the name of Jesus.

    5. 31:16 Anointed and sealed with the Spirit

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1:21–22 Ephesians 1:14

      • Steve turns to one result of the yes we have in Christ, reading on in verse 21: God makes us stand firm in Christ, anointed us, set his seal of ownership on us, and put his Spirit in our hearts as a deposit guaranteeing what is to come. It is similar to where Paul says the Holy Spirit is the guarantee until we acquire possession of it. The promised Spirit comes when we believe as proof that God’s promises are true.
      • The word “anointed” is used of Jesus’ consecration as Messiah and of David chosen as the future king, and Paul uses it here of us. Every believer is anointed in that sense. Jesus, the yes to all God’s promises, was anointed to carry out his rescue mission, completed it, earned us the right to become children of God, and empowers us to carry it on. “If Paul can write long sentences, so can I.”

    6. 32:54 “Today this Scripture is fulfilled”

      Scripture: Luke 4:18–19 Luke 4:21

      • Early in his ministry, after the temptation in the wilderness, Jesus is handed a scroll of Isaiah in the synagogue in Nazareth and reads in Luke 4: “The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor… to proclaim the year of the Lord’s favor.”
      • No one had a problem until he broke the silence in verse 21, this time not with a baby’s cry but with a God-man’s conviction: “Today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing.” Jesus fulfilled all that was promised, and he is still carrying out that rescue mission through his followers.

    7. 33:56 Promises are fuel for mission

      Scripture: John 14:12

      • All of God’s promises are guaranteed by Jesus, and they are fuel for us to live as anointed ones on mission. They aren’t meant to be art in your home but to be lived out. In John 14:12 Jesus says whoever believes in him will do even greater things than these because he is going to the Father.
      • Not greater in power, but in extent: the church has grown from 12 disciples to 500 to 10,000 by the end of Acts and an estimated 2.4 billion Christians today. The opportunity is there, but are we living out the call? We stand on God’s promises for strength today and hope tomorrow, and they free us to live for Christ and show his love, empowered by the Spirit.
      • Final truth: Jesus is the evidence that God keeps his promises and will finish his work in you.

  6. 35:21 Jeremiah’s promise of a new covenant

    Scripture: Jeremiah 31:31–34

    • Steve returns to Jeremiah. In the mess of captivity, Jeremiah received a promise of hope that would not be fulfilled for generations. He reads Jeremiah 31: the days are coming when the Lord will make a new covenant, put his law in their minds and write it on their hearts, be their God, and forgive their wickedness and remember their sins no more.
    • This new covenant would be realized at the coming of Christ: a deeper, unbreakable, personal relationship with God that meets the needs the law covenant with Moses failed to meet, written on hearts, not stone. In Christ that promise is ours. God’s Spirit lives in us, his word is written in us, and his forgiveness is final, so we don’t just know or talk about the promises; we get to live them.
    • God has never broken a promise and never will. Every promise finds its yes in Jesus. So go live as his anointed, Spirit-filled, new covenant people in a world desperate for hope, declaring with our lives that God is faithful and his promises are true now and forever.

  7. 37:14 Closing prayer

    • Steve thanks God for the power of his word and that his promises depend not on us but on him. God never promised comfort or all our wants, but he promised all our needs. He prays the church will remember that God’s past promises are evidence he will continue to provide grace, and thanks him most of all for Jesus as the evidence that God keeps his promises and will finish what he started.
