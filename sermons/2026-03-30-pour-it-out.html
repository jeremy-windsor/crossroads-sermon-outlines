Overview

  1. 00:30 Final week of Broken, holy ground

    Scripture: Matthew 26:1-16

    • Josh welcomes the church to the final week of Broken. They have been walking Christ’s passion predictions to prepare for Holy Week. Events move fast: triumphal entry, Last Supper, Gethsemane, arrest, trial, cross, burial. Everything leads to the cross.
    • Matthew 26 is not only leading to the cross. It is interpreting the cross. Three scenes, each with coordinates and conflict: Bethany, worship versus waste; the upper room, faithfulness versus betrayal; Gethsemane, submission versus resistance.
    • He reads Matthew 26:1-16: Passover in two days, the Son of Man handed over to be crucified; priests plot at Caiaphas’s palace, but not during the festival; a woman pours expensive perfume at Simon the leper’s house; disciples call it waste; Jesus calls it beautiful, preparation for burial, to be told wherever the gospel is preached; Judas takes thirty pieces of silver.

    1. 03:11 The cross is the key

      Scripture: Revelation 12:1-17

      • As they read toward the empty tomb in Matthew 28 next week, conflict with the enemy rises. John gives imagery in Revelation. Without the cross you cannot understand God, yourself, existence, or be saved.
      • He asks for reverence at Bethany, the upper room, and Gethsemane. He ends at Gethsemane on purpose: he is not sure a sermon can do it justice. It is a moment to pray and meditate. Make time this week to be silent there.

  2. 04:24 Bethany: Mary worships, Judas walks

    Scripture: John 12:1-8 John 11:32

    • Two main characters: Mary’s worship and Judas’s betrayal. Leaders plot, Judas walks, disciples miss the moment, Mary worships. Jesus had just raised Lazarus. Passover is two days away. Seventeen eyewitnesses. Matthew does not name Mary; John does.
    • Josh recommends the Logos app for linked synoptic panels. Mary is mentioned three times in the Gospels, always at Jesus’ feet: listening, grieving Lazarus, anointing. She loved him. She was not embarrassed to let spirituality pour out. She knew she was safe.
    • Josh wishes the modern church looked less like Pharisees and more like the women around Jesus. Do not lose gentleness. If you are holding a rock, drop it and grab perfume.

    1. 07:07 She poured out wealth and glory

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:15 1 John 4:19 Matthew 19:16-22

      • Combining the accounts, she anoints head and feet and wipes his feet with her hair. Paul calls a woman’s long hair her glory. She pours wealth and surrenders glory: humility, love, devotion, gratitude. Worship, not waste, because we love him because he first loved us.
      • The Pentecost heart asks what we must do. Love God, then love people second most, even the hard ones, with action not heart-emojis: physical needs, prayer, and say you are doing it because of him.
      • Two reactions: pour out or walk away sad. Mary does not care about the price tag, or you are the rich young ruler who likes the idea of Jesus but is blind to his eternal worth. You can be close enough to smell his breath and still want to erase him.

    2. 10:40 Chronological snobbery and a named apostle

      Scripture: John 12:6 Matthew 10:1-4 John 15:4-6 Matthew 7:21-23 John 13:18-21

      • C.S. Lewis called looking down on Judas chronological snobbery. His protest sounded spiritual; the disciples joined. John saw through it: he did not care about the poor; he was a thief with the money bag. That is church hurt.
      • Judas was named an apostle, gifted to heal, three years of ministry. Stay connected to the vine. Miracles in Jesus’ name do not prove salvation. Matthew 7: not everyone who says Lord, Lord. Fruit is obedience.
      • When Jesus washed feet he said one was not cleansed. He who shared my bread lifted his heel. The disciples had no idea which one. Judas broke bread; Jesus washed his feet. Assuming there are no Judases among Christians today would be tragic. He was with the chosen, not one of the chosen.

    3. 14:06 Examine yourself; keep the door open

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 13:5 Matthew 7:3-5 Matthew 13:24-30

      • Do not walk the lobby with pitchforks. A Crossroads mob got shot down by the elders. Do not investigate whether others do God’s will. Paul: examine yourself. You will find a log. Remove it, then speak truth in love.
      • Discernment watches actions, patterns, teaching, repentance. Condemnation decides someone’s standing before God. You may say that is not repentance, obedience, love, or kindness. You may not say that person is not a believer. Wheat and weeds grow together.
      • Josh once sounded like wheat and looked like a weed. God changed his heart. If you talk like wheat and live like a weed, it is not too late until the trumpet. Be discerning about fruit, humble enough to start with yourself, loving enough to keep the gospel door open.

  3. 17:53 Worship or waste, and the first right response to the cross

    Scripture: Matthew 26:12

    • Judas wanted political freedom and a treasurer’s fortune. After another crucifixion prediction he took Satan’s path instead. Do you worship Jesus or evaluate him? Firstfruits or dented leftovers? Identifying Judas-like qualities is the first step to killing them.
    • Jesus defends Mary. Nothing done for Jesus in love is ever a waste. He interprets the act as burial preparation. Josh calls this the holiest place in the New Testament: the first time humanity responds appropriately to the cross. One person who gets it can fuel a lifetime of ministry.

    1. 20:48 Anointing oil and incense in one jar

      Scripture: Exodus 30:22-38

      • Exodus 30: anointing oil sets apart tabernacle, altar, ark, priests, kings. Incense rises as prayer. The haze in the room is a visual of praise going up. Mary applies perfume like oil and fills the room like incense. She treats him as God in the flesh. Jesus names it burial. Temple consecration meets Messiah’s mission.
      • Ordinary-looking worship—gift, serving, song—consecrates the place and the one who enters. Jesus connects her act to the gospel with one word: poured. We were enemies; he takes the cost so we have peace. The jar had to be broken. Beauty in Christ comes through breaking. We are broken to be poured out as he was.

    2. 24:45 Paul counted it garbage

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:1 Philippians 3:7-15 Matthew 6:19-20 Hebrews 12:2

      • A cloud of witnesses has been here. Paul: whatever was gain is loss for Christ; dung, that I may gain him and be found in him with a righteousness from God by faith. I want to know Christ, resurrection power and sufferings, becoming like him in death. Not already arrived; press on. Forget what is behind.
      • You have everything in the resurrected Christ. He already has you; hold the one who grabbed you. Do not lose heart in trials. Streets of gold, a victor’s crown, your name in the book. Every tear stores treasure moth and rust cannot touch. Press on. Heaven is waiting.

  4. 28:02 Upper room: faithfulness versus betrayal

    Scripture: Matthew 26:17-25 Matthew 26:26-30

    • Passover preparations. Jesus reclines with the Twelve and says one will betray him. One after another: surely not I, Lord. The one who dips with me. Woe to that man. Judas: surely not I, Rabbi. You have said so.
    • Bread: this is my body. Cup: my blood of the covenant poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins. He will not drink this fruit of the vine until he drinks it new in the Father’s kingdom. They sing a hymn and go to the Mount of Olives.

    1. 29:44 The morsel went to Judas

      Scripture: John 13:27

      • Up to the end the disciples did not know the traitor. Josh can walk the lobby and know who likes whom. Jesus was so perfect in patience that no one had a clue. How well do you love people who do not deserve it? Sit at his feet more.
      • Leonardo’s picture: Judas at the Lord’s left, the place of honor, jostling for the first dipped morsel—not yet the Lord’s Supper. Jesus gives the bread to Judas. Collision of God’s goodness and man’s wickedness, sovereignty and responsibility. He does not just say love your enemy. He does it. We were once his enemies.
      • John 13: as soon as Judas took the bread, Satan entered him. What you are about to do, do quickly.

    2. 32:37 A new and better covenant

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:26 Exodus 24:8

      • After Judas leaves, Jesus takes unleavened bread and the cup from Passover to picture his death. Josh says no text indicates a physical change in the elements. They remain bread and wine; done in response to the cross they spiritually become body and blood.
      • The supper looks forward until he comes. Passover pointed to the Lamb; the supper announces the work is finished. Exodus 24: Moses sprinkles blood, the blood of the covenant. Jesus seals a better covenant with the Son’s blood, not an animal. Before the cross death was the end; because of it, death is a doorway. Slow down at the table. Hold that promise of drinking it new in the Father’s kingdom.

  5. 34:57 Gethsemane: submission versus resistance

    Scripture: Matthew 26:36-42 Matthew 17:5

    • Sit here while I pray. Peter, James, and John. Soul overwhelmed to the point of death. Father, if possible take this cup; yet not as I will. They sleep. Watch and pray. The spirit is willing, the flesh is weak. Second prayer: if it is not possible unless I drink it, your will be done.
    • Peter’s later resistance versus Jesus’ submission. Last week on the mountain: this is my Son, listen to him. Had Peter listened, Josh does not think he would have denied him three times. The church has fallen asleep in prayer. If Jesus relies on prayer when sorrowful, so should we, and we should let others contend for us.
    • Elders and pastors are available. Pray with a life group leader, a friend, a stranger. In the spirit of Gethsemane, do not sleep through one another’s agony.

    1. 37:52 Not fear of death. The cup of wrath

      Scripture: Galatians 3:13

      • Jesus was not afraid of death in Gethsemane. If he were, he would have taken Satan’s wilderness deal. He was about to drink the cup of wrath, bearing the sins of the world.
      • Paul, Stephen, Peter, Polycarp, Joan of Arc, Tyndale, Bonhoeffer, Jim Elliot: Jesus is worth more than my life. Only he was made sin and became a curse. The Father has never forsaken his own, yet he forsook the Son.
      • Before you toss the empty cup, hold it. Thank you that the cup of wrath is empty. The cup of suffering remains. I will have trouble, but I trust you. Thank you for a cup I can drink without perishing.

  6. 39:30 Nothing done for Jesus in love is wasted

    Scripture: Matthew 26:13

    • Bethany: costly perfume, not waste. Upper room: hospitality to an enemy, hope to friends. Gethsemane: crushed, surrendered, the only way to victory. Your love, service, song, and faith in pain matter. Heaven celebrates. Jesus wins, so do not calculate profit and loss. Pour out, serve, give, pray.
    • The story looks grim and ends in resurrection. He is writing that story in every life that trusts him.
    • They are a living fulfillment of verse 13: wherever this gospel is preached, what she has done will be told. From a first-century feast in Bethany to a storefront church in Anthem. Mary has been preaching twenty centuries. Pour it out: if a song, sing; if a gift, give; if a mission, go; if a promise, keep it; if the needy, meet the need.

    1. 43:30 Rahab walking out, then circle up

      Scripture: Joshua 2:1-21 Joshua 6:20-25

      • Close your eyes. A city with impenetrable walls, a prostitute holding a promise. Seventh round, seventh trumpet, walls down. Rahab and her family walk out under Yeshua’s rescue. That is the victory: not temporary. You are that woman walking out under his banner. If Christ wins like that, why fear wealth, reputation, health, life? Pour it all out.
      • Josh prays: fill the room, minister to what he cannot see. Then he calls the church not only to examine themselves in rows but to gather in circles, tell one person what the bread and juice mean, take them, and pray for someone. A church that is not praying is dying. Lift each other up.
