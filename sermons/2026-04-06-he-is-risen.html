He Is Risen!

Josh Wyatt · Crossroads Church

Josh Wyatt

Overview

  1. 00:42 He is not here. He is risen

    Scripture: Matthew 28:1-10 Matthew 28:11-15 Matthew 28:16-20

    • Josh reads Matthew 28: Mary Magdalene and the other Mary go to the tomb at dawn. An earthquake, an angel like lightning, guards like dead men.
    • The angel: do not be afraid; you are looking for Jesus who was crucified. He is not here. He is risen, just as he said. Come see, then go tell the disciples he is going ahead to Galilee.
    • The women leave afraid yet filled with joy. Jesus meets them: Greetings. Josh laughs that the line must have been fun. They clasp his feet and worship. He sends them to tell his brothers.
    • Guards report to the chief priests. A large sum of money buys the stolen-body story. Josh asks for a boo. The eleven go to the mountain in Galilee, worship, and some doubt. Jesus claims all authority and gives the Great Commission, promising to be with them to the end of the age.

    1. 02:55 If this is true, nothing is more urgent

      • If this is historically accurate—and Josh says he has zero doubts—then nothing is more important, crucial, or urgent than believing it and surrendering, as the women did.
      • He thinks the bigger if is claiming it is not true. He gauges the room: if the tomb is empty not because of CPR or a stolen body but because God breathed life into a dead body, is anything more important?
      • If it is true, a man of the home gets the privilege of leading his family toward eternal life, storing up treasures for a future with Jesus. Belief in that future must alter current reality. If current reality is not altered, Josh says you do not actually believe the future.

    2. 04:43 True faith produces visible change

      Scripture: Matthew 7:17-20 2 Peter 1:4 Luke 6:45 Luke 6:46-49

      • On the Sermon on the Mount Jesus warns about false prophets: a good tree cannot bear bad fruit. Internal nature determines fruit. True faith produces visible change. Belief brings a new nature; Peter calls it a divine nature.
      • Josh says it is recorded by two historically recognized authors. Jesus cycled the same sermons; people heard and followed or heard and hated.
      • According to Luke, good people bring good things out of the good stored in the heart. When you believe, the Spirit sets up residence. Overflow is leftovers, not performance. You are not pouring it out; it is excess from what God already produced.
      • Jesus teaches obedience; the modern church, Josh says, teaches self-love. Why do you call me Lord, Lord, and not do what I say? Hearers who put words into practice dig down to rock; the flood cannot shake the house. Hearers who do not practice collapse. Before the message: which builder are you?

  2. 06:58 Every other answer, then the announcement

    • If you are not digging down to build on Christ, the next minutes could be the most important of your life. Humanity searches why we are here, what is wrong with the world and with me, and what happens after we die.
    • Winter itself, even for desert dwellers, paints death and return so people get curious. There is no shortage of answers.
    • Ancient Greek philosophy treated the body as a prison. Eastern religions aim to escape a cycle of suffering through enlightenment. Islam hopes good deeds outweigh bad on a hair-thin bridge over hell. Josh tells Christians witnessing to a Muslim: Jesus carried our judgment on the cross; stop worrying about a bridge and start worshiping Jesus.
    • Modern secularism says nothing matters: chance, time, a clump of cells. Christianity stands apart.

    1. 09:06 God came to you

      Scripture: Romans 10:9

      • Christianity does not start by explaining how to get to God. It opens by explaining how God came to you. Not advice. An announcement: God took on flesh, predicted his unjust Roman execution, was buried in a borrowed tomb, guarded, rose on the third day, and appeared to over 500 eyewitnesses, many of whom gave their lives so children would know Jesus conquered sin, Satan, and the grave.
      • If you declare with your mouth Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him, you will be saved.
      • Today they will witness baptism. If you have confessed and are prepared to say Jesus is Lord—not Lord, Lord, but I will not obey, but his desires over yours—do not let anything keep you from the water. Clothes are available, or go home wet.

  3. 10:38 Remember the cross when you approach the tomb

    Scripture: Matthew 28:5-6

    • Walk slowly through the announcement. Early Sunday, the women see an angel: do not be afraid; you are looking for Jesus who was crucified. To appreciate resurrection, remember crucifixion. The angel confirms it succeeded.
    • Tacitus, born about AD 55, writes that Christ, from whom Christians took their name, was put to death under Tiberius at the hand of Pontius Pilate.
    • Josh describes crucifixion in a desert climate: nails, torn tendons, migraines, a thousand deaths. Josephus says even wartime enemies pitied the crucified. Death rarely came before thirty-six hours unless legs were broken or a blow under the arm (crura fracta) kept the victim from breathing. Neither happened to Christ, who hung about six hours—testimony, Josh says, to how severe the flogging was.

    1. 13:05 Crucified by design, not accident

      Scripture: John 3:16 Romans 8:32 Isaiah 53:4-5 1 Peter 1:18-19 Matthew 1:21

      • They just finished a four-week series called Broken on Christ’s passion predictions, all on YouTube. Remember: God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son. God did not spare his own Son but delivered him over for us all. Not cosmic child abuse: Jesus freely offered himself.
      • Seven hundred years earlier the Jewish Scriptures: he took up our pain, was pierced for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; by his wounds we are healed. The angel confirmed crucifixion before showing where he lay. Josh wears the cross because as terrible as the suffering was, it is precious.
      • Peter: you were not redeemed with silver or gold from an empty way of life, but with the precious blood of Christ, a lamb without blemish. Joseph is told to name him Jesus because he will save his people from their sins. A savior cannot be precious if he is dead.

  4. 15:22 Seven reasons the resurrection changes everything

    • The resurrection is as vital as the crucifixion. The women wondered how they would move the stone, yet walked there anyway. Jesus taught his resurrection four times in Matthew alone. Many still disbelieve for no good reason.
    • Most accept Jesus as a historical figure, the trial before Pilate, even tablets placing Pilate in that window, and the crucifixion—yet not the raising. Evidence alone does not produce faith. Only God can. Josh is a preacher, not an apologist. Instead of seven facts unbelievers will ignore, he gives believers seven reasons this is history’s most significant moment.

    1. 16:39 Son of God, Scripture, and our future rising

      Scripture: John 10:17-18 Philippians 2:6-8 1 Thessalonians 4:13-17

      • One: it proves Jesus is God’s Son. He said he could lay down his life and take it up again, then took a servant’s nature.
      • Two: it confirms the truth of Scripture. If he did not walk out, Scripture would be full of error.
      • Three: it assures our future resurrection. Paul to the Thessalonians: do not grieve like those with no hope. The Lord will come with a shout; the dead in Christ rise first; the living are caught up; we will be with the Lord forever. If Jesus was not raised, there is no future resurrection.

    2. 18:20 Judgment, priesthood, power, inheritance

      Scripture: Acts 17:31 Hebrews 7:23-28 Psalm 110 Romans 6:4-5 1 Peter 1:3-5

      • Four: it proves future judgment. Paul to Athens: God has set a day to judge the world by the man he appointed, and gave proof by raising him.
      • Five: it is the foundation of Christ’s heavenly priesthood. Unless you sit in a Bible-thumping church weekly, this may be new. Hebrews 7: death prevented other priests from continuing; because Jesus lives forever he has a permanent priesthood and always lives to intercede. He offered himself once for all. The oath is in Psalm 110; they will break it down in the summer series. If Josh dies this afternoon he is no longer lead pastor. Jesus is the forever priest. No other priest needed.
      • Six: it gives power for Christian living. Fallen humanity cannot live for God by its own power. Even praise without him is noise. Homework: Romans 6. Buried with him in baptism so we may live a new life.
      • Seven: it guarantees our future inheritance. Peter denied Christ; Jesus restored him. Peter: new birth into a living hope through the resurrection, into an inheritance that cannot perish, spoil, or fade, kept in heaven, shielded by God’s power.

  5. 22:33 Come and see, then go and tell

    Scripture: Matthew 28:6

    • Back to the women. Heaven has nothing to hide. Come see the place where he lay—not curiosity, but because this changes everything, including Crossroads in Anthem in 2026. Ground zero where death was defeated. Slow down. Let it bring peace. The grave is not the end. Jesus holds the keys. Happy Easter.

    1. 23:40 The stone was rolled away for them

      • Many assume the stone let Jesus out. Days later he appears in kitchens eating fish and comes through locked doors. The angel sat on the stone. It was still there when the women arrived. It was rolled for them to see in: grave clothes still wrapped in the body’s shape, no struggle.
      • We cannot drive by the tomb this afternoon, but we have the Word, changed early believers, 500 who saw him at once, the church persevering through persecution, and enemies coming to faith. If this were hallucination from Judean grass, they would have woken hungover, never willing to die for it.

    2. 25:49 Do not be afraid. Worship

      Scripture: Luke 24:5-6

      • Do not let unbelief discourage you from sharing. Matthew 28: come and see, then go and tell. Christianity is not merely the golden rule. It is faith in a living Savior. Do not be afraid. He has conquered death.
      • Why look for the living among the dead? He is not here. He has risen. Greetings again. They clasp his feet and worship. Thomas needed to touch; Mary just fell down. The empty tomb does not invite applause. It demands surrender. He went in dead and came out alive. Sin could not tempt him. The grave could not hold him. Amen.

  6. 27:41 Jesus carries a sword

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:19-20

    • America wants a Beanie Baby Jesus. Jesus carries a sword. He divides. Josh is not his own; he was bought at a price. Others with the same information reject him.
    • The guards were like dead men: could not move, but could see and hear. Released, they do not go to Roman superiors. They go to the Jewish priests. Why? Shared interest: self-preservation. Together they invent the only other empty-tomb story: stolen body.

    1. 29:16 Friends or enemies

      Scripture: Matthew 12:25

      • If the body was stolen, two suspects: friends or enemies. Not a tourist April Fool. Friends could not: they left convinced he was dead and they were next. Enemies would not: stealing the body would bolster resurrection belief, the thing they were trying to prevent. A house divided cannot stand.
      • Even if they did, why not produce the body and crush the movement? And you do not undress a stolen corpse and fold the clothes. The guards saw the empty tomb, the angel, the clothes. The elite pay them to lie because Jesus threatens their temple jobs.
      • Unbelief is not always lack of evidence. It is unwillingness to accept the truth. Two groups, same tomb: women worship and tell; religion bribes and covers. The resurrection still divides. Josh has not seen these things, yet believes.

    2. 31:46 Worship and doubt on the same mountain

      Scripture: Matthew 28:17

      • Matthew 28:17: they worshiped, but some doubted. This mountain has both. This church will walk with you. Josh has been burned by church too. Ask questions, go to lunch, work it out. Do not stay anxious. He is frustrated with Christians who judge doubters. What if the Lord expects you to help them find their testimony? Jesus was sent to save sinners. Do not let sanctification become self-righteousness.

  7. 33:10 All authority, then make disciples

    Scripture: Matthew 28:18 Acts 2:33 Acts 7:55-56 Colossians 3:1 1 Corinthians 15:25-28 Matthew 28:19-20 2 Peter 3:9

    • To doubters and worshipers: all authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Peter at Pentecost: exalted to God’s right hand. Stephen sees the Son of Man standing there. Colossians: set your hearts on things above where Christ is seated. He must reign until all enemies are under his feet; the last enemy is death.
    • From that authority he moves history: politicians, governments, markets, weather, stars. When the king cannot die, his kingdom cannot fail. He has already won.
    • The command: go, make disciples of all nations, baptizing and teaching, and I am with you always. Christianity is a missionary faith. God does not want anyone to perish. Who do you need to tell?
    • Go is not the command. You do not have to go anywhere to make disciples. Start in your marriage and home. The only command is make disciples. This is why he was crucified, rose, and was given authority: a people whose sins are forgiven, hearts full of God’s love, spending lives and fortunes so others enjoy Christ. This is why everything exists.

  8. 37:54 The table stays rooted in finished work

    Scripture: John 2:1-10

    • Before Christians go, they gather. Communion reminds us kingdom work stays rooted in finished work. Unwrap the bread and juice.
    • John 2: Cana. Wine running out is public shame, not a party foul. Jesus provides the best, not the cheap. The master of the feast notes everyone serves good wine first. Youth boys on Wednesday: God’s philosophy is suffering then glory; Satan’s is glory then suffering. The world gets you drunk on early best, then sneaks in what no one wants. Jesus brings the best at the end.
    • Water into wine previews the cross: he would pour out blood, drink the wine of wrath so the bride drinks the wine of joy. The good wine never runs out.

    1. 41:42 Bread, cup, water, and a struggling pastor

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:23-26

      • Bread: body broken. Cup: blood shed. No one comes because they have it together, and no one enters the water because they finally cleaned up. You drink and you enter because of finished work, then go into a world drunk on bad wine and offer grace, forgiveness, and resurrection hope.
      • The church does not exist to build a brand or mimic corporate America, but to change the way people see the church: serve, love, give at cost. If the world is all you have, this is as good as it gets. If you have Christ, the best is yet to come.
      • Take the bread if Jesus is Lord. Drink if he is Savior. If this is the first declaration, get baptized. The waters are ready.
      • Josh prays in transparency: he has been struggling and cannot name why. An elder reminded him it is okay to struggle and hurt. He asks God to minister to anyone else carrying that, to make this a place where it is okay to be a little messed up, to stay humble and love others, and he thanks God the tomb is empty. He looks forward to seeing Jesus face to face.
