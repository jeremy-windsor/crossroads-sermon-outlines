Overview

  1. 00:10 Hold Fast: truth and love

    Scripture: 2 John

    • Today begins a two-week series through 2 John called Hold Fast. The letter addresses how believers live together, and John sets the standard for Christian living with two concepts: truth and love.
    • A community with truth but no love is missing the mark, and so is a community with all love and no truth; some have been in churches like both. To avoid falling out of balance, we must hold fast to both.

  2. 01:00 The love crowd and the truth crowd

    • We like to talk about truth and love more than we practice them, and each of us leans one way. Some gravitate toward speaking and acting in love; others toward speaking truth and “tough love.” You know who you are, and it shapes how you minister and share the gospel.
    • The love crowd memorizes all the verses about grace and none on holiness; the truth crowd memorizes the holiness verses and none on grace. To the truth crowd: speaking the truth is love, but how you do it matters, and you don’t have a license to be a jerk. To the love crowd who want to love first and share truth later: you can do both at once; “I believe in you.”
    • Jesus didn’t make the distinctions we make. He is truth and love in flesh, and his followers will be committed to both equally. John witnessed that perfect blend and was changed forever; he contends you cannot separate truth and love, neither dominant nor informing the other, but wrapped together in perfect balance. That is called godliness.

  3. 02:55 John’s letters

    Scripture: 1 John

    • Second John is toward the back of the Bible, after 1 John, which the series will refer to a lot. Josh recommends reading all three of John’s letters during the series; they hold amazing instruction for the household of faith, and the more you read, the better you and the church become.
    • Second and Third John were written when John was an old man near the end of his ministry. He contributed five books to the New Testament: the Gospel of John, Josh’s personal favorite, the three letters, and Revelation.

    1. 03:48 The beloved disciple

      Scripture: John 20:19–22

      • John was in Jesus’ inner circle with Peter and James. He nicknamed himself the beloved disciple, and you aren’t supposed to nickname yourself, but Josh digs his confidence in the Lord’s love for him and thinks more of us should be that confident.
      • John stood at the foot of the cross, ran to the empty tomb, and saw the resurrected Savior; “this dude’s resume is stacked.” In John 20, Jesus stood among the locked-in disciples, said “Peace be with you,” showed his hands and side, sent them as the Father sent him, breathed on them, and said, “Receive the Holy Spirit.” John walked with Jesus and received the Spirit from him, so when John speaks, we should listen.

  4. 05:36 Love divine truth

    Scripture: Deuteronomy 32:1–2 John 1:14 John 14:6–7

    • If Josh could summarize John’s message in one sentence, it would be: love divine truth. Remember Mary and Martha: Martha busied herself keeping the house in order while Mary sat at Jesus’ feet hanging on every word, and Jesus rebuked Martha. There is no better use of your time than listening to his words.
    • God is the author of truth, and he pours it out like a rainstorm. Deuteronomy 32: “Let my teaching fall like rain and my words descend like dew.” His words give life. There is only one truth, and it belongs to God, but he pours it out like rain.
    • His word even took on flesh: “The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us… full of grace and truth.” The faithful church declares that the God-man is declaring divine truth. Have you heard him? “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.”

  5. 07:31 Why we love the truth

    • We love the truth because we are saved by it and sanctified by it. It shows us our faults and errors and that God’s ways are better than ours. We are judged by it, and we can rightly judge others by it; yes, you should rightly judge others.
    • We speak the truth, meditate on it, and not only hear it but obey it, walk in it, and live by it. This short letter has major implications, and Josh challenges the church to reflect on John’s message and recommit to being lovers of the truth.

  6. 08:30 The text: 2 John 1–6

    Scripture: 2 John 1–6

    • Josh reads the letter: the elder to the lady chosen by God and her children, whom he loves in the truth, because of the truth that lives in us forever; grace, mercy, and peace from God the Father and from Jesus Christ will be with us in truth and love. This showering of heavenly gifts comes to us through the truth.
    • John rejoices to find some of her children walking in the truth, and gives no new command but the one from the beginning: love one another, and this is love, that we walk in obedience to his commands.
    • Underline the word truth: it appears five times in four verses, the Greek word aletheia.

    1. 09:44 One letter to a lady, one to a man

      Scripture: 3 John

      • Zoom out: 2 John is from the elder, John, to the lady chosen by God; 3 John, on the very next page, is from the elder to a gentleman named Gaius, a dear friend. One letter to a woman and one to a man, both believers, carry the same instruction: walk in the truth, live in the truth.

  7. 10:15 There’s another way to walk

    • Why is this such a big deal to John? Because there is another way to walk and live. We are told to know and love the truth, but we live in a world full of lies, a world that loves lies.
    • Read John long enough and you see a compare-and-contrast style, especially in the first letter.

    1. 10:46 Light and darkness

      Scripture: 1 John 1:5–6

      • The first contrast is light and darkness: God is light, and in him is no darkness; if we claim fellowship with him yet walk in darkness, we lie. Light is what is right, pure, true, and holy; darkness is what is sinful and evil, and in the dark you can’t tell good from evil.
      • Josh hates escape rooms; they give him anxiety, nobody’s interpretation of the clue is ever right, and he always needs the lady behind the TV to give the next hint. Now imagine an escape room in the dark: you have no shot. With light you can at least identify the problem, and darkness cannot exist where there is light.

    2. 11:58 Loving the Father or loving the world

      Scripture: 1 John 2:15–16

      • Another contrast: do not love the world or anything in it; if you love the world, love for the Father is not in you. That may hit the “love bugs” like a ton of bricks.
      • The world here isn’t creation; look at a cactus silhouette in the evening and praise God for it. It isn’t your neighbor either; be a light to them, take them brownies, invite them to church. John means the believer’s love of the material things of this world. You can have nice stuff; it just shouldn’t have a crown.

    3. 13:23 Love and fear

      Scripture: 1 John 4:16–18

      • Another contrast is love and fear: God is love, whoever lives in love lives in God, and perfect love drives out fear, because fear has to do with punishment.

    4. 13:52 Walking in the truth or walking in a lie

      Scripture: Romans 1 Psalm 58:3

      • John sheds light on both sides of a conflict. He tells the lady and Gaius to walk in the truth; the other side is to ignore it, or as Romans 1 says, suppress it. That crowd is anti-truth; Satan has had his way, and they believed a lie.
      • They don’t only believe a lie; they walk in it, give themselves over to it, and, as Psalm 58 says, spread it: from the womb they are wayward, spreading lies, dragging others in. That is how all of us lived before the truth about God was revealed. There are two kinds of people: those who walk in the truth and those who walk in lies.

  8. 15:09 “Whom I love in the truth”

    Scripture: 2 John 1

    • Look again at verse 1: “whom I love in the truth.” What does it mean to love someone in the truth? It isn’t sentimentalism or humanistic compassion and kindness; it is a divine love that pours out from a knowledge of the truth revealed in Christ. Truth is the basis of real love.

    1. 15:56 The doctor with the cure

      Scripture: Luke 5:31–32 1 Timothy 3:15

      • Ray Comfort of Living Waters uses this in street preaching: imagine you are a doctor, and a fit patient who just ran a marathon thinks he is perfectly healthy, but his tests show he will die in two weeks without treatment. You have the cure in your pocket, but he won’t take it because he thinks he’s fine. A good doctor shows him the test results, then offers the cure.
      • If your doctor hid bad results and said you were fine, you should be ticked off; he cared more about how you feel than whether you live. Jesus says it isn’t the healthy who need a doctor but the sick, and he came to call sinners to repentance. Paul calls the church the pillar and foundation of the truth, so we are off to a bad start if we can’t even talk to someone about their sin.

    2. 17:43 Love doesn’t relax its standards on sin

      Scripture: John 15:18–19

      • Agape love doesn’t mean tolerance, affirmation, or leniency. Not telling someone the truth doesn’t prove you love them; it proves you love yourself. You don’t want the fallout, the risk to your reputation, the care needed to help them recover, or the people lined up against you once you draw a line in the sand. Love and truth cannot be separated; you can’t love them without telling them the truth.
      • Jesus warned in John 15: if the world hates you, it hated me first; you don’t belong to the world, I chose you out of it, and that is why it hates you.
      • The day the church fails to uphold the truth is the day it stops being the church. God hates deception, so how must he feel when his church turns from the truth to win a popularity contest? Some churches soften the truth to win converts, to what? They present their own gospel, affirm and call it love, look the other way and call it love: “God made you that way, you don’t need to change.” That is a lie straight from Satan. The true church holds on to the truth like the last parachute in a nose-diving plane.

    3. 20:09 Only one doctor has proven his cure

      • Yes, God made you in his image with worth and value, but sin came through Adam and spread like a disease, and no one was immune. Many doctors claim a cure, but only one has proven it, with an empty tomb behind him: Jesus. Without him your disease leads to death; with him it won’t, and he promises resurrection and a life without sin, pain, or death, because Jesus killed death three days after he was crucified.
      • The sin you cling to is the sin he bled and died for, so repent and start living for him. He didn’t make a way for you to continue in sin but to overcome it. Grace isn’t a license for lawlessness; it is a tool to help you kill your sin, and when you fail, you get up and fight again by grace upon grace. The one who did it for Josh can do it for you.
      • Peter describes the Lord holding back Christ’s coming so that as many people as possible can be saved. Imagine how angry God is when churches try to save people with a false gospel while he holds eternity back. Josh fears that day for those who preach it. “We are truth keepers.”

  9. 22:12 Love and truth are good together

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 13:6

    • Love and truth are inseparable, and they are good together, like a good wine and cheese. First Corinthians 13 says love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. You don’t increase love by softening the truth; decreasing the truth diminishes your love until nothing is left. Real love requires affection for what is true and right.

    1. 22:51 Kids running around like wild animals

      • If parents say they love their kids but let them run wild with no discipline, boundaries, or consequences, that isn’t love; it is chaos and laziness, handing them over for convenience instead of sacrificing and fighting for them.
      • “But Josh, they get upset when I discipline them.” Who cares what a 5-, 10-, or even 14-year-old thinks of you? What will they say when they are 22? Josh’s 18-year-old daughter is often frustrated by their boundaries, and he loves her too much to care what she thinks of his parenting style.

  10. 23:58 Mindy’s story

    • A proud dad moment: Josh’s daughter went to a women’s conference instead of prom, “drop-kicking Satan in the face.” There she met Mindy, whose testimony opened many eyes and deeply touched Josh’s wife. He invites Mindy up, especially for parents on the fence about how God wants them to lead their homes.

    1. 24:48 “I was walking in my own truth”

      • Mindy was baptized in November. She didn’t grow up in the faith; she was walking in her own truth. The first time she attended church, in second or third grade with a friend, she told her mom, “They fed me wine and snacks. I want to do that again.”
      • She was led to believe there was no God, though friends talked to her about the Bible. One night in fifth grade she asked God to prove himself by making her alarm clock flicker or lightning streak across the sky. Nothing happened, and then her whole body felt electrified, she got goosebumps, and she cried a happy, joyful cry. She didn’t know then what the Holy Spirit was, but she knew it was him, and it became an inside joke between her and God when people said there was no God.

    2. 27:02 What could have been done differently

      • Asked what could have supported her better as a child, Mindy says boundaries, discipline, and hard conversations. Diagnosed young with ADHD, she acted out, and when asked why she went to school she laughed, “To see my friends, duh.” Her parents didn’t know how to deal with her, so she had a lot of autonomy, which felt cool and got her into a ton of trouble. She dropped out of high school because she thought she knew better than all the adults.
      • She urges parents of difficult children, especially those with ADHD, not to turn their backs on them but to give them all the support and boundaries they can.

    3. 28:38 God was there on the streets

      • With stronger boundaries she wouldn’t have had the experiences she had, but she doesn’t want pity; each experience molded her. As a young teen on the streets she was beaten, sexually assaulted, and kidnapped, and learned to live in fight or flight. She wants women and girls who are struggling to know they are never alone.
      • God has proven over and over that he was part of her journey: he kept her safe on the streets and when she was held against her will. She has forgiven everyone who wronged her. As Josh said the week before, her experiences don’t define her; they help her help others realize that no matter how dark the hole, they can make it out.

    4. 30:36 From dropout to coach

      • Mindy went back to high school, graduated, went to college, and earned a master’s degree with honors. She now works in finance as an operational leader at a large hospital, is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu purple belt, coaches women at her gym, and teaches free jiu-jitsu classes to women every Friday night to help people stay safe.

    5. 31:27 Boundaries show your love

      • Josh praises God for her faith and testimony. After first service, a woman told him she used to lie to friends that her mom wanted her home by midnight, because her mom never set a time and she wanted them to think she was loved. She didn’t know her mom loved her until she was 40; when she asked why, her mom said, “I didn’t think you needed it,” and she answered, “I didn’t think you loved me.”
      • Kids resist boundaries, but they need them; boundaries shape them and show them your love. Josh prays for Mindy, thanking God for meeting her in her room that night in fifth grade and asking that her painful journey keep pointing people to her Lord and Savior.

  11. 33:34 Jesus isn’t a bad electrician

    • Josh likes to pretend to be an electrician. When the wires got crossed in a three-way switch at home, his genius fix was a smart switch on one end and telling the kids to use Alexa.
    • Our wiring gets crossed too, and nothing goes right. But Jesus doesn’t slap a Band-Aid on it; he can rewire everything like new. Some of your children aren’t walking in the truth, but Josh has confidence God can do it, pleads with him to, finds joy in the children who are walking in the truth, and trusts God with those who aren’t.

  12. 34:39 To young families: don’t play fast and loose

    • Josh pleads with young families: don’t play fast and loose with your responsibility as parents. Discipline your children and set boundaries for as long as they are under your roof. Show them how much you love them, even if they hate you for it.

    1. 35:04 Prevention, not just recovery

      • A recent study claims 79 percent of churchgoing men watch porn regularly, and 55 percent of married men in the church. Josh’s heart breaks for them, because they are robbing their marriages of intimacy. Recovery is great, but where is the talk about prevention? Our children deserve better. American Christian men scream about the nation’s decline while sneaking off with somebody’s daughter on a screen to cheat on their wives. It has to stop.
      • He isn’t speaking from superiority. He is guilty: he watched porn behind his wife’s back, pleaded with God to remove it, and God did; he pleaded with her to forgive him, and she did. He isn’t condemning anyone; this is love. It doesn’t have to be like this, and change starts in the home, and early.

    2. 36:46 Restore the fear of the Lord to our homes

      Scripture: Proverbs 31:30 Psalm 112:1

      • We need to restore the fear of the Lord to our homes. Proverbs 31: charm is deceptive and beauty is fleeting, but a woman who fears the Lord is to be praised. Men, the call is the same, from Psalm 112: blessed is the man who fears the Lord and greatly delights in his commands. Some don’t even know his commands; how can they delight in them?

    3. 37:15 Advice from Josh, not the apostle John

      • Parents and grandparents want their children walking in the truth, so Josh offers advice, clearly “from Josh, not the Apostle John”: stop letting your kids on the internet without a leash. Many adults shouldn’t be online without accountability either; the world would change overnight if everyone had to submit their browser history to their grandmother and their pastor.
      • Your seven-year-old doesn’t need an iPhone. Social media is ruining how we communicate and connect, yet we let kids scroll at snack time and train them to be addicts. Ban social media; they might want it, but they don’t need it.

    4. 38:42 Secret sin

      Scripture: John 8:11

      • The internet has made way for the most drastic increase in secret sin ever seen. Everyone has some, even just presenting your life on Instagram as something it isn’t. Secret sin shows you aren’t walking in the truth, because the truth would find you on the ground surrounded by people ready to stone and humiliate you, dismantle the crowd, and say, “Neither do I condemn you; go and sin no more.”
      • Secret sin means you are suppressing the truth. The gospel is for the sick and hurting, yet you insist you are healthy. How many are trying to offer soul care to their neighbors while leaving their own festering wound untreated?

  13. 39:34 Love your neighbor as yourself

    Scripture: Galatians 5:14 1 John 4:19 2 John 6 Romans 5:5

    • Paul says in Galatians 5 that the entire law is fulfilled in one command: love your neighbor as yourself, and in obeying it you fulfill your command to love God. Rarely taught: this command doesn’t speak poorly of self-love; it promotes it, because the best way to love yourself is to recall how deeply God loves you, and your neighbor is better loved when you know how God loves you. It trickles down; as John says, we love because he first loved us. If your love isn’t rooted in his love, you will constantly struggle with his command.
    • This is love, that we walk in obedience to his commands; true love is the mark of his true church. Paul says in Romans 5 that God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit, and you can’t contain it. God didn’t pour it and put a lid on it; there is always an overflow.

  14. 41:06 Obedience: the ultimate expression of love for God

    Scripture: Joshua 22:5 Mark 12:30–34

    • Closing, Josh says the ultimate expression of your love for God is obedience. Studying Joshua for the summer series, he loves what Moses’ successor told Israel: be very careful to keep the commandment, to love the Lord your God, walk in obedience, keep his commands, hold fast to him, and serve him with all your heart and soul. This has been the command from the beginning.
    • In Mark 12 Jesus names the greatest commandments: love the Lord your God with all your heart, soul, mind, and strength, and love your neighbor as yourself. The man answers, “Well said, teacher,” that this is more important than all burnt offerings and sacrifices, and when Jesus saw he had answered wisely, he said, “You are not far from the kingdom of God.”
    • Josh doesn’t know how far anyone is from the kingdom of God, but however far you have drifted or fallen, he invites you to come back home today and recommit your life to Christ.

  15. 42:47 Communion

    Scripture: Hebrews 11:6 Romans 5:8

    • Communion is for believers; anyone who doesn’t believe can let the plate pass with no pressure. Without faith that Jesus is God in the flesh, who lived the life we couldn’t, died the death we deserved, and rose three days later conquering sin and death, it is impossible to please God, and it is just a saltine and possibly expired juice.
    • With faith, the bread and juice are the most intimate way to recall God’s great love for us, demonstrated in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

  16. 43:54 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays that the church would not only hear the truth but respond to it, evaluating their lives against God’s ways and actively choosing his ways; rejecting our own way for his is obedience at its core.
    • Some must let go of things that will be painful and hard to say no to, but in Jesus’ name and his strength they can lay aside addictions and patterns of behavior and see their homes raised up in his way. They repent and run back to him, knowing he receives them with open arms, forgiven and at peace through the blood of Jesus.
