Overview

  1. 00:10 The moped and the Corvette

    • One of Steve’s all-time favorite preacher stories: a moped driver pulls alongside a brand-new cherry red Corvette Stingray and asks to take a peek. The impressed owner opens the passenger door, then, when the light turns green, slams it and floors it to show off. Seconds later the moped screams past him, again and again, until it stops on a dime, does a 360, and drops to the ground. The Corvette man pulls over: “Is there anything I can do for you?” “Yeah, you can open your door and unleash my suspenders.”

  2. 01:27 Culture drags us, especially in identity

    • That’s our culture, dragging us back and forth in whatever direction and at whatever speed it chooses, and nowhere more relentlessly than in personal identity. Wanting to be something other than what we are isn’t new; every generation has wished to change how they look, where they live, their parents, their body type.
    • As a kid Steve spent an entire summer identifying as Zorro: hat, cape, sword, worn all the time, even to bed, until his mom had to scrape it off. At middle school church camp he started identifying as Julie’s boyfriend; she never participated, but that didn’t stop him dreaming. Back then it was called wishful thinking, and as long as it wasn’t stalking it was innocent.
    • What used to be wishful thinking has become the personal identity movement, an all-out assault on everything we once cherished and held sacred.

    1. 03:20 From wishful to weird

      • Who hasn’t wanted to be different? Steve wishes he were as extroverted as he appears on stage; some wish to be less anxious, free of an addiction, more fit, less angry, or in control of their tongue. But it’s one thing to be a seventh grader who wishes he could date Julie; as a late-60s married man, that’s just weird, maybe even criminal.
      • Weird is where we are: someone born one race identifies as another and expects everyone to play along. At the Olympics beginning this week, 20 events will be mixed gender, meaning men will compete in women’s events. The movement has become a cultural tsunami, a snowball gathering speed that threatens to envelop us, and everything seems up for grabs.

  3. 04:59 Two questions: how did it gather speed, and how is it working?

    • Steve asks two questions: how is it working out for us, and how did this movement gather such speed? He answers in reverse order. According to Pew research, it wasn’t until 2013 that most Americans even owned a smartphone; think of the damage those little boxes have caused in only 11 years. In 2009 hardly any teens were on social media; by 2015 nearly 90% were, for multiple hours a day.
    • Dr. Jean Twenge, professor of psychology at San Diego State University, agrees: constant smartphone access is the primary fuel of this identity crusade. There were no other significant cultural changes in that time, in parenting, divorce, or finances; smartphones restructured kids’ free time so they no longer spent it with actual friends face to face, while peers and heroes gained huge platforms with high visibility and little accountability. Steve is convinced smartphones and social media are why the movement exploded.

    1. 07:32 How is it working out for us?

      • Twenge’s book on those now 12 to 26 says it in the title: iGen, why today’s super-connected kids are growing up less rebellious, more tolerant, less happy, and completely unprepared for adulthood. She describes a generation marked by fear, anxiety, and an overwhelming sense of not being good enough, because online they saw everyone’s best and knew they weren’t.
      • No wonder 70% of high school girls experienced profound sadness or hopelessness in the past year, and nearly 25%, one out of four, wrote out a suicide plan. The epidemic hits boys as much as girls: between 2011 and 2021 clinical depression among teens and young adults doubled; from 2007 to 2019 suicide rates for those in their early 20s rose 41%, and for 10- to 14-year-olds they tripled.

    2. 09:43 Good advice that can’t cure the disease

      • Twenge’s advice to young people and parents: put down the smartphone, talk to actual people, limit social media, exercise, eat healthy, and get a job. That is really good advice that could alleviate many symptoms, but it won’t cure the disease.

  4. 10:21 A broader war over who you are

    • Smartphones are a symptom of a far broader war: relentless lies, destructive ideologies, and corrupt messaging. And it isn’t just our kids; we can cluck our tongues, but all of us are striving to be something less, more, or other than the person God made us to be.
    • Not as you wish you could be, nor as others tell you you should be: your identity should be formed only as God alone sees you. Like the moped man, culture is dragging us toward danger. If you fail to embrace the you God created you to be, you can never become the you he has always wanted you to become; you are a pinball tossed between your own whims and the deceitful messaging others pile on you, until you embrace yourself as God made you.

  5. 11:54 Romans 12:1: in view of God’s mercy

    Scripture: Romans 12:1

    • That brings us to Romans 12, where the apostle Paul, who had his own identity issues, writes, “Therefore, I urge you, brothers, in view of God’s mercy.” Steve stops right there: if you’re ever going to change the way you view yourself, you first have to change the way you view God.
    • Since identity issues veer into God’s lane, it’s easy to think God is mad at you. But Paul appeals from the standpoint of mercy. He doesn’t say in view of God’s wrath, though God is opposed to everything evil; nor God’s judgment, though we will all stand before his throne; nor how bad you’ve been, though we are all a mess. He appeals to God’s mercy.

    1. 13:10 What mercy is

      • Simply put, mercy is not getting what I deserve. Steve deserves hell and eternal separation from God, and so do you, but he won’t get any of it. So in view of God’s incredible, astonishing, undeserved mercy, why would he not offer his body as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God?
      • Having shown you his mercy, God wants you, and Paul urges you, to live to please him, because your true identity is only fulfilled as you please God. You please him by living for him, identifying yourself as nothing more, nothing less, and nothing other than a fragrant offering of holiness, obedience, and surrender. Why? Because of his mercy. From now on your life is always to please him, not you; that’s why Paul calls it a sacrifice.

  6. 14:57 A living sacrifice

    • But Paul says a living sacrifice, which is good news. In the Old Testament the thing sacrificed died; Steve could say it was toast, which is theologically accurate but might anger PETA supporters. The priests slit the animal’s throat, spread the carcass on the altar, and cooked it to a crisp. God doesn’t require that of us because Jesus already became our once-for-all dead sacrifice on the cross for the whole world.
    • God now wants living sacrifices, which creates a problem: living sacrifices have a nasty habit of crawling off the altar. A living sacrifice requires a deliberate, methodical, painstaking decision every day, hour, and minute to offer and reoffer your life, including your physical body, to the God who has shown you radical mercy.

    1. 16:50 What kind of smoke will rise?

      • So whenever you face an identity decision, ask yourself: as I fire up the altar, I’m about to make some smoke, and it’s my call what scent rises to fill the nostrils of God. Will the identity I exhibit in my attitude, actions, or words be a sweet-smelling aroma, or will it stink to high heaven and stank up the place?

    2. 17:41 Up the altar and down again

      • Steve gave his life to Jesus at nine years old, 60 years ago this week, and has loved Jesus every single day since. Yet on the living sacrifice part he has logged a lot of miles falling off his altar and climbing back up. By the silence, he guesses he’s the only one.
      • Some climb God’s altar, then get scared it isn’t mercy and God will zap them, so they run for the hills. Some fear God will tire of their lousy track record and vaporize them, so they sneak down the back side hoping he won’t notice. Others stay but keep juking to be a moving target, or keep one foot on the ground for a fast getaway. Steve was never a horrible infidel, just a fairly decent guy, but there were times he could have used an escalator on his altar, offering his body to God only temporarily, for the same reason you slip off yours.

    3. 19:45 How did it go this week?

      • Like Paul, it’s Steve’s job to urge you: in this past week alone, how did it go? Did you send heaven a pleasing aroma, or was it a bit pungent? He doesn’t want to guilt anyone, but since we are all horribly inconsistent, offering your body can’t be one time. It’s a great first move, but how do we sustain it? Is there a spiritual navigation app so Stevie always follows God’s plan and not his own, and stops the climbing-up-and-crawling-down drill? Yes: verse 2.

  7. 21:03 Romans 12:2: don’t be Jell-O

    Scripture: Romans 12:2 Romans 12:2

    • “Do not conform any longer to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind.” Steve loves it even more in the J.B. Phillips paraphrase: “Don’t let the world around you squeeze you into its mold.”

    1. 21:30 Jell-O and the human brain

      • Every time he reads that, he thinks Jell-O. Americans buy nine boxes every second, and it’s not every food you can play or wrestle in. Jell-O takes on many personalities: in a bowl it’s a bowl, in a glass it’s a glass, add fruit and nuts and it congeals around them. It’s multigenerational: dessert in assisted living, Jigglers for preschoolers, and college kids swallow theirs with a shot. To the scientist it is gelatinous material from animal bone, skin, and connective tissue with coloring and flavored sugar; pretty much nutrition free.
      • Why this useless information? Technicians at St. Jerome Hospital in Batavia, New York, hooked an EEG machine to a mound of lime Jell-O and found virtually identical qualities to the human brain. That doesn’t surprise Steve, because Jell-O is what we do: put us in practically any circumstance and our default is to conform to whatever expectations and social constructs are set for us. We are Jell-O, baby.

    2. 24:11 Keep on not being conformed

      • Paul says don’t be Jell-O; don’t conform to the world’s mold. He writes it in the present tense, so it reads, keep on not being conformed: a continual process, an ongoing refusal to congeal your life around the pattern of this world.

    3. 24:46 The pattern of this world

      Scripture: 1 John 2:15–16

      • The big, out-of-control pattern these days is the personal identity movement: this is how I identify, therefore this must be who I am. The apostle John encountered a similar pattern 2,000 years ago, and it sounds current: do not love the world, for everything in the world, the cravings of sinful man (you are what you have), the lust of his eyes (you are what you do), and the boasting of what he has and does (you are what people say you are), comes not from the Father but from the world.
      • Every day our minds are bombarded by sights and sounds designed to appeal to sinful cravings, lustful appetites, and pride, producing desires that want us to act out. That is the pattern: look at images that feed my ego, think about engaging with them, think enough that I want to, until ultimately I do. That’s why Paul is emphatic: don’t go there; don’t be Jell-O; don’t be conformed to the predictable, unreliable thinking that has landed you in so many ditches.

  8. 27:09 Be transformed

    • Instead, be transformed. The Greek word gives us “metamorphosis”: not a slight alteration but a complete overhaul, one thing becoming an entirely different thing inside and out, mentally, physically, emotionally, spiritually. In view of God’s mercy, Paul says, that’s what I want for you: to be totally changed.

    1. 27:53 By the renewing of your mind

      • Keep reading: metamorphosis happens only by the renewing of your mind. Something has to change about the way you think, because you can’t go on thinking the way the world thinks and not eventually be conformed to it. That change only happens through a day-by-day, year-in-year-out renewal process, the only way to end the conforming and be equipped to see through the world’s destructive schemes. It takes a different kind of mind.

    2. 29:03 The mind of Christ

      • The Bible calls it the mind of Christ. It sounds mystical but isn’t: it is the ability to look at life the way Jesus does and see the world as he sees it, to discern what is good and not just what appears good, to separate the eternal from the temporal, and to embrace that I am who I am because that’s who Jesus says I am. The mind of Christ understands I wasn’t put here for my own gratification but to worship and glorify the God of all mercy as his holy, pleasing sacrifice.

  9. 30:00 Holding tightly to the word of God

    Scripture: Hebrews 4:12

    • There is only one way to get the mind of Christ: holding tightly to the word of God, the only message that is living and active and sharper than any double-edged sword, penetrating to divide soul and spirit, joints and marrow, and judging the thoughts and attitudes of the heart.
    • God’s word isn’t an ancient document that died long ago; it lives, and it is as vital today as when God first spoke it. And it cuts: it exposes the garbage thinking that dominates our world, cuts through nonsense, and frustrates the lame excuses and clever rationalizations we hide behind. The Bible assassinates the personal identity movement and cuts to the core of who we are, because whatever Scripture says is true is true.

    1. 31:33 The sword and the helmet

      Scripture: Ephesians 6:17 2 Timothy 3:16 Romans 12:2

      • That’s why Paul says take up the sword of the Spirit and use it; don’t depend on clever, persuasive arguments, but slay the evil dragons that jerk you around. He also says take up the helmet of salvation, which is also the word of God. Why two words of God in the armor? The sword is offensive and the helmet defensive. To renew a mind corrupted by the world you need both: a weapon, and a sentinel to guard against frontal attack and to teach, rebuke, correct, and train you in righteousness.
      • Then you can test and approve what God’s will is, his good, pleasing, and perfect will. That is how offering your body becomes a move toward God that lasts: as you regularly fill your mind with his mind, it’s no longer about how you identify or your perspective. The only question is what God says, what his will is, and who he says that I am.

    2. 33:24 Are you giving yourself to his word?

      Scripture: 1 Peter 2:5

      • The only way to stop conforming is a new mind, and the only way to a new mind is a death grip on the word of God, by which he informs and empowers his children and counteracts the lies swarming around us. So are you giving yourself to the faithful intake of his word? Steve is serious: your destiny is at stake. Only through the systematic feeding of God’s word can you overhaul your mind.
      • Only then, instead of living like a lump of Jell-O or a wimpy suspender dude, can you be transformed into living stones that God forms into a spiritual house, strong, firm, and settled in your convictions, knowing, not just identifying, that Christ is at work in you. You will still have struggles and temptations, but you bring your burdens, heartaches, needs, and temptations face to face with the living, cutting word of God.

  10. 35:29 Steve’s story: the Cracker Barrel parking lot

    • About 15 years ago Steve was slogging through the darkest season of his life. After an 18-month battle with a rare, aggressive metastatic breast cancer, his late wife Cindy went home to be with Jesus. Months later he sat in his car after another grief counseling session; he sat in his car a lot then, because his so-called home was empty, just four walls and a place not to sleep.
    • He sat feeling sorry for himself, asking the same whining questions on repeat: “God, why? Why didn’t you give us a different ending?” Maybe he thought badgering God would get a different answer. But in that Cracker Barrel parking lot he sensed an unspoken voice whispering to him.

    1. 36:59 “Is Romans 8:28 still in the Bible?”

      Scripture: Romans 8:28 Romans 8:28 1 Thessalonians 4:13

      • Before anyone writes him: Steve believes God almost exclusively speaks through his written word. But when you fill your mind with God’s mind and are willing to listen, he will speak, not audibly, but those with ears to hear will hear loud and clear.
      • What he heard was a simple question that shook him: “Steve, is Romans 8:28 still in the Bible?” He paused, and it came again. He answered out loud, feebly: “Yes, Lord, I suppose it is.” Romans 8:28 says that in all things God works for the good of those who love him, who have been called according to his purpose. “So you’re saying that verse is still in there? Huh. Still in mine too.”
      • “Look, son, from time to time really bad things happen, and they seem to happen to you a lot, and this one is about as bad as it gets. Go ahead and grieve, but not as others who have no hope.” In other words, don’t just follow your heart; fill your mind with God’s mind, because that’s the only way your heart will see what your eyes cannot.

    2. 39:28 Healing is always about God’s glory

      • Wallowing in grief and lost, Steve asked why God didn’t heal her when he and so many others prayed. “Steve, healing is never about the person being healed; it is always a function of my glory.” He didn’t understand a word. “I know, but one day you will.”
      • “Do you realize that every person who has ever been healed still died?” Lazarus, raised by Jesus himself, still died; so did the blind man who could see. No one is walking Jerusalem today saying, “I’m 2,347 years old and I can’t die.” Healing is always about bringing God maximum glory by whatever means. “Take Cindy. My greater glory was to have her with me and not with you.” Steve can’t say he enjoyed the conversation, but he knew the voice was true.

    3. 41:44 “It’s going to turn out good”

      • Thankfully the voice wasn’t through: “I know this isn’t the outcome you wanted and your life feels like one big mess. But where’s that verse again? Even this thing that feels so awful, by the end of the story it’s going to turn out what?” Good. And it has. Steve is so glad he was reminded that Romans 8:28 is still in God’s book, because he would need it again when Jessica died.

  11. 42:51 God weaves all things to good

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1:20 Genesis 1

    • However many promises God makes, they are always yes in Christ, so the God who promised to work all things for the good of those who love him will do it, and it will take some work. Some things are just good: in Genesis 1 God made the birdies and said good, then mangoes, good, the whole chapter good. But then Adam took a bite, and since then many things have to get to good. God takes some things from this pile and a chapter from that story and keeps weaving until they finally get to good.

    1. 44:06 For those who are called and who love him

      • The promise isn’t for everyone, just for those who have been called. No time to unpack it, but if you’re in Christ, saved and being sanctified, knowing heaven is your destiny and that one day, like Cindy, like Jess, and a host of other heroes, you’ll fall into the arms of your forever Savior and King, you’ve been called. And if you love him: Steve loves Jesus with everything he is and everything he’s not.
      • The promise isn’t contingent on your perfection or how few trips you make up and down the altar. The only qualifiers are: did Jesus save you, and do you love him? Sixty years ago Steve asked Jesus to save him, cancel his sins, and adopt him; he was saved in that moment, though his life has been a mess ever since, more than he’d like to admit, and that’s true for you too.

    2. 45:32 If it isn’t good yet, God’s not done yet

      • For everyone called and everyone who loves him, God has gone on record: whatever in your life you can’t identify as good, he is going to work on it. And it gets better: not just your thing, but all things, all the way to good. You won’t just identify it as good; it will be good.
      • This is worth the price of admission: whatever in your life you can’t currently identify as good, no matter how you try, means this just isn’t the end yet. If something in your life isn’t good yet, it’s only because God’s not done yet. Let that simmer.

  12. 47:02 Prayer and affirmation

    Scripture: Romans 8:28 Proverbs 30:5

    • Steve asks the church to bow their heads and sit with this prayerfully, because many need to find hope in this promise. He urges them to invite the mind of Christ to invade their minds and let his promise transform both their minds and their tired, weary hearts and change how they see their circumstances, because God always keeps his promises.
    • To put a seal on it, he leads the church in a two-line affirmation based on Romans 8:28, still in their Bibles: in all things, not just some, God works for the good, not just perceived good, of those who love him and are called by him. Since every word of God proves true, he urges them, as Paul did, to say it by faith, calling to mind the most impossible thing they face: “If my something isn’t good yet, it’s only because God’s not done yet.” Amen and amen.
