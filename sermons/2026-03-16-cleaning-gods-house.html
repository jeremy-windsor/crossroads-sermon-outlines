Overview

  1. 00:18 Test everything. Draw out, do not pour in

    Scripture: Acts 17:11

    • George Shankool introduces himself: elder, wife Alicia, kids George, Natalia, and Jeremiah, owner of Saba’s Mediterranean in Phoenix. Used to talking to people, not down from a stage.
    • He teaches heavy in the text. Put on thinking caps. Acts 17:11: receive the word and test everything against Scripture, whether the teacher is George, Josh, Eli, a favorite pastor, or a podcaster.
    • He is approaching through a historical, premillennial lens that differs from some other teachers in the body. If you differ, still weigh it against the text.

    1. 02:17 Angry Jesus is the most abused snapshot

      • Iconic righteous anger: whip, tables, coins. We treat it as a snapshot and miss the why. Remove the 21st-century Western lens and put on a first-century Jewish one. We jump to what’s in it for me.
      • This passage is among the most abused because it is a rare moment of disruption rather than gentleness. Christians gravitate to angry Jesus when it suits an agenda, flip tables we have deemed flippable, and baptize unrighteous outbursts. If we apply before we understand context, we are following impulses, not Jesus.

    2. 04:30 Exegesis and eisegesis

      • Exegesis: draw out. Let Scripture speak. Like an archaeologist brushing dust, not burying artifacts. Eisegesis: reading in—politics, frustrations, forcing the Bible to say what we want.
      • George likens eisegesis to a scientist inserting false evidence, then names Darwin and evolution as a digression. Eisegesis is not a small study mistake. It redirects truth and turns the Word into justification for opinions. Only God’s truth changes lives. Anything else is us talking to ourselves.

  2. 07:11 Four things Jesus is highlighting

    Scripture: John 2:13-22 Psalm 69:9

    • By looking at the timing we see his heart. First, he is removing sin from his home. Second, warning Israel of coming judgment and exile. Third, clearing a path to God for those considered unclean. Fourth, pointing people to that path, the cross. Then we can apply as a body and as individuals.
    • He reads John 2:13-22. Passover at hand. Oxen, sheep, pigeons, money changers. Whip of cords, coins poured out, tables overturned. Do not make my Father’s house a house of trade. Disciples remember: zeal for your house will consume me. Jews demand a sign. Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it up. Forty-six years to build. He was speaking of his body. After the resurrection the disciples believed the Scripture and the word he had spoken.

    1. 09:26 Two cleansings, one exclamation mark

      Scripture: Matthew 21:12-16 Genesis 41:32 Daniel 9:24-27 Zechariah 9:9

      • This is one of two distinct table-flipping events. Matthew 21 is after the triumphal entry, also at Passover. Not a one-time outburst. Repetition in Scripture is an exclamation mark, like amen, amen, or Pharaoh’s doubled dream: it is sure because God ordained it.
      • John 2 at the beginning of the ministry, Matthew 21 at the end: bookends of prophetic significance. In Matthew he comes down the Mount of Olives as Messiah and King, fulfilling Daniel 9 and Zechariah 9:9. Religious leaders reject him.

  3. 11:38 Three rebellions, same pattern

    Scripture: Exodus 32:1-8

    • The Bible is one story. History is prophetic. This was not Israel’s first rejection. By two or three witnesses a matter is sure. Pattern: God calls, they rebel, exile, then restoration.
    • First rebellion: the wilderness. Saved by the lamb’s blood at the first Passover, then a golden calf while Moses is on the mountain. That generation dies outside the land; Caleb, Joshua, and the children enter. Passover when Jesus flips tables connects: he is the true lamb.
    • Second: after the land, not happily ever after. Split north and south, idolatry, Assyria then Babylon, temple destroyed on the ninth of Av. After exile, God gathers them again.

    1. 14:29 Third rebellion: they reject God in the flesh

      Scripture: Haggai 1:2-11 Genesis 3:15 Leviticus 26:40-45 Zechariah 12:10

      • Back in the land they rebuild defenses, businesses, homes. Haggai rebukes them for leaving God’s house in ruins. They rebuild the temple. Over time leaders become prideful, worship traditions, money, power. Self-righteousness is idolatry because God alone is righteous. They reject Jesus. Rome destroys the second temple on the same ninth of Av. God does not deal in coincidences.
      • George sees restoration begun in our lifetime: May 14, 1948, Israel restored to the land, homes and businesses rebuilt, house of God still in ruins. He ties post-exilic Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi to a coming physical temple that will not save—sacrifices filth beside Christ’s—but will stir hardened hearts. Nobody was ever saved differently. Genesis 3:15: grace through faith. The law exposes sin; they boasted in it.
      • Malachi’s rejected offerings; Elijah turning hearts; Leviticus 26:40-45, sin in the singular, they repent, God remembers Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Zechariah 12:10: Spirit of grace on the house of David; they look on him whom they pierced and mourn as for an only child. The singular sin: rejecting God in the flesh.

  4. 20:22 Passover: the man of the house removes leaven

    Scripture: John 1:29 Revelation 13:8

    • A Passover law: the man of the house removes leaven. Leaven pictures sin: it putrefies and puffs up. Jesus fulfills the law by casting sin from his home, making way for the cross. First Passover in Egypt: spotless lamb, blood on doorpost and lintel, the destroyer passes over.
    • John the Baptist: behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world. Revelation: the Lamb slain from before the foundation of the world. In Hebrew thought, past tense of a future event marks surety. The plan of redemption was sure before creation. Do not ask how a holy God allows suffering. Ask how a holy God created us knowing we would sin and that it would be his blood on the cross. The cross was not plan B.

    1. 23:06 He is quoting, and they knew it

      Scripture: Jeremiah 7:2 Jeremiah 7:11 Isaiah 56:4-8 Romans 11:17-21

      • Leaders had memorized Scripture. When he whips and quotes, they know the passage. John 2: do not make my Father’s house a house of trade. Matthew 21 adds: my house shall be called a house of prayer, but you make it a den of robbers. Blind and lame come and are healed. Children cry Hosanna to the Son of David. Leaders are indignant. Out of the mouth of infants you have prepared praise.
      • Matthew 21 acts out Jeremiah 7: stand at the gate of the Lord’s house; has this house become a den of robbers? I have been watching, declares the Lord. Jesus is God in the flesh, watching. Jeremiah’s charges: deal justly, do not oppress foreigner, fatherless, widow, do not shed innocent blood, do not follow other gods. He exposes injustice. Their father is the devil though their mouths say Yahweh.
      • Isaiah 56: house of prayer for all nations—eunuchs and foreigners who bind themselves to the Lord. Mercy for those leaders called unclean. Israel was to be a light to the nations and failed. Warning to the church: do not repeat it against the Jews. Romans 11: do not boast against the natural branch.

  5. 30:05 The court of the Gentiles became a market

    Scripture: Psalm 8:2

    • Passover is a pilgrimage feast. Estimates of over a million in Jerusalem. Tables stood in the court of the Gentiles, the largest area, the only place non-Jews could come. A low wall; death for a Gentile crossing. Then court of women, Israel, priests; holy place; holy of holies once a year on the Day of Atonement.
    • Money changers and animal sellers turned the only Gentile prayer space into chaos. Pilgrims bring a sacrifice; priests find a flaw, take the animal as a down payment, sell an approved one, then resell the rejected animal. Roman coins have a man’s image, so exchange into shekels with a fee. A graven image is unfit for God and fit to line hypocrites’ pockets. Extortion in God’s name. People copy their leaders: transactional religion, pride, greed.
    • Leaders are angry that children cry Hosanna, not angry at themselves. Psalm 8 is not a clever comeback. It is a messianic declaration. Disciples remember zeal for your house. Leaders demand a sign. Destroy this temple: they hear stones; he means his body. The true meeting place between God and man is standing in front of them.

  6. 37:57 The sign of Jonah, then a living temple

    Scripture: Jonah 1:1-17 Matthew 12:39-41 John 4:23-24 1 Peter 2:4-5 1 Corinthians 3:16-17

    • Why did Jonah run? Not only fear. He knew if Nineveh repented God would spare them, and that God would use Assyria to judge idolatrous Israel. He fled to prevent enemy repentance. A fish, three days, Sheol imagery, then a message to Gentiles. Jesus, three days in the heart of the earth; because of Israel’s hard heart the gospel goes to the Gentiles, even to Rome, who will also be the instrument of judgment as Babylon was. The sign of Jonah slaps the leaders: death, burial, resurrection, salvation for the unworthy, power over life and death.
    • He cleanses the house from sin and makes room for the cross, the true Passover lamb whose blood saves from the second death. Isaiah and Jeremiah warn: repeating the fathers’ sins. In 70 AD, forty years after the cross, Rome destroys the second temple on the same day Babylon destroyed the first. Jewish scribes recorded that for forty years the crimson cord on the scapegoat did not turn white on the Day of Atonement. George’s answer: they rejected and crucified Messiah.
    • How do believers worship with no physical temple? In spirit and in truth. We are the body, living stones, temple of the Holy Spirit. God is zealous for his house. Have we closed the message to people we call unclean? Treated Israel as they treated Gentiles? Built a holy checklist? The law is a mirror, not a ladder. None of us has God’s nature. It was meant to humble Jew and Gentile alike and lead us to the cross.

  7. 46:16 The royal law of love, then a testimony

    Scripture: Hebrews 7:18-19 Matthew 22:37-39 1 John 3:23 John 3:16

    • Freed from the law of sin and death. Hebrews 7: insufficient old law replaced by the law of Christ, the royal law of love. Love God; love neighbor. 1 John 3:23: we love God by placing faith in Jesus. We love neighbor because he first loved us. Faithfulness to a spouse or feeding children is love, not mere penalty-avoidance. Worldly love affirms sin. John 3:16 is self-sacrifice. Serve God and each other that way.
    • Stop asking is this a sin as a checklist. Ask: is this loving God and them? Internet comments, gossip, holding back time, talent, treasure. Freely give what you love most to whom you love most. Firstfruits, not scraps. Rules cannot change the heart. Holiness is received through the love of Christ, grace through faith, then it overflows. We love because he first loved us. He gave his best while we were enemies.

    1. 51:18 Nineteen, bullets, and a God who had other plans

      Scripture: Romans 12:1-2 1 Corinthians 11:28 Romans 3:23

      • Before faith: gangs, drugs, violence, crime, sexual immorality, money, power, greed, lust, pride. He thought he had fallen too far to balance the scales. Rock bottom: a gang member tried to murder him and a friend. Driving 35th Avenue at 125 miles an hour, bullets, friend shot in the face. At nineteen he knew he was about to die.
      • Head belief in Jesus without fruit. He did not ask forgiveness. He apologized, told God he deserved hell, and asked God to help his parents and sister past his murder. God had other plans. He is a walking testimony of forgiveness, grace, and the Spirit. Far from perfect. He walks by the Spirit to show Christ’s love because he was loved first. Gifts for God’s glory, never his own.
      • Examine hearts. Repent hidden sin. Release unforgiveness. Recommit, or come to faith now. You are not promised tomorrow. Living sacrifice, not conforming to the world. Ask Jesus to cleanse leaven from the gates. Stop honoring God with lips only. Ambassadors, not sons of the devil. This is the warning and the call.
      • Examine yourselves before communion. 1 Corinthians 11. The ground is level at the cross. All have sinned. George prays for humility, repentance, reverence, eyes for the lost as image-bearers, and that everything would honor Jesus’ name. Amen.
