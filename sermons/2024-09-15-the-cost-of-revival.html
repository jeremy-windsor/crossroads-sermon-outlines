Overview

  1. 00:04 Week five: the kingdom on the move meets resistance

    • Welcome to week five of the six-week series Unstoppable. Surveying the early church, we have seen the boldness of the apostles, the bravery of martyrs like Stephen, and the gospel taking hold in the hearts and minds of thousands. The kingdom of God is on the move and taking ground, and fast.
    • As last week’s message on Stephen showed, when the kingdom takes ground it is almost always met with resistance. Acts describes not just the amazing works of the Spirit but the profound hardship faced by those who took the gospel to the nations. True, Spirit-filled revival makes the enemy work overtime: faithful men and women see the fruit of the Spirit’s work, and they also face extraordinary discouragement and hostility for simply pursuing the mission our Lord gave us.

  2. 01:11 What revival is, and what it isn’t

    Scripture: John 16:33

    • There is a lot of talk about revival in the church. When revival is real, and not a hyper-emotional stage show with Benny Hinn swinging his Armani coat around slaying people in the Spirit, it is a beautiful thing. Elijah points to the First Great Awakening, when Jonathan Edwards and George Whitefield preached repentance and grace into an increasingly secular, rationalistic culture and re-energized the faith in the American colonies.
    • But for the most part revival has become sensationalized, measured by how much people get the touchy-feelies rather than whether people are being raised to spiritual life by the Holy Spirit. Churches plan revival services, not realizing revival is not up to man. Read Acts as a whole and you see that revival comes at a cost, and that it always falls under the providence of God, not man’s plans and desires.
    • Jesus warned his disciples about the hardship they would face. Pastor Steve encouraged the church last week with the verse Elijah wants to set the tone: John 16:33, “In this world you will have trouble, but take heart. I have overcome the world.”

  3. 02:58 Acts 16 and the cost of pursuing revival

    Scripture: Acts 16

    • Today’s text is Acts 16. Elijah’s hope is that through this chapter and the ones around it the church would understand the cost of pursuing revival, and learn to take joy in the Lord’s plan for salvation instead of becoming preoccupied with what we think the Spirit’s plan should be, because very rarely does God work the way we think he should.
    • He prays that God would open hearts and minds to his word, help them not bump up against it, and if they do, submit to it, giving thanks that God revealed himself through Jesus Christ, who lived the life we couldn’t and died the death we deserved.

  4. 04:02 Skipping chapters: equipped to feed yourself

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:11–12

    • “Eli, aren’t we skipping a lot of chapters?” Yes: Acts has 28 chapters and the series has six weeks. It was never meant to be exhaustive, and if you rely on Sundays for all your Bible reading, you are missing the point.
    • The teaching and pastoral teams believe their job is to equip the church for the work of ministry. Ephesians 4:11 says Christ gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, and teachers to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ. If you wait to be spoon-fed, you will starve. Give a man a fish and you feed him for a day; teach a man to fish and you feed him for a lifetime.
    • No one should feel left behind. If you struggle with what time with God looks like outside church, talk to any of the pastors; they are here through the week. They would much rather talk about being intentional with the Lord than read brother so-and-so’s latest end-times predictions, which Elijah just deletes at this point.

  5. 06:02 The story so far: Acts 7–15

    • So as not to miss what God did in the nine chapters between last week’s passage and this one, Elijah does a quick flyby of the story so far.

    1. 06:20 Acts 7–9: Stephen, the scattering, and Saul

      Scripture: Acts 7 Acts 8 Acts 9

      • Acts 7: Stephen goes before the Sanhedrin and is stoned for his unapologetic faith, the first early church martyr. Acts 8: Saul of Tarsus leads the persecution, the church scatters, and Philip takes the gospel to Samaria with signs, wonders, and deliverance from evil spirits; the stories of Simon the sorcerer and the Ethiopian eunuch further the bringing in of the Gentiles, a big point Acts makes: salvation is now of the Jew and the Gentile. Homework if you haven’t read it.
      • Acts 9: Saul is converted on the road to Damascus and called by Christ himself to ministry, which shows that God makes his enemies into his friends, good news for all of us. Debating the Greek Jews in Damascus, he becomes their next target; the persecutor becomes the persecuted. Then come the healing of Aeneas and the raising of Tabitha, or Dorcas in Greek. “No, I didn’t call you a funny name.”

    2. 07:56 Acts 10–15: the Gentiles brought in

      Scripture: Acts 10 Acts 10:15 Acts 11 Acts 11:18 Acts 12 Acts 13–14 Acts 15

      • Acts 10: God uses visions to show Peter and Cornelius that the Levitical ceremonial law is fulfilled in Christ: “Do not call anything unclean that which God has called clean.” Christ is the perfect Passover lamb, so there is no need for ceremonial law. Theologians call this type and antitype: an event or person that points to a greater fulfillment. As God delivered Israel from literal bondage in the Exodus, Jesus delivers us from spiritual bondage; many call Jesus the greater Moses.
      • Acts 11: Peter explains his vision and the people rejoice, “So then, even to Gentiles God has granted repentance that leads to life,” and Saul and Barnabas preach in Antioch. Acts 12: Peter is arrested and broken out of prison by an angel, much to King Herod’s dismay, and Herod, worshiped as a god, is struck dead and eaten by worms. Yes, this is the New Testament.
      • Acts 13 and 14 are mostly missionary work. In Acts 15 Paul and Barnabas defend salvation by grace alone against the party of the Pharisees, and a letter goes to the Gentile churches with Judas and Silas. Later a sharp disagreement parts Paul and Barnabas, and Barnabas fades off the pages of Scripture.

  6. 10:07 Chapter 16: Paul goes back to Lystra

    Scripture: Acts 16:1–2 Acts 14

    • Chapter 16 begins Paul’s second missionary journey. In Derbe and Lystra he meets a disciple named Timothy, son of a believing Jewish woman and a Greek father, well spoken of by the brothers in Lystra and Iconium. These were churches Paul planted about five years earlier.
    • Acts 14 tells us Derbe was a massive success on Paul’s first journey, but Lystra was another story. The people first worshiped Paul and Barnabas as gods, calling Paul Hermes and Barnabas Zeus and preparing sacrifices. While the apostles did damage control, Jewish leaders from Antioch and Iconium persuaded the crowd they were blasphemers worthy of stoning. From Zeus and Hermes to blasphemers.

    1. 11:29 Evil isn’t threatened by what isn’t true

      • Notice the religious leaders had no problem with people worshiping false gods like Zeus and Hermes, because evil isn’t threatened by what isn’t true. They were concerned with their earthly status, not their heavenly destination. The enemy is just fine with us distracting ourselves with idols: false gods keep people in chains, while the one true God offers freedom and good news to the captive. Something to think about as we interpret the world around us.

    2. 12:05 A boldness to finish what he started

      Scripture: Acts 16:3–5

      • Most of us would be hard pressed to go back to a place like that. But Paul was given a boldness that only comes from God, a boldness to finish what he started, and ignoring the threat to his own well-being he heads back to Lystra, where he meets his famous protégé, Timothy.
      • Paul circumcised Timothy because of the Jews in that area. Elijah clarifies: this was for ease of evangelism, not a salvation thing; Timothy didn’t need it, but it removed a barrier. As they delivered the Jerusalem decisions from town to town, the churches were strengthened in the faith and grew daily in numbers.
      • Paul likely had some trepidation; you couldn’t pay Elijah to go back somewhere like that. But without the gumption to return to a hostile place, Paul may never have met Timothy, who became an absolute warrior for the gospel. The providence of God is an amazing thing: he used imperfect men and messy situations to accomplish his perfect purpose, and he is in the same business today.

  7. 13:56 Verse 6: kept by the Spirit

    Scripture: Acts 16:6–10

    • Paul and his companions were kept by the Holy Spirit from preaching in the province of Asia, the Spirit of Jesus would not let them enter Bithynia, and they went down to Troas, where Paul saw a vision of a man of Macedonia begging, “Come over to Macedonia and help us.”
    • Why would God forbid the gospel being preached? Simply timing. There was nothing wrong with Paul’s desire; God had another, better plan. We aren’t told how God said no, but Paul heard loud and clear that Ephesus would come later, not now. And it did: Acts records a visit, and we have the letter to the Ephesians.

    1. 15:25 When God slams the door

      • This isn’t just a history lesson. Many times we feel God leading one way only for him to close the door: a career path, a relationship, a big life-changing decision. We run through what feels like a wide-open door only to have it slammed in our face.

    2. 15:57 Berklee, and running from the call

      Scripture: Proverbs 3:5–6

      • From 13 to 18 Elijah played guitar three to six hours a day until his fingers hurt, not to be a rock star but to get into Berklee College of Music, one of the most prestigious music schools in the world, with about a 30 percent acceptance rate at the time. He did the work, the practice, the grades, the gigging, the audition prep, and he got in.
      • Soon after, it became clear he was pursuing that life to run from what God was so clearly calling him into. He wishes he could say he piously laid down his will, but that would be a big fat lie: he knew God was calling him into ministry and wanted zero part of it. Church people made him queasy. “Sometimes y’all still do.” He is kidding, mostly.
      • Was it hard to lay down a dream he worked for tirelessly? Absolutely. Did God honor his decision to submit? 100 percent. Had he gone to Boston for four years he wouldn’t be here, and might not have the life he has: work he considers a privilege, a wife wildly out of his league he looks forward to coming home to, a beautiful home, two crazy dogs, and a bearded dragon.
      • Dropping out of college for ministry wasn’t his plan, but he wouldn’t do it differently, because seeing God’s faithfulness through his stupidity, rebellion, and running strengthens his faith to this day. God’s plan is always better than his: “Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways submit to him, and he will make your paths straight.”

    3. 18:55 Guiding by hindrance

      • The Holy Spirit often guides us as much by closing doors as by opening them. Pastor David Guzik calls this guiding by hindrance: God often tells us where to go by telling us where not to go.
      • Troas was at best third on Paul’s list, but his circumstances made clear it was exactly where the Lord was leading. Unlike Elijah, Paul was beautifully responsive to the Spirit’s direction and willing to lay down his plans, and we are about to see why.

  8. 19:39 Verse 9: the Macedonian call

    Scripture: Acts 16:9–10

    • The vision took Paul and his crew from Asia Minor to the continent of Europe, the very first gospel endeavor into Europe. You and I are beneficiaries of Paul’s second missionary journey. Paul’s team set out to reach a few major cities in their region, and God gave them an entire continent to win for Christ.

    1. 20:27 The switch in pronouns

      • Did you notice the switch in pronouns? Luke goes from “they” to “we,” the only time it’s okay to change the pronouns. In Troas Paul picks up Luke. So not only does Paul meet Timothy; God’s redirection of his journey is the reason we have a Gospel of Luke and a Book of Acts. God was growing his church and putting in place the events for us to have the canon of Scripture.

    2. 21:12 Leadership submitted to the Lord

      Scripture: James 3:1

      • Elijah admires Paul’s immediate submission to the call. Paul didn’t hesitate, and his team followed suit: when leadership is submitted to the Lord, it influences those being led to do the same.
      • James 3:1 says not many should become teachers, because we who teach will be judged more strictly, which makes Elijah’s palms sweat. Leaders face strong expectations because their influence can be empowering or incredibly damaging to those the Lord entrusts to them. This was a strong, godly man leading a strong, godly team willing to go where the Lord called.

    3. 22:05 A side note: hands and feet, not country clubs

      • Elijah thinks strong leadership is incredibly lacking in the American evangelical church. In Crossroads Cares meetings on Mondays he hears about struggling single mothers who gave up reaching out to their megachurch leadership because it was too busy planting another satellite campus or releasing its next worship album. All that influence and money, and a person in need can’t wake the sleeping giant, so they come to the little church of 400 to see if it will listen.
      • Crossroads is always happy to help whoever God sends, but it breaks his heart. If a church’s mission doesn’t involve consistently being the hands and feet of Jesus, it doesn’t matter how many campuses it plants, albums it releases, or conferences it hosts: it is failing. If every church cared for the people God entrusted to it instead of creating a country club atmosphere, things would look different.
      • The church doesn’t need more generic, repetitive CCM K-LOVE music. It needs men and women on fire for Christ, willing to go where the Lord calls and radically love those living on the margins. Rant over.

  9. 23:46 Philippi and Lydia

    Scripture: Acts 16:13–15

    • Paul and company reach Philippi, a hub of the European world considered as prestigious as Rome itself, founded by Philip of Macedon, father of Alexander the Great. Here is the first European convert, Lydia, a dealer in purple cloth from Thyatira and a worshiper of God. The Lord opened her heart to respond to Paul’s message; she and her household were baptized, and she persuaded them to stay at her house.

    1. 24:50 The Lord opened her heart

      • What stuck out to Elijah is the phrase “The Lord opened her heart to respond to Paul’s message.” A necessary element of evangelism is asking God in prayer to open the hearts of the people we talk with. You can have the moral, cosmological, and teleological arguments memorized, or the most tear-jerking testimony ever; no apologetic prowess or emotional pull will open a heart of stone. Only God can.
      • Accepting this makes evangelism so much easier. Salvation belongs to the Lord, not to our communication abilities. Better to make the attempt and fall flat on your face than to let it pass and regret it later.

    2. 25:48 A stone in his shoe

      • If you can put a stone in someone’s shoe to get them thinking about the gospel, you have done great work. Elijah reads Greg Koukl of Stand to Reason: there is pressure to close the sale fast, but a wise ambassador seasons his words. Sometimes the fruit is ripe and falls into your basket; usually it isn’t, and dropping a message that seems meaningless or unbelievable can make someone harder to reach. Koukl’s modest goal is to put a stone in his shoe, a nugget of truth that annoys him in a good way and keeps poking at him.
      • Everyone wants to be a harvester, but the church needs more gardeners: people less concerned with the harvest and more with planting, watering, and weeding until the fruit is ripe. Elijah is convinced it is a patience issue. Before talking with someone, ask, “Lord, give me one thing I can say, one question I can ask, one thought I can leave that will get them to think about the grace and mercy of the cross.” You have no idea how God will use that stone in their shoe.

  10. 28:14 Verse 16: the slave girl and the beating

    Scripture: Acts 16:16–24

    • Right after this evangelistic success comes heavy hostility. A slave girl with a spirit of fortune telling, who earned her owners a great deal of money, followed them for days shouting, “These men are servants of the Most High God.” Finally Paul became so annoyed (“Can’t you just hear him?”) that he commanded the spirit in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.
    • Her owners, seeing their hope of money gone, dragged Paul and Silas before the magistrates as Jews throwing the city into an uproar. The crowd joined the attack; they were stripped, beaten with rods, severely flogged, thrown into the inner cell, and fastened in the stocks.

  11. 29:37 This is the cost of revival

    • Bringing it full circle: this is what Elijah meant by revival coming at a cost. Steve did a fantastic job last week explaining what actual persecution is and how Americans really don’t face any. Your discomfort or outrage does not equal persecution; if it did, everyone would be persecuted, since being outraged is in style. Many Christians say they hate that mentality, then adopt it every time the world acts like the world.
    • For us the cost of true revival looks more like being labeled extremists or zealots, or in more intense cases, the powers that be trying to shut down gatherings to prevent the spread of the gospel. That is real oppression, and he isn’t downplaying it.

    1. 30:40 Brothers and sisters abroad

      • Many brothers and sisters internationally face much more dire threats: beating, imprisonment, even death. We won’t drive our air-conditioned cars to church if it takes more than 20 minutes, yet churches in China, Afghanistan, India, and Ethiopia travel for hours, sometimes on foot, to meet in secret and sing to the God who calls his children by name, knowing the risk.
      • The church is growing most where persecution is extreme, while American attendance goes down the drain in one of the easiest places to be a Christian. If that doesn’t make you uncomfortable, it should: if you won’t stand for truth when things are easy, what makes you think you will when things get real? We should stop the fake platitudes and going through the motions and take notes from our faithful brothers and sisters abroad, so we are prepared if things get worse here.

  12. 32:06 Verse 25: praying and singing at midnight

    Scripture: Acts 16:25

    • Paul and Silas give a wonderful example. About midnight, freshly beaten and imprisoned for living out their faith, they don’t grumble, whine, or shake their fist at heaven; they pray and sing hymns to God.
    • What is your first response when things get real: anger at God, or praise? Elijah is preaching to himself; he whines way too much. Our mindset would shift if our first response were to praise God for what he has already done rather than be angry about what we wish he would do.

    1. 33:13 Singing is no small thing

      • Singing praise is a necessary part of the Christian walk, because it helps us recall God’s promises and the mighty works he has already done. God gives 50 direct commands to sing praises to his name, and singing is mentioned over 400 times in the Bible. If you think it isn’t important, you are just wrong.

    2. 33:41 Being real about corporate worship

      • Elijah and the worship team don’t get up each week to showcase their gifting or put on a show, but to bring glory to God and remind the church what the Lord has done, is doing, and promises to do. If you intentionally arrive after worship or leave before the last two songs to chat in the lobby or beat parking lot traffic (not the family that ran late because the kids were crazy), you deprive yourself of being uplifted by the truth you sing, and you deprive the Lord of something he asked us directly to do. If that offends you, you are probably the one who needs to hear it.
      • The God of the universe took on flesh and gave up his life so we could know him, and we can’t lift up a song of praise for 20 minutes out of a 75-minute service? There is time set aside for fellowship. Let’s be a church that fills the room with the singing of the saints, unashamed and rowdy in our praise, treating that time like the life source it is.
      • Maybe that’s on him for not making it clear that worship is a priority; he is making it clear now. Just as it is important to be fed by the word, it is important to lift a song of praise with fellow believers, even in a smaller group. Paul and Silas understood this, singing with joy in the darkest of circumstances.

  13. 36:02 The world is watching: the jailer believes

    Scripture: Acts 16:25–34

    • Singing is important for our edification and a necessary part of our witness, because the world is watching. The other prisoners were listening. A violent earthquake shook the prison, the doors flew open, and everyone’s chains came loose. The jailer drew his sword to kill himself, but Paul shouted, “Don’t harm yourself. We are all here.” Trembling, he asked, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?” “Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household.” He washed their wounds, he and his household were baptized, and he was filled with joy. Hallelujah.

    1. 37:16 Why the jailer believed

      • Nothing in Scripture says so directly, but Elijah thinks the Roman guard was more convicted by the love, grace, and shared salvation shown by Paul and Silas than he was freaked out by the earthquake. They held fast to the joy of the Lord in prison and showed immense concern for the soul of a guard who likely beat them hours earlier.
      • When everything pointed to another miraculous jailbreak, they stayed for the sake of one soul, seeing a chance for him and his household to come to saving faith. God honored their discernment, and the guard and his whole family were brought to faith in Christ.

  14. 38:05 How the world sees you live out your faith

    Scripture: Philippians 1:21

    • How the world sees you live out your faith really matters, because non-believers typically aren’t reading the Bible, but they are watching how Christians behave. When the body of Christ cares and prays for those who curse us, sings with one voice to the God of creation, and loves those society deems unlovable, the world can’t help but take notice.
    • So stop being ashamed of the message you carry. Elijah prays the church comes eager to give back to the Lord through time, attention, and praise, and leaves eager to take the message of repentance, grace, and hope to the people around them. If forced to choose between living out our faith and real suffering, remember Paul’s words: “For me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain.”
    • We carry the life-giving gospel, and the Spirit of God within us drives us toward Christlikeness and bears witness to his grace and mercy. Don’t become numb to it or fall into complacency and lukewarmness; live for God’s glory and never miss an opportunity to lift up a shout of praise.

  15. 39:41 Don’t be afraid of the cost of revival

    • Most of all, don’t be afraid of the cost of revival. Real, faithful, worshipful Christianity demands that we follow Christ’s example of self-sacrifice, and it will be hard. But every day you take up your cross and follow him with everything you have will be worth it, because the payoff far outweighs the cost.

  16. 40:06 Closing prayer

    • Elijah prays that the church would take these words to heart and lift up songs of praise unashamed, not worried about how they look or sound, as their only response to what God has done. As they come to communion, he asks that they lay their doubts, anxieties, fears, and trials at the cross, trusting that God is Lord over the hills and the valleys.
    • He prays the Spirit would work in their hearts through the week, so this would not be going through the motions or pure religion, but an exercise in love: for God, for his church, and for those who have yet to find him.
