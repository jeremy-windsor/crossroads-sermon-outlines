Overview

  1. 00:07 The final week: Jesus is yes to every promise

    Scripture: Genesis 50 2 Corinthians 1:20 Romans 1:16

    • It is the final week of the series in Genesis, picking up in chapter 50. Josh hopes the church has seen that God has been working a plan to save their souls for generations, and that Jesus isn’t Plan B; he was the plan from the beginning and is yes to every promise.
    • Paul proclaimed this to the people in Corinth: no matter how many promises God has made, they are yes in Christ, and through him the amen is spoken to the glory of God. We never grow out of proclaiming that the work is done. We aren’t ashamed or bored of the gospel, because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes. That complicated Greek word “everyone” means all, every kind: everyone.

  2. 01:36 Those who ignore God and those who take him at his word

    Scripture: Luke 22:42

    • Josh prays the church would see the difference between how God handles those who ignore him and those who take him at his word. Many have made an art form of ignoring God, questioning, challenging, and doubting at every chance.
    • Believers don’t have an answer for everything; Josh has a lot of questions. The difference is that he doesn’t need the answers, because the fewer he has, the more he places his life in God’s hands: “I don’t know, Lord, but I love you.” Moses had questions, and God didn’t seem to like that very much; Josh prefers the Abraham method: just believe and go. We seek to look like Jesus in Gethsemane: not my will, but your will be done.

  3. 02:50 Pray for God to humble you

    Scripture: 2 Samuel 7:18

    • Many church members ask Josh about their prayer life. The truth is many prayer lives lack depth because few ask God to work on them. People love asking God to work on their behalf, but when did you last ask him to put you to work on his behalf? If you want an amazing prayer life, ask God to humble you: “I double dog dare you.”
    • Some of Josh’s favorite men of God prayed about what God had already done rather than asking for more. King David sat before the Lord and said, “Who am I, Lord God, and what is my family, that you have brought me this far?”

    1. 04:16 “I’m just looking to go deeper”

      Scripture: 1 Peter 5:6

      • One phrase grinds Josh’s gears: “I’m just looking to go deeper.” Do you want to dig a hole? Is there something greater than God sending his Son to die for you? What is deeper than daily reflection on the yes to God’s promises, or humbling yourself to obey the one who died to give you everlasting life? If you mean a deeper desire to know him, great, but Josh doesn’t think that is what people mean.
      • Nothing in the New Testament teaches that knowledge brings a greater relationship with God; ask the man crucified next to Jesus. Humility, not a well-articulated thesis on the sovereignty of God, is the key to intimacy with God. Peter says, “Humble yourselves, therefore, under God’s mighty hand.” The humbled Christian has hit bedrock; you don’t get deeper.
      • Stop treating God like a genie in a bottle and ask him to humble you, to teach you to do his will rather than grow more comfortable with yours.

  4. 06:10 Why do bad things happen to good people?

    Scripture: Matthew 19:17

    • If humility is good, then when God humbles us through trial or suffering, that must be good too, which answers the question people use to stump Christians: why do bad things happen to good people?
    • Many Christians reply, “No one is good but one.” Thank you, Captain Obvious; Jesus does say in Matthew 19 that there is only one who is good. But people asking aren’t questioning their own goodness; they are questioning God’s. Quoting that doesn’t answer them. Show them proof that God uses bad things to do good things.

    1. 06:59 Providence, not just miracles

      • They are asking about a theme known as God’s providence. You won’t find the word in Genesis 50, but it is an undeniable theme throughout Scripture.
      • A miracle is an act outside the bounds of natural order, like Jesus walking on water. Josh’s son keeps saying he can run across the water and sinks like a boulder every time; God could let him, though Josh doesn’t see why he would.
      • Providence is the power of God overseeing his creation and working out his plans within the laws he set. Despite Satan’s efforts to derail the plan, God works through both our good and our bad intentions to bring about his intentions. Josh thinks that is more amazing than miracles.

  5. 08:47 Enter Joseph

    Scripture: Genesis 50:15 Genesis 37 Genesis 39

    • Joseph is awesome. God uses nine words to tell us who created the world, but Joseph gets 13 chapters, 25 percent of Genesis. Today Josh narrows to one moment in Genesis 50:15, but first catches the church up.
    • In Genesis 37, teenage Joseph dreamed his brothers bowed to him; jealous, they planned to murder him but sold him instead and told Jacob he was dead, a family lie that lasted 22 years. In chapter 39 Potiphar bought him. Joseph was a stud (“unlike me, he probably had hair”), Potiphar’s wife wanted him, and he ran. Quick lesson on handling sexual sin biblically: men, run.
    • Falsely accused of rape, Joseph was imprisoned, interpreted Pharaoh’s dream, became second in command of Egypt, and saved millions of lives through a seven-year famine. You’d be hard pressed to find a better man in the Old Testament.

    1. 10:46 Reconciliation and Jacob’s death

      Scripture: Genesis 45 Genesis 49

      • In chapter 45 Joseph reconciles with his brothers and finally sees his dad after 22 years; with all the weeping and kissing, “they went through at least 15 boxes of tissues.” The family moves to Egypt and Joseph cares for them. In chapter 49 Jacob blesses his twelve sons, the twelve tribes, and dies at 147.
      • The question hanging over chapter 50: Jacob is gone and Joseph is a high-ranking official. How will he handle the other eleven now that their father is dead?

  6. 11:42 “What if Joseph holds a grudge?”

    Scripture: Genesis 50:15

    • Verse 15: when Joseph’s brothers saw their father was dead, they said, “What if Joseph holds a grudge against us?” First things first: God’s providence is good; yours is not. Relationships suffer when you deal in what-ifs.
    • Henry Winkler, the Fonz, said assumptions are the termites of relationships. When you assume, you wrongfully present yourself as omniscient. How prideful: how could you know someone’s reaction or intentions?

    1. 12:26 A little texting exercise

      • It has gotten worse as texting became our main communication. Josh shows sample text conversations and asks who thinks the person really wants to get coffee; the room splits between optimists and skeptics. Texts lack tone and nuance, and, for those under 30, emojis don’t solve it: “Is that thing crying or laughing? It looks miserable.”

    2. 14:00 Two mistakes and a fabricated message

      Scripture: Genesis 50:16 Genesis 50:17

      • The brothers make two mistakes: they assume Joseph was dishonest for two decades instead of honest, and instead of talking face to face they send the Egyptian equivalent of a text: “Your father left these instructions before he died.”
      • Eleven brothers develop the plan around a conference table. They don’t pray, and they don’t go to Joseph and let him smash their assumptions. Josh imagines the text: “Hi Joseph, we forgot to tell you Daddy said on his deathbed he wants you to take good care of us. We know we sold you, and that’s messed up, but Dad’s dying wish was that you let that go.” Verse 17: “I ask you to forgive your brothers the sins and the wrongs they committed.”
      • Josh pictures them huddled around the iPad: Reuben the firstborn should know better, Simeon and Levi “have so much self-control,” and is Judah, Dan, Asher, or Benjamin going to speak up?

  7. 15:55 Joseph wept

    Scripture: Genesis 50:17

    • When the message came, Joseph wept. Josh counts seven scenes of Joseph weeping in Genesis, unsurprising given the rejection he faced: hated by his brothers, sold, jailed, lied about, forgotten by his cellmates.

    1. 16:23 Manly men do cry

      Scripture: John 11:35

      • You are free to disagree, but Josh thinks manly men do cry. Everyone points out the unfair constraints culture puts on women, but what about those on men: be tough, don’t be emotional? What is strong about bottling up emotions? Crying is a sign of healing too, and men do themselves a disservice holding it in out of concern for how they will be viewed. Jesus wept. Joseph wept.
      • Godly men in Scripture weep not only over their own sin but over the sins of others.

    2. 17:23 “You insist that I’m like you”

      Scripture: Genesis 49

      • Joseph weeps because the message is a lie. He was there in chapter 49; he heard his father’s blessing and watched him take his final breath. The brothers are saying, “We don’t trust you. No one is that merciful. We’re on to you.”
      • Josh imagines Joseph replaying it: you sold me, I was separated from my family 22 years and never said goodbye to Mom, but I forgave you, fed you, and cared for you, and you think I’ve been working a plan of revenge? “You insist that I’m like you, but I’m not.” Joseph’s heart is broken.

    3. 18:45 Afraid others are like you

      • It is fine to build boundaries with someone who has repeatedly proven untrustworthy. But if someone has a track record of trustworthiness and you still don’t trust them, look in the mirror: maybe you are afraid they are like you. Just because you aren’t trustworthy doesn’t mean no one is. The brothers assume Joseph’s default setting is theirs.

  8. 19:29 Overwhelming guilt

    Scripture: Psalm 38:4–5

    • Guilt is overwhelming; the wounds sin inflicts on others and ourselves can get out of control if we don’t forgive and forget. The psalmist writes in chapter 38, “My guilt has overwhelmed me like a burden too heavy to bear. My wounds fester and are loathsome because of my sinful folly.”
    • A guilty conscience needs no accuser; guilt is its own accuser, and no affliction is greater. The natural response to its unbearable weight is to drag others down: misery loves company. Shakespeare said suspicion always haunts the guilty mind. After 40 years, Joseph forgave them, but they never forgave themselves.

    1. 20:42 Let them sit with it

      • Watch and learn: Joseph doesn’t say a word; he just weeps. When people carrying unresolved guilt project it onto you, sometimes the best thing you can do is let them sit with it.

  9. 20:58 The dream fulfilled: what would you do?

    Scripture: Genesis 50:18 Genesis 37

    • The brothers threw themselves down before him: “We are your slaves.” Remember the teenage dream of his brothers falling at his feet, which they swore would never happen; they wouldn’t have to bow to someone else’s slave. Now the ones who sold him as a slave have become the slaves and fell at his feet, just like the dream in chapter 37.
    • What will Joseph do? Josh asks for an honest answer, not a church answer. Think of the person who caused you the most pain, who manipulated it back onto you, who made you pay for doing right. If your arch-nemesis lay at your feet, would you talk trash (“How’s the weather down there, peasant?”), or, for passive-aggressive Christians, take mental pictures to scrapbook their walk of shame? Would you go in for the kill or be kind?

  10. 23:06 “Don’t be afraid. Am I in the place of God?”

    Scripture: Genesis 50:19

    • Joseph said, “Don’t be afraid. Am I in the place of God?” The brothers can’t see anything through their guilt; they assume the worst because they did the worst. Ask people who make dangerous assumptions: what if you’re wrong? Have you asked them? Joseph doesn’t hate them or want his pound of flesh; he forgave them five chapters ago.
    • Guilt is dangerous if you don’t deal with it. Like rust, it may not seem a problem at first, but left alone it eventually destroys anything good about the metal.

    1. 24:23 Get up: how do you respond to praise?

      • First, Joseph says, “Get up.” Go fall at the feet of God and worship him, but not me; I’m not worthy.
      • How do you respond to praise? Josh confesses it is a real temptation in his job. “Great sermon today, Josh” can get “I killed it today; how blessed are you,” or “Thanks for encouraging me, I needed that, but God wrote the letter; I’m just dropping it off at your front door.”
      • Josh thinks culture has figured out that fame and fortune aren’t the end game; the happiest people aren’t rich and famous. He thinks Taylor Swift isn’t happy, and says just because people want to hear what you have to say doesn’t mean you have something worth saying. How hard must it be to humble yourself before God when your fan base thinks you are a god? Joseph knows there is only one worthy of praise.

    2. 26:52 Not the one to judge

      Scripture: John 5:22

      • Joseph also makes clear he isn’t the one to judge them. Judgment needn’t confuse the church: we are supposed to make right judgments, but we can’t always see the heart, so ours are sometimes wrong and never final. John 5 says the Father has entrusted all judgment to the Son. That is final judgment; until then we are called to judge.
      • Josh is grated by “God knows my heart” from someone who just finished cussing you out. True, God does know the heart. But we have the Holy Spirit to convict of sin and righteousness and the Scriptures to train us, so we can see the fruit, or the stink fruit, in someone’s life and get a pretty good idea of their heart. God alone judges with pinpoint accuracy, but that doesn’t mean we shouldn’t call out wrongs, and Joseph calls his brothers out.

    3. 28:52 Pointing others to God

      • We must understand where our work ends and God’s begins, and Joseph does it beautifully: you intended to harm me and did something evil, and now you put words in our father’s mouth and fall at my feet when you should be falling at God’s. Only God can judge your heart; run to him and get right with God.
      • Instead of rehearsing his hurt, Joseph points them to God. The believer grows stronger as he points others to God; if you want to grow deeper, do that more. The brothers have a distorted view of God. When people sin against you it is hard not to take it personally, but the bigger issue is between them and God.

  11. 30:13 Joseph’s theology of suffering

    Scripture: Genesis 50:20

    • Joseph lays out some of the most important words in the Bible, his theology of suffering, injustice, evil, and hardship: “You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good to accomplish what is now being done, the saving of many lives.”
    • Joseph’s life was marked by rejection and suffering, and many can relate. Josh asks who is in a tough season to raise a hand: “If you can’t do it here, where can you?” Maybe life is just hard, you feel discouraged and depressed, some even with thoughts of suicide. Joseph knows your pain.

    1. 31:16 Calling evil what it is

      Scripture: Matthew 16:23

      • Joseph calls them out: this wasn’t careless words, harsh feedback, or a joke taken too far. It was a deliberate, premeditated effort to hurt and humiliate him.
      • Josh confesses he needs to grow here. He is a “gentle giant,” scary outside and a teddy bear inside. Shortly after he became lead pastor, an elder, Bob, told him, “Josh, you need to toughen up,” and he was right. Josh has been cussed out and just said, “I hope you have a nice day.” That isn’t the loving thing; we need to call evil evil. How can he point people to God if he doesn’t first point to the evil in them?

    2. 33:10 But God

      • You did this, but God did this. God uses bad things to do great things; no matter what people do, God is bigger and better and can bring beauty out of the ugliest situations. This is the source of hope for all believers, and every chance he gets, Joseph points his brothers to God; that is why his faith is so strong.
      • You thought you were in control and could oppose God’s plans, but God was there when you huddled to throw me in a pit and when you huddled around the iPad to invent a lie about Father. Instead of continuing in evil, trust the one who can turn it for good. Potiphar’s house prepared him to lead Egypt through famine; prison put him where Pharaoh would need him; God placed him to serve a nation as part of the promise to Abraham.

  12. 35:02 The divine eraser

    • Think of your life as a timeline. If God gave you a divine eraser that could reach into your past, are there parts you would erase? Before you go nuts with it: if you erase that moment, what ministry opportunities get removed with it? Who have you uniquely impacted, and who wouldn’t know Jesus, if not for your testimony?
    • Ministry comes out of pain, brokenness, and suffering, and the pain is what makes your testimony powerful. Some want to erase a first marriage: he or she intended to harm you, but God intends it for good; who can you help? Some want to erase health problems: that disease intends to harm you, but God intends it for good; who in that waiting room needs to hear about your hope? Some want to erase a childhood with an evil father who hit, hurt, and took advantage of them: God intends it for good.
    • When the Lord heals our brokenness we may have scars, but we come back stronger, and from that strength we preach and his church is fueled. He gives ministries, platforms, and microphones to tell how he turned evil to good. If you are struggling to find purpose in the church, don’t erase that moment; it is the key. God will use your suffering to help someone else.

    1. 38:09 Comfort we ourselves receive

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 1:3–4

      • Paul says to praise the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our troubles so that we can comfort those in any trouble with the comfort we ourselves receive from God.
      • Joseph takes this mindset; he wouldn’t change a thing, because he gets to see the saving of many lives. Have you seen a soul turned heavenbound? Praise God.

  13. 38:57 Our God is big

    • The problem is that people’s view of God is too small. Our God is big. Why did God put Joseph through all that? He didn’t; his brothers did an evil thing, and God turned it for good. God knew they would do everything to avoid bowing at their brother’s feet, yet he accomplished it anyway. They showed their intentions, and God showed he is sovereign.
    • God is weaving together a masterpiece while we run around with our little brushes saying, “Look at my picture.” He doesn’t need you to be the artist; he is the artist. That is what clicked for Joseph: God’s intentions are greater than theirs.

    1. 40:22 Do you trust him to work it for good?

      • Do you believe God will work it out for good, even if it takes 40 years, even if you never see it? Josh imagines Joseph wrestled with God in the carriage to Egypt and had doubts in jail, but he kept walking by faith. In prison he didn’t know what God was doing; in chapter 50 he knew exactly what it was for, and he didn’t waste it.

  14. 41:13 Joseph speaks kindly

    Scripture: Genesis 50:21

    • “So then, don’t be afraid. I will provide for you and your children.” Joseph reassures his brothers, speaks kindly, and restores them gently: God has changed my heart; I don’t want revenge; I just want to love you like God has loved me.
    • Josh asks the church to check their hearts: How are you doing loving and forgiving those who wronged you? Are you blessing, serving, and meeting the needs of others? Are you the great avenger, or do you trust God to use it for good?

  15. 42:06 The whole Bible points to Jesus

    Scripture: Hebrews 9:28

    • Genesis is only one book. The Bible is 66 books by 40 authors on three continents in three languages over nearly 2,000 years, and it all points to one person: Jesus.
    • The story continues: God’s people grow from about 60 to a nation of two to three million, because he said he would. Moses meets the burning bush, leads the people out of Egypt, and receives the Ten Commandments; Joshua leads them into the promised land, where they bury Joseph; judges and kings come and go; Isaiah and John the Baptist say Jesus is coming: repent and be baptized.
    • Joseph was sent to save millions; Jesus was sent to save everyone and bless all the nations. Hebrews 9 says Christ was sacrificed once to take away the sins of many and will appear a second time to bring salvation to those waiting for him.

    1. 43:27 Stop making excuses

      • Some need to put their lives in the King’s hands today. Stop covering your tracks, justifying who you are, hiding, and drowning in guilt like Joseph’s brothers. Let go and say, “Jesus, I’m yours forever. Forgive me. I don’t need to know the details; I just need to know you love me. Remember me when you come again.”

  16. 44:07 Communion: drop your eraser

    • The church moves into communion and a song Eli will lead. Josh asks everyone to hold the eraser they were given and consider what to do with it: don’t erase or write off your story; it is there for a purpose. If you are ready to say, “Lord, even if I could, I wouldn’t erase it,” drop your eraser in the glass containers at the communion tables. Somebody is watching and needs to hear from you.

    1. 45:31 Closing prayer

      Scripture: 1 John 1:9

      • Josh asks God to shower the church with grace, mercy, and forgiveness: you say if we confess our sins you will forgive and purify us from all unrighteousness. “If my life has to be a disaster, but I can use that disaster to bring people to you, then so be it. Your will be done, not mine.”
