Overview

  1. 00:19 Week four: Israel had no king

    Scripture: Judges 17:6 Matthew 5:48

    • Josh opens week four of the Without a King series: remembering what life was like for God’s people in the period of Judges fills us with gratitude for our wonderful Savior, King Jesus.
    • He draws attention to Judges 17: “In those days Israel had no king; everyone did as they saw fit.” That is part of what makes our pursuit of Christ so valuable. Without a standard to reach for, we set our own. Jesus commands, “Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect,” but without his words spurring us on, we would settle for being better than that guy, or better than our parents.

  2. 01:38 God uses man to disprove man

    Scripture: Isaiah 53:6

    • Scripture seems to disprove the character of everyone but one. Part of why God gave us the Old Testament is to show what happens when ordinary men do the work: here’s the law, but who can keep it? Here’s the priesthood, but who can keep it pure? Here are the judges, but who can live forever? Pick a king, until he becomes self-serving. Send prophets to guide the kings, until the people kill the prophets.
    • God isn’t throwing things into history to see what sticks, like a chemist running an experiment. This is a gracious, intentional, patient demonstration of what won’t work, so his people see we need a greater lawkeeper, sacrifice, priest, judge, king, prophet, and Savior.
    • God uses man to disprove man and to prove God. Given the opportunity, you will go astray; we all, like sheep, have gone astray. In the worst circumstances and the best, you will fall short. But God loves us enough to teach us over and over that we need something we can’t obtain for ourselves.

  3. 03:21 What sin kept us from doing, Jesus accomplished

    Scripture: Romans 6:23 Romans 8:15 2 Timothy 1:7

    • This truth is at the core of the gospel: “For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.” What sin kept us from doing, Jesus accomplished for us. Through his perfect life, faithful death, and resurrection he broke the chains of sin and brought us near. Now we are not only forgiven but empowered by his Spirit to walk in God’s will.
    • Romans 8 says the Spirit we received doesn’t make us slaves to fear but brought our adoption, so we cry, “Abba, Father.” And Paul tells Timothy the Spirit God gave us does not make us timid, but gives us power, love, and self-discipline.

  4. 04:20 Josh used to be timid

    • Josh used to be timid, a coward (he knows people are laughing). He avoided confrontation at all costs and wouldn’t speak the truth or share the gospel because he wanted people to like him and didn’t want to be an outcast. He wasn’t generous because he felt God should have given him more. He isn’t up here as someone who has figured it out; he has tried all the wrong ways and has some wisdom by experience.

    1. 05:09 Whatever you fear

      • Some are crippled, as he was, by fear of the future: a bad health report, a marriage falling apart, kids making stupid decisions. Some fear being alone; some fear never being alone again. Some fear ending a relationship they know they shouldn’t be in, or entering a new one.
      • Wherever you are, we will look at a man who wrestled with fear and doubt like we do. Josh’s hope is simple: God doesn’t wait for the courageous to be willing; he wills the man to be courageous. God loves to lead the trembling into triumph.

  5. 06:09 Israel oppressed by Midian

    Scripture: Judges 6:1–6

    • Josh reads the text: Israel did evil in the eyes of the Lord, and for seven years he gave them into the hands of the Midianites. Israel hid in mountain clefts, caves, and strongholds. Whenever they planted, the Midianites, Amalekites, and other eastern peoples came like swarms of locusts with their livestock and camels, ruined the crops all the way to Gaza, and spared no living thing, until Israel cried out to the Lord.
    • Don’t lose track of the story: whose land is this? Israel’s, given by God. How humiliating, to go from conquerors to servant farmers. Why would God put them through this? Two reasons: God hates sin, and God loves his people.

  6. 07:25 Sometimes the help comes with a sermon

    Scripture: Judges 6:7–8

    • When Israel cried out because of Midian, he sent them a prophet. Josh compares it to having a heart attack and, instead of the defibrillator, getting an informational brochure on heart-healthy habits laid on your chest. They asked for help, not a sermon. But sometimes the help comes with a sermon.
    • Israel needed to be reminded that their problem wasn’t the sea of Midianites and hungry camels; it was their disobedience. God can remove our consequences as he pleases, but don’t forget the consequences are there because of your decisions. God didn’t leave his people; his people left him.

    1. 08:33 God doesn’t let his children sin successfully

      • Many Christians today want God to show up for them, but God wants to expose something in their hearts first. Why hasn’t God shown up? Maybe you aren’t listening.
      • That is why Josh’s first advice to a new believer is to hang on for dear life. If you are truly an adopted child of God, he won’t let you slip away into the shadows. Spurgeon said it best: God doesn’t let his children sin successfully. There were things Josh never thought God would want to remove from his life, a whole mess of them he thought were probably fine, and he was wrong.

    2. 09:34 Struggle can be the lighthouse home

      • Not all suffering is discipline; sometimes it is just suffering because we live in a fallen, dark, and lost world. But sometimes it is divine, strategic struggling to bring you home. Sometimes struggle is the lighthouse you need to find your way back.
      • Too many Christians ask God to get them out of it when he is trying to teach them through it. Josh asks: as you look at your trials, have you honestly asked God what he wants you to learn, sat with him, searched his Scriptures, and said, “Lord, would you educate me on why my hardship is so big right now?” If you cry out to God for help and he sends you a sermon, listen to that sermon and respond.

  7. 11:00 What the prophet preached

    Scripture: Judges 6:8–10

    • The prophet said: “This is what the Lord, the God of Israel, says: I brought you up out of Egypt, out of the land of slavery… I said to you, ‘I am the Lord your God; do not worship the gods of the Amorites…’ But you have not listened to me.”
    • Any great sermon has two key elements: remind God’s people what he has done, and call them to respond. Some circles would object, “Josh, why is there no altar call here?” Please stop. You aren’t saved because you prayed a prayer or walked an aisle. You don’t clean yourself up and then God comes; he comes while you are a mess.

  8. 12:21 Encounter the gospel in Judges

    Scripture: Judges 6:11 Romans 5:8

    • The angel of the Lord came and sat under the oak at Ophrah, where Gideon was threshing wheat in a winepress to hide it from the Midianites. This is a Christophany, a pre-incarnate appearance of Jesus Christ, before the virgin birth, Bethlehem, or the manger.
    • God’s response to man’s disobedience, even in the Old Testament, is to step down with a word. Josh thought immediately of Romans 5: “God demonstrates his own love for us in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.” God works his salvation plan even though Israel hasn’t listened. Man can’t; man won’t; but God does.

  9. 13:09 Threshing wheat in a winepress

    • Gideon is preparing wheat in a winepress to hide from the Midianites, because he is afraid. Threshing meant tossing wheat in the air so the wind removed the chaff and the good grain fell to the ground. A winepress is underground. Pop quiz: where doesn’t the wind blow? Underground.
    • Josh pictures Gideon tossing wheat with no wind and snacking on it, but asks before we judge: have you ever done something stupid because you were afraid? “Me too.”

  10. 14:03 “The Lord is with you, mighty warrior”

    Scripture: Judges 6:12 Psalm 103:10

    • The angel says, “The Lord is with you, mighty warrior.” It seems odd; we know Gideon is in the winepress because he is a coward. But every Christian must see this: God didn’t call Gideon because he was bold; he called him and then made him bold. God doesn’t go find brave people; he makes brave people. God leads the trembling into triumph.
    • Two unchanging traits of God: in his mercy he doesn’t give us what we deserve, and in his grace he gives us what we don’t deserve. Psalm 103 reminds us he does not treat us as our sins deserve or repay us according to our iniquities. God doesn’t approach things like we do.

    1. 15:02 Picked last for dodgeball

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 1:26–29

      • Josh polls the room on being picked last for dodgeball. He appeared athletic, so people picked him, then quickly learned he couldn’t dodge a truck in park. On the playground we are taught to admire strength and seek natural talent.
      • But God runs his kingdom in reverse, choosing the weak things to accomplish great things for his glory. Paul reminds the Corinthians: think of what you were when you were called; not many were wise, influential, or of noble birth, but God chose the foolish, weak, lowly, and despised things so that no one may boast before him.
      • Who in the room was a mess when God called them by name? Josh finds joy and comfort that God sees his own body of work, not Josh’s. He knows he is a coward, but somehow God sees a warrior.

  11. 16:38 “If the Lord is with us, why has all this happened?”

    Scripture: Judges 6:13 Joshua 24:15

    • “Pardon me, my lord,” Gideon replies (at least he uses his manners), “but if the Lord is with us, why has all this happened to us? Where are all his wonders that our ancestors told us about?… But now the Lord has abandoned us.”
    • Josh feels bad for Gideon. He knows what the Lord did in Egypt, but no one in his family thought to tell him about their disobedience. God didn’t abandon them; his parents abandoned God. This is what you get when a household isn’t dedicated to the Lord: kids who wrestle with God because they only have half the story. His ancestors explained what God did but not what God requires.
    • Gideon lacked faith and courage, but it isn’t as if his dad was walking around saying, “As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord.” His dad has an altar to a false god in the backyard.

    1. 18:11 Generational wealth is knowing God

      • Gideon has the nerve to ask God why amazing things don’t happen anymore. People want to beat Gideon up, and he deserves it, but Josh wants to deal with the speck before the plank: aren’t parents supposed to model counting blessings in the home?
      • Earlier in the series we saw the problem of passing down generational wealth, which then wasn’t just a trust with a deed and cash; it was knowledge of who God is, what he has done, and what he requires. You can’t point out Yahweh’s blessings to your kids when they see you bowing to idols in your backyard.

  12. 19:23 “Go in the strength you have”

    Scripture: Judges 6:14

    • Gideon asks why God doesn’t work wonders anymore, and Josh loves the Lord’s answer: “Go in the strength you have and save Israel out of Midian’s hand. Am I not sending you?” When you go in obedience, your strength is just fine. Where are my wonders, Gideon? Go, and I’ll show you; in fact, I’ll show them through you.

    1. 20:01 Wonders around you, or through you?

      • Are you looking for God to work wonders around you so you can experience and benefit from them, or pleading for him to work through you so someone else may? Like Gideon, many ask where his wonders are. Josh says look around: this room is filled with God’s wonders.
      • Are you waiting for him to split the sea or send rain? The church declares he killed death. Lives are being healed and souls redeemed; God is gathering his chosen people through the gospel by his Spirit. There is only one sign you need: God took on flesh, fulfilled the law, absorbed God’s wrath for sinners on the cross, lay in a tomb Friday and Saturday, and on Sunday walked out with an eternal victory over Satan, sin, and death.
      • People were killed for preaching, preserving, writing, translating, and believing this gospel, and believers in 2025 still cling to it. You ask where his wonders are; the wonders are written down for you to read. Josh asks back: where’s your Bible? Are you even reading it? It’s written for you.

    2. 22:29 We come from a long line of underdogs

      • Most believers here don’t root their faith in some old dead guy’s testimony. It is cool that people 2,000 years ago believed what we do, but wipe out all of history and there is still a church, because we have had a real, personal, undeniable encounter with Jesus Christ.
      • “I was one way, and now I’m completely different, and the only explanation is Jesus.” Josh was selfish and is becoming selfless, with the occasional relapse, his wife would add; prideful and now humble; a fool now leading with wisdom; he hated books and now can’t put the good book down.
      • We come from a long line of underdogs: Jacob the deceiver, Joseph the slave, Moses the adopted prince turned murderer, shepherd, leader, and prophet, Gideon the farmer, Jephthah the son of a prostitute, David the youngest son. God uses tax collectors, murderers, women, fishermen, doctors, peasants, and prostitutes. His wonders are all around. Are you a wonder of God? Josh knows he has watched God work in his life.

  13. 24:20 “My clan is the weakest”

    Scripture: Judges 6:15 Judges 6:16

    • They keep talking, and Gideon is so polite: “Pardon me, my lord, but how can I save Israel? My clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my family.” The Lord answers, “I will be with you, and you will strike down all the Midianites together.”
    • Josh loves Gideon’s humility, but can’t he see that it is because of his weakness that God chose him? God uses people who know they could never do it unless God did something miraculous: the lowly, the insignificant, the ordinary. That is good news for many of us; as for the self-appointed elite, you aren’t as great as you think you are.
    • Gideon knew he didn’t bring much to the table, and that is a good place to start. God goes out of his way to call the least expected so he receives all the glory, because the smart, the beautiful, and the talented end up confusing God’s wonders as their own. When God selects someone we never expected, we look at what was done and say, “Yep, that has to be the Lord.”

  14. 25:58 A waiting God

    Scripture: Judges 6:17–18 Revelation 3:20

    • Gideon asks for a sign that it is really the Lord and begs him not to go until he brings an offering, and the Lord says, “I will wait until you return.” That blows Josh’s mind. He is taken immediately to Revelation, where he stands at the door and knocks. Who has a waiting God like this?
    • Many Christians need more of a Gideon mindset, a deep desire to know a word is actually from Yahweh. Josh has heard comforting things from the lips of man, but doesn’t let them take root or drive his joy unless he has seen they are from God.

  15. 27:17 The offering and “The Lord is Peace”

    Scripture: Judges 6:19 Judges 6:20–24

    • Gideon goes to prepare a young goat. Josh guesses that takes at least an hour (a man in the room confirms “about an hour”), and the Lord is just sitting there waiting. How patient and kind he is.
    • At the angel’s word Gideon places the meat and bread on a rock; the angel touches them with his staff, fire flares and consumes them, and the angel disappears. Gideon cries out that he has seen the angel of the Lord face to face, but the Lord says, “Peace! Do not be afraid. You are not going to die.” Gideon builds an altar called The Lord is Peace: shalom, complete, success, finality.

    1. 28:47 Is God loving? Should you fear him?

      Scripture: Matthew 19:26

      • Gideon wants assurance like we all do, but when he gets it, it isn’t comforting; it is frightening. Modern readers picture frolicking through the park holding hands with Jesus. We are friends with Jesus, but the romcom thing with you and Jesus is not a thing.
      • Many would want to reassure Gideon that God isn’t a big scary God. Josh says: is God loving? Yes. Should you fear him? Yes. Gideon’s reaction seems strange because of what side of the cross we are on. What makes grace so amazing is holiness, the distance between how awesome God is and how wicked we are, and how far he travels to come down to our level. That is what makes “I will be with you” so amazing.
      • When God asks you into something too big for you, don’t highlight your deficiencies but his proficiencies, because a timeless truth spans from BC to AD: with God all things are possible.

  16. 30:34 Win the battle at home first

    Scripture: Matthew 25:21

    • Behind the battle between Israel and Midian is a battle at home, and it is true for us too: before you win battles for the Lord out there, you need to win at home. Gideon’s family worshiped Baal, so challenging the Midianites meant defying his father and embarrassing his family and neighbors.
    • Gideon had an amazing experience and got to test God; now it is God’s turn to test Gideon. If you can’t walk in faith at home, you’ll never walk tall out there. God loves to build character in a leader before their coming-out party, and Jesus confirms it in his parable in Matthew 25: “Well done, good and faithful servant! You have been faithful with a few things; I will put you in charge of many things.”

  17. 31:51 Tearing down his father’s altar at night

    Scripture: Judges 6:25–27 Matthew 7

    • Verse 25: that night the Lord tells Gideon to take his father’s second bull, tear down his father’s altar to Baal, cut down the Asherah pole, build a proper altar to the Lord, and offer the bull with the pole’s wood. Gideon takes ten servants and obeys, but at night, because he is afraid of his family and the townspeople.
    • Leave it to Christians to call him a coward for going at night. But by our Lord’s approach to judging rightly in Matthew 7, examine your own weak faith first. How much do you trust the Lord? Are you bold in the building but invisible in the wild? Being a Christian in here should be the easiest part of the week. Anyone can follow Jesus when it’s convenient and the crowd is cheering; will you follow him when it costs you, or when no one is looking?

    1. 33:27 Tear down the old altar first

      • You can’t build an altar to the Lord unless you first tear down the idols you worshiped before. What did you worship before the Lord? Are you trying to toss Jesus on top of all that? It won’t work. Our God is a jealous God. He doesn’t share the platform, play second fiddle, or sing background vocals; he is the main event and will not share his glory.

    2. 34:05 Critics instead of leaders

      • Sadly, most Christians who taunt Gideon for taking care of business at night are refusing to take care of business at all. In a digital world of endless scrollers, commenters, haters, and armchair bloggers, we have created generations of critics instead of leaders. The church doesn’t need more social media theologians; we need more doers of the word.

  18. 34:38 God works on Joash’s heart

    Scripture: Judges 6:28–29 Judges 6:31

    • In the morning the town finds Baal’s altar demolished and the bull sacrificed on the new altar, opens an investigation, and finds Gideon responsible. Josh guesses one of the ten servants snitched; with so many pictures of our Savior here, it seems fitting Gideon had his own Judas.
    • The town pressures Gideon’s father, and Joash answers the hostile crowd: “Are you going to plead Baal’s cause?… If Baal really is a god, he can defend himself when someone breaks down his altar.” Josh loves that God used Gideon’s obedience to work on his dad’s heart, an encouragement to anyone ministering to their family. It wasn’t what Gideon said, but what he showed.

    1. 35:51 Live the sermon

      • Most of Josh’s conversations with the church are about how to minister to family. Some feel convicted to speak the truth in love to unbelieving friends, co-workers, and family, and they should follow that lead. But he suggests another way to respond to that conviction: instead of just speaking the truth, live it out. Instead of preaching the sermon, live the sermon.

    2. 36:27 Josh’s sister Jess

      • They are coming up on the second anniversary of the death of Josh’s sister Jess, pictured holding Nolan. He and his family miss her so much; she left too soon, and his heart goes out to others grieving.
      • Months before she died she called while struggling through a divorce, and they dropped everything to help settle her into a new apartment and love on her. Weeks later his sister Andrea told him Jess said she had never experienced love and grace like that. Josh could have preached a whole series at Jess, but the greatest sermon could never have communicated half of what they said by serving her.
      • Sometimes the best witness isn’t a lecture in the spotlight but faithful obedience in the night. That night Gideon discovered that if you step out in obedience, even trembling, the Lord will cover you with his protection and ensure he alone gets the glory.

  19. 38:09 The Spirit comes on Gideon

    Scripture: Judges 6:34

    • The Midianites and their allies are about to make their annual invasion, and the Spirit of the Lord comes on Gideon; he blows a trumpet summoning the Abiezrites to follow him. This is where it turns: people who wanted to kill Gideon are now willing to kill for him. That is 32,000 reasons Gideon should believe God is in this, yet he still doubts God’s promise and comes up with an idea Josh says up front is not a good one.

  20. 38:56 The fleece test

    Scripture: Judges 6:36–40

    • Gideon asks God to put dew only on a wool fleece with the ground dry, and God does it; he wrings out a bowlful of water. Then, “Don’t be angry with me,” an important line (why would he be angry, Gideon?), he asks for the reverse: dry fleece, wet ground. Talk about dragging your feet. That night God does it.
    • We’ve modernized the fleece test. Josh has watched people move their families across the country because of one, putting conditions on God in their pride: if I get a text from this person at this time, we’re moving; if the weather changes, I’ll break up with him. Stop asking for signs and start reading his word. Are you equally yoked or not? You don’t need the weather to tell you.
    • Gideon’s fleece was a sign of distrust, not a model for determining God’s will; it reveals unbelief, not faith. We have it not so you put a fleece on your doorstep this afternoon, but to see how gracious God is: patient, not angry, stooping down to reassure his trembling servant, “I am here with you.”

    1. 41:03 Weak faith is more than enough

      Scripture: Hebrews 11

      • Don’t be too critical of Gideon. As you’ll see if you keep reading, he has a massive task ahead, and Josh gets why he drags his feet. Most of us would just tell God no without a fleece, like Moses: “Here I am, Lord… Pharaoh? You might want to send somebody else.”
      • There is nothing wrong with noticing Gideon’s weak faith, as long as we understand weak faith is more than enough. For all the questions, doubts, wrestling, and foot-dragging, he is still in the Hall of Fame in Hebrews 11.

  21. 42:10 Speak the promise: “He is with me”

    Scripture: Judges 6:16 Matthew 28:20

    • Many are walking out their faith with fear and trembling like Gideon, and Josh leaves one phrase: never forget how Yahweh ministered to Gideon in verse 16: “I will be with you.” That promise will carry you through anything, and Jesus came in the flesh to confirm it for all believers: “I am with you always.” There is no fear those words won’t drive out.
    • When grief hits over loved ones lost, or a diagnosis tells you your body is failing, speak the promise: he is with me. Josh has everyone turn to a neighbor, look them in the eye, and say, “He is with you,” because if you can’t speak the promise in here, you’ll never speak it out there.

    1. 43:45 Take every thought captive with a promise

      Scripture: Matthew 28:20

      • Some are battling addictions that aim to distract, destroy, even kill you: speak his promise. If you’re worried your neighbor will know you’re struggling, look around; we are all struggling, and no one here is fooling anybody. Speak it over your spouse and minister to them.
      • This is how the church overcomes: we take every thought captive and replace it with a promise, and the day goes differently. Heading into surgery, scheduling chemo, planning a funeral: remember he says, “I am with you to the very end.” Another day of job searching: he is with you. If these promises aren’t filling your mind the second light hits your corneas, read more and preach more.

  22. 45:04 We have the cross, not a fleece

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 1:18

    • Josh hears the Gideons in the room wanting to be sure: how do I know you’re really with me and in control? Put the fleece down; we have something better to reassure us God is on our side: the cross. His punishment brought us peace.
    • Paul tells the Corinthians the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to those being saved it is the power of God. On the cross everything bad about you was placed on Jesus and everything good about him was placed on you: the great exchange. You didn’t deserve it, can’t earn it, and can’t see heaven without believing it.

  23. 46:08 God’s most marvelous wonder

    • Josh asks everyone to close their eyes and listen, not to fancy words, but to a heart God has reshaped. Considering God’s holiness, one truth keeps coming to him: you are so much worse than you think you are. No effort can fix your position before Almighty God. He is holy; you are not; his perfect justice demands punishment.
    • But the gospel says Jesus, fully God and fully man, lived the life we couldn’t, died the death we deserve, and rose again in victory. The Creator’s message is simple: repent and believe; turn from your sin and trust in Jesus Christ.
    • If you know deep in your bones he is calling you, you have just experienced God’s most marvelous wonder, the miracle of repentance happening in hearts right now, which floods your life with forgiveness, shalom, and eternal life. It isn’t the end of the journey but the beginning of something new. It won’t be easy, but no matter where life takes you, he will be with you to the very end.

  24. 47:52 Closing prayer

    Scripture: John 14:6

    • Josh prays that he cannot get over the picture of God sitting and waiting while Gideon prepares an offering in the kitchen, a waiting God and patient Savior, and that it brings him peace.
    • He prays that anyone putting off following Jesus would see that picture, stop dragging their feet, and run to him to experience mercy, grace, and the greatest wonder the world has seen: that God gave his Son to save sinners. God is waiting because he desires that no one should perish, and Josh prays that everyone from a 10-year-old to an 80-year-old would gather around the promise of eternal life through him, the way, the truth, and the life.
