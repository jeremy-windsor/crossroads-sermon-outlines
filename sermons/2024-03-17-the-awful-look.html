Overview

  1. 00:08 Fix your eyes on Jesus

    • Steve begins by doing something usually saved for the end: he asks the church to bow in quiet reverence and take an unhurried, thoughtful look at the one who endured the cross and scorned its shame. Picture him in agony, listen as the nails are driven into his hands, look into his face as the spit from the angry crowd mingles with his blood from that awful crown.
    • Then, eyes still closed, ask him why he so willingly endured such undeserved abuse. As Steve sees it there are two reasons: respectful obedience to his Father, and unbridled compassion for you. The death of Christ was directed by the Father but driven by his love. He died that we might live; he was wounded that we could be healed.

    1. 01:48 By his wound you have been healed

      Scripture: 1 Peter 2:24 Isaiah 52:14

      • Steve puts a verse on the screen: he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, so that we might die to sin and live for righteousness; by his wounds we have been healed. The truth is inescapable: the cross had your name on it, the crown of thorns was fitted for you, and the pain you deserved he took, all of it.
      • But Steve says the phrase should read “by his wound,” singular. The treatment of Jesus was so awful that not even The Passion of the Christ could capture it; his body was so mangled that he didn’t have wounds so much as one long unbroken wound. Isaiah said his appearance was disfigured beyond that of any man, and according to Peter it is that one wound that has allowed you to be healed.

  2. 03:36 The onramp: Peter’s denial and the look

    Scripture: Luke 22:60–62

    • The Road to Redemption leads to the holiest day on the Christian calendar, and its onramp is Peter’s infamous denial, a denial he adamantly said he would never make: “These other turkeys may let you down, but even if I have to die, I’ll never deny you.”
    • As Jesus is taken into custody, Peter warms himself by the fire. A peasant girl says he was with the Nazarene; he denies it and moves away from the light, but she follows and tells her friends. Then his accent gives him away, and again he says, “I don’t know the man.”
    • Luke tells us that just as Peter was speaking, three stunning events happened in rapid fire: the rooster crowed, the Lord turned and looked straight at Peter, and Peter remembered. He went outside and wept bitterly.

    1. 05:27 You know that look too

      • Before you judge Peter, Steve says, you know that look. You fall prey to your own weakness, something you swore you would never do again, and then a reminder flashes, a picture, a line from a song, a knowing look, and you are in the same courtyard seeing the same mutilated, bloodied face of Jesus, now lined with the disappointment and heartache your betrayal caused. The look lasts a moment, but like a laser it burns a hole in your soul.
      • That’s Peter: his future was as bright as the brightest star, and now he is running and weeping, seeking the safety that only comes from hiding in the shadows.

  3. 06:31 God’s first response to sin: hell

    Scripture: Mark 9:48

    • Before time began, God knew he would have to respond to our sin. He knew we would persistently do what belittles his name and robs him of his glory, and that we would betray him by surrounding ourselves with God’s stuff rather than God himself. Because God is utterly holy, he can’t ignore these hits to his glory.
    • God’s first response is hell, and it is still very much part of God’s plan: a real, literal, eternal place. From Jesus himself, those not in the kingdom will be thrown into the darkness, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth, where the worm never dies because your flesh is never consumed and your torment will never end. It is the blackest darkness, filled with the stench of burning sulfur and the smoke of an unquenchable fire.

  4. 08:36 God’s second response: the cross of Christ

    • Hell isn’t where God wants you to go, and he finds no joy in it. That is why he had a second response, another ugly thing: the cross of Christ, a cross initially intended for us, since we are the ones who stole his glory, but shouldered by him.
    • When Jesus died, nature itself convulsed: the ground shook, tombs cracked open, the sky went black, and the sun hid. Yet this was exactly what was supposed to happen all along.

    1. 09:55 The cross was always God’s plan

      Scripture: Acts 2:23 Acts 4:27–28 Luke 19:10 John 3:14

      • Before the beginning, God promised that at history’s perfect moment a lamb would come, the Lamb of God, to do for us what we could not do for ourselves. As bad as it got on the hill, the cross was not an accident or an afterthought; it was Jesus’ ultimate assignment.
      • In the very first gospel sermon, Peter said Jesus was handed over by God’s set purpose and foreknowledge. Herod and Pontius Pilate conspired against Jesus, but they only did what God’s power and will had decided beforehand should happen. The death of God’s one and only was his idea all along, not added after Adam was found in the bushes or scrawled in the margins after the fruit crossed Eve’s lips.
      • So unwavering was God’s plan that Jesus planted the tree from which the beams of his cross would be carved and placed in Earth’s belly the ore for the nails. Jesus knew his destiny from infancy: the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost. He even knew how: just as Moses lifted up the snake in the desert, the Son of Man must be lifted up. He knew it meant mocking, flogging, beating, and betrayal, and he came anyway.

    2. 12:44 God isn’t Harold with a purple crayon

      Scripture: Isaiah 53:10

      • Many view the cross as an ancient version of Harold and the Purple Crayon, a children’s book Steve used in consulting work: a kid draws himself out of every complication, a shortcut, a pie, a boat. Steve would hand leadership teams a crayon to inspire creative solutions.
      • But God the Father isn’t Harold, and Jesus didn’t sketch our redemption by the seat of his pants, as if only after the world got screwed up God nervously grabbed a crayon and drew a cross. The cross was always the plan. Seven hundred years before Jesus arrived, the prophet said it was the Lord’s will to crush him, and crush means blood, a lot of blood. Later Peter described the gore and made sure we knew Jesus did it in our stead.

    3. 14:46 You must take the look

      • If the blood talk bothers you, it bothers Steve too, but you cannot tell the story of Jesus without it. Just as his birth was accompanied by blood, his death is drenched with it.
      • Taking a long, studied look at the cross is painful, and the Father and the Son knew it. But if you ever hope to find forever healing, you absolutely must take this look. It is painful, and it is essential.

  5. 15:38 Five non-negotiable rules for redemption

    • Steve unpacks five non-negotiable rules for how redemption simply has to happen.

    1. 15:48 Rule one: blood is required to cover sin

      Scripture: Leviticus 17:11 Hebrews 9:22

      • In Leviticus, Israel’s constitution, God said the life of a creature is in its blood, given to make atonement on the altar. Blood is the reconciling agent, the cleansing, covering connection that delivers the forgiveness we must have because we have belittled his name and robbed him of his glory. Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sin.
      • That is why God in the Old Testament can come across as grumpy: you can’t stroll into his presence having robbed him of his glory without incurring his wrath. The sons of Aaron approached the Lord without the benefit of blood and were struck dead; the well-meaning Uzzah tried to steady the falling Ark and was struck dead. You cannot come near to God still covered in sin.
      • Steve doesn’t much care for that either (“Let’s close in prayer. No, I’m just kidding”), but this is God’s world and he can set it up however he chooses. If you want forgiveness, it will take blood; somebody has to pay for this crime. So the Lord established a plan by which even sinful people get a temporary hall pass into his presence.

    2. 18:21 Rule two: the blood must be from a substitute

      Scripture: Genesis 3:7 Genesis 3:21 Genesis 22:7–8

      • Whenever we, Peter included, choose earthly comforts over the comfort of God himself, blood must flow, but for the plan to work it has to be someone else’s blood.
      • Substitute blood finds its genesis in Genesis. After Adam and Eve ate the fruit, their eyes were opened and they knew they were naked, so they covered themselves with fig leaves, a wardrobe malfunction waiting to happen. God made them garments of skin, and to get skin you have to kill an animal. Steve imagines Adam’s horror watching a defenseless creature struck down: “Why did I eat that stupid apple?”
      • On Mount Moriah, God told Abraham to take his only son Isaac to the altar. Isaac asked, “Dad, we’ve got wood, we brought fire, but where’s the lamb?” Abraham, choking back tears, said God would provide the lamb. As he raised the knife, a voice called out, and he found a ram caught by its horns and sacrificed it instead of his son. That story was a foreshadowing of an even greater substitute: another only son, who would not be spared, offered as the full payment for all of humanity’s sins.

    3. 21:29 Rule three: the substitute must be without blemish

      Scripture: Exodus 12

      • In Exodus 12, after nine horrific plagues, Pharaoh still won’t release the Israelites. In the tenth, the death angel will pass over Egypt and every firstborn son will die. In every Israelite home a perfect, blemish-free lamb was to be slaughtered and its blood smeared on the door frame, so that the angel would see the blood and pass over.
      • Once again blood is God’s chosen vehicle for salvation: in the garden, blood is required for the covering; on Mount Moriah, it had to be a substitute’s; and at the Passover, the lamb had to be without blemish, no taint or defect of any kind.

    4. 22:57 Rule four: lean on the substitute and apply the blood

      • At Mount Sinai the Israelites received the law, including the Levitical sacrificial system, another glimpse of how humans can tap into the grace of God, and it required even more blood. Whenever sin took place, the offender brought a male animal without defect to the house of God.
      • So in the Old Testament people at church knew what you had been up to by the animal tucked under your arm. Today the bigger the Bible, the more holy the carrier; back then, the bigger the animal, the bigger the screw-up. You stroll in with a scrawny pigeon while Brother Daniel hauls in a 900-pound bull: “Hey, Brother Daniel, have a bad week?”
      • At the altar the sinner laid his hand on the animal and leaned his weight on it, saying in effect, “I transfer my guilt, my sin, to this lamb.” That is rule four: having selected the substitute, I must lean on it and personally apply its blood over my sin. Then an amazing transfer takes place: my sin becomes the lamb’s sin, and I walk away forgiven.
      1. 25:29 Can you imagine living like that?
        • Every day the priests stood at the altar sprinkling blood, so commonly that historians say the average Hebrew’s clothing was permanently bloodstained. Find an animal, go to the priest, lean on it, catch its blood, sprinkle the altar, and only after that mind-numbing routine are you forgiven.
        • Then on the way home someone cuts you off with their goat, or cuts in line at Starbucks, and you respond with a double-fisted, single-fingered salute, and you have to turn around, find another lamb, and do the whole drill again.
      2. 26:59 God was frustrated too, and Isaiah saw a “him”

        Scripture: Isaiah 1:11 Isaiah 53:5 Isaiah 53:6

        • If the people were frustrated, God was more so: “Your sacrifices, what are they to me? I have more than enough of burnt offerings; I have no pleasure in the blood of bulls and lambs.” In other words, it was never about the sacrifice. God was showing how disgusting, horrible, and costly sin is, and the people turned it into another religious routine to impress him.
        • So when Isaiah said he was pierced for our transgressions and crushed for our iniquities, it was a breath of fresh air. People had been piercing and crushing animals for years, but now, instead of an “it,” there was coming a “he”: we all like sheep have gone astray, and the Lord has laid on him the iniquity of us all.
        • Instead of trading my sins to an animal, God would send another kind of lamb and transfer the sins of all mankind, past, present, and future, to his shoulders. Instead of temporarily covering sin, this lamb would remove it forever.

    5. 29:18 Rule five: a sacrifice to end all sacrifices

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:4

      • His blood would provide permanent forgiveness: a sacrifice to end all sacrifices must be made. Israel desperately wanted the lamb to come quickly, but after Isaiah died, seven hundred more years passed of daily sacrifice, even though Scripture says it is impossible for that kind of blood to take away sin.
      • Why wait? It wasn’t time yet, and God had said it had to be the blood of him; only his blood can take sin away.

  6. 30:31 Grading God on the rules

    • Before heading home, Steve grades God on how he did with these rules.

    1. 30:37 Rule one: done in Jesus

      Scripture: John 1:29 Matthew 27:46

      • Blood is required to cover sin, and the him who would come did come and provide the blood, so rule one is already accomplished in Jesus.
      • It took 700 years, but when the time was right a stranger appeared in the wilderness, a total freakazoid in camel hair, eating locusts and drinking honey, “venti, extra whip, definitely not decaf.” John the Baptizer said the one coming after him was so great he wasn’t worthy to lace up his Nikes. When he saw him, he remembered Isaiah’s prophecy and shouted, “Behold the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world.”
      • Out of obedience to the Father and love for us, Jesus willingly emptied himself and became our lamb. Yet hardly anyone recognized him. The religious crowd tried to trip him up and then tear him down; his own family scorned him; the crowd that waved palm branches and shouted “Hosanna” screamed “Crucify him” five days later.
      • Jesus carried his own instrument of death to a bloody hillside, nails were driven into his hands and feet, and his cross dropped with a thud between two thieves. For six hours the promised Lamb dangled between heaven and earth while the crowd taunted, “Come down from that cross, then we’ll believe you.” There was no rescue, only a shout of agony, “My God, why have you forsaken me?” He knew why, and he stayed there until he died, the lamb who would give up his blood for the Peters of this world, and the Steves, and you.

    2. 34:26 Rule three: the only one without sin

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:21 1 Corinthians 5:7

      • In all human history only one person has lived without sin, so only one is qualified to be our substitute. That is why God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God, and why Paul triumphantly calls Jesus our Passover Lamb.

    3. 35:10 Rule five: once for all

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:18 Hebrews 10:10

      • Because of Jesus there is no longer any sacrifice for sin: no more lambs, no more blood. We have been made holy through the sacrifice of the body of Jesus Christ once for all, so rule five is checked off too. Not another lamb is required.

    4. 35:49 Rule four is why Peter was still hiding

      • Something is missing before the final grade: rule four, the reason Peter was still hiding and weeping. Forgiveness is not supplied until the blood is personally applied. Like that first Passover, you must take the blood Jesus freely provided, splash it across your heart, lean on the Passover Lamb, and personally hand him all the ugly garbage of your life so that he alone can cover it.
      • If you aren’t willing, God cannot and will not forgive you; there is no grace, no redemption, no forgiveness, because God has no other plan. On judgment day he won’t ask about your good deeds, your values, who you voted for, or the people you helped. All he will want to know is what you did with the blood of his Lamb: did you splash it across the door of your heart, or did you do as Peter did and just cry and hide? God doesn’t want or need your tears.

  7. 38:12 The transaction is already complete

    • Steve has given his life to teaching this plan and believes it with all his heart, yet finds it almost incomprehensible that the Creator would trade places with an undeserving, evil person like him. The glory of the gospel is that God didn’t just make the offer; the transaction is already completed, as much as God can do.
    • The lamb without blemish has already been pierced for all your transgressions and crushed for all your iniquities, every sin you have done and still plan to do. Jesus has fulfilled four of the five rules, and the only one left can be accomplished only by you, which means only you stand between you and forgiveness.

    1. 40:05 On the cross, you were on his mind

      • When Jesus was on the cross he saw you, in the vortex of your guilt and shame, battered by your sin, betrayed by people you love, alone and afraid, weeping and hiding, and he refused to let you stay there. He marched into hell itself so you wouldn’t have to. He didn’t have to; he chose to. He left heaven so you could go to heaven.
      • Even if your story fills 157 dumpsters with putrid, stinking garbage (“I’m talking to you, Peter, and I’m talking to me”), Jesus did the deed anyway, and in the mind of God it has already been filtered through the blood he shed. The deal is struck; the only thing left between you and forgiveness is this one last rule.

  8. 42:04 How to apply the blood

    • If you don’t know how, start by admitting, first to yourself and then to God, that you need exactly what he came to provide, that the blood shed on the cross is your only hope and there is no other salvation. Then place your sins where God has already put them: on him.
    • By faith, come to him and tell him you want his covering, that you want more than the world to be in him. There are no magic words; if it is the truth of your heart, God will get it.

    1. 43:14 A prayer to repeat

      • Steve asks everyone, especially those already in Jesus, to bow and repeat a simple prayer, which could be someone’s day of salvation: “Jesus, I accept you as the Lamb of my life. Take me, Lord, the good, the bad, and the so very ugly. Take every part of me, and by your blood cover me as only you can. I receive you, Jesus, as the Lord and Savior of my life.”
      • To anyone who truly meant it, Steve says it is his great joy to welcome them to God’s forever family: now forever covered, never again condemned. Welcome to the company of the redeemed.

  9. 45:05 The first next step: baptism

    • After 60 years walking with Jesus, Steve knows there are a lot of nexts, and the first is a no-brainer: baptism. If you are ready, the baptistry is available right now, and someone in the lobby will help; if not, it is ready next week too. This is the first step and the big step.

  10. 46:01 Closing prayer before the table

    Scripture: Romans 8:1

    • Steve prays that words escape him to thank God for what he has done. He doesn’t understand how the plan came to be, but God is God and he is not, and he freely receives the gift of Jesus’ blood. He gave his heart to Jesus at nine years old, almost 60 years ago, and is grateful for the grace showered on him time and again.
    • He thanks God for the meal that celebrates his body broken and blood shed for our sins, asks the Lord to meet them at the table, and prays for the peace of knowing there is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ.
