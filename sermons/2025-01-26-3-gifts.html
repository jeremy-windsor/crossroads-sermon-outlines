Overview

  1. 00:11 Trapped between past and future

    • Last week the church saw the announcement of God’s servant in two parts: his message, “Repent and believe the good news,” and his method, which has messed up people like Josh and the church.
    • God is working on Josh, and part of him daily wrestles with the man he was: the heaviness of having hurt so many people with his words and actions, a trail of pain that can overwhelm him. He also wrestles with what is ahead. Everywhere he looks there is evil and temptation, and in one moment he could fail to say no and tarnish his Redeemer’s reputation. He looks at others falling and thinks he’ll never do that, then realizes they probably thought that too.
    • In a way he feels trapped between his past and his future, and in comes Jesus: repent and believe the good news. Each day there is a choice: look behind and drown in shame, look ahead and drown in anxiety, or look up and believe the good news.

    1. 01:53 Marked with a seal

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:13–14

      • Why does this matter for someone already saved? Paul told the Ephesians that when they heard the word of truth and believed, they were marked in him with a seal, the promised Holy Spirit, a deposit guaranteeing our inheritance. If you have faith in Jesus, the deposit is paid; it is just a waiting game. Eternity isn’t a coin flip; it is a guarantee. When a believer trips on Satan’s traps, he falls into the arms of Jesus.

    2. 02:40 Christ came to save sinners

      Scripture: Mark 2:17 Romans 5:6 Romans 5:8 1 Timothy 1:15

      • This is what Jesus, the apostles, and the pastors want the church to fight never to forget. Jesus said it isn’t the healthy who need a doctor but the sick; he came to call sinners. That theme holds through the New Testament: Paul told the Romans Christ died for the ungodly, and two verses later, God demonstrates his love in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Timothy is told to build the church on the trustworthy saying that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, “of whom I am the worst.”
      • Jesus didn’t come to save sinners so they could drown in shame over the past or crumble under anxiety about tomorrow. He came so sinners could have peace with God, look up to their heavenly Father, and depend on him who has handled our past and our future.

    3. 03:48 Stop going to war with yourself

      • If you have peace with God, would you stop going to war with yourself? Does your opinion of yourself matter more than God’s? Many believers struggle here, Josh included, and it is another form of pride.
      • Because of the cross, God looks at you and sees Christ’s righteousness, not your sinfulness. Josh is concerned that many are redeemed in God’s eyes but look in the mirror with condemnation. He has been there. You are looking around when you need to look up: read the red letters, get to know the one who died to save your soul, read his promises, note his gifts, and hit your knees to tell God why you need a Savior. He already knows; you are the one who needs to hear it. Then believe Jesus was sent to save you from it.

  2. 05:16 Gift one: forgiveness

    Scripture: Mark 2:1–12

    • Josh will show three gifts Jesus offers all who trust him: forgiveness, fulfillment, and freedom.
    • First, forgiveness, in Mark 2: when Jesus came home to Capernaum, the crowd was so large there was no room even outside the door. Four men carrying a paralyzed man couldn’t reach Jesus, so they dug through the roof and lowered his mat. Seeing their faith, Jesus said, “Son, your sins are forgiven.” Teachers of the law thought, “He’s blaspheming! Who can forgive sins but God alone?” Jesus, knowing their thoughts, asked which is easier to say, and to show the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins, told the man to take his mat and go home. He did, and everyone praised God: “We have never seen anything like this!”

    1. 06:46 Four friends who made a way

      • There is a lot to admire about these four men. Jesus intends to make his followers fishers of men, and whether in life groups, the men’s breakfast, or worship, this is the attitude you would expect: they cared about their friend getting help more than anything, and believed Jesus could and would help him.
      • Josh wonders whether he has people like this in his life. They could have looked at the crowd and said Jesus is busy, come back Tuesday when it’s less full. Instead they made a way.

    2. 07:47 Are you known?

      • Most walked in under their own power and can’t relate to the man on the mat, but are you known? If you missed a couple of weeks, would anyone notice or come knocking? Many are annoyed when people notice they were gone (“You eyeing me at church, bro?”), but we should praise God for people like that.
      • In a crowd it is hard to expect this, but in a life group amazing things happen. Does anyone know you well enough to help you back on track? If you are angry, bitter, or full of unforgiveness, are friends ushering you into the Lord’s presence, even willing to carry you? Following Christ is a team sport.

    3. 09:00 You don’t have friends like this unless you’re a friend like that

      • These four friends tell us the man on the mat was a good friend too, because you don’t have friends like this unless you are a friend like that. Many fall into a toxic atmosphere: wanting to be carried on the mat but unwilling to do life in community, unwilling to join a life group yet expecting everyone to be there in the desperate hour. “Where were you?” “I don’t even know your name, man.”

    4. 09:40 Unspoken expectations

      • Josh deals with this a lot in pastoral counseling. He is sensitive to church hurt and has some himself, but the question that usually needs asking is: did you contribute to your own hurt? Did you have unspoken expectations, bring traditions from a previous church, or expect your new pastor to have your old pastor’s gifts?
      • A few years ago a member called to say Josh wasn’t a true shepherd and didn’t care, which blew him away; he has been called many names, but uncaring, unavailable, and unapproachable usually aren’t among them, and his personal number is on his business card. Their close friend had been in the hospital without a visit from him. Josh apologized and said he didn’t know they’d asked, and the member said they hadn’t asked, but “a shepherd wouldn’t need an invitation.” Josh wholeheartedly disagrees. Unspoken expectations are unhealthy; if you have one, tell him.
      • We’re all different. Josh’s wife has strict instructions not to let visitors in if he is hospitalized; he’d love prayers, but those robes don’t close well in the back.

    5. 12:19 When Josh’s sister died

      • Josh shares something deeply personal for the first time. When his sister died, some elders felt led to come to his house to pray for him, but he just wanted to be alone. It was the worst day of his life: he had to tell both his parents, in back-to-back calls, that they had lost their middle child, and he was done with words for a while.
      • His wife encouraged him, so he let them come. He didn’t allow it because he needed it from them, but because they might have needed to show that love. He loves praying with others, but he wanted to wrestle with the Lord alone.

    6. 13:20 Be honest about your needs

      • Josh wishes we had the details of how the five friends planned to get to Jesus, the staff meeting before church, and would bet his lunch it was the paralyzed man’s idea in the first place. We need to be honest about our needs and be in community. We can’t get mad that our need went unnoticed when we never shared it, or that no one reached out when we never tried to be in relationship with them.

    7. 13:53 Don’t just say “let’s pray about it”

      • These men didn’t just say “Let’s pray about it,” maybe Josh’s least favorite Christian phrase; they made a plan to address the need. He commends anyone who stops and prays right then when asked. Pastor Ken screens the church’s prayer requests and sends them out, and Josh stops and prays every time he sees one; he would love the whole church on the prayer team, which people can join on the mobile app. Giving the churchy “I’ll pray for you” and never doing it is a lie; just say, “I’m here for you, man.”
      • We also fall short when we pray for someone’s physical need but do nothing about it when we could. Crossroads Cares isn’t just for people out there; it is for us. The lobby isn’t for talking about the playoff game; it is for asking a brother how to pray for him and how his marriage is going, offering to share it on the mobile app if he wants the church praying, and submitting any need to Crossroads Cares for him.
      • We are all called to be ministers so the spiritual and physical needs of the church family are met and no one goes unnoticed. If someone shares a need, close the loop, as these men did. Jesus noticed their faith and effort and rewarded it. Don’t just say you’ll carry someone’s mat; carry it, and when obstacles come, get creative and push through, because we are all shepherds.

  3. 16:41 His greatest miracle: “Your sins are forgiven”

    Scripture: Mark 2:5

    • In verse 5 Jesus performs his greatest miracle ever: “Son, your sins are forgiven.” That is why we must bring people to Jesus; his forgiveness meets everyone’s greatest need. His greatest concern is our hearts, not our hurts.
    • Nothing has escaped the stain of sin. It brought death and separation, and affects relationships, our environment, and physical, mental, and emotional health, bringing guilt, dissatisfaction, discontentment, and ultimately death. Jesus proved his power to forgive sin by showing his power to cure this man. Those who believe rejoice; those who deny him ridicule.

  4. 17:30 Verse 8: the God who reads hearts

    Scripture: Mark 2:8

    • Verse 8 should open our eyes: immediately Jesus knew in his spirit what they were thinking. He proved himself God by forgiving sin and healing, then read the hearts of the front-row Pharisees, which only God can do. Josh’s preaching game would go up if he could read hearts.
    • Omniscience, God being all-knowing, is either the most terrifying or most comforting thing about him. Apart from grace it is terrifying that not a single thought in your head is your own. In grace it is a wonderful comfort: God knows every sin you have done, thought about doing, and will ever do, knows you better than anyone, and still loves you. That kind of love could change a person, and it has changed many, including Josh.

  5. 18:55 Which is easier?

    Scripture: Mark 2:9

    • Jesus asks which is easier: to heal the man or tell him he’s forgiven. Saying “you’re forgiven” is easier, because forgiveness is invisible and can’t be confirmed, so Jesus backs up his words by healing the man and sending him home.
    • Many modern so-called healers work around this by keeping healing invisible too. Josh names Benny Hinn, who he says is financially abusive, targeting poor people with promises of financial blessing, and claims healings without letting anyone properly check them. While people like that cover up years of deceptive practice, Jesus puts himself to the test. Transparency abounds; Jesus backs up his claims.

  6. 19:47 A Bible study that night: Psalm 103

    Scripture: Psalm 103:2–5

    • Josh likes to imagine the man on the mat went home and led a Bible study that night. “What do you want to study tonight?” “How about Psalm 103?” Praise the Lord, my soul, and forget not all his benefits, who forgives all your sins and heals all your diseases, who redeems your life from the pit and crowns you with love and compassion.
    • It is one thing to read Scripture and another to memorize it, but entirely another to meditate on it after you have witnessed it in your life.

  7. 20:39 Critical spirit or repentant heart?

    • The religious leaders missed the point of church that day. Had they come with open ears and repentant hearts instead of a critical spirit, they would have learned the timeless truth that sin is like a disease and forgiveness is like a cure, and maybe their sins would have been forgiven too. Some need to check how they come into this place: with a critical spirit or a repentant heart?

  8. 21:18 Gift two: fulfillment

    Scripture: Mark 2:13–17

    • Another blessing Jesus offers is fulfillment, in verse 13. Jesus saw Levi son of Alphaeus at the tax collector’s booth and said, “Follow me,” and Levi got up and followed. At dinner at Levi’s house, many tax collectors and sinners ate with Jesus, and the Pharisees asked why. Jesus said, “It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick. I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners.”
    • As Jesus builds his ministry team, the qualifications aren’t what you’d expect. Levi was a Jewish tax collector employed by Herod Antipas: disgraced.

    1. 22:26 God qualifies those he calls

      • That tracks with God’s recruiting strategy: Jacob was a cheater, David a murderer and adulterer, Noah a drunk, Jonah stubborn, Gideon insecure, Martha a worrier, Thomas a doubter, Elijah depressed despite fire from heaven, Moses a terrible public speaker, Zacchaeus short, Abraham and Sarah old, and Lazarus dead. God doesn’t call the qualified; he qualifies those he calls. Levi didn’t argue or hesitate; he got up and followed.

    2. 23:02 Discipleship math

      • Count the cost: Andrew, Peter, James, and John, called in chapter 1, worked for Zebedee and had a fallback plan, and in fact they do go back to fishing. Levi didn’t have that luxury; Rome would never give him his job back. He burned bridges and cut ties with Rome forever, so he might as well take a new name: Matthew, which means gift of God.

    3. 23:43 Your platform for ministry

      • Josh loves that Levi used his platform for Christ. He didn’t blow up his relationships from his tax-collecting days to go hang out with church people; he threw a dinner party with Jesus and told them why he was changing.
      • Jesus repeatedly slowed down to speak to anyone with questions. Christians have gotten too used to hearing that Jesus ate with sinners and skim over it, but he didn’t care whether they were poor or rich, liked or disliked; he cared about their hearts. Yes, turn away from your sins, but don’t turn away from the people you knew when living in sin. That is your platform for ministry; they need Christ like we do, and you might be their best shot at hearing of his love.

  9. 24:55 Those who stood against Jesus

    • Jesus goes head to head with several groups throughout Mark. The Pharisees were strict, influential tradition hardliners who taught obedience to the tiniest parts of Jewish law and rejected Jesus because he didn’t follow their traditions and spent time with sinners, essentially everything he is doing in Mark 2.
    • The Sadducees were a wealthy upper-class priestly group who rejected the authority of everything after Deuteronomy, profited from business in the temple (“hello, conflict of interest”), and denied the resurrection of the dead. The teachers of the law interpreted the law for a living, focused heavily on tradition, and rejected Jesus as Messiah because he didn’t obey their traditions. The Herodians, seen in chapter 3, were Herod’s supporters who tried to trap Jesus with questions, afraid he would cause political instability.
    • The theme: everyone against Jesus was for tradition, wealth, and power.

  10. 26:37 Three kinds of patients

    • Jesus cannot heal everyone, not because he lacks the ability or power, but because they lack the desire. The sick who need a doctor come as three kinds of patients: those who don’t know about Jesus, those who know about him but refuse to trust him, and those who won’t admit they need him. We must share the message of Christ’s love to find out which patient we are dealing with. It is like triage.
    • Fun fact: Josh was going to be a paramedic but found out the hard way he can’t do trauma; he would pass out and join you. That is also why he doesn’t visit hospitals; he has gone to pray for someone and ended up in the ER for over 8 hours, and a lady in the service can attest to it.
    • The only way to know whether someone is sick because they don’t know about Jesus is to tell them about Jesus, and unless we admit we are sinners deserving God’s wrath, we can’t be saved.

    1. 28:24 The great physician

      Scripture: Luke 19:10

      • From the bottom of his heart, Josh tells anyone sick with sin that Jesus is the only physician with a cure; if he healed Josh, he can heal you. He is always available, never takes a holiday, never botches the diagnosis, his cure always works, and he covers all your medical expenses. Luke 19 says the Son of Man came to seek and save what was lost.
      • Jesus saves sinners. Are you a sinner? Then run to him; if you can’t run, walk; if you can’t walk, crawl; if you can’t move, raise your hand and the church will carry you. Stop coming and leaving without putting your life in the great physician’s hands.
      • The real tragedy is that no one in this room can now claim not to know about Jesus. The only question left is: do you trust him? The Christian has faith and then asks questions to grow it; the Pharisees asked questions to decide whether they would have faith, and without faith there are always more questions.

  11. 29:45 Fasting and the bridegroom

    Scripture: Mark 2:18–22

    • In the rest of Mark 2, John the Baptist’s disciples and the Pharisees were fasting, and people asked why Jesus’ disciples were not. Jesus answered that the guests of the bridegroom can’t fast while he is with them, but the day will come when he is taken, and then they will fast. No one sews unshrunk cloth on an old garment or pours new wine into old wineskins; new wine goes into new wineskins.
    • Fasting, like any discipline done the right way, can align us with God’s heart, humble us, and grow our faith; done the wrong way it is a meaningless ritual. The Pharisees fasted twice a week like clockwork and announced it so everyone would know how holy they were, but fasting is a tool applied when needed. John’s disciples fasted in repentance, anticipating Christ; now that he is here, why would they fast?
    • No, intermittent fasting isn’t biblical; trying to fit into a Speedo isn’t fasting, and neither is giving up Starbucks for a week or cutting carbs. Fasting is abstaining from food with an emphasis on prayer, meditation, reading the word, and focusing on God alone, quieting the noise to focus on his will. Through it God can help with grief, decisions, humility, and repentance, but if it isn’t motivated by love for God, it is just noise.

    1. 32:04 Saying “I do” to Jesus

      • Josh loves the title Bridegroom; Jesus’ first miracle was at a wedding feast in Cana. Being a Christian isn’t so different from marriage, by design, since God made both. Two people don’t marry because they know each other, are fond of each other, or feel strongly; they commit publicly, often sealed with two words: “I do.”
      • Salvation doesn’t come from knowing Jesus was a good guy and teacher or having positive feelings about him. It comes when we say “I do” to Jesus: from this day forward, for better or worse, richer or poorer, in sickness and health, promising to love and cherish him until we see him face to face. Anyone ready to give their life to Jesus can walk to the baptistry during the prayer and say their “I do.” Don’t wait any longer.

  12. 33:24 Gift three: freedom

    Scripture: Mark 2:23–28

    • Josh loves all three gifts, but freedom is his favorite. One Sabbath, Jesus’ disciples picked heads of grain, and the Pharisees asked why they did what was unlawful on the Sabbath. Jesus answered: have you never read what David did when he and his companions were hungry, eating the consecrated bread lawful only for priests? “The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath. So the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath.”

    1. 34:14 Man’s additions to God’s command

      Scripture: Exodus 20

      • This was a big deal: God gave Israel the Sabbath after the exodus as a unique gift, so violating Sabbath traditions was like declaring war on the religious establishment. In Exodus 20 Moses prohibited work on the Sabbath without specifics; reading on, it was wrong to kindle a cooking fire, gather wood, carry things in and out, or sell.
      • Jewish tradition kept adding: travel restrictions, and a ban on tying knots, except women could tie knots in their girdles, so you couldn’t tie a rope to a bucket at the well, but you could tie your wife’s girdle to it. It became a massive, crushing, legalistic burden.
      • Jesus wasn’t violating God’s command to keep the Sabbath holy or encouraging his disciples to; he was violating man’s additions to it, seemingly on purpose. His argument is simple: if a hungry king and his men could eat the holy bread from the tabernacle, the Lord of the Sabbath can let his men eat grain from his fields. God has always cared more about helping people than holding tradition. If Jesus cared about man’s tradition, why not wait a day?

  13. 36:22 The shriveled hand

    Scripture: Mark 3:1–6 Mark 2:27

    • Another time, in the synagogue, a man with a shriveled hand was there, and some watched to see if Jesus would heal on the Sabbath. Jesus had the man stand up in front of everyone, hiding nothing, and asked whether it is lawful on the Sabbath to do good or evil, to save life or to kill. They remained silent. Angry and deeply distressed at their stubborn hearts, he told the man to stretch out his hand, and it was completely restored. Then the Pharisees plotted with the Herodians how they might kill Jesus.
    • Two chapters into Mark, they already want to kill him, because Jesus says time and again that human needs are more important than religious rituals. “I want the heart.” The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath.
    • Believers who are tuned out for being firm in the faith shouldn’t be discouraged; their Lord knew exactly what that was like. One point from Jesus shuts down the legalists: since evil doesn’t take a Sabbath, why should good? Save this person or let them die? In the kingdom of God the answer is always save a life.
    • Jesus watched their hearts harden, and it made him angry. A house full of tax collectors and traitors, a dinner party with sinners and swindlers, doesn’t make Jesus angry; only the self-righteous can do that. They would rather protect their traditions than see a man healed.

  14. 38:30 Not in this place

    • Not here, not in this place. As long as the Lord lets Josh lead this church, they will never grow weary of doing good or serving one another. The whole place will stop to pray for you, to point you back to Christ, and to watch you get in the water and say your “I do” to Jesus.
    • Come up with a marriage in shambles, and they will walk with you, because many have been there. Confess your addictions, and they will lock arms with you, because they are addicts too, turned to Christ. Drag what you are hiding in the shadows into the light, and they will speak grace and truth and shower you with the love of Christ. Your past and future don’t matter; the only thing that matters is how you respond to Jesus Christ right now.

  15. 39:41 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays that God is convicting him of how easy it is to develop programs, lists, and our own standards, and of looking at ourselves with condemnation when God sees us without blemish. He asks the Lord to make them servants, set apart for his work, with hearts broken for what breaks his.
    • He asks God to show them the hurting and make the church a place where people grab hands and help each other up: “It’s okay. Jesus loves you. I love you. You messed up, but let’s go to him,” running to Calvary again, as the first time, until he comes to take them home. “That’s the church.”
