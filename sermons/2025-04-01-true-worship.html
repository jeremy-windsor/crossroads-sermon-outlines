Overview

  1. 00:40 Imagine coming home from exile

    • Elijah asks the church to imagine being an Israelite returning from exile: stepping over broken stones where houses stood, weeds growing through once-busy streets, dust blowing through the ruins. Then you see where the temple used to be, the house of the true and living God, now a pile of rubble with the altar, the very heart of worship, buried beneath debris and decades of neglect.
    • You’d think the priority would be rebuilding the walls, securing the city, or laying the temple’s foundation. Instead the people do something that seems backwards: they rebuild the altar first. Having seen the destruction their pursuit of their own glory brought, they ground their efforts in a celebration of who God is and a repentant heart before laying a single brick.
    • Many of us are striving to rebuild things in our lives apart from God’s help, but the Lord wants us to submit to his way, his plan, and his timing. Are we sprinting toward our own ambition, or saying, “Lord, your will be done, not mine”? Elijah hopes the church learns from this passage that true revival isn’t emotionalism or signs and wonders but a return to the Lord, to worship and holiness. He then prays for the time together.

  2. 03:48 Week three of Reclaim: worship at the center

    Scripture: Ezra 3

    • Elijah, the worship pastor at Crossroads, greets the church and notes they are in week three of Reclaim, a series through the book of Ezra. He loves the book because it emphasizes God’s faithfulness and his providence, his sovereign and purposeful control over history, and also the importance of worship and devotion to the Lord as the center of our lives and efforts.
    • Today’s focus is the latter: what worship is, and how our perspective, actions, and ambitions change when we submit our lives in reverence to him. They will be all over Scripture, but grounded in Ezra 3, reading through the whole chapter.

  3. 05:01 Reading Ezra 3:1–6

    Scripture: Ezra 3:1–6

    • Elijah reads the first six verses: the people assemble as one in Jerusalem; Joshua, Zerubbabel, and their associates build the altar of the God of Israel in accordance with the law of Moses; despite their fear of the peoples around them they sacrifice morning and evening; they celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles and present the regular offerings, though the foundation of the Lord’s temple had not yet been laid.

  4. 06:10 Unity, the seventh month, and God’s timing

    Scripture: Ezra 3:1 Ezra 3:1 Leviticus 23 Hebrews 13:8

    • The first thing Elijah notices is the unity behind the decision. He loves how the ESV renders verse 1: the people gathered as one man to Jerusalem. The unity of God’s people has always mattered to God, which is part of why we still gather, for community, love, and encouragement, and to declare his praises with one voice as he commands.
    • Returning to Judah wasn’t enough; worship had to be resumed. The seventh month, Tishri, is the month of the Feast of Tabernacles, detailed in Leviticus 23, which commemorates Israel’s forty years wandering in the desert after slavery in Egypt. With the festival on the horizon, the people felt an urgency to rebuild the altar. What better way to celebrate a return from exile than to remember how faithful the Lord had been?
    • Elijah doesn’t believe in coincidences. Before a new foundation is laid, the Israelites get to submit their future plans to God and remember how faithful he has been to show them mercy and lead them toward restoration, though they didn’t deserve it. That should sound familiar: our God is the same yesterday, today, and forever, always good, kind, patient, and full of grace.

  5. 08:31 Do we have an urgency to worship?

    Scripture: John 4:24

    • Elijah’s first question: are we like the Israelites, making worship an utmost priority, or do we pay lip service to God once a week and check a box to feel virtuous? Your body being present and your lips moving doesn’t mean you are worshiping. Worship is a posture of the heart first, and what we do is only worship when it flows from a thankful heart that seeks to honor him.
    • He recalls Jesus with the Samaritan woman in the Gospel of John: God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth.

    1. 09:25 Worship in spirit and in truth

      • To worship in spirit refers to the inner, heartfelt, genuine nature of our adoration, beyond outward displays or formalities. To worship in truth means we don’t base our adoration on “the touchy feelies” and emotional moments, but on a true understanding of who God is and what he has revealed in his word, especially in the person and work of Jesus Christ.

  6. 10:04 Worship on God’s terms

    Scripture: Ezra 3:2 Ephesians 2:8–10

    • Verse 2 says they built the altar to sacrifice burnt offerings “in accordance with what is written in the law of Moses.” For them and for us, worship combines adoration, remembrance, and obedience to God’s commands. They didn’t invent their own methods; it is about approaching God on his terms, not ours.
    • The inward work of the Holy Spirit produces outward fruit: when there’s heart change, there’s life change. Elijah reads Paul in Ephesians 2: by grace you have been saved through faith, not by works, for we are God’s handiwork created in Christ Jesus to do good works. God renews our hearts and minds so we can offer our bodies in service. It’s not one or the other, it’s both; others should see the hope of the gospel in our lives, not just hear us “yap about it.”

    1. 11:30 Two core values: be doers and sing praise

      Scripture: Matthew 5:14–16

      • That is why Crossroads puts such a high premium on service and singing praise, two of its core values: be doers, and sing praise. Making them a priority brings encouragement and growth to us and overflows to others, so that through joyful praise and humble service those who don’t yet know him may draw nearer.
      • Jesus put it beautifully in Matthew 5: you are the light of the world, a town built on a hill cannot be hidden; let your light shine before others, that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father in heaven.

    2. 12:35 There’s no such thing as a private Christian

      • There is no such thing as a private Christian. The church was never meant to be a lighthouse locked from the inside where the faithful huddle and admire the glow; Christ makes his people a city on a hill, a bonfire in the black of night. The world isn’t suffering from too much Christian witness; it is starving for it.
      • Many believers live as though holiness were a private hobby rather than a public declaration. The Great Commission wasn’t given so we could build spiritual bomb shelters, but so the church may storm the gates of hell with the gospel and proclaim Christ’s lordship over every square inch of creation. So let’s not whisper when we were made to shout, or retreat when called to advance; let’s not be Sunday-morning Christians but 24/7, 365 Christians.

  7. 14:17 Verse 3: worship in spite of fear

    Scripture: Ezra 3:3

    • Despite their fear of the peoples around them, they built the altar and sacrificed morning and evening. Why? Because worship is the heartbeat of survival, the anchor that helps you stand firm when the winds of fear howl. They didn’t wait for safety to worship; faith puts God first when every earthly instinct says wait.
    • The hostility was real: likely pagan nations seeking their destruction, including the Assyrians occupying the former northern kingdom. A group under 50,000, just beginning to rebuild, was ripe for the taking, yet they proceeded. Later in the series, Elijah notes, their devotion does bring adversity.

    1. 16:01 Don’t let the world define a good Christian

      Scripture: John 7:24

      • Some care far too much about what the world thinks of their faith, letting the world rather than God’s word tell them what makes a good Christian. Elijah isn’t interested in those who reject Christ and hate his word telling him how to live.
      • Critics, often politicians or celebrities championing causes that contradict Scripture, cherry-pick Jesus: “Jesus says don’t judge,” “the most quoted verse by people who don’t actually read the Bible.” Jesus says don’t judge hypocritically, not don’t judge at all; in John 7:24 he says stop judging by mere appearances but judge correctly. Discernment is not a sin; it’s a command.
      • Or they weaponize “love your neighbor as yourself” to mean affirm my lifestyle choices or you hate me. They quote Jesus when it suits them, but ask whether Jesus is God in the flesh and they stop caring what he says. Don’t let the world pacify your devotion to Christ. They may guilt, hate, or threaten you, but the world isn’t asking the church to be quiet, it’s demanding it, and we serve the king who shattered the power of death.

    2. 18:11 No neutral ground

      • Either Christ is Lord of all or he’s not Lord at all. Obedience isn’t merely avoiding sin; it is staying faithful in the face of opposition. In a culture of absolute compromise, will you plant your feet on the unshakable foundation of God’s word or bow to the golden calf of cultural acceptance? We have it easy compared to the rest of the world, but if the heat is turned up we will need Christians with unashamed devotion and a strong backbone.
      • The cross of Christ is our altar. We come to it in the storm and in opposition, because this is where our help comes from and where fear bows before the majesty of God. Set the altar in its place: put Christ at the center, not later but right now. Nothing, not exile, enemies, or fear, can take him from you. Elijah recalls a song the church sings: “No power of hell, no scheme of man can ever pluck me from his hand.”

  8. 20:03 Verses 4–6: worship is a daily sacrifice

    Scripture: Ezra 3:4–6

    • Elijah reads verses 4–6 again. The daily rhythm of sacrifice points to a life of continual surrender and dependence on the Lord. Worship is not an event but a lifestyle; not a mood, a moment, or a musical experience. Worship is a sacrifice, and sacrifice is costly.

    1. 21:00 More than four or five songs on a Sunday

      Scripture: Romans 12:1–2 1 Thessalonians 5:16–18

      • Some are in danger of believing worship is four or five songs on Sunday and the occasional worship night, and as worship pastor it is Elijah’s job to break that thinking. Those gatherings celebrate God’s faithfulness with the saints but don’t replace daily dying to self. Worship found in a building for an hour on Sunday but absent in your obedience the rest of the week is not worship; it’s a performance.
      • Paul addresses this in Romans 12: offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God, your true and proper worship; don’t conform to the pattern of this world but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. And in 1 Thessalonians 5: rejoice always, pray continually, give thanks in all circumstances.
      • How often did the Israelites sacrifice? Daily. Paul’s language is intentional: true worship gives ourselves to God’s will instead of being wise in our own eyes, a daily decision to recognize our desperate need for Christ. You don’t sing on Sunday and live like the world on Monday; you lay your whole self, hands, feet, mind, desires, and ambitions, on the altar of obedience. Deny yourself, take up your cross, and follow him.

    2. 23:08 Spurgeon: a sacred discontent

      • Elijah reads Charles Spurgeon’s sermon “True Worship”: all these formalities, routines, and outward forms, yet no heart, no soul; Spurgeon prays God would put in us “a sacred discontent with the merely outward worship” and make us long to offer the sacrifice of a broken and contrite heart.
      • God isn’t fooled by empty words or impressed by half-hearted offerings. He demands everything, and for those in Christ, everything is exactly what we should long to give. True worship flows from a desire to be like Christ, holy and set apart, and before Israel could sing songs of praise, they had to return to the altar.

  9. 24:22 Run to the altar

    Scripture: 1 John 1:8–9

    • How many need to run to the altar this morning, carrying something to repent of? Maybe you are like the prodigal son, running from your father and afraid to come home. Elijah pleads: don’t get comfortable in exile. Our heavenly Father is full of grace and mercy and promises to forgive; all we need to do is come to him in repentance and lay our sins, burdens, doubts, and anxieties at the foot of the cross.
    • He reads the apostle John’s first letter: if we claim to be without sin we deceive ourselves, but if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness.

  10. 25:18 Verses 10–11: praise when the foundation is laid

    Scripture: Ezra 3:10–11

    • After a month celebrating God’s faithfulness, the people began rebuilding the temple. Elijah reads verses 10–11: priests with trumpets, Levites with cymbals, singing with praise and thanksgiving, “He is good; his love toward Israel endures forever,” and all the people giving a great shout of praise.
    • “You guys complain about the drums being loud”; imagine being there, with trumpets, cymbals, shouting, and singing. Are you coming on Sunday ready to sing with praise and thanksgiving, or too busy talking politics over coffee in the lobby?

    1. 26:24 “As long as I don’t miss the message”

      • As worship pastor, Elijah has heard people justify skipping the singing with “as long as I don’t miss the message,” to his face, as if corporate singing were an opening act. He jokes that it doesn’t hurt his feelings if they don’t like his voice, which is why gifted vocalists lead too.
      • What concerns him as a pastor is viewing that time as unnecessary. A seasoned believer should know better, but a new believer may not understand why we sing, so he wants to make it clear.

  11. 27:26 Two reasons singing praise is a necessity

    • There are two reasons singing praise is a necessity in the life of every believer: it is biblical, and it is natural.

    1. 27:32 First, it’s biblical

      Scripture: Psalm 149:1 Psalm 96:1–3 Psalm 47:6 Ephesians 5:18–20 Colossians 3 Zephaniah 3:17 Ezra 3:11

      • The longest book in the Bible, Psalms, is a book of songs for God’s people to sing to God. Singing is mentioned over 400 times in the Bible, and 50 of those are commands. Elijah reads Psalm 149 (“sing to the Lord a new song, his praise in the assembly of his faithful people”), Psalm 96 (“sing to the Lord, all the earth… declare his glory among the nations”), and Psalm 47 (“sing praises to God, sing praises”).
      • Paul twice commands singing in our gatherings: Ephesians 5, be filled with the Spirit, speaking to one another with psalms, hymns, and songs from the Spirit, implying that singing praise is evidence of the Spirit’s work in us; and Colossians 3, showing this is a prescription for the universal church, not one church at one time.
      • God himself sings: Zephaniah writes that the Lord your God “will rejoice over you with singing.” Elijah is throwing a lot of Scripture at the church to show God’s desire for us to sing across the whole narrative of Scripture. The celebration in Ezra 3, “he is good; his love toward Israel endures forever,” answered by a shout of praise, is one more example of how God wishes to be praised.

    2. 29:58 Don’t stand there like a statue

      Scripture: Romans 1:16 Revelation 4:8

      • Singing is often accompanied by clapping, shouting, lifting hands, and kneeling. Every expression in worship must come from Scripture, but Scripture gives much more freedom than we tend to use. He is good and his mercy endures forever, so let the world know.
      • Elijah doesn’t know how anyone can sing about the blood-bought mercy of Christ and stand like a statue, hands in pockets, lips barely moving. The gospel is not cold theology; it is the power of God unto salvation, and when that takes root something in you has to respond. David danced before the Lord with all his might; the psalmists shouted, clapped, and lifted their hands; Isaiah saw the throne room and came undone; the angels cry “holy, holy, holy” and never stop. Yet some think the right response to God’s glory is stoicism.
      • He gets the struggle: maybe you don’t like your voice, feel too dignified, or think it’s awkward. But this isn’t about you; it is about a God so glorious and worthy that the only right response is to sing back to him. If we can yell at a football game or belt out lyrics at a concert but go silent when the gospel is sung, something’s off.
      • The angels aren’t whispering in heaven, and the redeemed don’t mumble; they roar with joy. Sing when you feel it and when you don’t, in the storm and in victory, because worship isn’t about your personality but reality: Christ has won, death is defeated, and sinners have been adopted, washed, and set free. So where is your joy, your awe, your response to the infinite worth of Jesus? If the tomb is empty, and it is, our worship should look like people who actually believe it.

    3. 33:16 Second, it’s natural

      • Music is a beautiful gift from God, and we have natural inclinations toward it. It helps us remember words and stirs our emotions, making it an effective tool to focus our minds and hearts on what God has done, is doing, and promises to do. Elijah quotes his favorite preacher outside the Bible, Jonathan Edwards, in “Religious Affections”: the duty of singing praises seems appointed wholly to excite and express religious affections, because such is our nature that these things tend to move our affections.
      • He bets the church can still sing songs from VBS, and leads a few lines of “Every Move I Make” and “Father Abraham” to prove it. Even Alzheimer’s and dementia patients who can’t name their spouse or children often know every word of a hymn. That’s no coincidence: God has woven music into the fabric of creation.
      • Song is the language of worship, and worship is the purpose of our existence: man’s chief end is to glorify God and enjoy him forever. True joy demands expression, and the highest joy, the knowledge of Christ crucified and risen, demands the loudest song. Let the redeemed of the Lord sing; you were made for it.

    4. 35:50 Corporate worship is oxygen for your soul

      Scripture: Luke 19:40

      • Elijah admits he gets really excited about this and wants the church excited too. Corporate worship isn’t just a good idea; it is oxygen for your soul. You need the voices of the saints beside you reminding you what is true when your faith is weak. Sing not as a burden but as the natural outpouring of a heart captivated by Christ, “for if we will not, the very stones will cry out.”

  12. 36:27 Verses 12–13: joy and weeping together

    Scripture: Ezra 3:12–13

    • There’s more to this worship service. Elijah reads verses 12–13: older priests, Levites, and family heads who had seen the former temple wept aloud while many others shouted for joy, and no one could distinguish the shouts of joy from the weeping; the sound was heard far away.
    • The younger generation, who never saw the first temple’s glory, shouts at the beginning of something new. The older generation weeps because they remember the grandeur of Solomon’s temple. In that moment joy and sorrow meet in worship.
    • Isn’t that just like our lives, and like worship? True worship isn’t always neat and tidy; sometimes it mixes celebration and lament, “God, you are so good” and “God, we miss what was.” Both are lifted to the Lord, and both are part of the foundation of revival. Some are shouting because God is doing something new; some are weeping because things aren’t like they used to be. Either way, will we keep worshiping, because God is still faithful?

  13. 38:50 The true temple

    Scripture: 1 Peter 2:5

    • The good news is that the temple in Ezra’s day was a shadow of what we experience now. Elijah reads 1 Peter 2:5: you also, like living stones, are being built into a spiritual house to be a holy priesthood offering spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.
    • The true temple, the true presence of God, came in Jesus Christ and now lives in every believer through the Holy Spirit. In Christ every tear will be wiped away, every sorrow turned to joy, and one day the sound of worship will never be mixed with weeping again.
    • So sing, whether rejoicing or mourning, feeling God’s nearness or waiting for restoration, because the foundation has been laid not just of a temple but of a kingdom that cannot be shaken. One day we will stand before the throne where there is only joy, glory, and the unending song of the redeemed. Until then, we worship.

  14. 40:13 Closing prayer

    • Elijah prays that Crossroads would be a church unashamed of its praise that sings loud and brings both joy and sorrow to the foot of the cross. He wonders whether the weight of the world sometimes pacifies our worship and asks that each would lean on God’s strength, made perfect in weakness, so that through the hills and valleys of life their song never changes.
    • He thanks God most of all for the cross, where he took on flesh, lived the life we couldn’t, and died the death we deserved, and prays the church would worship with the same thankfulness, humility, and awe in every season, thanking him for joy and for suffering, because both give an opportunity to worship.
