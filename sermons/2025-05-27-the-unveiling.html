Overview

  1. 00:13 Pull back the veil

    • If we could peel back the ceiling and pull back the veil on the heavenly realms, we wouldn’t just see pews, lights, and a Sunday gathering; we would see a battlefield, a stage, and a throne room. The text in Ephesians today isn’t surface-level Christianity or tips for a better week.
    • Paul continues to pull back the curtain on the eternal plan of God, hidden for ages, now revealed through Christ and entrusted to the church. The gospel isn’t human invention; it is divine mystery unveiled in Christ and handed to us not to admire but to proclaim.
    • God didn’t save you so you could sit comfortably, but so you could speak boldly. He raised you from death so you could walk in life and victory, not shuffle around like some sort of redeemed zombie.
    • The church is the megaphone of God’s wisdom to the watching cosmos. Angels are leaning in, demons are watching, and the rulers and authorities are taking notes, and the sermon they hear is what you preach with your life: your holiness, unity, love, and boldness.

  2. 01:52 Into the war room

    Scripture: Ephesians 3:1–13

    • This morning is a step into the war room to see the mystery that called us, the grace that equips us, and the cosmic stage that surrounds us. The question is whether we will live like it matters.
    • Elijah reads Ephesians 3:1–13: Paul, the prisoner of Christ Jesus for the sake of the Gentiles, describes the mystery made known to him, that through the gospel the Gentiles are heirs together with Israel, and that through the church the manifold wisdom of God is made known to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly realms.

  3. 03:49 Written from a prison cell

    • Paul writes from a prison cell, yet the letter is filled with the power of the Holy Ghost and with purpose. He isn’t bitter or resentful; he is overwhelmed by the grace of God and the riches of his kindness. This love, this gospel, this mystery revealed is for us, not just to believe in but to live in and be filled by.
    • Today is about identity, power, and grace: the more we are filled with God’s grace, the more we are transformed, and the more we can love others like Jesus and be bold ambassadors of the gospel. You can only love others to the measure that you are full of the love of Christ.

  4. 04:56 An exciting season and a call to pray

    Scripture: 1 Peter 5:8

    • Elijah greets the church and appeals to them. The care center is up and running, camps, VBS, and worship nights are coming, social media and generosity are growing, and the church gets to be a light to the city weekly through Crossroads Cares. He says this not to exalt the church but to exalt Christ, who leads the ministry by the Holy Spirit, and to encourage everyone to lean into God’s purpose whether the fields are green or dry.
    • He asks the church to pray earnestly that God would protect it, from the elders and staff to the volunteers and congregation; that they would submit to Christ in reverence; and that they wouldn’t stumble into the church-growth models so much of the American church has fallen prey to, but trust the Lord to build his church in his time and way, by his Spirit. The enemy is crafty and prowls like a lion, so they need the prayers of the saints.
    • Rather than wait, he prays right then for God’s hand of protection over the church, from Josh to Chris to himself, the staff, elders, and congregation, in seasons of triumph and of mourning.

  5. 07:35 Where we’ve been: week five

    Scripture: Ephesians 1 Ephesians 2 Ephesians 3:1

    • In week five of a 12-week verse-by-verse series, about halfway through the letter, Elijah recalls where they have been. In chapter 1, Paul lifts us to the heavenly places, not to float but to see the sovereign God who predestined us before the foundation of the world, redeemed us by the blood of Christ, and sealed us by the Holy Spirit, so that we would praise his glorious grace with our very lives.
    • Chapter 2 drops us from the heavenlies into the graveyard: you were dead, not sick, struggling, or limping. Yet God, rich in mercy, raised us up with Christ and seated us with him. Salvation isn’t your achievement; it is a resurrection, grace from first breath to final heartbeat. And Christ shattered the dividing wall between Jew and Gentile; he didn’t call a truce, but made one new man, one household, one temple in which God now dwells.
    • So when Paul says in chapter 3, “For this reason I, Paul, a prisoner,” he isn’t apologizing or explaining himself away. He is doubling down, because when you have been caught up in the mystery of Christ, chains and prison doors are a minor detail.

  6. 09:54 The mystery revealed

    Scripture: Ephesians 3:1–6

    • Elijah reads verses 1–6 again. The mystery of Christ, hidden for generations and now unveiled by the Holy Spirit, is that Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of the promise in the Messiah through the gospel.
    • This isn’t God revising the script or changing his mind halfway through history; it is the curtain pulled back on a plan in motion from the foundations of the world: Gentiles grafted in, outsiders turned into heirs. That is great news for most in the room, who would qualify as Gentiles. God hasn’t loosened the borders of Israel; he has redrawn the map entirely.
    • The gospel doesn’t adjust; it reveals. What was hidden is now shouted from the rooftops. God isn’t subtle about his grace; he sends his apostles with fire in their bones and revelation in their mouths.

    1. 11:58 Exclusive in its claims, inclusive in its application

      Scripture: Galatians 3:26–29

      • Elijah prays the church would follow the apostles in carrying the message of grace with confidence in words and lives. Grace was never meant to be a kept secret in our cute little buildings; it is an all-consuming fire seeking to cover every square inch of this world.
      • It is exclusive in its claims and inclusive in its application: faith in Christ is the only way, no matter what progressive Christianity and new-age nonsense say, but the path is open to all kinds of people. As Paul says in Galatians 3, there is neither Jew nor Gentile, slave nor free, male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus, and if you belong to Christ you are Abraham’s seed and heirs according to the promise.

    2. 13:07 Blood-bought inclusion

      • We tend to hear “inclusion” as a Hollywood thing or a PR stunt from a progressive HR department. When Paul talks about inclusion, he means blood-bought inheritance. He isn’t opening a side door for a few Gentiles to slip in quietly; he is blowing the front doors off the house and saying, “This was always meant for you.”
      • Imagine living in the servants’ quarters your whole life and finding out the title deed already had your name on it. We were always in the King’s plan. The gospel doesn’t invite you to the table as a tolerated guest; it sits you down as a full-blooded son.

    3. 14:10 Don’t confuse mystery with murkiness

      • When Paul says “mystery,” he doesn’t mean a riddle, fog, or something for clever minds to unlock. He means something hidden in the heart of God for ages that has now burst into the light. The mystery isn’t a puzzle to be solved; it is a plan to be proclaimed.
      • Modern minds despise this clarity. We live in a fog of spiritual relativism where truth claims are treated as closed-minded and bigoted, but Paul calls this the mystery made plain: not vague spirituality for private consumption, but revealed glory for public declaration.
      • God didn’t whisper this truth so you could mutter it politely; he revealed it so you could herald it boldly. The gospel isn’t confusing; it’s confronting. It doesn’t ask you to solve it; it demands that you submit to it. It isn’t a fog but a blazing sun: Christ has come for the nations, the far off, the forgotten, for us.

    4. 15:39 You’ve been appointed

      • If that is true, everything in your life must be recentered around that revelation. You aren’t here or in the church by accident. Before the foundation of the world, God decided to display his grace in you, not as a self-righteous pedestal or a bludgeon to beat others with, but so you could walk in it and let it be a light that draws others to him.
      • So stop acting like you’re lucky to be here; you have been appointed. Get out of the spiritual fog and stop mumbling like you’re unsure. God has granted you victory. Now live like it.

    5. 16:40 To the box-checkers and the self-righteous

      • To those checking a box: Elijah loves them and would rather they be here than anywhere else, but their passivity is more harmful than helpful. If the gospel has been background noise, it is time to turn the volume up. God wrote you into this story before time began, so don’t drift, downplay, or compromise; get in step with the plan.
      • To those who think they have earned their way in: your self-righteousness does violence to your witness. The mystery of the gospel doesn’t flatter your résumé; it flattens it. Jew or Gentile, rich or poor, lifelong Christian or recent convert, the gospel makes one people, one inheritance, one glory, all to the praise of his glorious grace.
      • So are you living like a passive observer or a Spirit-empowered heir? What would change if you really believed God chose you to display the riches of his grace to the world? Elijah challenges the church to ask that this week.

  7. 18:28 Grace with a job description

    Scripture: Ephesians 3:7–9

    • Elijah reads verses 7–9. Paul was made a servant by the gift of God’s grace through the working of his power, and that grace was for preaching the boundless riches of Christ to the Gentiles.
    • This is grace with a job description. Grace didn’t give Paul a warm hug; it gave him a hammer. It didn’t put him in a spiritual hammock to coast and ponder the finest points of theology; it handed him a mission and said, “Go.” Paul isn’t floating on a cloud of forgiveness but marching with fire in his bones. This grace comes with power, and power never comes without purpose.

    1. 19:55 The least of all the saints

      • Paul’s humility in taking up God’s purpose jumped out at Elijah. Paul calls himself the very least of all the saints, and it isn’t false humility; he knows what he was before grace got him: a persecutor, a blasphemer, a self-righteous murderer. Do you remember where you were before grace got you? Don’t dwell on the past, but remember what God saved you from.
      • God turns his enemies into friends. The church just sang, “Once your enemy, now seated at your table,” and that is as much Paul’s reality as ours. When grace grips him, it won’t let him sit still. Unlike many celebrity pastors and megachurch leaders, Paul doesn’t preach for a platform or a brand; grace threw him to the ground and lifted him up with marching orders.

    2. 21:25 We have neutered grace

      • The modern American church has neutered grace: declawed, domesticated, soft, polite, sentimental, a slogan for T-shirts and coffee mugs, a spiritual bubble bath for people who don’t want to change. But grace made Paul a servant and turned a terrorist into a preacher.
      • The glorious grace of God puts steel in your spine and truth on your lips. Grace doesn’t coddle; it commissions. It wrecks you, confronts your sin, exposes your pride, and levels selfish ambition, and only then raises you up and sends you out. Paul didn’t receive grace and go on vacation; he got drafted. The man killing Christians was now building the church, and he knew what he had been forgiven of.
      • If your version of grace hasn’t radically reoriented your life, changed your desires, or made you dangerous to the gates of hell, what you are experiencing may not be grace but complacency. Elijah knows that didn’t land great, but he loves the church enough to tell the truth. If you say you’ve received grace, where’s the fruit, the fire, the fight? Grace isn’t a license to stay the same; it is power to war with your sin, serve when exhausted, speak when afraid, and love when it costs you.

    3. 23:59 What is our excuse?

      Scripture: Matthew 28:18–20

      • If Paul could be beaten, tortured, imprisoned, and shipwrecked, call himself the least, and still preach to the nations, what is our excuse? You don’t need a title, a microphone, or permission. Thinking the work of the gospel belongs only to people with “pastor” in their title is a consumerist mentality that has produced passive, ineffective Christians and the emasculation of the American church.
      • The Great Commission is for all believers. Our roles will look different, but we must not settle for a whisper when God has called us to shout. Stop waiting, get in the game, and proclaim the riches of Christ at home, at work, and in your circle. Grace sends.
      • In case the church has forgotten the mission, Elijah reminds them of Jesus’ words in Matthew 28: all authority has been given to him, so go and make disciples of all nations, and he is with us always. We have our marching orders; the only question is whether we will obey them.

  8. 25:38 The church is God’s megaphone

    Scripture: Ephesians 3:10–13 Revelation 12:10–11

    • Elijah reads verses 10–13. The mystery of the gospel forming one new people isn’t only for human eyes but for the rulers and authorities in heavenly places. The church is God’s megaphone declaring his victory to the powers in heaven and on earth. When the church walks in unity, truth, and grace, it preaches a sermon angels long to hear and demons long to silence. The church is God’s mic drop in the face of every power that comes against his plan.
    • This isn’t poetic language; this is war. Elijah reads Revelation 12: the accuser has been hurled down, and they triumphed over him by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony. We war against powers and principalities, but their fight is in vain.

    1. 27:27 Not saved to be safe

      Scripture: Romans 1:16–17

      • God has promised the church victory, so why have so many churches traded the fight of faith for Sunday convenience? We have made Christianity about comfort, safety, and good vibes when the New Testament calls us to blood, sweat, tears, and triumph. Why are we playing church when commissioned as an army, tiptoeing through culture wars afraid to offend demons, asleep in the barracks while war rages in our homes, schools, pulpits, and souls?
      • We weren’t saved to be safe or cozy, or filled with God’s power to post cute Bible quotes and stay quiet when the battle reaches the front door. The gospel isn’t therapy; it is transformation. Grace isn’t an anesthetic; it is ammunition. Paul writes in Romans 1:16 that he isn’t ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes; the righteous will live by faith.
      • Wake up: are you living by faith? You are the display of God’s wisdom, the voice that silences the accuser, ground zero of spiritual warfare. Don’t shrink back: stand up, speak up, bleed if you must, because the Lamb has already won. Will we walk in his victory or play well-behaved losers in a war already won?

  9. 29:41 Verse 12: access with freedom and confidence

    Scripture: Ephesians 3:12–13 Ephesians 3:12 Romans 5:1–2

    • Before responding in worship, Elijah rereads verses 12 and 13. Verse 12 is one of the most scandalous sentences Paul ever wrote. To the Jewish reader, access to God was restricted by temple curtains, sacrifices, and fear; to the Gentile reader, being welcomed into the presence of a holy and just God was absurd. But Paul says you can walk into the throne room, not head down, not through a priest, not after a guilt trip, but with freedom and confidence.
    • You aren’t a cosmic benchwarmer or a second-tier saint. In Christ you don’t need to grovel outside the temple; you have been handed the keys to the throne room, and the King calls you by name. This fuels everything Paul has said: we proclaim the mystery, suffer well, and love sacrificially, not to earn favor, but because we already have full access to the God of glory.
    • Romans 5 opens with it: justified through faith, we have peace with God and access by faith into this grace, and we boast in the hope of the glory of God. Are you boasting, believer? Access to God isn’t the reward of the spiritually elite but the birthright of the blood-bought. Don’t squander it by refusing to drag your sin into the light; God invites you into relationship and promises to go before you and set your feet on solid ground when you fall short.

  10. 32:09 Verse 13: suffering that is your glory

    Scripture: Ephesians 3:13

    • Paul writes from prison, hardly a picture of victorious ministry by worldly standards, and yet the man with every reason to be discouraged tells them not to be discouraged. He understands his suffering is a testimony to the work of the Spirit. He isn’t a victim of Caesar but a vessel of Christ, dedicated to preaching the gospel and equipping the saints.
    • If you have been sleeping, wake up. If you hear nothing else, hear this: to silence the enemy, don’t just preach loud when life is good; bleed well when life gets hard. Hell can handle a loud church; what it can’t handle is a faithful one that worships through suffering and clings to Christ when the story gets dark.

    1. 33:31 Your suffering confirms the gospel

      Scripture: Revelation 12:11

      • Paul calls his suffering their glory because it confirms the gospel he preaches: a suffering Savior producing suffering servants to produce a victorious church declaring the praises of a God who wastes nothing. They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony.

    2. 33:58 Approach boldly, suffer faithfully, live loudly

      Scripture: Romans 8:28–30

      • Discouraged by your weakness? You have access. Overwhelmed by your calling? You have boldness. Afraid your pain or past sin disqualifies you? Paul says no, it is your glory. In Christ the mystery is revealed, the mission is clear, the cosmos is watching, and the throne is open. So approach boldly, suffer faithfully, live loudly.
      • The world won’t applaud and hell will rage, but the sovereign God is for us and working in all things for our good. Elijah reads Romans 8: in all things God works for the good of those who love him, and those he predestined, called, and justified, he also glorified. Take heart in seasons of mourning and loss and of harvest and gain.
      • There is no better example than the cross: God used the darkest day in history to bring about the greatest good the world has ever known. How much more can we trust him to turn our mourning into dancing and our suffering and shame into glory?

  11. 35:58 Come to the table

    • Before the Lord’s Supper, Elijah implores the church not to make it empty tradition. At the table we proclaim the Lord’s death over our lives until he comes again. It is where the mystery of the cross becomes personal, where we stop striving and performing and start proclaiming, because the meal is about what he brought to us, not what we bring to God.
    • If you feel unworthy, good; that is the point. It isn’t about your résumé but his blood. If you have been discouraged, distracted, or disengaged, the throne is open and his grace is for you; if you have been coasting, this is the moment to repent, reset, and return to the call.
    • Come with freedom and confidence, not because everything is going well, but because Christ has won the war: as citizens of a kingdom, saints called to proclaim, sons and daughters with nothing to prove and everything to receive. The bread and cup are your access to the King, your commission to love and live boldly, and your glory to suffer if need be for the gospel.

  12. 38:00 Closing prayer

    • Elijah thanks Jesus for living the life we couldn’t and dying the death we deserved, and asks him to root out any complacency, to have his way in their hearts, this place, and this ministry, to help them discern what needs to be brought into the light and what idols need to be torn down, and to help them lift up a song in the middle of the storm.
