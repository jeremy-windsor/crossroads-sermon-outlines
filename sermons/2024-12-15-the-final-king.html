Overview

  1. 02:07 How many of you love your name?

    • By show of hands, who loves their name? Parenting below average, but they nailed the name. Who is still trying to figure out what their parents were thinking? Josh offers grief counseling.
    • Josh loves his name, a preacher’s name. His first name connects him with a man of God surrounded by a nation of wimps unwilling to walk into God’s promise, who went in anyway against insurmountable odds, a military leader who led his home well because they served the Lord. His middle name, David, is a shepherd who slew giants, a warrior king, poet, and songwriter filled with the Spirit who loved the Lord with all his heart, and also a royal screwup, “so that checks out.”
    • His last name makes people think of the legendary lawman known for Tombstone, Arizona and the shootout at the OK Corral, with a reputation for being gruff, tough, and fearless. His parents hit a bullseye; his sisters would tell you they didn’t get so lucky.

  2. 04:22 Names can be funny, but they aren’t usually informative

    • It could have gone another way. In 2020, some parents named their children Corona. Then there are names like Anita Bath, Amy Stake, and Terry Bow. One psychologist studied over 15,000 juvenile delinquents and found those with odd or embarrassing names were in trouble four times as often; of course they were, they had to fight to survive recess and defend their Snack Pack. Kids are cruel, so please research baby names and run them past a trusted friend.
    • We name children with no idea how they will turn out. Josh’s parents didn’t know he would be a strong leader who loves music and wants to punch mean people in the face (he doesn’t anymore; the Spirit tells him no). Joshua was a top-five boys’ name from 1983 to 2008, and plenty of Joshuas don’t know the Lord.
    • A name can be funny or embarrassing, but it usually isn’t informative. And his parents conveniently waited until they knew he was a boy; they just got lucky picking a name that describes him.

  3. 08:08 A child named 700 years before he was born

    Scripture: Luke 1:28–33 Philippians 2:9

    • Today Josh tells of a child named 700 years before he was born, the most important name ever given. When Gabriel came to Mary he said, “Greetings, you who are highly favored, the Lord is with you,” and Mary was greatly troubled; we find comfort in “the Lord is with you,” but she was troubled. The angel told her not to be afraid: she would bear a son and call him Jesus, the Son of the Most High, who would reign on David’s throne forever, and his kingdom would never end.
    • Time itself is divided by the birth of this child. This is the name above all names, the most powerful name in the world: Jesus Christ.

  4. 09:25 Isaiah 9: a ray of light in the gloom

    Scripture: Isaiah 9 Isaiah 7

    • As we pick up in Isaiah 9, things look bleak: defeat, captivity, famine, distress, darkness, anguish, because God’s people turned their backs on the Lord and trusted in the kings of Assyria and Egypt. They walked by sight, not by faith.
    • But a ray of light comes, first in Isaiah 7 with the promise of Immanuel, fleshed out in Isaiah 9: the Messiah will establish his kingdom. Isaiah 9 refers to his birth, his first advent, and his reign, his second advent, and gives some of his names and titles.

  5. 10:28 Reading Isaiah 9:1–7

    Scripture: Isaiah 9:1–7

    • Josh reads the first seven verses: there will be no more gloom for those in distress; he will honor Galilee of the nations; the people walking in darkness have seen a great light; he has shattered the yoke that burdens them; for to us a child is born, to us a son is given, and the government will be on his shoulders, and he will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace; of the increase of his government and peace there will be no end, and the zeal of the Lord Almighty will accomplish this.

  6. 12:06 Unto Us: “There will be no more gloom”

    Scripture: John 8:12

    • The series is called Unto Us because the church wanted to look at a few classic texts in Isaiah to build its understanding and joy in the true meaning of Christmas. Josh loves the opening phrase, “There will be no more gloom.” Isaiah contrasts light and darkness, and Jesus uses the same imagery in John 8: “I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness, but will have the light of life.”

    1. 12:44 Galilee of the Gentiles

      Scripture: Matthew 4:13–16 John 7:41

      • We know this prophecy refers to Christ because Matthew quotes it eight centuries later: leaving Nazareth, Jesus lived in Capernaum, in the area of Zebulun and Naphtali, to fulfill what Isaiah said: Galilee of the Gentiles, the people living in darkness have seen a great light.
      • Zebulun, Naphtali, and Galilee were devastated when the Assyrian army came; Galilee was lost, and a Galilean Messiah seemed crazy. John 7 shows the attitude in Jerusalem: some said he was the Messiah, while others asked, “How can the Messiah come from Galilee?” Matthew reminds his Jewish audience that Isaiah prophesied it long before.
      • If you are a skeptic, Josh hopes you wrestle with this all Christmas season: Jesus convincingly fulfilled Old Testament prophecy time and again, and if that isn’t enough evidence that the Bible is reliable, truthful, and authoritative, nothing ever will be. Just as Isaiah predicted eight centuries earlier, the despised, sin-stained, rebellious Galileans were the first to set eyes on the Messiah. Jesus launched his ministry in Samaria and Galilee rather than Jerusalem and Judea, a reminder that his gospel was for the whole world.

  7. 14:54 Step into the light

    Scripture: Isaiah 9:2

    • On those living in the land of deep darkness a light has dawned. We need that light; you need it; the world needs it.
    • Josh pictures someone walking the dog at sunrise during this week’s cold snap, if you can call it that, stuck in the shadow of a house while the pup makes a pit stop, with the sun breaking right in front of them. What will you do? Step into the light. That is the gospel invitation of the Christmas season. He could not make it any more clear who he is and what he came to do.

  8. 16:00 “To us a child is born, to us a son is given”

    Scripture: Isaiah 9:6 Isaiah 9 Isaiah 11

    • Verse 6 shows both the humanity and the deity of the Lord. Josh once kept inviting Jehovah’s Witnesses in for a meal and preaching salvation by grace through faith, not works, until they stopped coming; maybe the gospel is too simple for some. You can present and defend the Trinity with two page turns in Isaiah: in Isaiah 9 a child who is Mighty God, and in Isaiah 11 the Spirit of the Lord resting on him. Father, Son, and Spirit.

    1. 16:57 Heaven gives, earth receives

      Scripture: John 1:1–3 John 3:16–17

      • We accelerate over “unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given” too quickly. Heaven gives; earth receives. Christ’s birth was not his beginning, because he was there in the beginning: John 1 says the Word was with God and was God, and through him all things were made. Your conception was your beginning; the manger in Bethlehem was not Jesus’ beginning. He is God, the Alpha and Omega, with no beginning and no end.
      • From heaven’s perspective, for us Jesus Christ arrived, but for heaven Jesus Christ departed: God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, not to condemn the world but to save it. Heaven gave him so that earth could receive him. Merry Christmas.

  9. 18:27 Wonderful

    • He will be called Wonderful. Words meant to have power get overused and lose it: this restaurant, car, or movie is wonderful. The church loves recommending things to its pastor, and Josh honestly checks them out, and often doesn’t know what they saw. A word like “wonderful” gets dumbed down when you don’t know the difference between Taco Bell and Joy Ride Taco House (and if you don’t, go find out).
    • He is awesome, powerful but gentle, wise but humble, truthful and gracious, radiant and compassionate, glorious, magnificent, breathtaking, supreme, unparalleled, incomprehensible. There is no other name like Jesus. He is man and God, carpenter and Creator. He is perfect, and Josh hasn’t just heard about him; he has walked with him.

  10. 20:43 Counselor

    Scripture: Psalm 73:24

    • He will be called Wonderful Counselor. Psalm 73: you guide me with your counsel, and afterward you will take me into glory. He is doing a work in Josh; is he working in you? Some Google things (“very 2010 of you”), some still ask their old man, most ask AI, Siri, or Alexa. As Josh leans on him and seeks his counsel, he knows him in a way he never has before, and it grows every day.
    • He is the Word; the Creator makes himself available to creation through the Counselor. When Josh leans on him, he is reminded that he is his rock and salvation; remaining in him he is sustained by the true vine; when he fails he has an advocate; when he calls out he has a mediator; when he confesses he has a Savior; when life gets shaky he has a cornerstone; when he needs help he has the Servant; and when he is not enough, one stands beside him saying, “I am.”
    • Josh loses sleep thinking you might not know him like this, and it saddens him to think you might celebrate Christmas with no idea whom you’re celebrating. All creation, history, humanity, and his church are shouting his name, and even those who don’t call on him are shouting his name. Are you prepared to receive his counsel? Step into the light.

    1. 23:02 Counsel for the defense

      Scripture: John 8:7

      • “Counselor” can also mean an attorney who defends or prosecutes. The husband-and-wife law firm on the billboard along the 17 can’t win this case for you, no matter how big their billboard. But Jesus can. He ran to counsel the woman caught in adultery and ended that trial with one sentence: “Let any one of you who is without sin be the first to throw a stone.”
      • Jesus doesn’t want your perfection; he did that already. He wants your pursuit, for you to abide in him and stay near him whether the crowd is with you or you’re on your knees in the dirt alone. If you have Jesus, you lack nothing.

    2. 24:04 Saved but unwilling to be guided

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:16–17

      • Many Christians are happy to be saved but don’t want any guidance: give me salvation but not instruction. Josh warns as clearly as he can: Satan salivates at the chance to convince you you’re saved while you live a fruitless life. Where will you go when you find out you’re really not?
      • The more time you spend with the Wonderful Counselor, the more you will look like him. 2 Timothy says all Scripture is God-breathed and useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting, and training in righteousness. The Wonderful Counselor breathed it out; the question this Christmas is, are you breathing it in?

  11. 25:10 Mighty God

    • Early in his walk Josh wrestled with God being a baby. “Mary, Did You Know?” gives the moment reverence, but Josh has been in delivery rooms, and they are intense; nobody is singing nice romantic baby songs. Once, while his wife was pushing, he had to lie flat on the floor while nurses rushed to give him alcohol swabs, graham crackers, and apple juice. There goes the man card. God answered Eve’s disobedience with birth pains and then chose that way to send his Son, and people say God has no sense of humor.
    • The one who spoke something into nothing became an embryo. Josh remembers his first diaper change, black sticky tar and 58 wipes, and the fear of realizing he hadn’t thought this through: “Man up, you got a boy to raise.” He honestly wondered whether Jesus had a baby glow or a halo, and whether his first diaper was as rough on Joseph as Nolan’s was on Josh. He isn’t mocking; he struggled with the Creator of the universe depending on a young couple for life: God as a fetus, Jehovah in diapers.

    1. 28:26 What makes the incarnation amazing

      • Then Josh realized that is what makes the incarnation so amazing and unbelievable, why it takes a month to wrap our hearts around it: God came in a humble, helpless way, with no hype, no palace, no shelter, no crown. We are quick to follow Christ’s example after his baptism, but he sets an example in the manger: humble yourself before God; become like a child.
      • “Mighty God” in Hebrew is El Gibbor: God’s strong hero or king. Jesus is the hero of the universe. The Eternal One entered time, an arrival only God could accomplish, sent to conquer sin and death as the sacrificial lamb for mankind. He was and is Immanuel, God with us.

    2. 29:37 Small packages, big statements: write your testimony

      Scripture: Isaiah 9:2

      • God has never been shy about using small packages to make a big statement. Josh never thought God could use him the way he has, and if God can use him, he can use you. Too many Christians underestimate the statement God wants to make with their lives; the point isn’t to sit and listen to Josh. You have a message.
      • This Christmas, write out your testimony, one page, not six for the long-winded, and at the bottom write who God wants you to share it with, then go tell it. The people walking in darkness have seen a great light, and Josh’s testimony is that he is one of those people. If Mighty God did it for him, he can do it again: heal your broken heart, meet your every need. He overcame death; nothing is too hard for the Lord.

  12. 30:48 Everlasting Father

    Scripture: Psalm 103:13 Proverbs 3:12

    • Josh loves this third title, though he had to wrestle with it. It speaks to his concern for us: he cared for us before we knew we needed it, cares for us now, and cares for our future. You know you will live forever, which is terrible if you don’t believe and great if you do; if you believe, you have a Father forever.
    • A father shows care, protection, and discipline. Psalm 103: as a father has compassion on his children, so the Lord has compassion on those who fear him. Because he is everlasting, his compassion is permanent: based not on your performance but on his permanence, bringing stability and consistency where you have none. Proverbs 3: the Lord disciplines those he loves, as a father the son he delights in. He delights in you forever.

    1. 32:11 How is Jesus the Everlasting Father?

      Scripture: Hebrews 13:8 Daniel 7:14

      • People struggle with this title because these are names for Jesus: “Josh, I’m trying to keep my doctrine of the Trinity organized, and Isaiah comes in with Everlasting Father.” Break it down. “Everlasting” refers to his eternal nature; he existed before creation, and Hebrews 13 says Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever. It also speaks to his eternal kingdom: Daniel 7 says he was given authority, glory, and sovereign power, with an everlasting dominion that will not pass away. Christ reigns in believers’ hearts now, and “everlasting” also points to a future literal earthly kingdom where he will reign. He is our constant now and forever.

    2. 33:14 Compassion and protection

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:2 Matthew 11:28–30 John 10:28–30

      • “Father” speaks to his compassion and care: he is our good shepherd and the way. As we would follow a good earthly father’s example, we fix our eyes on Jesus, the perfecter of faith. He shows his compassion in Matthew 11: “Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest… for I am gentle and humble in heart.” That sounds like a good father.
      • Like a good father, he is a protector. One of Josh’s favorite verses is in John 10: “I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; no one will snatch them out of my hand… I and the Father are one.” Through life’s uncertainties we can lean on the unchanging nature of Christ.

    3. 34:37 A temporary father

      • Josh considers himself a good father (ask his kids), but he is only their temporary father, and he doesn’t know how much time he has left with them. The fatherly care of Christ will be constant for them long after he is gone. That is what he wants them to know: if you don’t have me, you will always have him. He praises God for his faithfulness.

  13. 35:13 Prince of Peace

    • The fourth title comes with incredible comfort. If you’ve had bad leaders, the first question about an everlasting King is what sort of king he is. The wonderful news: he is a peaceful King who comes in peace, establishes peace, and is the prince not of relative peace but of lasting peace.
    • There have been 13 years of war for every year of peace. We are drowning in conflict; everyone wants peace on earth but no one delivers, because sin keeps throwing the world into chaos. Only one would dare run on the campaign slogan “enduring peace,” and his name is Sar Shalom, Prince of Peace.

    1. 36:41 Peace with God, with others, with ourselves

      Scripture: Romans 5:1–2 Hebrews 12:14 John 16:33

      • First, peace with God. Romans 5:1: since we have been justified through faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.
      • Second, peace with others. Hebrews 12: make every effort to live in peace with everyone and to be holy. The Holy Spirit helps us be patient, forgiving, gentle, kind, and generous, especially with brothers and sisters in Christ.
      • Third, peace with ourselves. In John 16, speaking to men who would give their lives to preserve the message, Jesus says, “I have told you these things so that in me you may have peace. In this world you will have trouble. But take heart! I have overcome the world.” Jesus has settled our hearts: our sins are forgiven, our guilt is gone, our present is Christ, and our future is heaven. Is the Prince of Peace reigning in your life? If not, step into the light.

  14. 38:07 The government on his shoulders: the final King

    Scripture: Isaiah 9:6 Isaiah 9:7

    • Right before the four names, verse 6 says the government will be on his shoulders. Clearly our government is not on his shoulders. Many Christians have sighed with relief since November 5th, but we still have big problems, and if it were on his shoulders there would be none. The phrase refers to a future event when Christ comes again. The child has been given, but before the government would be on his shoulders, he first had to carry a cross on his shoulders.
    • Verse 7: of the increase of his government and peace there will be no end; he will reign on David’s throne with justice and righteousness forever, and the zeal of the Lord Almighty will accomplish this. Christ will reign, and when he does there will be no end. He will not be a king among other kings; he will be the final King.

  15. 39:16 The gospel according to Isaiah

    Scripture: Hebrews 4:15 1 Thessalonians 4:16 2 Peter 3:13

    • Eight centuries before Christ was born, the gospel was announced: to us a son is given. The God-man, truly God and truly man, lived the perfect life, fulfilled the whole law, was tempted in every way yet did not sin, willingly died in the place of sinners, absorbed the wrath of God, and was forsaken on the cross so that those who believe could be forgiven. Three days after he was buried he walked out of the tomb triumphant over death, hell, and Satan. Death died in AD 33, and the one who killed death appeared to 500 eyewitnesses before ascending to rule at the right hand of God.
    • He is coming again. Those who died in him will rise first, those alive at his return will never die, and those who trust in Jesus will be with him forever in a new heaven and new earth where righteousness dwells, and his kingdom will never end.
    • The zeal of the Lord Almighty will accomplish this. He doesn’t know how to fail; his speaking is his doing. Whatever you are going through, you can have absolute, 100% confidence that the Lord will do it.

  16. 40:59 Listen to the Wonderful Counselor

    Scripture: Matthew 11:28

    • Josh pleads with all he has left: if you’ve been ignoring the Wonderful Counselor, ignore him no longer. Listen when he says, “Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.” It is the most loving, genuine counsel you will ever receive. Seek Christ, and when you find him, rejoice. Merry Christmas.

  17. 41:55 Closing prayer before the table

    • Josh prays that he just wants to run to the Father, like a son embracing his military father coming home through the airport after months or years, but the Lord has told him to wait, so he will wait. He prays that anyone else feeling what he feels would be calm and confident, not condemned but saved, rescued, accepted.
    • He thanks God for that peace and comfort: they are no longer at war with him, they come to the table as guests in his home to dine with him, and he never grows tired of hearing them sing or call on his name. He loves them now and forever.
