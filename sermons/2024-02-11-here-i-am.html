Overview

  1. 00:29 Genesis 22 and the covenant promise

    Scripture: Genesis 22 Genesis 12:2–3 Genesis 15:4 Genesis 21:2

    • As the series in Genesis continues, the church lands in chapter 22. Genesis has two major themes: chapters 1 to 11 are about creation, and chapters 12 to 50 are about the covenant.
    • In chapter 12 the Lord promises Abram a great nation, a great name, and blessing: “God’s like, I got your back.” In chapter 15 comes the promise of a son from his own body. In chapter 21 Sarah becomes pregnant and bears a son in Abraham’s old age, at the very time God had promised.
    • Josh asks the church to imagine the joy Abraham and Sarah must have felt: finally, their miracle son, the first baby under the promise, the first star.

  2. 01:40 Josh’s joy over his son Nolan

    • Josh remembers the joy when his son Nolan arrived: the first diaper (“it was disgusting”), strapping him into a tiny car seat, the slippery first bath, the first words, the first time on the toilet.
    • Watching him grow up has been an amazing gift. Nolan is becoming a young man with passions and gifts; he loves the Lord, they talk about salvation, he loves to worship, and he is learning drums, “so now if you complain about the drum, you got to go through me.”
    • Josh was proud the other day when he played his kids a show on Amazon that celebrates Satan and asked them to call out everything wrong with it. Both kids tore it apart: “Scripture says this … God’s not like that.”

  3. 03:45 Abraham’s bond with Isaac

    • Josh imagines Abraham felt the same about Isaac: telling his son about God’s promises, the time the Lord cut the covenant with a blazing torch, the time God destroyed Sodom for its wickedness. They were bonding.
    • It isn’t only memories that grow our connection to a person; it is also the circumstances that lead us to them. Josh’s connection with Nolan deepens when he considers Nolan’s mother and the trials he faced before meeting her. Abraham would have been no different.

  4. 04:50 Nine trials before the greatest test

    Scripture: Genesis 22:1

    • Abraham faces his most difficult trial in chapter 22, but it is not his only one. The chapter starts, “Sometime later God tested Abraham,” and during that time we count nine great trials: leaving his homeland, a famine, his wife taken, fighting kings, running to Hagar, circumcision at an old age (“I shouldn’t have to explain why that’s a trial”), Sarah captured, sending Hagar away, and dealing with Ishmael.
    • God was working on Abraham through all of it, strengthening him for an even greater test.

    1. 05:44 God doesn’t put heavy burdens on weak shoulders

      • God won’t test a baby believer the way he tests a mature believer. If you are on a milk diet, the Lord isn’t having you bench press to failure. God grows our faith through testing, and the trials don’t get easier; expect them to become exceedingly great as you look more like Jesus. Don’t mistake following Jesus for a smooth, paved path to heaven.
      • Our country’s most elite soldiers, Navy SEALs, Delta Force, Army Rangers, go on the hardest missions. BUD/S, the Navy SEAL training ground, is a 24-week course with a dropout rate around 80 percent; many dream of being elite, but few endure the test.
      • Why would God grow you as a soldier for Jesus only to send you on the same missions he sent you on as a grunt? The more you look like Christ, the bigger the storm you can endure and the greater the missions he sends you on.

    2. 07:48 A Christian school this fall

      • This has hit Josh square in the face as lead pastor. After launching Crossroads Cares in 2021 he thought, “I think we’re good now,” but God keeps putting things on his heart.
      • God has put it on his heart to start a Christian school, launching this fall in the church’s space. They don’t have all the answers, but they are passionate to do more to equip children with a biblical foundation so they aren’t afraid to send them to the universities: “That’s our mission field. We want to equip them to take it back.”

  5. 08:54 A season of rest doesn’t mean the work is done

    • As chapter 22 begins, Abraham has had a time of rest from the trials, enjoying his family and raising the promise. But the Lord is calling.
    • Believer, take notes: a season of rest doesn’t mean the Lord is done putting you to work, and still waters don’t mean he won’t send a storm. Are you as excited to answer his call while resting as you are in the storm?

  6. 09:30 “Here I am”

    Scripture: Genesis 22:1

    • God tested Abraham, called his name, and Abraham replied, “Here I am.” Josh compares Adam and Abraham: when the Lord reached out to Adam after the fall, he hid; when the Lord called Abraham, he said, “Here I am, Lord.”
    • In trials we tend to hide from the Lord and make excuses to distance ourselves; at peace we are more available. Strong believers say “Here I am” whether there are waves or not.

  7. 10:10 A test Abraham didn’t see coming

    • Josh is a terrible test taker. He can know a subject front to back, but when the little bubble shows up, he falls apart. He struggled in school his whole life until college, where so much of the work was papers and presenting.
    • Many in the room have heard this story and know a test is coming. Abraham doesn’t. He doesn’t know how chapter 22 is going to go; he is living it. Keep that in mind as we learn from him.

    1. 11:14 Tested, not tempted

      • There is a big difference between being tested and being tempted. God tests us to increase our faith; Satan tempts us to destroy it. Satan works to get you to fall apart; God works to make you more dependent.
      • This isn’t a WWE showdown, God and Abraham entering the Octagon to see who comes out on top. God is for Abraham, not against him.

  8. 11:47 “Your only son, whom you love”

    Scripture: Genesis 22:2

    • God said, “Take your son, your only son, whom you love.” Josh comes from a long line of only sons: his dad, himself, and his son. With two sons he would love them both, but there is something to be said about there being only one.
    • God has watched Abraham with Isaac and knows how much he loves that boy. Unlike many fathers today who think fatherhood ends with getting a woman pregnant, Abraham knows showing up matters, and he takes seriously his job to shape Isaac into a man of faith.
    • Some say Isaac was between 15 and 35; some commentators say a teenage boy, and Josephus says he was in his 30s. We don’t really know, but Abraham was 100 when this miracle baby was born. He tucked him in, taught him to hunt, fish, and pray; they counted the stars and remembered God’s promises together, a bond so deep only death could break it.

  9. 13:10 Go to Moriah: “go kill your son”

    Scripture: Genesis 22:2 2 Chronicles

    • Then God hits him with this: take Isaac and go to the region of Moriah. Fun fact: according to 2 Chronicles, that’s where Solomon built the temple and where the sacrificial system would be established. Sacrifice him there as a burnt offering on a mountain I will show you. In other words, “Abraham, go kill your son.”
    • Josh understands how people react, because he reacted the same way. Murder is against the sixth commandment; God punished Cain for murdering his brother. A father’s role is to protect his children; Abraham waited 25 years for this son; and a burnt offering means killing the boy, dismembering him, and burning him until he is consumed. “This is messed up.”

    1. 14:32 Josh’s own wrestling

      • After watching his parents lose a child last year, and watching his dad preach at his daughter’s funeral, Josh struggled more with this text. It is one thing to speak at your child’s funeral; it is another to be the one responsible for the death.

    2. 15:08 “Here I am, Lord. What’s next?”

      • Every step of the way God said go and Abraham obeyed. Then God calls and Abraham answers, eager: “What’s next? I can’t wait to see what you have for us.” And God says, “Go kill your son. No, not that one. That one.” How is he going to explain this to Sarah?
      • It is the greatest test God has ever put on his creation. It is one thing to trust God while you wait for something, and another to trust and obey him after you have received it: “I have it. Why do I need God?”

  10. 16:11 Loving God above his dearest gifts

    Scripture: Matthew 10:37 Romans 4:21

    • Sometimes the Christian is called to step away from that thing to such a degree that they step away from those dearest to them. Jesus doubles down in Matthew 10: anyone who loves father or mother, son or daughter more than him is not worthy of him. This radical call to obedience isn’t some obscure Old Testament event.
    • Christians will be marked by love for one another, but if asked they would give it all up for God; they would rather deny their mother than deny their Creator. Believers learn from Abraham: Isaac was loved dearly, “but let Isaac die before I failed to trust God.” Paul says in Romans 4 that Abraham was fully convinced God was able to do what he promised.

  11. 17:12 Two great loves: Isaac or Yahweh

    • Abraham is an old man who could be a grandpa soon. Josh is looking forward to being a grandpa himself: watching his kids be interrupted, clean up vomit, and get talked back to, “talk about payback.”
    • Abraham had to choose between two great loves, Isaac or Yahweh. What’s it going to be?
    • Josh calls it a gentle rebuke (“when I’m rebuking you I’m hard on you … just kidding”). He has been convicted himself: many have made the kids God blessed them with into idols. Balance. We are quick to make the gifts into gods, but you came into this world with nothing, everything you have is because of him, and when you die “you don’t get a checked luggage.”

  12. 18:37 Early the next morning: obedience without words

    Scripture: Genesis 22:3

    • Early the next morning Abraham loaded his donkey, took two servants and Isaac, cut the wood, and set out. How can we not admire the lack of words from Abraham here?

    1. 19:09 Christians talk too much

      • “Maybe I’m just a jerk,” Josh says, but he thinks Christians talk too much. Most are anxious to be heard, but few are content walking faithfully behind the scenes doing the work of the Lord.
      • He has frustrating meetings with people who come in to say, “We love your preaching, but…” He admits there are blind spots and ways to improve, but often they wish the church looked more like the one they just left.
      • He prays for more Christians who let their walking do their talking, and celebrates Jean and Paul, who have served families in the nursery for years without a complaint; Peggy and Steve, who taught his children about Jesus for years and asked nothing in return; Tracy, Chuck and Jen, Mike, and Michelle. They don’t care about recognition, which is exactly why we should give it: to God be the glory.

    2. 20:52 Rise early and just go

      • Anyone can talk about or plan a trip to Mount Moriah, but very few after a sleepless night will rise early, put their head down, load the donkey, and just go.
      • Abraham is one of the few: immediate obedience, full submission, no questioning, arguing, bargaining, talking back, or stalling. No words at all, just trusting God.

  13. 21:29 A three-day road trip

    • From Beersheba to what would become Jerusalem was about 50 miles, about an hour in a Tesla but different on a donkey. A lot of bonding can happen on a three-day road trip. Josh imagines donkey races, tag with no tag-backs, s’mores, bocce, maybe a girl Isaac liked at “Jehovah University,” and Isaac saying, “Dad, thanks for showing up for me.”

    1. 22:18 Don’t make promises you don’t intend to keep

      • One thing Josh learned quickly as a father: don’t make promises you don’t intend to keep. Nothing is more devastating to a family than a dad who loves to talk about who he is without ever showing who he is, and that isn’t just a dad thing.
      • When Josh tells Nolan they will do something, Nolan builds it up big; sons look at their old man like he is Superman. They ride bikes, read the Bible, toss the football, swim, play Xbox, and wrestle together. Isaac is just enjoying being with Superman. He doesn’t know his dad is going to bind him up.

  14. 23:31 Why Abraham didn’t turn back

    Scripture: Genesis 22:4 Hebrews 11 Genesis 22:5

    • On the third day Abraham saw the place in the distance. Josh couldn’t understand how Abraham stayed faithful for a three-day trip to kill Isaac, and Scripture says nothing about inner turmoil or struggle.
    • Then he remembered Hebrews 11. It isn’t that Abraham believed God would stop him or knew it was a test; Abraham believed that if he did kill Isaac, God could raise him. That is why he tells his servants, “We will worship and then we will come back to you.”

  15. 24:30 What worship is: first and best

    • A lot of Christians don’t really know what worship is. Worship is submission and obedience. It is giving God first fruits, not the bottom fruit that got squished in the basket, but the first, most beautiful piece you pick. Worship is sacrifice, first and best. Do you give God your best, or are you in the habit of giving him the rest?
    • God commanded his people to give the first and best of the harvest, proclaiming that all they had came from and belonged to him. It was an act of faith and gratitude that anticipated what God would provide later: “We might come up short, but we know God’s going to give more.”

    1. 25:34 First fruits, and the rest of the fruit

      Scripture: Jeremiah 2:3 James 1:18 2 Corinthians 5:17 Romans 8:23 1 Corinthians 15:20 1 Corinthians 15:55

      • Jeremiah said Israel was holy to the Lord, the first fruits of his harvest: God’s first fruits but not his only fruit, because more nations would come. James the brother of Jesus calls believers first fruits: new creations, but only first fruits, because a day is coming when he makes all things new.
      • Paul says we have the first fruits of the Spirit, just a taste. If you are watching yourself grow apart from the world, that is the Spirit’s sanctifying work driving you toward holiness, and the Lord will finish that separation.
      • Paul calls Jesus the first fruits of those who have fallen asleep. Anytime you see first fruits, ask what the rest of the fruit is: Christ’s resurrection assures us of our resurrection, to which we say, “Where, O death, is your victory?”
      • What does your worship look like? Are you offering God your best because you know the best is yet to come, or the rest because you think the best has already come?

  16. 27:22 Isaac carries the wood

    Scripture: Genesis 22:6 John 1:29

    • Abraham placed the wood on Isaac and carried the fire and the knife himself. Who else carried the tool for his own death? Jesus, 2,000 years later on this same hill, carried the wood himself. The son on Mount Moriah wasn’t sufficient; the Son on Golgotha is all-sufficient: “Behold the Lamb of God.”
    • Josh is a forward thinker, but now and again he looks back and wonders what his life would look like without the Lamb. Then he remembers Isaac climbing the mountain: “I know exactly where I’d be, just climbing a hill to my death.”

  17. 28:28 “God himself will provide the lamb”

    Scripture: Genesis 22:7–8 Genesis 22:8

    • Isaac wondered about life without a lamb too: “The fire and wood are here, but where is the lamb?” Abraham answered, “God himself will provide the lamb for the burnt offering, my son.”
    • Isaac was looking for a lamb that wouldn’t come until much later in human history. Remove the comma in verse 8: God himself will provide the lamb for the burnt offering, my son.

  18. 29:05 Isaac submits

    Scripture: Genesis 22:9

    • Abraham built an altar, arranged the wood, bound Isaac, and laid him on it. Isaac had strengths, patient, levelheaded, a caring husband, and weaknesses: he lied under pressure, looked for a way out of conflict, and played favorites with his sons. But no one can deny he was a submissive son.
    • Josh jokes about his almost-70-year-old dad, sitting in the room, trying to strap down his 38-year-old son: “it used to happen; it’s not happening now.” Josh, though, could pin Nolan with one arm, for now.
    • Abraham couldn’t even carry the wood up the mountain. Josh would take a 20-year-old who just hiked a mountain with a backpack over a 115-year-old with nothing in his hands, any day. Isaac had a way out and could defend himself, but he submitted to his father and the plan. “Wonder where he learned that faith from.”

    1. 31:07 The irony at the cross

      Scripture: Matthew 27:40 Revelation 5:12

      • That is the irony of the crowds mocking Christ: “Save yourself. Come down from the cross if you are the Son of God.” Coming down wouldn’t prove he is God. God isn’t afraid of death; death should be afraid of God. His desire to stay on the cross proves he is God.
      • Jesus wasn’t forced onto the cross, overpowered, or bound. Every second he suffered was a willing act of God to save your soul, and we join the saints who say, “Worthy is the Lamb who was slain.”

  19. 32:07 The knife raised

    Scripture: Genesis 22:10

    • Abraham took the knife to slay his son, and Josh could barely handle it. He had to deliver the news to his parents that their second-born was dead; he can’t get their screams out of his head, and that day still haunts him. This is worse, because it isn’t news; Abraham has to do it. “I don’t think I have this much faith.”

    1. 32:57 But Abraham does

      Scripture: Hebrews 11 John 20:29

      • Hebrews 11 says Abraham believed that even if he killed his son, God would raise him, and he had seen it: God brought life to Sarah’s barren womb, brought his wife back twice, and brought back the promised land. His story is watching God bring back things that couldn’t be brought back. “Jehovah Jireh, I don’t know how, but I trust him. Even if I kill the promise, God can raise the promise.”
      • Abraham didn’t grow up hearing about an empty tomb every Easter, singing about graves into gardens, or hearing Jesus tell Lazarus to come out. He believed in resurrection before it was displayed, and Jesus says, “Blessed are those that don’t see yet believe.”
      • Abraham’s faith is simple and unshakable: nothing is too hard for the Lord, so nothing he asks I won’t do. Josh admires and hungers for that kind of faith.

  20. 34:41 The angel calls: the test is over

    Scripture: Genesis 22:11–12 Matthew 16:25

    • With the knife raised, the angel of the Lord calls, “Abraham! Abraham!” and says, “Do not lay a hand on the boy. Now I know that you fear God.” The test is over, Isaac is unharmed, and Abraham gets it: let go of Isaac, and I won’t take him. Will you let go immediately?
    • Josh recalls Jesus’ words in Matthew 16: whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for him will find it.
    • To fear God is to see him as sovereign, trust him completely, and obey him whatever the cost. The worship of a true believer doesn’t look for reasons to hold on to things; it seeks to let go of things, trusting Jehovah Jireh. God’s approval matters far more than man’s.

  21. 36:08 The ram in the thicket: substitutionary atonement

    Scripture: Genesis 22:13 Romans 3:23–25 Ephesians 2:8 2 Corinthians 5:21 1 Peter 3:18

    • Abraham saw a ram caught by its horns and sacrificed it instead of his son. The theological term is substitutionary atonement, and you need it to understand the cross and why the gospel is such good news.
    • Jesus is “God in a flesh suit.” He lived the perfect life and died on the cross to pay the penalty for our sins, the death we should have died. All have sinned and fall short; God presented Christ as a sacrifice of atonement. We all deserve hell, separated from God by nature and by choice; either we die for our sins or Jesus dies as our substitute.
    • With the ram, we see substitution displayed for the first time: the son goes free and the ram’s life is taken. At the cross there was a glorious exchange, the righteous for the unrighteous, the just for the unjust, to bring us to God.

  22. 37:54 “On the mountain of the Lord it will be provided”

    Scripture: Genesis 22:14 Isaiah 53:5 Revelation 1:5–6 1 Peter 1:19

    • Abraham called the place The Lord Will Provide. Every believer who follows the Lord no matter the cost understands the name Jehovah Jireh.
    • What will be provided? God’s Son, his only Son, the Son whom he loved. Jesus would be betrayed with a kiss, denied by his friends, beaten, crowned with thorns, dragged through the streets, nailed to a cross, and hang for hours fighting for every breath, and not once did anyone say stop. God drove the knife into the heart of his only begotten Son: he was pierced for our transgressions, and by his wounds we are healed.
    • The Virgin-born Son didn’t walk down that mountain; he was carried down and placed in a tomb. And Abraham was right: God does raise the dead. Three days later, not a donkey road trip but a three-day spiritual war, Christ defeated sin and death forever.

  23. 40:07 If God is for us

    Scripture: Romans 8:31–32

    • If God did all that and is for us, who could be against us? He who did not spare his own Son will graciously give us all things. If God loved you enough to send his Son while you were still a sinner, why would he turn his back on you now? You won’t be crushed under this trial; you’ll be fine because Jesus is fine. He is alive and well.
    • Josh asks: is your faith running up Mount Moriah, or is it back at the homestead, unwilling to get out of bed?

  24. 41:14 Respond: declare and believe

    Scripture: Romans 10:9

    • The next minutes are time to respond: communion, singing, praying, giving, repenting, and if God moves hearts, maybe a baptism.
    • Don’t make this complicated. Paul says if you declare with your mouth that Jesus is Lord, nothing above him, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. That’s it. If you are ready, don’t wait: get up, be baptized, believe, and be saved.

  25. 42:36 Prayer: hide, or say “Here I am”

    • Josh prays for the Lord to move hearts, bring the partially obedient to full obedience, turn baby believers into mature believers, and grow the church, not for the same missions but for harder ones, for his glory.
    • There are two ways to respond: hide, or say, “Here I am, Lord.” He prays that some who are hiding will step out from behind the bush and say, “Here I am, Lord, a sinner in need of your grace. Use me, take me.”
