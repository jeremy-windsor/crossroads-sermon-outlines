Overview

  1. 00:22 The healed invite the unhealed

    Scripture: Mark 3

    • The church picks up its journey through Mark in chapter 3. Jesus has already set himself apart from every other first-century street preacher; as last week showed, his take on the Sabbath drew a clear line in the sand. You either witnessed the great physician, loved him, and told the world about him, or you heard his teaching, hated him, and told Rome about him.
    • Every time he healed someone, that person told their friends, they came to see, and before long the multitudes came rushing in. Those who were healed bore witness to their unhealed friends. Sound familiar? The strategic plan for kingdom growth hasn’t changed: the healed invite the unhealed to Jesus.
    • If you’ve experienced the grace of God, the only logical next step is to tell others. As the kingdom grows we are robbing hell of men’s souls; this is the great robbery. You are not healed to stand still and keep your mouth shut. You are healed to serve the unhealed. This is why you have breath in your lungs.

    1. 02:44 A basketball dad’s testimony

      • Josh saw this this week at his son Nolan’s basketball practice, wearing his “Jesus is King” swag. A basketball dad, a former NFL player who had led the league in interceptions and accomplished everything he wanted, couldn’t wait to tell Josh his testimony: there was more than he could have imagined in Jesus.
      • This is our job, and it isn’t complicated. It doesn’t require a PhD or an IQ above 100; it is just faithfulness. We wear Jesus on our sleeve and tell the world what he did for us, and when we do, the crowds will begin to develop.

  2. 03:58 How Jesus handled the crowds

    • Jesus approached crowds differently than we might think. When we see crowds growing, the temptation is to do everything we can to keep them from dwindling. Many megachurch models keep up fancy marketing campaigns, a hot take from the pastor every week, and something new every six months. Then some genius in the boardroom says that if they don’t preach about homosexuality or sin, or challenge the body to be sanctified by the truth, they might see even greater numbers.
    • But Jesus never cared about the numbers. He often escaped the crowds to focus on his disciples and equip them for the work that would continue after he returned to heaven.

    1. 05:26 Jesus reads hearts

      • Last week’s message was eye-opening: Jesus reads hearts. He knew most people in the crowds were shallow and insincere, came with their own agenda and ideas, and weren’t even there for him. They just wanted to be healed so they could get back to their life. We can’t read hearts, but we can know them by their fruit.

    2. 06:14 Be cautious with crowds, even this one

      • Josh says we need to be cautious with crowds, even this crowd, because many will gather for the benefits with no desire to die to self, deny self, pick up the cross, and follow Jesus. Proceed with great caution entering a church interested in keeping the crowd or raising signs to make a name for itself. A church focused on crowds usually ends up forgetting about Christ.

    3. 07:00 Find a church interested in pleasing Christ

      • Many people keep moving from place to place because they don’t find what they think they want. Josh suspects they are looking for the wrong things. Find a church interested in pleasing Christ, and honestly, who cares if there’s a crowd? Turn the chair around and use it as a footrest.

  3. 07:34 Mark 3: a diverse and dangerous mob

    Scripture: Mark 3 Mark 3:8 Mark 3:10

    • In Mark 3 people were coming from hundreds of miles away to see Jesus, a national and ethnic clash all rushing toward him. Verse 8 lists Judea, Jerusalem, Idumea, the regions across the Jordan, and around Tyre and Sidon. The crowds were big, diverse, and so large they were dangerous; “mob” may be the better word.
    • Verse 10 says those with diseases were pushing forward to touch him, like charging through a group in 2020 without a mask. Jesus is trending in Judea. How does he respond to sudden fame? Josh points to three responses: Jesus gives new names, a new family, and a new kingdom.

  4. 08:55 New names: Jesus appoints the Twelve

    Scripture: Mark 3:13–19

    • Josh reads verses 13–19: Jesus went up on a mountainside, called those he wanted, and appointed twelve to be with him, to be sent out to preach, and to have authority to drive out demons. Simon he named Peter; James and John he named Boanerges, “sons of thunder”; then Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas Iscariot, who betrayed him.

    1. 09:30 Twelve tribes, twelve apostles

      Scripture: Genesis 12:1–3 1 Peter 2:9 Matthew 21:43

      • The number twelve is significant because there are twelve tribes in Israel. It isn’t just to keep things neat; it is a strong statement from the Godhead. In Genesis God started with Jacob’s twelve sons, and Josh has the church try to name them together. In Exodus God built them into a mighty nation chosen to bring the Messiah into the world so that through him all nations could be blessed.
      • That promise is in Genesis 12: “all peoples on earth will be blessed through you.” Israel walked into the promise but fell into a spiritual wilderness, ready to reject her own Messiah. Yet God was establishing a new, holy nation; Peter says we are a chosen people, a royal priesthood, a holy nation. As the twelve tribes founded Israel, the twelve apostles were the foundation of this new nation, and in Matthew 21 Jesus says the kingdom will be taken away and given to a people who will produce its fruit.
      • A disciple learns by doing, like an apprentice or, for Star Wars nerds, a Padawan. Jesus was training these men to keep establishing a kingdom that bears fruit until he returns. Many call this the church age or the age of grace; it started at Pentecost, is happening right now, and will continue until Jesus returns, and it all started with these twelve men.

    2. 12:32 God’s method: flawed people, so he gets the glory

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 1:27–29

      • In week one the church saw God open the heavens at Jesus’ baptism. It blows Josh’s mind that the one who can tear open the sky chooses to communicate the most important message ever, the message of reconciliation, through twelve flawed men. He is still doing it: Josh asks why God, who pulls the sky back like a bed sheet, would choose him to preach and shepherd this place.
      • Paul answers: not because he is the best among us, but because God chose the foolish things of the world to shame the wise, so that no one may boast before him. From Abraham and Moses to the sons of thunder to you and me, God uses flawed people so that he gets all the glory.

    3. 13:47 The power of a name

      • Levi, James, John, and Simon all receive new names: Levi is Matthew, “gift of God”; Simon is Peter, “the rock”; James and John are Boanerges, “sons of thunder.” Back then your name was supposed to speak to the kind of person you were. After a life-altering moment you could change your name or add to it, though you always kept your original name. Jesus understood the power of a name.
      1. 14:40 Vin Diesel is really Mark Sinclair
        • We see this in Hollywood. Josh grew up on Fast and the Furious, cried when Paul Walker died, shaved his head in high school to look like Vin Diesel, and was voted a Vin Diesel look-alike his sophomore year, which was his peak. Then he ruins the franchise: Vin Diesel’s real name is Mark Sinclair, which sounds like an accountant who still asks his mom to iron his dress shirts. There is no way Mark Sinclair becomes one of the world’s highest-grossing actors, but Vin Diesel can. If you want to reinvent yourself, you need a stage name.
      2. 16:27 Naming is an act of authority

        Scripture: Genesis 1 Genesis 1:3

        • Naming is also an act of authority: if you name it, it’s yours. In Genesis 1 God didn’t wave a wand or snap his fingers; he created by naming things into existence. “Let there be light,” and there was light, and he called it light, sky, sea, night. We are bad at naming, but God is good at naming.

    4. 17:01 Jesus names to reinvent

      • Jesus names to reinvent. He didn’t look at the twelve and think they had it together. We rightly look back on them with reverence, since the foundation of our faith is built on their teaching, but that isn’t how Jesus saw them. He chose them not for their skills or resume but for what he was going to give them, and he is still doing it; Josh calls himself living proof.
      • If you are weak and Jesus calls you strong, you’re wrong and Jesus is right. Josh was a rebel; now he is a relative. Jesus doesn’t name poorly. He gives you a new name not so people see you differently but so you see yourself the way he sees you. When you ditch the crowds for Christ, you receive a new name and a new identity.
      1. 18:52 What the gospel is about
        • This is at the core of the gospel. The gospel was never intended to celebrate who you are or create a pathway of affirmation; it is intended to rescue you, throw you a rope, pull you out, transform you, and send you out. That is why we preach it: not to keep a bunch of bottle-fed baby Christians, but to grow mature Christians who aren’t always complaining or pointing a finger, but read God’s word and say, “Christ, make me more like you.”

    5. 20:00 Whatever names you owns you

      • Naming is an act of authority, and whatever names you owns you. In pastoral counseling Josh tries to identify what has named a person. If your identity is wealth, you’re nothing without it; if it is being a mom, you’re nothing when they don’t need you; if it is being an athlete, you’re nothing when you can’t perform; if it is politics, every four years you face an existential crisis. When those things name you, you are always on the verge of crisis; when Jesus names you, you find solid ground.
      • That changes everything. A Christian businessman praises the Lord when business is good and when it is slow, because his value is in what Jesus says about him, not the bottom line. A Christian mom raises her kids in service to the Lord, and when the kids start ghosting her, she is good, because her Savior always listens; mothers need to hear that they are his before they are theirs. A Christian athlete is still valuable when the strength is gone, because he is still his.
      1. 21:51 Naming ourselves to keep our identity
        • What doesn’t work is when we start naming ourselves. A name Josh sees a lot in our culture is “gay Christian.” Setting the cultural moment aside, that is naming to keep your identity in what you want it to be instead of taking on an identity in Christ; it says you are only Christian if you can first be gay. Josh lovingly asks anyone struggling with this: what if Jesus calls you out of your sinful lifestyle? He called Josh out of his, and the Spirit in him may do similar things in you.
        • The same goes for “recovering Christian.” No, you’re just a Christian; we’re all recovering, so join the party. Names matter: if you named you, you are always in crisis, but if God named you, you are always at peace.

    6. 23:18 Simon the Zealot meets Matthew

      • Josh’s two favorite names on Mark’s list are Matthew and Simon the Zealot. The Zealots were Jewish extremists organized to overthrow Rome, and the historian Josephus called them “daggermen.” Imagine Simon the daggerman, the fierce patriot, meeting Levi, employee of the month for Herod: Assassin’s Creed versus Michael Scott.
      • God doesn’t care whom you served, so long as now you serve him. This team of misfits had serious potential for fireworks, but be warned: when you are given a new name, your enemy can become your brother.

  5. 24:20 A new family: “He is out of his mind”

    Scripture: Mark 3:20–21 Mark 3:22

    • Next Jesus introduces a new family. A crowd gathers so that he and his disciples can’t even eat, and his family goes to take charge of him, saying he is out of his mind. Mary and Jesus’ half brothers, not believers at this time, think he is nuts. It is one thing to get it at work and another to get it from home.
    • The teachers of the law from Jerusalem say he is possessed by Beelzebul, another name for the devil meaning “master of the house,” and that he drives out demons by the prince of demons. His birth family thinks he is crazy, and the teachers say his power comes from Satan, not God.

    1. 25:13 The attacks get more vicious

      • For younger Christians: the more you walk by the Spirit, the more vicious the attacks get, so you must cling to the truth. The teachers don’t want to believe his power is from God, because then they would be forced to accept him as Messiah. Their pride and hunger for power made the truth impossible to accept. If they make Jesus out to be a bad guy, a liar, or a lunatic, maybe they can keep some control.

    2. 25:53 “How can Satan drive out Satan?”

      Scripture: Mark 3:23–30

      • Josh loves the Lord’s response. Jesus speaks in parables: how can Satan drive out Satan? A kingdom or house divided against itself cannot stand. No one can enter a strong man’s house without first tying him up. People will be forgiven all their sins and blasphemies, but whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will never be forgiven, because they were saying he has an evil spirit.

    3. 26:41 The unpardonable sin

      Scripture: Mark 1:15

      • Josh thinks that last line is crucial, and it is sad how many believers live worried they have committed the sin of blaspheming the Holy Spirit. Widely called the unpardonable sin and mentioned in Matthew, Mark, and Luke, this blasphemy refers to unbelief and unrepentance, the ongoing rejection of the work of the Holy Spirit, which is ultimately rejecting God.
      • On arrival Jesus commanded, “Repent and believe the good news,” his first sermon from week one of the series. If you obey Jesus, you cannot commit this eternal sin. Jesus is warning these teachers about the path they are on. There is no forgiveness for rejecting his forgiveness and no grace for those who reject his grace; the only sin God cannot forgive today is the rejection of his Son.

    4. 28:00 “Who are my mother and my brothers?”

      Scripture: Mark 3:31–35

      • In the middle of this heavy day, almost comedically, his mother and brothers arrive and send someone in to call him. You know you are in trouble when your mom and siblings show up at work. Concerned by the rumors, they traveled 30 miles from Nazareth, a big deal back then, to plead with him to take some PTO and come home to rest.
      • Told they are outside, Jesus asks, “Who are my mother and my brothers?” Looking at those seated around him, he says, “Here are my mother and my brothers. Whoever does God’s will is my brother and sister and mother.”

    5. 29:08 No permission to disown your birth family

      Scripture: Mark 3 Mark 3:21 1 Timothy 5:8

      • Josh is clear: Jesus gives no permission here to disown or disrespect your birth family because you now have an adopted one. Most of us don’t come from an ideal family, and some really struggle with the families we have been given, but we must approach this with discernment in light of what all Scripture teaches about family.
      • Remember the family’s accusation in verse 21: he is out of his mind. If Jesus went home with them, he would give his accusers more ammunition: “See, he knows something’s not right; gather round, he needs our prayers.” Instead he uses the moment to introduce a spiritual family of all who do the will of God. Since his half brothers didn’t yet believe, Jesus seems to have felt more at home with sinners, tax collectors, and even Zealots than with James or Jude.
      • Jesus isn’t telling believers to abandon their families to serve God; often your greatest service to the Lord will be within your family. God never commands that he be your only relationship, only your only God: God first and greatest, family next. A family emergency would be different, because it is God’s will that we care and provide for our families; in 1 Timothy the young pastor is told that anyone who does not provide for their own household has denied the faith. But caring for your family is different than letting it pull you off the front lines. We can’t let those dearest to us hijack us from the will of God.

    6. 32:17 Jesus isn’t looking to make friends

      • Three weeks into the series, it should be obvious Jesus isn’t looking to make friends. Family was incredibly important in Jewish society; imagine the criticism he took for that “Sunday sermon” and for leaving his family outside. It had to be eye-opening for the twelve.

    7. 32:44 Born again into God’s family

      Scripture: 1 Peter 1:22–25 2 Peter 1:3–4 Ephesians 2:8–9

      • In Christ we receive a new name and are adopted into a new family. How do we enter it? Don’t ask Nicodemus, because this rattled his brain: we are born again, a spiritual birth. Peter explains that we have been born again, not of perishable seed but imperishable, through the living and enduring word of God; the grass withers and the flowers fall, but the word of the Lord endures forever.
      • When a sinner trusts Jesus as Savior, they are born again, immediately enter God’s family, and take on a new nature. Peter says we participate in the divine nature: his divine power has given us everything we need for a godly life through his very great and precious promises. Paul says we can now call God Father.
      • Our transition from rebellion to relatives isn’t because of anything we have done; it is by grace alone. Ephesians 2: by grace you have been saved through faith, not by works, so that no one can boast. All we can do is what we have been commanded: repent and believe.

  6. 34:31 A new kingdom

    • Once we repent and believe, Jesus gives us new names, a new family, and finally a new kingdom.

    1. 34:38 The victory came after the death

      • The crowds hoped Jesus would deliver the nation and defeat Rome. A glance at Golgotha shows it: when Jesus was crucified only one disciple, John, stayed with him. Everyone wants to be on the winning side, and people left because they saw defeat. Not until Mary visited the tomb three days later did the crowd start growing again, and this time there was no confusion: the victory came after the death, so the victory must last forever.
      • Everyone expected Jesus to be a good Jewish boy loyal to his family, but he was sent to be the firstfruits of an eternal, spiritual family of those who trust him and do his Father’s will. Everyone expected him to restore Israel to her glory days, but he announced a new, spiritual kingdom that will never end.

    2. 35:47 Why Jesus taught in parables

      Scripture: Matthew 21:45

      • Jesus explained this kingdom with parables. Instead of a lecture, he grabbed his listeners’ attention by forcing them to think. “Parable” comes from two Greek words meaning “to cast alongside,” close to “parallel”: a story placed alongside a teaching to help us understand its full meaning. His parables demanded a response, and that is one reason the religious leaders wanted to kill him; Matthew tells us they knew he was talking about them, and it ticked them off.

    3. 36:29 Ending up in a sermon

      • Josh admits this happens more than you think: people have left the church because they thought he was talking about them. He isn’t name-dropping; he doesn’t announce that Mike took his kids to Disneyland and missed worship night, so he’s out. Josh is far more critical of himself than anyone; he wrestles with the word all week, killing things in himself and reorienting his heart before he preaches. He is humbled, and passionate that the church be humbled too.
      • Still, if you hang around a preacher, you might end up in a sermon; 5,000 words a week is a lot of work. His wife thought he had embarrassed Nolan, so after a family meeting Josh kept asking his son whether he was embarrassed, realized he was the one who needed to feel better, and offered to pay for the rights to his name. The next week Nolan asked, “Am I in the sermon this week?” If ending up in Josh’s sermon is bad, ending up in one of Jesus’ parables is far worse; those were written down in billions of copies.

    4. 39:24 Parables hide and reveal: how well you listen

      Scripture: Mark 4 James 1:22

      • Jesus’ parables both hide and reveal the truth. He used something familiar to explain truths about the kingdom; stubborn, prideful listeners were confused, while humble listeners acknowledged their ignorance, submitted to the Lord, and began to understand.
      • In Mark 4 Jesus uses the word “hear” 13 times. The key to maturity in the faith isn’t what you say but how well you listen and respond to the truth. We have killed active listening in a culture always listening to a podcast or playlist; this is listening with a desire to understand and obey, listening in pursuit of action. As James says, do not merely listen to the word and so deceive yourselves; do what it says.

    5. 40:36 The parable of the sower

      Scripture: Mark 4

      • The first parable in chapter 4, the sower or the four soils, helped the disciples see why Jesus wasn’t impressed by large crowds: most would never produce fruit from changed lives because the seed fell on poor soil. The seed is God’s word, and the sower is any faithful servant who shares Christ’s love. That should encourage everyone who takes the truth to the streets: the seed is good and must be shared, but not all seed will sprout.
      • Josh has sat with many who shed tears over family members they can’t seem to reach. Don’t be discouraged if you don’t always see results; some people aren’t ready. Be patient, as the Lord is patient with them. Keep being faithful with the word day in and day out, prepare, preach, pray for the Holy Spirit to help them, and trust God with the results.

    6. 42:05 The parable of the lamp

      Scripture: Mark 4:21–25

      • Jesus asks whether you bring in a lamp to put it under a bowl or bed instead of on its stand; whatever is hidden is meant to be disclosed. “Consider carefully what you hear. With the measure you use, it will be measured to you,” and whoever has will be given more.
      • The lamp was a clay dish of oil with a wick, and it had to burn the oil, but if you use it up you will be supplied with more; if you choose not to use it, you lose it. The apostles were like that lamp, and they teach us to be too. To light the world with truth you must first be filled with the truth, so the more we hear the word, the better prepared we are to share it. The moment we think we know all there is to know, what we thought we had is gone. You can’t give what you don’t have; how much light you take in determines how much light you share.
      • This kingdom runs on his word, and his servants carry his word. Don’t waste your breath on cover-ups and pretenders: one day God will reveal all things, and there will be surprises. It isn’t your job to uncover them; the Lord will do it.

    7. 44:11 Closing: the parable of the mustard seed

      Scripture: Mark 4:30–32 John 20:28 Revelation 5:9–10

      • As the band comes up, Josh closes with the parable of the seed: the kingdom of God is like a mustard seed, the smallest of seeds, which grows into the largest of garden plants with branches where birds perch in its shade. Depending on where you stand, that is either a great encouragement or a grave warning.
      • Jesus predicts a kingdom of unimaginable size from tiny beginnings. He started with twelve, it grew to tens of thousands, then dwindled to only three. Just when all hope seemed lost, on that morning there was one, then three again, then ten, because Thomas was still doubting, then “My Lord and my God,” and there were eleven. The rock preached at Pentecost and there were 3,000; worship roared, baptism waters stirred, the Spirit was received, and the church of Jesus Christ was born. Then 5,000, 10,000, hundreds of thousands, nations turning to Christ.
      • The church is a mess and Satan seeks to destroy her, but the world keeps hearing about Jesus, the gospel keeps growing, and it still grows today. One day saints from every nation will gather before his throne and sing a new song: “You are worthy to take the scroll and to open its seals, because you were slain, and with your blood you purchased for God members of every tribe and language and people and nation.” Josh asks the church to stand and thank the Lord together.
