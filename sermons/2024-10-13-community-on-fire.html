Overview

  1. 02:05 Gathering for worship and the word must be a priority

    • Besides coming home to his wife and kids, these minutes of the body of Christ gathering to praise the Lord and receive his word are some of Josh’s favorite moments. Nearly every broken home he encounters has one thing in common: worship and teaching are nowhere close to where they should be on the list of priorities.
    • To young parents: the odds of your child being a pro athlete are less than 1%, and skipping church for sports isn’t raising that percentage. He challenges them to compare the time a child spends on the fundamentals of their sport with the time spent on the fundamentals of their faith.
    • Church attendance has been declining, with a bit of a resurgence among Gen Z; recent studies show Americans attend once a month or less. We fail to saturate our children in the word of God, then shake our fist at God when our daughter goes to ASU and abandons the faith. She was a great volleyball player, but her house was built on the sand.
    • Worship together, pray together, be in the word together: daily in your house and weekly in his house.

  2. 04:21 Messy people in a holy huddle

    • Josh loves preaching about the family of God because we get sidetracked, distracted, and sloppy. Sinners receive Jesus’ grace, form a holy huddle, try to show grace to others, and discover they are really bad at it: bad at grace, kindness, patience, and gentleness.
    • We expect everyone to share our preferences and avoid people messier than us, not realizing that fifteen years ago that was us. We have a strong memory for others’ shortcomings and convenient short-term memory loss for our own. We keep records of wrongs while Jesus keeps wiping our record clean. We are called to imitate him, not them.
    • Many Christians are happy being 50% like Jesus, or 60% if they are overachievers: “I want Jesus, but on a discount.” Clearance Jesus.

    1. 06:09 Forgiveness, worry, and trusting God

      • We are afraid to forgive others like Jesus because we lack faith in God’s justice. We don’t trust God to avenge us, so we do it ourselves, forgetting that they have to speak to God about what they’ve done, and so do you.
      • It is the same reason we worry. Josh thinks the local church has taught very softly on worry; it is a sin against God. Worry is our effort to deconstruct God’s providence and build a case for our own: “If only God knew what I knew.”

  3. 07:17 1 Thessalonians 5:12: it’s all about his family

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:12 Matthew 18:20

    • In 1 Thessalonians 5:12 it is all about the family, not your family, his family. Paul uses familial language throughout his letters, calling believers brothers, sisters, and children; his favorite is “brethren,” used 60 times. Paul viewed the local church as a literal family of believers born again by the Spirit and sharing God’s nature.
    • One of the most devastating lies in the church is “I don’t need a church home.” People quote Jesus, “Where two or three are gathered,” and stay home with their wife and their puppy. Jesus wants us in community and submitting to the authority of the church in matters of discipline and forgiveness, and Paul confirms this.

    1. 08:25 Josh’s twenty-year story of reconciliation

      • No church is perfect, and Josh has been burned too. He recently reconciled with the people he held responsible for his church hurt in his youth. He held a grudge against them by name for years and hated them; time didn’t heal it, Jesus did. He just had lunch with one of them and offered to preach at his church on reconciliation.
      • We think God has a two-year plan for our lives; sometimes it is twenty. Josh doesn’t think he reaches that reconciliation if he had been disconnected from the body all those years. You need this family to grow in faith, use your gifts, serve, and grow in generosity. The strong need the weak and the weak need the strong.

  4. 09:57 What makes a church healthy

    • It isn’t enough to say we need the church; we need a healthy church. Josh’s staff told him a new family planned to give the church six months before deciding to make it home. He loved that, but it won’t take six months.
    • A nugget from a pastor’s son raised in the church: where the leadership of the church goes, so goes the church. Healthy elders build healthy staffs, healthy staffs build healthy teams, and healthy teams build healthy people.
    • Josh invites people to look up the elders on the website and have dinner with them. After their monthly meetings he praises God in the parking lot for godly men who build each other up and hold his feet to the fire. A healthy elder freely offers time, prayer, counsel, gifts, and money; they aren’t CEOs but shepherds, humble and hungry, and like our Lord they are not here to be served but to serve.

  5. 12:39 Three things: love, help, and worship with your leaders

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:12–13

    • Healthy leadership shows up in a healthy connection with the body, which Paul addresses in his closing remarks to the Thessalonians. Three things: we must love our leaders, help our leaders, and worship with our leaders.
    • Jesus is coming back; it could be this generation or a thousand years. Until then, Paul says, this is how you live together. Josh reads verses 12 and 13: acknowledge those who work hard among you, who care for you in the Lord and admonish you; hold them in the highest regard in love because of their work; live in peace with each other.

  6. 13:39 Love your leaders by understanding them

    • Some versions say “appreciate.” The word is a call not just to know a person but to understand them: not just that his name is Josh and he is lead pastor, but his calling, testimony, beliefs, values, and how he leads his family.
    • This is true of all who labor for the gospel among you. The staff are listed on the website for members, not newcomers, so they can grab a name, take someone out for coffee, and understand what makes them tick, how they came to know the Lord, and what their gift is.
    • Josh could share countless stories of people, even in this service, who don’t care to understand a person and just want to voice their opinion. “It has to stop,” even if the church dwindles to 50 people. It is easier to be unkind, careless, and critical toward pastors you haven’t taken time to know.

    1. 16:03 Called a bigot on the way out

      • When Josh preached on sexuality (and may have joked about someone’s favorite politician), a person left early calling him a bigot. He asks those who have shared a meal with him and Kristen whether he is intolerant. He is an introvert who sometimes gets annoyed, even with himself, but tries to be kind to all people. Scripture teaches that we should know one another, including the pastors and elders who serve us.

    2. 17:22 Supporting leaders financially

      Scripture: 1 Timothy 5:17 1 Corinthians 9:14 1 Timothy 5:18

      • A lot of people don’t like this, but you also need to support your leaders financially. Josh reads 1 Timothy 5:17 on elders worthy of double honor, and Paul tells the church in Corinth the Lord commanded that those who preach the gospel should receive their living from the gospel.
      • Some say Paul didn’t accept support. Wrong, he did; and Paul also didn’t have a wife, didn’t have kids, and didn’t live in Anthem. The worker deserves his wages.

    3. 18:17 Not all church models are created equal

      • In a pastor-led church without denominational guidelines, the pastor is the only vote: he decides his pay, the property, and the buildings, which is obviously problematic. Crossroads was planted by CCV in 2005 and has grown independent while keeping a healthy relationship. It is an elder-run church whose elders aren’t off in la-la land; they are here, raising their families, and this is where they worship.

    4. 19:42 Do you trust your leaders?

      • Josh knows it seems self-serving, but he is being faithful to the text. Do you really understand your pastors and elders, love them, trust them to make sound decisions and steward God’s resources? If not, you probably don’t know them. If you sincerely have tried and still don’t trust them, he says in love, find a church where you can trust the leadership, because only there can you flourish.

  7. 20:40 Highest regard in love

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:13

    • Paul says to hold them in the highest regard in love because of their work. Josh, an optimist, would give the capital-C church a D+. He doesn’t see many pastors who look like they are being hit with Christian love and affection every day.
    • A Barna statistic he saw said one in five Protestant senior pastors had considered suicide or self-harm in the past year. It seems they might be isolated, lonely, and think their church doesn’t care about them.

  8. 21:57 “Who admonish you”

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:12 Hebrews 13:17

    • The word means to warn and reprimand firmly. It is necessary work few are willing to do; churches have cast it aside, thinking a good program is enough.
    • Leaders are called to be shepherds among the sheep and overseers who admonish them, and the balance is constant. Some want their pastor to be their best friend, which weakens his authority; all oversight and no relationship gives you a dictator. Both are bad.
    • If the Holy Spirit leads an elder or pastor to rebuke you and call you back to obedience to God’s word, listen to them. Josh reads Hebrews 13: have confidence in your leaders and submit to their authority, so that their work will be a joy, not a burden. He doesn’t blame burned-out pastors as much as the churches tasked with cultivating joy in the work. “That’s your job.”

    1. 24:21 Every layer of authority submits

      • As the church submits to its shepherd, the lead shepherd submits to the elders. His son asks who the elders submit to; Josh answers, God, and that is good enough. The elders give an account for how they manage him and the body, so they should have joy in their work.
      • Want to know if you’re in a healthy church? Ask the elders. Josh names the chairman, Dave Caldwell, and invites people to ask him about Josh’s heart, whether he is a joy to lead or a pain. If we honor those keeping watch over us, there is joy in the work. That is number one: love your leaders.

  9. 25:59 Help your leaders

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:14–15

    • Josh reads verses 14 and 15: warn those who are idle and disruptive, encourage the disheartened, help the weak, be patient with everyone, and don’t pay back wrong for wrong. Strong leaders develop more leaders, and faithful pastors grow more pastors.

    1. 26:22 The lie: spectating is sufficient

      • Another lie Satan has trickled into the church: if you give to the church and the church does good work, you did good work. It is sneaky because giving is good, but you are called to give and serve. It is not either-or; it is both-and.

    2. 27:06 Titus 2: ministry flowing every direction

      Scripture: Titus 2 Ephesians 4:12–13

      • Titus 2 is this week’s homework. Church teaching should equip believers to make better Christians: better men making better men, better women making better women. Teach older men to be self-controlled so they can teach young men; teach older women not to gossip so they can teach young women.
      • Ministry can’t just flow from the pastor to the people; dead churches flow that way. A living church flows from her to her, you to him, you to her. Josh sums up his job in one sentence: to equip his people for works of service, so that the body of Christ may be built up to unity and maturity.

  10. 28:30 Warn the idle and disruptive

    Scripture: Matthew 18 Proverbs 6:19

    • Your leaders can’t help everyone, and Paul gives examples, starting with the idle and disruptive, or “unruly.” The word described a soldier who wouldn’t take orders or march in line: either complacent or rebellious, someone who doesn’t care how the world views the church, or agrees there is a problem but won’t be part of the solution.
    • These people need to be dealt with immediately, and you don’t have to wait for leadership: you have Josh’s permission to challenge it. Matthew 18: go to them; if you can’t win them over, bring someone; if that fails, bring them to the church. Otherwise they become bitter, a pariah. It is our job to make sure the unruly don’t rule.
    • Many churches are full of armchair critics. Crossroads wants to change how people see the church not to attract people, out of revenge, or because it thinks it alone has it right, but because the church carries a message of hope that can change the world. “You don’t have to agree with us, but please don’t stand in our way.”
    • The Lord hates a person who stirs up dissension in the community. We must be bold, confident, and strong enough to warn them, with kindness and love, but we must call it out.

  11. 31:24 Encourage the disheartened

    • The disheartened are the risk-averse among us. They fear change, so they avoid a life group that might make them deal with things, avoid ministry outside their comfort zone, and don’t try things for fear of failing.
    • The courageous Christian is called to lock arms with the disheartened and walk together. Too often the courageous all end up in one spot having a bonfire while the disheartened go home early. We have to do better at encouraging our discouraged brothers and sisters.

  12. 32:43 Help the weak

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:14 James 5:14–16

    • Verse 14: Paul doesn’t mean the physically weak, though we should care for them, but the spiritually weak, those with doubts. “Anybody struggled with doubts? Come on, liars.” Those with doubts need to be strengthened by those with confidence.
    • Josh calls James 5 one of the most misinterpreted texts he has heard in the church: James is talking about those weak in the faith and vulnerable to attack. Most Christians stop at “the prayer offered in faith will make them well,” but James goes on: if they have sinned they will be forgiven, so confess your sins to each other and pray for each other that you may be healed.
    • Our weakest moments are worked out in biblical community. The church is busy concealing sin when it should be confessing, so that others can encourage us to repent. When repentance happens in community, you have an army of confident believers seeking to restore you. Josh might be strong today and weak tomorrow; we need each other, and the modern church seems to lack the humility to live this way.

  13. 35:03 Be patient: the IOU candy story

    • Paul knows how hard life-on-life ministry is, so he reminds us to be patient. Josh, the youngest of three after Andrea and Jessica, spent Saturday nights locked in the church office with no kids’ programming. The office ladies kept a loaded candy supply on the honor system, and Josh had no honor.
    • The kids wrote IOUs on sticky notes and ate all the candy. His dad was furious, paid the bill, and spanked them; they deserved it. Having seen church budgets, Josh suspects the office supply line was out of control because of their paper-clip statues and staple-gun fights.
    • Two of those kids are serving the church today. To children’s and youth workers: be patient. That kid hitting his head against the wall might end up a pastor, and you might end up in one of his sermons.

  14. 37:29 Don’t pay back wrong for wrong, but don’t ignore it

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:15

    • Josh admits he can get fiery and understands wanting payback, especially against a believer who should know better. But the clear instruction is not to repay evil for evil; the Christian waits patiently and trusts the Lord to sort it out.
    • The Christian living in sin wants him to stop preaching there: “Let me do my thing; I need some privacy.” But doing good for someone doing wrong isn’t pretending it didn’t happen. You don’t look out for someone’s good by ignoring their wrong.
    • Healthy, mature Christians walk that tightrope imperfectly, willing to have uncomfortable conversations in love and patience without choking the truth. If someone won’t tell you what you need to hear for fear of hurting your feelings, they aren’t striving for your good; they’re making way for you to keep doing wrong.

  15. 39:13 Worship with your leaders

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:16–23

    • Recap: we are called to love our leaders, help our leaders, and finally worship with our leaders. Josh reads verses 16 through 23: rejoice always, pray continually, give thanks in all circumstances, do not put out the Spirit’s fire, test prophecies, hold on to what is good, and may the God of peace sanctify you through and through.
    • Worship is the most important activity a church family can do together, and we worship with joy.

    1. 40:08 Rejoicing through grief

      Scripture: Romans 8:18

      • Some of Josh’s most memorable worship came on the heels of his most painful moments. The first time he worshiped here after his sister passed away was one of the most intimate times of worship he has had. He doesn’t remember the songs or who was there; it was just him and God. He was hurting, and God was helping.
      • He would hardly call that time joyful, yet he was rejoicing: his pain colliding with his confidence. His body shook, but his faith didn’t quiver once. He can’t stay down long because God has loved him, saved him, provided for him, and promises more. Paul’s words encourage him: our present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory that will be revealed in us.

    2. 41:51 Unconfessed sin robs joy

      • The only thing that can truly destroy a Christian’s joy is unconfessed, unrepented sin. If “rejoice always” seems too high a bar, there is probably sin in your life that needs to be confessed and repented of.

    3. 42:25 Pray continually: just don’t hang up

      Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:17

      • Every believer fueled with eternal joy has a continuous connection with God through prayer. A church that isn’t praying is dying. This doesn’t mean mumbling prayers down the street; it means constantly considering God.
      • When Josh and Kristen were dating long-distance, they sometimes ran out of things to say and just sat on the phone. It was amazing to know she preferred silence with him to conversation with anyone else. God doesn’t need you to speak to know your heart. Just don’t hang up; stay on the line.

    4. 43:35 Praying for “us” against the evil one

      Scripture: Matthew 6:13 Matthew 6:11–13 Ephesians 6 Ephesians 6:12

      • Preparing for The Invisible Enemy, prayer kept coming up. When the Lord taught us to pray he had the enemy in mind: “Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one.” The posture isn’t “I need”; it is always “us”: give us, forgive us, lead us, deliver us.
      • Prayer is personal, in our closets, and public, something we do for and with one another. In Ephesians 6, after the armor of God, Paul includes prayer, because our struggle is not against flesh and blood but against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly realms.

    5. 45:03 Retaliate with Prayer

      Scripture: Isaiah 41:10

      • The church is launching a prayer campaign called Retaliate with Prayer, because Christians rarely respond to the world with prayer. Bracelets in the lobby are for those taking the challenge, worn for the rest of the series: every day pray for someone in this church by name.
      • Josh tells them what to pray, Isaiah 41:10: do not fear, for I am with you; I will strengthen you and help you; I will uphold you with my righteous right hand. He models it: “Lord, I pray for Luke that he would not live in fear but remember that you’re with him forever.”
      • The hard part: while here, find someone to pray for out loud. Four weeks left means four people here and 28 at home. If you don’t know 28 people, introduce yourself at the water station and ask their name. Don’t take the bracelet if you won’t lean in. The enemy isn’t taking long weekends, and neither should we.

    6. 47:46 Give thanks in all circumstances

      Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:18

      • Worship without joy, prayer, and thanksgiving isn’t worship; it’s just music. The more we hear the message of Christ, encourage one another with it, and love one another, the more we give thanks, and that is true worship. It is unnatural, even sinful, for a Christian to be ungrateful.

    7. 48:18 Don’t put out the Spirit’s fire

      Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:19

      • The Spirit puts a love and joy in our hearts that we should never let go out. Josh asks who loves camping, then disqualifies the RV crowd: that’s a house with wheels. Real camping means the fire goes out in the night and you freeze.
      • Letting the fire go out is frustrating because it is so much harder to start a fire than to throw on a log. Complacency is from the evil one. Fan the flame; feed the fire.

  16. 49:16 “The one who calls you is faithful”

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:24 John 19:30 Ephesians 2:8 Matthew 1:21

    • Paul is talking about our salvation: the Lord will finish what he started. Some say, “Didn’t he already say ‘It is finished’?” He saved you, he is saving you, and he will save you. Salvation happened on Calvary, by grace through faith; it is happening now; and it will happen when Christ returns.
    • Right now Josh is being saved: no longer a slave to sin, Christ lives in him, he has power to say no to sin and yes to the Spirit. “Do your worst, Satan.” And when Christ returns, God will give us a new body to match our new nature, and we will live forever in his presence. He will do what he always said: save his people from their sins.

  17. 50:53 Call on his name

    Scripture: 1 John 1:9 Romans 5:8

    • Call on the name of Jesus today. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness. This is the flame that must never burn out of this place: God demonstrates his own love for us in this, while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

  18. 51:22 Prayer at the Lord’s Table

    • Josh thanks God for saving us and asks him to change this church forever, radically changing how we view ourselves, one another, and this church, for his glory. He comes as a broken man, one of the worst, claiming a victory of grace through Jesus.
    • He prays that anyone feeling brokenness, loneliness, or disconnection would realize God has always been there and turn and seek him. Holding the juice and the bread, they remember the cross, freedom from sin then, help conquering sin now, and change forever when he comes. “We wait for you, as long as it takes.”
