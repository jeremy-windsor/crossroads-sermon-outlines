Overview

  1. 00:35 Authentic Faith Defined and Cultivated

    • Josh defines authentic faith as reasoned, confident obedience to God’s word, locates its object in Jesus, and explains how Scripture, relationship with Christ, and the witness of faithful people cultivate it.

    1. 00:35 Is faith merely irrational optimism?

      • Josh opens by asking whether Christian faith is manufactured hope, illogical belief in the impossible, or a “holy huddle” hoping for the best.
      • He rejects blind optimism, mere intellectual assent to doctrine, and belief contrary to evidence as counterfeit faith or superstition.

    2. 01:06 Authentic faith is confident obedience

      • Josh defines authentic faith as “confident obedience to God’s word in spite of circumstances and consequences.”
      • Faith does not discard reason, logic, or common sense and is not a spiritual game of Marco Polo played with closed eyes.

    3. 01:31 God speaks; believers trust and act

      • Faith follows a pattern: God speaks, people hear his word, trust it, and act upon it.
      • Even when circumstances appear impossible or consequences seem frightening, believers obey because God knows what he is doing.

    4. 02:00 Faith is only as good as its object

      Scripture: Genesis 1:3 John 1:14

      • The world misses that faith’s worth depends upon its object; for Christians, that object is Jesus Christ.
      • Faith is the heart’s response to God’s revelation—not merely belief that God exists, but trust in God.
      • God’s word accomplishes what he speaks: the Creator took on flesh, defeated death at the cross, and promises life to all who come to him.

    5. 02:55 Faith can be cultivated like a plant

      • Faith can be nurtured, fed, watered, and brought into the light.
      • Josh illustrates this with the plant his wife placed in his gray “man cave”: she once killed every plant like Sid mistreated toys in Toy Story, but persistence eventually filled their house with greenery.

    6. 04:05 Withered faith reflects a diminished view of Jesus

      • Some believers’ faith remains weak because they are not doing what growth requires.
      • Since Jesus is faith’s object, weak faith reveals a weak understanding of who Jesus is and what he has promised; as Jesus increases in a person’s understanding, faith grows.

    7. 04:33 Let God’s word revive withered faith

      Scripture: Genesis 1:3

      • Josh urges daily immersion in Scripture rather than hearing God only on Sundays.
      • Believers should learn Jesus’ words, teachings, journeys, responses to hearers and opponents, and the testimony of his closest friends and Roman soldiers.
      • The voice that spoke light into the void can speak life into dried-out faith, turning fixation on the past and present into confidence about the unseen future.

    8. 05:15 Know Jesus and learn from the faithful

      • The greatest means of strengthening faith is a vibrant friendship with Jesus.
      • Another means is knowing faithful people, both living and ancient; Hebrews 11 has transformed many withered lives into vibrant gardens by showing faith in action.

    9. 05:45 Abel demonstrates faith worshiping

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:4 Matthew 23:35

      • Abel was righteous by faith, as Jesus confirms in Matthew 23; his obedience cost him his life.
      • Cain was religious but not righteous because he lacked faith, while Abel’s faithful worship continues to preach.

    10. 06:06 Enoch walks and Noah works by faith

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:5–6 Hebrews 11:7 Hebrews 12:1

      • Enoch shows faith walking with God amid wickedness; prayer, meditation, fasting, Scripture, worship, serving others, and speaking God’s word all cultivate that walk.
      • Abel died for faith while Enoch never died, showing that God gives different assignments to those who trust him; mature faith responds, “God, you know what you’re doing.”
      • Noah shows faith working under social pressure: he picked up a hammer, obeyed, influenced his family, and prepared an ark.
      • Josh calls these earlier believers “the cloud of witnesses who went before us,” whose lives demonstrate faith in action.
      • Jesus later used the flood to warn people about his return; Josh presents the cross as Christ’s blood-soaked ark and urges listeners to enter before the door closes.

  2. 07:58 Abraham: Faith Leaves, Waits, and Dies Looking Forward

    • Abraham’s call, waiting, and pilgrim identity show that saving faith leaves the old life, obeys without demanding certainty, and invests in promises future generations may enjoy.

    1. 07:58 The patriarchs demonstrate faith waiting

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:8–12

      • Josh reads Hebrews 11:8–12: Abraham obeyed without knowing his destination, lived as a foreigner with Isaac and Jacob, and looked toward God’s enduring city.
      • Sarah trusted the faithfulness of the one who promised, and God produced descendants as numerous as the stars and sand.

    2. 08:44 Faith responds while God is calling

      • Abraham’s journey was not his own idea; faith made him willing to go wherever God directed.
      • Josh notes that “when he was called” can convey an ongoing action: Abraham was effectively packing while God was still speaking.

    3. 09:26 Sovereign grace called Abraham out of paganism

      Scripture: Joshua 24:2 Isaiah 51:1–2

      • According to Joshua 24, Abraham came from an idolatrous home and society near the Tigris, Euphrates, and future Babylon.
      • Isaiah calls him “the rock from which you were cut,” emphasizing that God graciously called him so the world would be blessed through him.
      • God did not choose Abraham because of merit: when God called, Abraham listened; when God promised, he believed; when God commanded, he obeyed.

    4. 10:32 Saving faith begins a new life

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:17

      • God’s call to Christ also calls a person away from an old way of life.
      • As Abraham left paganism for a new land, identity, and life, believers become new creations: the old is gone and the new has come.
      • This separation does not produce instant perfection, but it creates a heart willing to surrender everything that dishonors God.

    5. 11:18 Every believer must leave a personal Ur

      Scripture: Romans 12:2 2 Corinthians 6:14

      • Faith trusts God enough to abandon self-made plans before knowing what will replace them.
      • Every journey of faith leaves behind its own “Ur”—patterns of sin, self-rule, unbelief, and worldly values.
      • Josh connects this to Romans 12’s call to renewed minds and Paul’s warning not to be yoked with unbelief; believers cannot thrive while refusing to leave the old life.

    6. 12:32 The faithful obey without seeking permission from the old life

      Scripture: Galatians 1:14–16

      • Josh cites Paul’s testimony that, when God revealed his Son and called him to preach among the Gentiles, he did not first consult another person.
      • Abraham did not seek approval from his pagan family, nor did Paul seek permission from those who raised him in Judaism.
      • Their immediate obedience warns believers against submitting God’s call to the judgment of people rooted in the life they are leaving.

    7. 13:19 Faith does not look back

      Scripture: Genesis 19:26 Revelation 21:5

      • Josh points to Lot’s wife in Genesis 19 as a warning and declares that the faithful journey onward into the unknown.
      • They move with complete confidence that God is making all things new rather than longing for the life behind them.

    8. 13:53 Generations build what they may never personally enjoy

      • Josh compares faith to planting an oak tree for grandchildren, early frontiersmen envisioning a nation “from sea to shining sea,” and railroad workers laying tracks west from Omaha.
      • Hebrews 11 depicts generations laying stones, promises, sacrifices, prayers, and prophecies into a story larger than themselves.
      • They did not see the ending, but their work led toward Bethlehem, Calvary, and an empty tomb no authority could explain.

    9. 15:11 Foreigners welcome promises from a distance

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:13–16

      • Hebrews says these witnesses were still living by faith when they died, having seen and welcomed promises they did not fully receive.
      • Josh recalls two lifelong promises: the faith in Christ he professed at age seven in Newburgh, Indiana, and his marriage vow to remain faithful to his wife.
      • Seeing himself as a foreigner on earth helps him reject momentary pleasure and temporary gain in favor of heaven and eternity.

    10. 16:31 The patriarchs died living by faith

      • A stranger on earth does not need to blend in; Josh says the goal is to please God.
      • God remains the hero of redemption, yet he makes “heroes out of zeros.”
      • Contrary to prosperity teaching, Hebrews emphasizes not that the patriarchs prospered, but that they died while still living by faith—the way Josh wants to finish.

  3. 17:16 Passing the Torch: Joseph, Moses, and Deliverance from the Old Life

    • The patriarchs pass God’s promise forward; Joseph’s bones become a lasting sermon, and Moses’ family and Josh’s own deliverance testimony show faith fighting old identities and bondage.

    1. 17:16 Faith entrusts the promise to another generation

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:17–22

      • Josh reads Hebrews 11:17–22: Abraham offered Isaac while trusting that God could raise the dead.
      • Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning their future; Jacob blessed Joseph’s sons while dying; Joseph anticipated the exodus and gave instructions about his bones.

    2. 17:55 Four imperfect generations carry one promise

      • Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph were flawed men, but they remained devoted to God and trusted his word.
      • Isaac passed the promise to Jacob, and Jacob passed it to his twelve sons.
      • Joseph’s faith grew despite betrayal by people who claimed to belong to the same family of faith.

    3. 18:45 Joseph refused to make suffering an excuse for unbelief

      • Egypt’s temptations and treasures did not pull Joseph from God.
      • He did not use his family’s abuse, his work, or undeserved imprisonment to justify unbelief.
      • He knew not only what he believed but whom he believed, remaining confident to the end that God would deliver Israel.

    4. 19:10 Joseph ordered his bones carried home

      Scripture: Joshua 24:32

      • After reconciling with his brothers, Joseph required them to carry his remains from Egypt to Canaan during the exodus.
      • Israel fulfilled that request and buried him at Shechem, as recorded in Joshua 24.

    5. 19:32 Joseph’s bones preached during captivity

      • His unburied remains silently reminded every generation that Egypt was not home and God’s promise still stood.
      • The prosperous were reminded that possessions were temporary; the enslaved were reminded that suffering was not permanent.
      • Against discouragement, the bones declared that God would take his people out—even if Joseph himself had to leave “in a bag.”

    6. 20:14 Genuine faith leaves a strengthening legacy

      • Joseph intentionally left a visible testimony that God’s people were foreigners in Egypt and heirs of a better country.
      • Josh asks what testimony he—and the congregation—will leave.
      • Authentic faith does more than endure; it strengthens others by handing God’s promises to the next generation.

    7. 21:03 Moses demonstrates that faith fights

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:23–29

      • Josh reads Hebrews 11:23–29: Moses’ parents hid him without fearing Pharaoh’s edict.
      • Moses rejected his identity as Pharaoh’s daughter’s son, choosing mistreatment with God’s people over sin’s fleeting pleasures.
      • He valued disgrace for Christ above Egypt’s treasures, kept the Passover, saw the invisible God, and led Israel through the Red Sea.

    8. 21:57 Moses learned courageous faith from his parents

      Scripture: Exodus 1:22–2:10

      • Josh tells parents that their children are watching and urges them to leave a testimony that lasts.
      • Amram and Jochebed hid Moses for three months despite thin walls and the danger of concealing a crying baby.
      • They placed him in a waterproof basket near Pharaoh’s daughter; Miriam then arranged for Jochebed to nurse and raise her own son.
      • Pharaoh drowned Hebrew children, yet God raised one Hebrew child as an Egyptian prince who would deliver his people.

    9. 23:17 Generational boldness prepares children to face Pharaoh

      • Moses’ parents made a stand regardless of the odds, and their courage later enabled Moses to confront the world’s most powerful ruler.
      • Their mission was not merely to save the boy but to teach him and mark him with God’s promise of a coming deliverer.
      • They raised him within the messianic hope in which Abraham rejoiced.

    10. 24:00 One divine Author joins Abraham’s hope to Christ

      Scripture: John 8:56 Philippians 2:8

      • Though Scripture came through many human authors, books, and settings, Josh stresses that divine Scripture has one nonhuman Author.
      • In John 8, Jesus says Abraham rejoiced at the prospect of seeing his day and “saw it and was glad.”
      • Josh connects Abraham’s expectation of Christ’s ministry—serving and humbling himself even to death on a cross—with believers’ confident expectation of Christ’s return.

    11. 25:06 Authentic faith refuses an earthly identity

      • Moses chose membership in God’s household over Pharaoh’s household.
      • Josh asks listeners to identify their own “palaces” of comfort, security, pleasure, abundance, success, and approval—and whether they would walk away.
      • Moses forfeited royal loyalty, embraced slavery with God’s people, counted it gain, and never returned to Egypt’s treasures.

    12. 26:15 Faith dismantles the palace of an old life

      • Josh testifies that authentic faith has dismantled parts of his own old life amid addiction, grief, and circumstances he thought he might not survive.
      • He describes two decades of nicotine addiction and pornography’s distortion of God’s design for intimacy.
      • These sins damaged relationships and nearly cost him his marriage.

    13. 27:04 Surrender succeeded where self-effort failed

      • Repeated personal attempts to defeat the addictions did not last, so Josh cried out for God to take his life and have his way.
      • He preferred a desert with God to a palace of addiction and asked to stop wounding his wife, God, others, and himself.
      • He prayed that past sins would display God’s mercy rather than remain a source of shame.
      • God helped him through a gradual, imperfect journey of sanctification rather than an effortless overnight change.

    14. 28:20 “Let me go” becomes “Let his people go”

      Scripture: Ephesians 6:12 Hebrews 11:16

      • Josh first confronted his bondage with the cry, “In the name of Jesus, let me go.”
      • Once free, he realized he needed to return for others and contend, “Let his people go.”
      • He calls listeners to release addiction, shame, and every Pharaoh-like power, trusting Christ to supply what they need.
      • Rejecting the world’s treasures, he chooses to face the spiritual forces of evil and head into the unseen where a better country awaits.

  4. 29:17 Jericho and Rahab: Persevering Faith and Redeeming Grace

    • Jericho shows perseverance when nothing visible has changed, while Rahab shows grace crossing barriers of ethnicity, reputation, and past sin to turn an enemy into family.

    1. 29:17 Forty years separate the Red Sea from Jericho

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:30 Numbers 14:26–35

      • Hebrews moves directly from Israel’s Red Sea crossing to Jericho, skipping a generation’s forty years of wilderness unbelief.
      • A journey of roughly thirty-five weeks became forty years because grumbling, discontentment, idolatry, and unbelief kept the older generation from the land.

    2. 30:14 Jericho looked impossible from the ground

      • Its walls were massive—wide enough for chariots, with homes built inside them.
      • From a human perspective the city could not be conquered, setting up the contrast between visible obstacles and divine perspective.

    3. 30:50 Canaan was not the true obstacle; unbelief was

      Scripture: Joshua 6:3–5, 15–20

      • From heaven’s perspective, Jericho announced that God’s people did not travel alone.
      • Israel could repeat the previous generation’s failure or obey God’s unusual instructions: march once for six days, seven times on the seventh day, then shout at the priests’ blast.
      • The people acted on God’s word, and the walls fell exactly as promised.

    4. 31:35 Battles are lost when believers stop marching

      • Josh argues that most defeats do not occur because the wall is too large but because people stop before completing God’s command.
      • Faith does not demand a loose brick as preliminary proof; it keeps walking when nothing has fallen yet.

    5. 32:18 Rahab is the unexpected hero of faith

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:31

      • Hebrews names Rahab because she welcomed Israel’s spies and escaped the judgment that fell on the disobedient.
      • As a prostitute, Gentile, Canaanite, and Amorite living inside the condemned walls, she was the last person people would expect among faith’s heroes.

    6. 32:59 God’s mercy crosses every human barrier

      Scripture: Joshua 5:13–15 Joshua 7:1–26

      • Rahab displays grace reaching beyond race, reputation, and past sin; God’s saving purpose was never limited to Israel.
      • In Joshua 5:13, the commander of the Lord’s army answers “Neither” when asked whether he supports Israel or its enemies.
      • The battle was ultimately about who belonged to God, requiring Joshua, Rahab, and Achan alike to submit to divine authority.

    7. 33:59 Faith moved Rahab from enemy to family

      Scripture: Joshua 2:8–14; 6:22–25 Matthew 1:5

      • When Rahab believed Israel’s God, she immediately ceased to be God’s enemy and entered his covenant family.
      • She married Salmon, became Boaz’s mother, entered David’s line, and was preserved in Matthew’s genealogy as an ancestor of Jesus.
      • Her movement from condemned prostitute to covenant daughter demonstrates the transforming reach of grace.

    8. 34:54 The cross gives rebels a new identity and family

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:3

      • Faith does not merely rescue from judgment; it turns enemies into sons and daughters.
      • Josh includes everyone in Rahab’s story: all were rebels and children of wrath until faith in Christ changed everything.
      • The cross gives believers forgiveness, a family, a King, an identity, a Father, and a Brother.
      • No sin, broken family history, addiction, or shameful past lies beyond Christ’s redemption; the family of faith is defined not by ethnicity, nationality, language, or status, but by trust in God’s promises.

  5. 36:28 Faith in Triumph and Suffering

    • Hebrews 11 refuses a prosperity-only account of faith: the same faithful God may give victory over suffering or victory through it, and endurance can require more faith than escape.

    1. 36:28 God works through imperfect people

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:32–34

      • Hebrews names Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the prophets, whose faith conquered kingdoms, administered justice, shut lions’ mouths, quenched flames, and turned weakness into strength.
      • Gideon was a fearful farmer, Barak faced an overwhelming army, and Samson accomplished great feats despite deep flaws.
      • Their stories emphasize not human greatness but God’s faithfulness to work through trusting people—even when they are a mess.

    2. 37:34 Women received their dead back to life

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:35a 1 Kings 17:17–24 Matthew 13:58 2 Kings 4:18–37

      • Josh highlights women whose faith accompanied resurrection miracles.
      • Elijah raised the widow of Zarephath’s child in 1 Kings 17, and Elisha restored the Shunammite woman’s son in 2 Kings 4.
      • Between those examples, Josh notes that even Jesus did not do many miracles where people lacked faith.
      • These mothers and prophets believed God could breathe life into death, and he did.

    3. 38:30 Celebration gives way to Hebrews 11’s hard turn

      • Josh connects the resurrection stories to the song “Rattle”: these believers did not merely sing “Live!” enthusiastically; they trusted God to act.
      • He then warns against ending the sermon on visible miracles, because verse 35 deliberately shifts from those delivered to “others” who suffered.

    4. 39:02 The “others” had the same faith and a different outcome

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:35b–38

      • Some were tortured, mocked, flogged, chained, imprisoned, stoned, sawed apart, and killed by the sword.
      • Others wandered destitute in animal skins, deserts, mountains, caves, and holes, though the world was not worthy of them.
      • Josh challenges American Christians to face the whole passage: some escaped the sword and others died by it—same faith, same God, different orders.

    5. 40:11 Victory can come over suffering or through it

      Scripture: Daniel 6:16–23 Daniel 3:16–27

      • Josh argues that prosperity teaching cannot survive Hebrews 11 because victory belongs both to those who live by faith and those who die by faith.
      • Victory over suffering occurs when God delivers, as with Daniel and the three Hebrews.
      • Victory through suffering occurs when God does not remove pain but glorifies himself within it, as in Job, Paul, and Stephen.
      • Faith can change circumstances, but it does not guarantee that God will do so.

    6. 41:08 All were commended despite unequal earthly results

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:39–40

      • Hebrews 11:39–40 says all were commended for faith, though none received the final promised fulfillment apart from God’s larger plan.
      • Josh contrasts people who prayed and saw cancer disappear with those who prayed and lost loved ones.
      • Some marriages were restored while others ended in divorce papers; some believers received promotions while others lost jobs because they would not compromise.

    7. 42:20 It can take more faith to endure than to escape

      Scripture: Daniel 3:16–18

      • Great faith may bring great calls to sacrifice and perseverance through suffering.
      • Like the three Hebrews facing Nebuchadnezzar’s furnace, believers obey even if God chooses not to deliver.
      • Whether they walk out alive or burn, the proper confession remains, “To God be the glory.”

    8. 43:00 Faith trusts the larger story

      • Faith is not confidence that God will write an individual’s preferred story; it trusts that he is writing a larger story than anyone can see.
      • Hebrews 11 portrays the whole body of believers testifying together to God’s goodness.
      • The chapter does not demand self-made heroes or voluntary martyrdom; it calls ordinary people to walk by faith in every circumstance.

  6. 43:32 Every Witness Was Looking Toward Jesus

    • Every witness looked toward Christ; Josh gathers the chapter’s sacrifices, deliverers, kings, and promises into Jesus and calls the church to fix its eyes on him.

    1. 43:32 The witnesses anticipated someone unseen

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:4–40

      • Abel expected a better sacrifice; Noah believed judgment would not have the final word; Abraham awaited God’s city.
      • Isaac blessed sons whose nationhood he would not see; Joseph expected God to bring Israel home; Moses valued Christ above Egypt.
      • Rahab trusted Israel’s King, David longed for an eternal King, the prophets announced a Savior, and sufferers anticipated a better resurrection.

    2. 44:21 The long expectation reaches Bethlehem

      Scripture: Luke 2:8–11

      • Generations kept looking until an angel announced to shepherds that a Savior—the Messiah and Lord—had been born in David’s town.
      • The promises welcomed from a distance converged upon Jesus Christ.

    3. 44:38 Jesus is the better Abel, Noah, and Abraham

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:24 Genesis 12:1–3

      • Jesus is the better Abel, whose blood cries not for vengeance but mercy; Josh calls listeners to come and be cleansed.
      • Jesus is the true Noah and the saving ark; listeners must enter before the door closes.
      • Jesus is the greater Abraham, leaving his Father’s home to bless every nation; Josh asks whether listeners will leave Ur to follow him.

    4. 45:00 Jesus fulfills the patriarchs, deliverers, kings, and sacrifices

      Scripture: Genesis 22:1–18 Genesis 45:4–8 Hebrews 7:23–25 Hebrews 10:10–14

      • He is the greater Isaac who was not spared on the altar and the greater Joseph betrayed by his brothers so they could live.
      • He is the greater Moses leading a greater exodus and the greater Joshua undertaking divine conquest.
      • He is the greater David who reigns forever, the undying priest, and the perfect once-for-all sacrifice.
      • Josh urges listeners to stop trying to earn what Jesus has already finished.

    5. 45:30 The risen King will make all things new

      Scripture: Revelation 21:5

      • Jesus conquered Satan, sin, and death.
      • He will return to judge heaven and earth and renew all things, prompting Josh to ask whether the congregation is ready for him.

    6. 45:44 Fix your eyes on the author of faith

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:2 Revelation 5:5–6

      • Josh names Jesus as the author and perfecter of faith, Alpha and Omega, slain Lamb, Lion of Judah, and Savior of the world.
      • He asks what more Jesus could do to prove his love or deserve trust, obedience, and worship.
      • The sermon’s final charge is to fix their eyes on Jesus and walk by faith together.

  7. 46:17 Closing Prayer: Authentic Faith Through Suffering to Glory

    • The closing prayer asks that hearing become embodied obedience, that worship be honest, and that communion strengthen faith willing to follow Christ through suffering to glory.

    1. 46:17 Prayer for reoriented hearts

      • Josh asks the heavenly Father to set the congregation’s hearts rightly and orient them after sitting in God’s presence and hearing his word.

    2. 46:36 Prayer that hearing becomes embodied response

      Scripture: James 1:22

      • He asks that the word come alive through actions, homes, relationships, and the community.
      • He prays for honesty in the room, urging people not to sing words they do not mean if they are not prepared to surrender fully.

    3. 47:16 Prayer for faith that walks through suffering

      • Josh introduces the response song, “Christ Be Magnified,” which expresses willingness not merely to hope for deliverance but to walk through suffering with God.
      • He asks for faith that acts, perseveres, and abides.

    4. 47:51 Remembering the cross and embracing Christ’s path

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:23–26

      • Holding the communion elements, Josh recalls that Jesus acted on humanity’s behalf: bearing sin, washing it away with his blood, and giving his broken body.
      • He remembers that Jesus rose three days later and conquered sin and death, securing his promise for those who trust him.
      • He closes by embracing Christ’s path through suffering to glory, affirming trust in him all the way, and praying in Jesus’ name: “Amen.”
