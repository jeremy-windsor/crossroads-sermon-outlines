Overview

  1. 01:06 Not a church discipline meeting

    • When Josh was growing up and his dad called a family meeting, everyone wondered who was busted. This isn’t a church discipline meeting; it is a good meeting to share everything God has been doing over the year.
    • Every year the church pauses as a discipline to state where it is. A healthy church operates a lot like a healthy family, and like a healthy believer who pauses to reflect on where they are with the Lord; the church checks in on the mission the same way.

  2. 02:03 Great communication and transparency

    Scripture: John 15:15

    • A healthy family needs great communication. In John 15 Jesus says, “I no longer call you servants… instead I have called you friends, for everything that I learned from my Father I have made known to you.” Jesus sets an example of communication and transparency, and the leaders want to follow it.
    • They aren’t perfect and will probably make mistakes, but they will always bring mistakes into the light, never cover them up, own their actions, and rest in his grace.

  3. 02:45 Complaining is contagious, and so is praise

    • Another reason for the meeting: complaining is contagious. When Christians get off mission and focus on being served rather than serving, a contagion of whininess sweeps through the lobby.
    • Praise is contagious too. So the church doesn’t just talk about its vision; it praises God for helping reach it. False prophets and hypocrites have soiled the church’s reputation, but God put it on their hearts to change the way people see the church by serving Anthem, Phoenix, and the world like they’ve never been served before, to God be the glory.

  4. 03:53 A sermon for the one giving it

    Scripture: Matthew 20:25–28

    • Today’s message is geared more toward the one giving it than the ones hearing it, because it is possible to have the head of a leader without the heart of a shepherd, and Josh doesn’t want that.
    • The leadership model comes from Jesus in Matthew 20: the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, “not so with you”; whoever wants to be great must be your servant, just as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve and give his life as a ransom for many.
    • This church isn’t Josh’s; it is his, and Josh serves God by serving the church. Pastors are servants who don’t lord it over anyone. His main function is preaching and teaching, and he is also an elder, an overseer; pastor and elder are really the same role, a sermon for another day.

    1. 05:18 Who builds the church?

      Scripture: Matthew 16:18

      • God is faithful to establish his church and direct it by his word. In Matthew 16 Jesus tells Peter “on this rock,” and without debating the rock, Josh points to what comes next: “I will build my church.” Pop quiz: who builds the church, Josh Wyatt or Jesus Christ? Jesus Christ.
      • Trying to build the church my way or our way always competes with his way, and this church will do it his way.

    2. 05:56 His church’s operating procedures

      Scripture: Ephesians 4:11–16

      • Paul lays out his way in Ephesians 4: Christ gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors, and teachers to equip his people for works of service, so the body is built up to unity and maturity, no longer infants tossed by every wind of teaching, but speaking the truth in love and growing up into Christ, as each part does its work.
      • Josh’s job is to equip God’s children for works of service. Church isn’t a spectator sport; you aren’t watching the pastor do it or telling him your preferences, you are joining him. His job is to preach and teach the word; the church’s job is to live it out together in humility.
      • That is why the first core value is “be doers” and the second is “be humble”: God gives each a different role, and the work isn’t for one man or one team but for all of us.

  5. 07:42 Attendance is not the goal

    Scripture: James 1:22

    • Attendance isn’t the goal; service to the mission is. A healthy church isn’t measured by a filled auditorium but by how many believers come to be filled with the word so they can pour themselves out in service. That is the metric that matters, and it is also the hardest to track, so many churches track other things.
    • The mission, to change the way people see the church, admits that something is wrong. Complacency comes first to mind: the church growth movement of the past 50 years filled auditoriums with people passively observing rather than actively serving. Works of service should be non-negotiable; as James says, don’t merely listen to the word and so deceive yourselves, do what it says.
    • Josh feels he has the best job in the world and loves using his gifts to serve the church, and he wants every believer to know that joy. If we only sit and observe, we waste the gifts God gave us.

  6. 09:23 Why the origin story matters

    • Last week the identity series ended with the reminder that identity isn’t rooted in what has been done to you or what others say about you. Still, past events shape how we view people and the world, and an organization looks a lot like the person leading it, so on a day like this the leader’s origin story matters too.
    • The mission didn’t come out of a brainstorming session about how to ruffle feathers, tick off other pastors, or stand out from the “19 million churches in Anthem.” God has been working it out in Josh’s life for decades.

    1. 10:35 What Josh witnessed

      • Josh grew up close to the inner workings of the church, maybe too close: two churches before this one, one around 2,000 and one approaching 5,000, with immediate and extended family on staff. He knows some have had great experiences with megachurches and rejoices with them, but not him.
      • He witnessed abuse, betrayal, and treatment that said, “If you aren’t with us, you’re dead to us.” At seventh-grade summer camp he saw youth interns haze students, duct-taping kids to bunk beds, interns exposing themselves and streaking through the halls, and grown leaders cheering as high school boys rode tricycles wearing nothing but a sock. When things get sexualized where young people are supposed to learn to live like Christ, his blood boils, and yours should too.

    2. 12:13 From pain, not pride

      • He isn’t saying this happens everywhere. At 38 and leading a church, some have taken his desire for change as arrogance, as if he thinks he can do it better than everyone else. It doesn’t come from pride; it comes from pain. His biggest fear is that the church gets in the way of what the Lord wants to do in someone’s life, when it is supposed to pave the way to Jesus.
      • Looking back, he asks what went wrong, how it got so bad, and how many others learned to hate the church as he did. He can’t know who has been hurt, but he can share his story and hope it opens pathways for others to heal.
      • The church has been a place of great pain in his life, and also a place of incredible healing: he has healed in the place that hurt him. That is the kind of church they want to be, a place to heal in the arms of Jesus.

    3. 13:40 What went wrong: the growth model

      • Josh believes the growth model went wrong. When a church thinks about growth in numbers more than growing mature disciples, priorities shift and the foundation becomes brittle. You can’t judge a church’s health by how many are there; rooms are packed in unhealthy churches.
      • Focused solely on growth, you chase benchmarks instead of chasing and shepherding souls, which is how unqualified youth pastors end up at camps like the ones he saw. When a church sees excitement, it asks how to keep the momentum going, when it should ask whether it can slow down and do this the right way.
      • If you leave bodies behind in pursuit of the mission, something is horribly wrong with you or with the mission. “Josh, we want to reach more people.” Of course, but at what cost? Lives and souls are at stake.

    4. 15:16 Co-laborers, not competition

      • Josh doesn’t see other churches as competition but as co-laborers for the gospel. A member recently became lead pastor at another church in the valley, and the two churches shower each other with support; he will always be their brother.
      • He has no problem with a church until it trades the mission for influence: “We can’t preach about that, we’ll lose people. Attendance is going down; we shouldn’t talk about this.” Talk about what, God’s word? He has heard it from their own mouths. Just teach people to obey his commands and let him grow his church. Stop the gimmicks; this will not be a church of gimmicks.

  7. 16:19 Be careful who you listen to

    Scripture: 1 Timothy 4:8–16 1 Timothy 4:12

    • Anyone can have a platform now. YouTube gives the little guys a voice, which is great, but sometimes the wrong guy gets a voice, so be careful who you listen to.
    • Here is a trustworthy voice, Paul to the young pastor Timothy: godliness has value for all things; don’t let anyone look down on you because you are young, but set an example for the believers in speech, conduct, love, faith, and purity; devote yourself to the public reading of Scripture, preaching, and teaching; watch your life and doctrine closely. That example, Josh says, is what we should be praying for.

    1. 17:50 “He just needs a few good men”

      • Josh and his dad recently recalled a Gaither Vocal Band song they used to listen to, “A Few Good Men,” and he plays it for the church.
      • That should be our prayer: churches led by faithful, gentle, loving, kind, obedient, selfless, godly men, not charismatic, skinny-jeans, smooth-talking narcissists anxious to be accepted. He isn’t trying to be mean; he owns a pair of tight jeans his wife loves. But there is wisdom in staying far away from these so-called pastors.

    2. 19:26 Lukewarm Christianity

      Scripture: Revelation 3:15–16

      • The idea that being a Christian is the good life, that you can follow Jesus without picking up your cross, is more widespread than you think and isn’t new. Jesus told the church in Laodicea, “Because you are lukewarm, neither hot nor cold, I am about to spit you out of my mouth.”
      • They want to be a church on fire for Jesus. It isn’t for everybody, and no hard feelings if it isn’t for you, but they want to equip devoted Christ followers at home, at work, and in the neighborhood. Plenty of churches want you to be a benchwarmer for Jesus; this isn’t one of them.

  8. 20:15 Revival in the Agora: Crossroads Speaks

    • In the New Testament the word for marketplace is agora, the town square where business and legal matters were settled. Speaking out there was an enormous risk, but it was the birthplace of the church, and Josh believes true revival will happen there. Many believers pray for revival expecting it in here; he thinks it will happen out there.
    • This month the church launches a new initiative, Crossroads Speaks, focused on getting its voice out into the community, the town square, the agora.

    1. 20:59 Crossroads Cares and the profane model

      Scripture: Luke 19:10

      • As Paul told Timothy, it isn’t just about speech but also conduct and love, and that is where Crossroads Cares has been huge, keeping eyes open to the needs of the people around us.
      • Some call it the profane model, from the word profanus, “outside the temple.” Truly seeking Jesus doesn’t lead you into the temple to stay but to train so you can go out. If the church chases Christ, it will lead them out there; after all, the Son of Man came to seek and save what was lost.

  9. 21:47 Celebrating what God has done

    • Josh invites the executive pastor, Chris, to join him. On Mondays the staff get to hear the life-change stories in homes and marriages that the church doesn’t always see, so they want to celebrate the movement of God among his people and then share goals for the next 12 months.
    • They will invite everyone to give their tithe, join a group, and sign up to meet needs, and to ask how they can put their God-given gifts to work to advance his kingdom.

    1. 22:50 Baptism

      • They start with a baptism video. Right after that baptism, the woman’s husband got in the water too, jeans and all, a whole family turning to the Lord. Josh has been collecting responses to his request to share what God is doing; Adam wrote that the Lord is changing him day by day, and many of those baptized this year are on that list.
      • Some say they have felt attacked since. Pastorally, they tell them that what might once have discouraged them is now encouragement and assurance. Baptism is the outward expression of an inward decision, and sometimes when you publicly say you are following Jesus, someone hits back because they don’t like that a life has been changed. Either way, God is working.
      • Having opened up about his megachurch experiences, Josh admits baptism is one area where his skepticism shows: when he hears 300 people were baptized, his first thought is, praise God, but are they being discipled and do they have someone to lean on when trials come? One favorite celebration: Luke said after his baptism, “I’ve never been hungrier for the word of God.”

    2. 26:00 Crossroads Cares

      • Crossroads Cares began in a difficult time. During COVID, churches closed their doors, and it began to weigh on them that the church should be out helping the sick, the last ones worried about getting sick, with more faith than the rest. They carried that burden, and it became a way for people to share needs in real time and for the church to step in.
      • Chris used to tell Josh that it used to be “if you build it, they will come,” but now if you build it, people just don’t care. The church has to get outside its four walls and take God’s love to people so they will care to hear the message of Jesus Christ, staying mobile and flexible while large churches close their doors.
      • Last year 400 needs were met, with more than 1,800 actions, people physically doing something. Year one met 124 needs, year two 294, year three 400. Public schools have made more than 200 requests for shoes for kids with holes in theirs; one put new shoes on and said, “I can run without it hurting.” Schools that are trying to get God out are coming to the church to help meet needs.
      • Once, as a youth pastor, one of them was only allowed to have lunch with students in a private room, so seeing counselors, teachers, and nurses feel the church’s support is an amazing feat. Kicks for Kids is going international, adopting schools and likely a first orphanage. Many needs are met within 24 hours, and when people ask who did it, the answer is the church, Jesus Christ’s church, which opens the door to share Christ. It has become a reputation: “You’ve got a need? Ask Crossroads Cares.”

    3. 31:30 Finances and giving

      Scripture: Psalm 50:10

      • You can’t meet needs without money. Crossroads Cares is well funded; as it is spent, it is replenished, sometimes shockingly fast, because God owns the cattle on a thousand hills.
      • The church stretched its budget 18 percent this past year, the biggest leap it has ever taken, and the church met it and exceeded it. There were moments of asking God whether it was the right thing, and he provided. Crossroads Cares remains funded for another year.
      • A new future improvements plan will address the nearly two-decade-old chairs from the days of unpacking them from a U-Haul at the Anthem school every week; a white stain is probably Christmas Eve candle wax, and a purple one is from the individual communion cups during COVID.
      • There were 104 new givers, 25 with recurring gifts. That matters not as “more people, more money,” but because it shows faithfulness and discipleship; the wallet usually lags behind when someone follows Christ, as with the rich young ruler and our tendency to hold on with a tight grip.

    4. 34:36 Engage and hospitality

      • Since 2020 the church’s front door has shifted from “the slidy thing” to “the clicky thing.” One elder watched services and kids’ content online for months before ever setting foot in the building, which is why Engage matters so much.
      • Many testified this year that they felt welcome, able to come in with whatever they carry and be accepted, loved, cared for, and discipled. The dramatic increases say people are hungry for the truth, so the church will keep preaching the word of God, and he will build his church.

    5. 36:04 Worship

      • The church remodeled the stage, replacing the old black curtains. Chris likes to move around, and the camera team used to laugh because he kept stepping out of the light and disappearing into shadows, especially dramatic online. Now the whole stage is usable, the audio is amazing, and the choir could use the entire stage. It wasn’t done to be pretty but to give their best without distractions.
      • They love singing about Jesus and will never stop declaring his word. If you have a gift of music and are withholding it, stop; they need your help. Chris keeps trying to fill in, and the team tells him that singing is not one of his gifts.

    6. 37:58 Youth

      • Youth ministry, a special place for the one who led it for almost a decade, is growing by leaps and bounds. Last year six students were baptized and four recommitted their lives to Christ, and serious Bible studies have started; there is a real hunger in the youth to understand what God is saying.

    7. 38:44 Kids for Truth

      • Kids for Truth, launched two years ago, set out to disciple elementary kids midweek while their parents attend groups. Registrations grew 24 percent from year one to year two.
      • Josh has seen growth in his 10-year-old son Nolan, and many parents say the same, because it isn’t babysitting: the kids learn core doctrine, apologetics, why we believe what we believe, and how to talk about it. If your family isn’t taking advantage of it, you are missing out.

    8. 40:07 Preschool memory verses

      • A video shows a preschool girl reciting her monthly memory verse, which she begged her mom to record so she could show Miss Holly. The beauty is not only kids excited to learn God’s word but parents helping them, so families study God’s word together. She read it upside down; she may be a future preschool director.

    9. 41:30 Bind marriage ministry

      • In its first year, Bind impacted 18 marriages, and 95 percent saw growth in all five areas it works on: connection, communication, conflict resolution, forgiveness, and intimacy.
      • A participant wrote that the course refreshed their 32-year marriage and made her fall even deeper in love with her husband. Whether your marriage is struggling or thriving, you never stop learning and appreciating each other; Bind runs again in the fall.

    10. 42:57 Life groups and men’s and women’s ministries

      • Life group participation rose 55 percent from the year before. This is discipleship, life on life, where people jump in to help and care for you when you struggle. After the last service, two couples, one of whom hadn’t been there in over a year, asked to join a life group. The men’s and women’s ministries are doing amazing things as well.

  10. 43:59 Where we’re going: the next 12 months

    • It is vital to pause and celebrate, and also to communicate where the church is going. The budget rises a modest 1.4 percent, based on current giving, which has worked out well; they ask for prayer for the stewardship team and elders.
    • The mission remains the same: change the way people see the church, even one person at a time, through life on life rather than attractional programs. The focus this year is twofold, teaching and serving, staying outward focused in the profane model, with three major goals.

    1. 45:21 Goal 1: classrooms

      • Two new classrooms open in the fall, with classes such as Christianity 101 and adult apologetics, and training space to help people prepare to give an answer for their hope on the streets, possibly including training for Crossroads Speaks.

    2. 45:57 Goal 2: the school

      • The church opens its school in the fall to educate kids well, both academically and in knowledge of the Scriptures, so they are ready to go into the world understanding their Bible.

    3. 46:32 Goal 3: the Care Center

      • The final goal is a Care Center just east of the classrooms, stocked with clothes, shoes, food, hygiene products, and dog food, volunteer run, where single moms, the homeless, and others can come in, grab what they need, be prayed for, and see the love of Christ.
      • It is the next phase toward the vision for 41st Drive, a place where ministry happens everywhere, from auto repair to legal advice, and people know the church will be there to walk with them.

  11. 47:34 Three invitations

    Scripture: Luke 11:42

    • The staff can’t do this without the church. Three requests, found on the app’s home page under “family meeting”: first, give your tithe; it is worship. A text from Luke 11 this morning: woe to the Pharisees who tithe mint, rue, and every herb but neglect justice and the love of God; “you ought to have done these without neglecting the others.” Both the tithe and serving with justice and love matter.
    • Second, sign up to meet needs. It is a commitment of attention, not time: you get an email when there is a need and help if it fits your schedule, building a database of people ready to step in. Third, get involved in a ministry and join a life group, being in community and in the word and sharpening one another. If you already do these three things, keep up the good work; if not, come on the journey.

  12. 49:13 His concerns or yours?

    Scripture: Mark 8:33

    • In Mark 8 Jesus rebuked Peter: “You do not have in mind the concerns of God, but merely human concerns.” Whose concerns do you have in mind? When your eyes are set on things above, your money, talents, and desires flow freely toward his church and the mission; when your own concerns are in mind, nothing flows. An old Bible professor lovingly called these folks “constipated Christians,” nasty, but it speaks to a deeper issue: no inward change.
    • Ask yourself every day, especially before walking in: are you here to serve or to be served? This church isn’t focused on pleasing the member or deciding by preferences; it is mission-driven, and even the pastor and elders come behind the mission and pursue it with the church.
    • The heart behind it: the church has lost its way and gotten distracted, and there is a call to lead it back to the mission, back to its first love, to seek and save what is lost. All that is left to ask is, are you with us?

  13. 50:57 Closing prayer

    • The prayer thanks God for the church and for all the ways he has resourced it and met needs. It all starts with what he does in hearts, when people repent of their sin, turn to him, and walk in his grace and mercy. The simple request is that he would keep using the church as he sees fit: your will be done.
