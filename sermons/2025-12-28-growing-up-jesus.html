Growing Up Jesus

Overview

  1. 00:45 Christmas gifts point to Christ’s greater gift

    Scripture: Romans 3:22-24 Romans 10:9-13 Romans 10:9

    • Chris recalls Christmas rest, food, family, giving, receiving, and the wonder of children opening gifts. That joy resembles seeing someone receive Christ’s forgiveness, grace, and mercy.
    • Romans announces one righteousness for Jew and Gentile: all sin, and all may be justified freely through Christ. Salvation is offered simply—confess Jesus as Lord, believe God raised him, and call on his name.
    • Jesus lived the perfect life people could not and became the sacrifice for sin. Do not admire Christmas while leaving his gift unopened.

  2. 05:11 What happened between the manger and ministry?

    • Christian holidays jump from Jesus’ birth to his death and resurrection, while Scripture says little about childhood and young adulthood.
    • Chris claims no special knowledge of the “lost years.” He will cautiously consider what life may have looked like for a Jewish boy who was fully God, fully human, and without sin.

  3. 06:46 From infancy, Jesus fulfilled the law

    Scripture: Luke 2:21 Matthew 5:17 Luke 2:22-24 Leviticus 12:1-8 Exodus 13:2 Luke 2:25-38 Luke 2:33 Luke 2:39-40 Luke 2:39

    • Jesus is circumcised and named on the eighth day. Born a Jew under the law, he fulfills prophecy and lives the law perfectly rather than abolishing it.
    • Joseph and Mary obey the purification, presentation, sacrifice, and firstborn-consecration requirements. Leviticus explains Mary’s purification and the affordable pair of birds; Exodus claims the firstborn for God.
    • Simeon receives Jesus and praises God for salvation to all nations, then warns Mary. Anna gives thanks and tells those awaiting redemption. These unexpected witnesses reassure the parents about the child’s identity.
    • Luke says they completed everything required before returning to Nazareth. Every step of Jesus’ life matters because he came to live beneath and fulfill the law.

  4. 14:17 A perfect child among imperfect people

    Scripture: Luke 2:39-40

    • Chris imagines parenting a sinless baby, toddler, and child. Genuine needs could produce crying, but selfish tantrums, refusal, and self-centeredness would not.
    • Jesus grew among imperfect parents, siblings, relatives, and friends without copying their failures. The thought of his brothers hearing “Why can’t you be more like Jesus?” supplies the comic edge.

  5. 17:37 At twelve, Jesus knows his Father and obeys his parents

    Scripture: Luke 2:41-52 Deuteronomy 16:5-6 Luke 2:48 Luke 2:49 Exodus 20:12; Deuteronomy 5:16 John 2:16 John 6:41-42 Luke 3:23 Mark 3:20-21 Mark 3:31-35 Luke 2:49 John 2:16 John 5:17-18 Luke 2:51-52 Luke 2:51-52 Matthew 13:53-57

    • Luke records the Passover trip, the missing twelve-year-old, three anxious days, and Jesus among the temple teachers. Deuteronomy shows that Jerusalem attendance was commanded, not merely family custom.
    • Jesus’ need to be in his Father’s house identifies God as his true Father without dishonoring Joseph and Mary. Later scenes in John, Luke, and Mark show how slowly family and neighbors understood that identity.
    • Although the temple felt like home, Jesus returns to Nazareth and obeys his parents because public ministry has not begun. He experiences ordinary stages of growth and gains favor with God and people.
    • Nazareth later recognizes the carpenter’s son and his family but takes offense when he returns teaching with wisdom and power. Chris asks listeners to consider their own ordinary sphere of influence.

  6. 26:28 A cautious reconstruction of eighteen unrecorded years

    Scripture: Hebrews 4:14-16

    • Scripture moves from age twelve to about thirty. Chris clearly marks his historical reconstruction as conjecture rather than revelation.
    • He proposes synagogue schooling, study of the law, entry into Joseph’s trade, years of carpentry, and possible family responsibility after Joseph’s death—while conceding that Scripture does not supply the details.
    • Hebrews provides the secure conclusion: Jesus lived outside a bubble, faced real temptation, never sinned, and can sympathize with human weakness.

  7. 31:42 The hidden years qualify Jesus to save and sympathize

    Scripture: Luke 2:52 Hebrews 4:15-16 2 Corinthians 5:21 Colossians 2:13-14 Isaiah 53:5

    • Jesus lived among real people, worked a real job, faced struggle, temptation, and loss, and kept the law in public life rather than escaping ordinary pressures.
    • Because the high priest was tempted without sin, believers approach the throne for mercy and grace. The sinless one then bears sin, cancels the legal debt at the cross, and fulfills Isaiah’s wounded servant.
    • The “in between” was a life people could not live, preparing Jesus to pay a debt they could not pay and defeat sin and death.

  8. 35:14 Prayer of gratitude for incarnation and atonement

    Scripture: Hebrews 9:22

    • Chris says ordinary words of thanks, surrender, and awe feel inadequate. Jesus entered his creation and lived human life as it was designed—perfectly.
    • He thanks Christ for taking human sin and dying on the cross, where forgiveness required blood, and asks listeners to grasp that the biblical life was history rather than a detached story.

  9. 37:29 Communion remembers the cross

    Scripture: Luke 22:19

    • Jesus came to pay for sin at the cross. The bread and cup remember his sacrifice, following his command to do this in remembrance of him.
