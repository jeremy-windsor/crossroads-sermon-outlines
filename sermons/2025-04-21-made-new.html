Overview

  1. 00:11 Happy Easter: victory on the cross and in the tomb

    • Christ follows up a victory on the cross with a victory in the tomb. Josh prays that those convinced of this victory would have hearts, minds, and homes filled with assurance, love, hope, and joy as they enjoy the harvest that comes because of Christ.
    • To the unconvinced: he has been praying for them all week. Some may be here kicking and screaming, and that’s okay; he was a kicker too, a kicker turned preacher. When the Lord grabs a heart, it is never the same. Over the past 38 hours the church has set its heart on the two most important events in human history: Christ’s crucifixion and Christ’s resurrection.

  2. 01:05 A confession about the “Chreasters”

    • Josh grew up in church, and what has pastors scratching their heads this time of year is the dramatic increase in attendance on Easter. Because honesty is the key to any successful relationship, he is upfront: there is a term for the Easter and Christmas crowds, “Chreasters.” It’s messed up, and he confesses he has used it.
    • Leave it to the church to name-call. Imagine his shock when he became the very thing he hated about the church. He asks forgiveness for his pride and arrogance, and on behalf of the church for hers. We get so hyped for Jesus that we forget how to be patient with others, even though the Lord is patient with us every day; we have perverted his grace and taken a moral high ground when we should be serving and loving.

    1. 02:42 The one time the church agrees

      • Pausing to defend the Chreasters, Josh asks: is it possible some people only come this time of year because it is the only time the church can actually agree on something? Christian unity is most visible in the spring.
      • Whatever the jersey, denomination, tradition, language, tribe, color, preferred translation, or end-times view, every Christian answers yes to the big three: Did Jesus live? Did Jesus die? Was Jesus raised? We may disagree on a lot, but during Holy Week Christian unity is at an all-time high, and he loves it.

  3. 03:54 Give the church another shot

    • Josh pleads with the visitors to give the church another shot; it doesn’t have to be this church, but try authentic Christian community again. There are bored, cranky Christians calling people names who seem holy, and, he lets them in on a secret, their lives are a mess.
    • But there are also amazing, loving, selfless, humble Christians who just want to love God and love others. Sometimes you have to deal with the bad to see the good. This church is full of committed family members who will walk with you, and who understand that the only tradition worth going to war over is the worship of our resurrected King, Savior, and God, Jesus Christ.

  4. 05:05 Everything pointed to Jesus

    • Josh is certain the creator of the universe willingly died on a cross and victoriously departed from the grave. This isn’t a hopeless shot in the dark or a thoughtless conclusion. He has considered it his entire life, compiled the evidence from his own life, from those before him, from creation, and from his fellow man, and read the Scriptures over and over. No matter what he saw, good or bad, everything pointed to Jesus.

    1. 05:41 When creation speaks

      • April is said to take its name from a Latin verb meaning “to open,” and right now the buds and blossoms are opening; yellow flowers are filling the pool skimmer. Open your eyes, because the universe is giving testimony to the risen king. A flower that was a pile of twigs two months ago is now worth clicking a heart over. Let creation speak: what you thought was dead is filled with life, and what you thought ended is a new beginning. How many seasons need to pass before you seek the Lord?
      • If a single blossom seems like no big deal, consider a dying woman with no energy left, given a final burst by the words of her favorite hymn so she can praise the Lord in her final breath. What is that spark but creation crying out that even in death there can be joy?
      • The clock is ticking and the seasons keep passing. Skip spring cleaning this year; it will just get dirty again. Instead, take this time to seek the Lord.

  5. 07:26 When history speaks

    • Some won’t listen when creation speaks. Will they listen when history speaks? There is more evidence that Jesus rose from the dead than that Christopher Columbus discovered North America, and it’s not even close.

    1. 07:44 Jesus lived, died, and was buried

      • First, Jesus lived. Historians inside and outside the faith agree; no one who wants to keep their job would dispute it.
      • Next, Jesus died, in the most brutal execution method ever conceived. He was beaten and nailed to a cross, and a professional executioner called the time of death at the scene. A spear went up through his rib cage into his heart, and water and blood poured out, indicating certain death.
      • Then he was buried. Joseph of Arimathea would have wrapped him head to toe in linen with a significant amount of spices. Throw away the picture of a single bed sheet laid over the body; it would have looked more like an ancient mummification, layers and layers of material and spices.

    2. 08:59 Don’t fart in the fort

      • Josh was known in his family for epic blanket forts, “not so much my preaching, but I can build a fort.” The problem is that blankets don’t ventilate, so the golden rule was: don’t fart in the fort. Put a blanket over your head at home and after a few minutes you will struggle to breathe.
      • He tells this because some will say Jesus never died. That is madness. If the crucifixion didn’t kill him, the spear did; and if the centurion somehow missed, which isn’t true because water and blood came out, Jesus would have suffocated. Islam, founded about 550 years after the resurrection, says Jesus didn’t die, but it takes a bigger miracle than the resurrection to believe that: the beating, the crucifixion, the spear, the suffocation, no medical treatment, gaping wounds, left in a tomb.

    3. 11:04 He predicted it, and he claimed to be God

      Scripture: John 8:58 John 10:30 John 14:6 John 6:38

      • Jesus lived, died, was buried, and three days later rose. All four Gospels record Jesus predicting his suffering, his death, and, to the day, his resurrection. That places him in a league of his own: if someone predicts their death, mad respect, but they’re dead. If someone accurately predicts both, we should probably listen and wonder what else he said.
      • He claims to be God. In John 8 he says, “Before Abraham was born, I am,” using the name God used with Moses at the burning bush. In John 10 he doubles down: “I and the Father are one,” and the people picked up stones to kill him. He tells them to look at his works and let his actions speak.
      • In John 14 he says he is the only way to God: “I am the way and the truth and the life.” In John 6 he says God sent him on a mission: “I have come down from heaven not to do my will but to do the will of him who sent me,” a will the church reflected on in Gethsemane two days earlier. Jesus is God in the flesh, came to die to take away sin, and said he would rise and give newness of life.

    4. 13:14 “Let my actions tell you who I am”

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:20–22

      • Jesus told the disciples it was fine if they didn’t get the full picture yet: watch this unfold and let my actions tell you who I am. Ever since Mary gazed into the empty tomb and saw the blood-soaked linens but nobody, the church has declared, “But Christ has indeed been raised from the dead, the first fruits of those who have fallen asleep… For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive.”

  6. 13:54 Evidence placed on purpose

    • The evidence for this monumental event is overwhelming, and it isn’t accidental. Jesus intentionally placed evidence for those who would come later. Call it breadcrumbs or M&M’s; Josh doesn’t care how you picture it, as long as it brings you to a risen Christ and eternal life.

    1. 14:20 He appeared to people who knew him

      Scripture: Isaiah 53:5 Isaiah 52:13

      • Jesus made sure to appear to people who knew him well before the crucifixion, who could identify him by his voice and by characteristics only they would notice. He invited Thomas to touch his nail marks and feel his side, gifting men with absolute proof.
      • These men grew up reading the scroll of Isaiah. Isaiah 53: “He was pierced for our transgressions… and by his wounds we are healed.” Isaiah 52: “See, my servant will act wisely; he will be raised and lifted up and highly exalted.” Thomas touched the servant Isaiah prophesied about, touched the Messiah, and was convinced.

    2. 15:37 Standing next to the one holding the keys

      Scripture: Revelation 1:18

      • Josh doesn’t think Thomas was any more convinced than he is right now, though he has seen nothing: “Surely he is risen. Jesus lives. He reigns. Death is not the end.”
      • In Revelation 1 Jesus tells his churches, “I am the Living One; I was dead, and now look, I am alive for ever and ever! And I hold the keys of death and Hades.” You can stand wherever and next to whoever you want, but Josh stands next to the one holding the keys. He is welling up with joy; he is supposed to preach but wants to sing and dance, and he doesn’t even like dancing.

    3. 16:32 Forty days of witnesses: his mother, James, and Paul

      • Jesus put witnesses in place so we could share the joy of his victory 2,000 years later, and our grandkids until he comes. For 40 days he developed witness reports, once appearing to over 500 people at one time. He ate, spoke, taught, moved through solid objects, and vanished instantly.
      • The witnesses included his mother. How hard would it be to convince your mom you are God, when you can’t even take out the trash when she asks? Josh pauses to have everyone hug their mom or blow her a kiss.
      • They included James, the brother of Jesus. It would take a resurrection miracle for your brother to call you God, yet James became the pastor of the church in Jerusalem. They included Paul, who wrote today’s text, who persecuted Christians as worshipers of a false god until Jesus appeared to him and he started praying for them instead. Minds and hearts don’t change like that without divine intervention.

    4. 19:11 The cloud of witnesses: baptism, communion, and Sunday

      Scripture: Romans 6:3–5

      • The trail of witnesses runs from one woman, Mary, to 11 men, to 500, to 3,000, to billions of believers across history. For centuries Christians have been baptized to identify with Jesus in his death, burial, and resurrection, believing that like him they will die, be buried, and rise.
      • In Romans 6 Paul says we were buried with him through baptism into death so that we too may live a new life, and Josh loves the last phrase: if we have been united with him in a death like his, we will certainly be united with him in a resurrection like his.
      • So Christians were baptized worldwide, took communion to remember his body and blood, and started meeting on Sunday. Getting Jews who loved to meet on Saturday to change their calendar was a huge deal, but not after witnessing Christ raised. Do you see how little tradition matters when Jesus is at the center? Today Christianity is the world’s largest faith community and Jesus the most revered name in human history.

  7. 21:05 How do you account for changed lives?

    • Some may remain unconvinced after creation and history. As Andrea said earlier, skeptics are welcome to ask questions here, but if you challenge us, we get to challenge you, and you need compelling evidence of your own. It always comes back to one thing: how do you account for a changed life, for billions of changed lives, for new desires at the very core of a person? These changes don’t always benefit people; some cost them greatly, and many happen when culture doesn’t demand it.

    1. 22:18 An example: turning from porn

      Scripture: Matthew 5:27–28

      • Our culture accepts porn as a reasonable alternative to sex, yet redeemed men like Josh are turning away from it in droves. Culture says you can watch porn and be a decent member of society, so there is no reason to kill it unless you encounter the teachings of Christ.
      • In Matthew 5 Jesus says anyone who looks at a woman lustfully has already committed adultery with her in his heart. In two sentences Jesus labeled every man in the room an adulterer. You have to explain what causes a stranger of Christ to become a servant of Christ.

    2. 23:18 Practicing Christians and real help

      • As of 2024, Pornhub receives 115 million visits per day, 2 billion a month, a poison taking over the minds of men and women. But viewership drops nearly 40% for Christians who attend church weekly: not professing Christians but practicing ones, not part-time but full-time. You have to explain that.
      • If you don’t know how to protect your children online, ask the church for help; you give your kid a curfew but don’t filter their websites, and we have to do better. Josh will personally stop what he is doing to help. If you are already struggling with addiction, there is a recovery program on Monday nights; you don’t have to fight alone.

    3. 24:43 “His followers made it all up”?

      • Skeptics say his followers made it up. Why? They gained nothing: they were rejected, beaten, tortured, and murdered. If anyone had motivation to lie, it was the Sanhedrin, whose leaders paid the soldiers to spread misinformation about the missing body because the truth would cost them influence, authority, and their way of life. The soldiers were paid to keep a lie, and it still got out.
      • Human nature is to preserve self. No one conjures up that kind of lie and then gives their life to protect it; when many people share a lie, someone eventually recants to save their own skin. See Watergate.
      • Peter is nailed to a cross upside down and still can’t say what would get him down: “I saw him. Jesus Christ is alive.” From coward to conqueror. If you can explain that without the resurrection, be Josh’s guest. He is confident that if circumstances demanded, he would give his life for the truth.

  8. 26:35 If Christ has not been raised

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:12–19

    • Josh grants this: take away the resurrection and there is no Christianity. In 1 Corinthians 15 Paul tells us what we lose: if Christ has not been raised, our preaching is useless and so is your faith; we are false witnesses about God; your faith is futile and you are still in your sins; those who have fallen asleep in Christ are lost; and if only for this life we have hope in Christ, we are to be pitied more than all others.
    • If Jesus is still dead, his bones are somewhere underground in Jerusalem. If he didn’t rise, neither will we. If death defeats Jesus, death defeats us all; if he didn’t win, no one will.

    1. 27:52 We don’t like death

      • We don’t like death. That’s why we go carnivore, cold plunge, and skip church for hot yoga. Deep down we know death is the enemy, not a natural part of life, an additive no one wants.
      • Josh’s wife won’t stop making sourdough. The dough gets its own basket and blanket, timers go off to tuck and fold, and he never thought he would be envious of bread. She says it has fewer preservatives; he wonders whether that adds four months or four years, and would rather watch John Wick. He expects to get in trouble for this.
      • But if death killed Jesus and not the other way around, we are hopeless.

    2. 30:00 If Jesus is dead, we lose everything

      • We would also lose the preaching and teaching of the apostles. Even setting aside that American Christians already treat their Bibles like paperweights, if Jesus is still dead it is a complete waste of time: no Christianity, no church, no truth, no Spirit, no forgiveness, no heaven, no hell, no eternal life, no judgment, no justice, no point. Everyone who has died is lost and nothing we do matters. If Jesus is dead, we lose everything.

  9. 30:36 The gospel in two words: “But Christ”

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:20

    • The gospel in two words: “But Christ.” The man writing this hated Christians, but now he is one, and he dedicated his life’s work to making sure every Christian knew what they had in Christ: “But Christ has indeed been raised from the dead, the first fruits of those who have fallen asleep.”

    1. 31:00 Falling asleep in Jesus

      • Most Christians hope to be alive when Jesus returns, and there is nothing wrong with that; “Maranatha” is Josh’s cry: come, Lord Jesus. But never dying isn’t all that exciting to him. From this text, falling asleep seems by far the greatest way to go: to enter heaven having walked the same path as Christ and the saints, to know death and resurrection as Jesus did.
      • Paul describes Christians this way: we don’t die, we rest. Our heads hit the pillow beat up, achy, sore, and weary, and we benefit from a good night’s sleep. How much more from eternal rest: no more trouble, tears, sorrow, pain, soreness, or weariness.

    2. 32:58 Soul and body reunited; all things made new

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:8 Revelation 21:5

      • Paul says to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord. A Christian dies and the soul goes to be with God, and one day soul and body will be reunited, just as happened to Jesus in the tomb. We will rise with eternal, glorified, sinless bodies, hopefully with better knees and without the bald spot.
      • We will enter God’s presence where he has made all things new, a new heaven and a new earth, and we will have been made new too: “He who is seated on the throne said, ‘Behold, I am making all things new.’” As Christians we are either laboring or resting. To know both death and resurrection is a divine blessing; Christians live differently, and we die differently too.

  10. 33:53 Christ the first fruits

    Scripture: Leviticus 23:9–11 Romans 5

    • What does it mean that Christ is the first fruits? Josh travels back to Leviticus 23: when Israel entered the land and reaped its harvest, they were to bring the priest a sheaf of the first grain, and he would wave it before the Lord so it would be accepted on their behalf, on the day after the Sabbath.
    • In the Jewish calendar Passover came first, then a Sabbath, then the feast of first fruits. That is what Paul means in Romans 5 when he says Christ came at just the right time. Christ, the Lamb of God, died on Passover; his body lay in the tomb on the Sabbath; and early on the first day, as the sun came over the horizon, he rose on the morning of the feast of first fruits.
    • Christ is the sheaf waved before the Lord on your behalf. He is one sheaf out of an entire harvest, and when the first sheaf had been waved, the whole harvest was considered brought before the Lord: dedicated, consecrated, and accepted. When Jesus was raised, all God’s chosen were virtually risen in him.

    1. 35:32 You are risen and made new today

      • Rescued child of God, it is time to rejoice: you are risen in Christ and made new today. Because Jesus is raised, we will also be raised. The tomb is empty, and that changes how we live and how we die. If we keep preaching and sharing Christ, it can change marriages, families, children, and grandchildren. Jesus changes everything.

  11. 36:04 Are you part of the harvest?

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:54–55

    • Josh has one question: are you part of the harvest? Do you believe in the resurrection of Jesus Christ for your sin, that eternal life is in him alone? Have you surrendered your life to him? Have you been crucified with Christ, or are you still living for yourself? If you see him on the cross and know it should have been you, then when you see the empty tomb, know that will be you one day.
    • Do you hate the things you once loved? Do you have more power against temptation? Do you want to be like Jesus, or do you just want his treasures? Josh can’t judge anyone, but he wants to empower them to judge themselves before God, uncover the truth in their hearts, and call on him to fix it.
    • He believes Jesus rose and that all who trust in him will be clothed in immortality and made new. On the day the last trumpet sounds, what Isaiah and Hosea wrote over 700 years before Christ will come true: “Death has been swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your victory? Where, O death, is your sting?”

  12. 37:30 A chance to respond

    Scripture: Romans 10:9–11

    • In Romans 10 Paul says that if you declare with your mouth “Jesus is Lord” and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved, and as Scripture says, anyone who believes in him will never be put to shame. That last line is the battle cry of Josh’s heart.
    • If you believe in your heart, profess it with your mouth today: the baptism waters are ready, so go get a T-shirt and get in the water. Those who became Christians today are invited to communion, proclaiming his death until he comes. It is also a time to give tithes and offerings in response to the Lord’s generosity, helping spread the gospel and serve the community, while the choir leads a celebration because Jesus is alive and one day will make everything new.

  13. 39:07 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays that the desire of his heart is to follow the Lord even when it costs him and to look more like him even when it means sacrifice. He comes humbled because of what happened that morning about 2,000 years ago, thanks God for resurrection life and for making us new, and asks him to convict hearts, change minds, and set the church apart for his glory alone.
