Overview

  1. 00:42 Week three: it was always the plan

    Scripture: Luke 24:25-27

    • Welcome to week three of Broken. As they prepare for Easter they remember not only that Christ died and rose, but that it was the plan—a move greater than Jonah, not an audible at the line of scrimmage. Prescripted before the game started.
    • Josh is always reminded of the risen Lord on the road to Emmaus, one of the most important New Testament moments. How foolish you are and slow to believe all the prophets have spoken. Did not the Messiah have to suffer these things and then enter his glory? Beginning with Moses and all the prophets, he explained the Scriptures concerning himself.

    1. 01:35 The first gospel and the accuser hurled down

      Scripture: Genesis 3:15 Revelation 12:10-12

      • Josh guesses Jesus began at Genesis 3: enmity between the serpent and the woman’s offspring; he will crush your head, you will strike his heel. Protoevangelium: first picture of a suffering Messiah and of victory over Satan, sin, and death. The method Satan meant for victory became his defeat.
      • John records a loud voice in heaven: now have come salvation, power, the kingdom, and the authority of his Messiah. The accuser who accuses day and night has been hurled down. They triumphed by the blood of the Lamb and the word of their testimony and did not shrink from death.

    2. 03:36 Isaac, Passover, bronze snake, the psalms

      Scripture: Genesis 22:1-14 Exodus 12:1-13 John 19:30 Numbers 21:8-9 John 3:14-15 Psalm 22:1 Psalm 118:22-23 Zechariah 12:10

      • No copy of the Emmaus sermon exists. Josh guesses Genesis 22: Isaac and Jesus both carry the wood; a substitute is provided for Isaac; Jesus is the substitute, the true lamb. He wishes God had given the outline, then admits that is foolish; the Lord wants us to make the outline, read the prophets, look for Jesus.
      • Exodus 12 follows naturally: unblemished lamb, the lamb must die, blood applied, only those covered are saved. In Egypt, judgment meets sacrifice. Everyone had just watched Jesus die at Passover. The lamb was always pointing to me. Tetelestai. Look to the lamb.
      • Numbers 21: bronze snake on a pole; anyone bitten who looks lives. Hands for snakebites; a rattlesnake four times; Josh’s scorpion-in-shorts story. Jesus tells Nicodemus this applies to him. Next series after Easter will go there. The Son of Man must be lifted up. Look to the lamb and live.
      • Psalm 22 describes crucifixion before Israel used the method. Jesus quotes it from the cross. Psalm 118: the rejected stone becomes the cornerstone. Zechariah: they will look on me, the one they have pierced, and mourn. Have you owned your culpability? At Pentecost Peter said God made this Jesus whom you crucified both Lord and Messiah.

    3. 09:59 Unity without uniformity, then Isaiah and Daniel

      Scripture: Acts 2:36 Isaiah 53:1-12 Psalm 16:9-11 Daniel 7:13-14 Luke 24:32

      • George last week saw significance in Israel’s 1948 re-establishment. George is a cherished brother. Josh asked him to preach knowing the view differs. Celebrate disagreement with unity, not uniformity. Josh believes God is sovereign over history and 1948 is remarkable, and he respects Christians who see those promises fulfilled in Christ and the church rather than a modern state. They do not call names here. George has godly fruit coming out of his ears. Thank you for covering so Josh could invest in his marriage.
      • Isaiah 53: theological explanation of the cross seven hundred years prior. Psalm 16: you will not abandon me to the realm of the dead. Daniel 7: after suffering, glory. The Emmaus big idea shatters a Messiah who only conquers. Two-stage mission: suffer, then enter glory. Hearts burned. Does anyone’s heart burn today? Moses and the prophets point to the Lamb, the servant, the king.

  2. 13:39 One goal: see Jesus the King

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 8:9 Philippians 2:6-8

    • Road, mountain, valley, lake. While he walked the earth he never ceased to be God or human. He was glorious in part because he restrained his glory. Though rich, he became poor. Though God, he made himself nothing, a servant in human likeness. If it is blessed to be made in God’s image, it is a horrible thing for God to take man’s image—yet he did, including the limitations, even obeying death. Humility is not weakness. Obedience is the path toward freedom.

    1. 15:28 The mountain: transfigured

      Scripture: Matthew 17:1-13 Luke 9:28-29

      • After six days Jesus takes Peter, James, and John up a high mountain. Face like the sun, clothes white as light. Moses and Elijah appear. Peter offers three shelters. A bright cloud: this is my Son whom I love; with him I am well pleased. Listen to him. They fall face down, terrified. Jesus touches them: get up, do not be afraid. They see no one except Jesus. Tell no one until the Son of Man is raised. They ask about Elijah; Jesus says Elijah has already come, and they understand John the Baptist.
      • If you want to see his glory, get away from the noise. Loneliness is often when God appears: Abraham, Hagar, Jacob, Moses, Elijah, Daniel, the woman at the well, John. Josh took off his Apple Watch for two weeks; haptic feedback would not let him rest. Technology is convenient and noisy. Do not forsake gathering. Transfigured gives us metamorphosis: outside change from the inside. Luke notes he was in prayer when his appearance altered. Prayer is powerful. Peter: it is good for us to be here. Even before words, bowing the head, chaos falls away. Pray continually. Like Mary, learn the closet.

    2. 20:15 Promise kept, Moses and Elijah, a white horse

      Scripture: Matthew 16:28 Mark 5:35-42 Revelation 19:11-16

      • Fulfillment of a promise six days earlier: some standing here will not taste death before they see the Son of Man coming in his kingdom—Peter, James, and John. They also saw Jairus’s daughter raised and went deep into Gethsemane. Josh’s way of explaining the healings: Jesus removes afflictions he would take on himself. Jairus’s daughter is raised because he will surrender to death. He transfigures to teach them he will be glorified in death.
      • Moses is the law, Elijah the prophets; all point to Christ and are fulfilled in him. John sees heaven open, a white horse, Faithful and True, eyes like fire, robe dipped in blood, Word of God, King of kings. Amen.
      • The mountain is not the everyday. The key to seeing Jesus in glory is hearing the Word. God shouts: this is my Son. Listen to him. The Godhead commands you to listen to the Godhead. They fall face down. You can have the right posture and the wrong response. Praise God righteousness depends on his perfection.

    3. 24:20 They spoke about his exodus

      Scripture: Luke 9:30-31 Matthew 16:24 Malachi 4:5-6 Revelation 11:3-6

      • Luke: Moses and Elijah spoke about his departure, which he was about to fulfill at Jerusalem. The word is exodus. Moses talks to Jesus about his exodus: a greater Moses delivering from a new Pharaoh—Satan, sin, and death.
      • Sixty-six books, forty-plus authors, three continents, three languages, about 1,500 years, yet one story: garden gone wrong, a cross, a garden made right. Tolkien, Rowling, Lewis display patterns first in God’s Word. You can even witness God in a non-believer’s creativity.
      • The transfiguration teaches that Jesus is glorified when we deny ourselves, take up the cross, and follow. The world says save yourself; the Christian says surrender. Cross before crown. That is why they must not tell yet: no glory apart from the cross. A temple on the mountain next week is not the way. Look at 70 AD.
      • Coming down, they still puzzle over Elijah and Malachi 4. Some think Elijah will be a Revelation 11 witness; others think John the Baptist fulfilled it. Either way, Jesus was promised by Moses and preceded by Elijah. He fulfills law and prophets.

  3. 30:01 Down the mountain: power in the valley

    Scripture: Matthew 17:14-20 Revelation 3:15-16 Revelation 2:20

    • From glory on a mountain to pain in the valley. A man kneels: mercy on my son, seizures, fire and water; disciples could not heal him. Unbelieving and perverse generation. Bring the boy. Jesus rebukes the demon; the boy is healed. Why could we not? Little faith. Faith as a mustard seed moves mountains. Nothing will be impossible.
    • If Jesus was frustrated then, how does he feel about us? We bet on addiction, worry finances, fear sharing, refuse to forgive, skip prayer and Scripture, panic, please man. Everyone loves Beanie Baby Jesus. Josh bets he is irritated. What if Jesus wrote us a letter? He did: lukewarm, Jezebel, compromise, false teaching. Cowardly Christians condemn faithful ones for naming sin. A sermon like his would empty parking lots like Blockbuster. Praise God he endures unbelief. Do not let patience make you content with less than extraordinary faith.
    • He still heals. Run to Jesus, not merely to disciples. He is the great physician. Nothing is impossible for the man or woman who trusts God’s power. Nurture the seed. Stay ready to rescue whoever he puts in the path.

  4. 35:00 King Jesus in humility: second passion prediction

    Scripture: Matthew 17:22-23

    • In Galilee: the Son of Man will be delivered, killed, and raised the third day. Disciples filled with grief. Second passion prediction in the series. Next week Matthew 26, which Josh calls the holiest moment in the New Testament.

    1. 36:35 The temple tax and a fish

      Scripture: Matthew 17:24-27 Exodus 30:11-16 Matthew 12:6

      • Only Matthew tells this. Collectors ask Peter if the teacher pays the two-drachma tax. Yes. Jesus already knows: from whom do kings collect, children or others? Children are exempt. So that we may not cause offense, throw a line; the first fish has a four-drachma coin for my tax and yours.
      • Matthew the former tax collector would remember. Not a Roman tax. Exodus 30: every Jewish male over twenty, half-shekel for temple upkeep. George said last week Jesus is greater than the temple. The curtain will tear. Why pay? So we may not cause offense. A coin in a fish, Peter who usually nets, a line this time. Josh could not catch a fish in an aquarium.
      • Christian freedom should never have victims. Be good citizens. Pay taxes, tithe, work hard, love wives and kids. We are headed to a heavenly kingdom and responsible for an earthly one now.

  5. 40:31 A hard stewardship decision

    Scripture: Philippians 1:3

    • Chris Towald has served as executive pastor five years, a decade of ministry with Josh, youth ministry, kayaks, kill ball. He invested in staff and people and supported Josh through the hardest ministry and life moments. Proud to call him a friend.
    • Attendance is growing, especially younger families; giving has not kept pace. After prayer Josh became convinced staff size kept them from servicing debt and the facility they hope to pass on. In unity and sadness the executive pastor role ends May 31, 2026. Not light, not quick, prayed, discussed by elders. Grateful. Committed to loving Chris and family. Holly remains on staff.
    • Pray for Chris, Holly, Laura, and Braden. Braden has ministry on his heart; Josh’s rebellion began in a church moment like this. Stay close to the Lord. Guard unity. Questions go to elders or Josh, not the parking lot. Keep leaning into the mission Chris helped establish in a cabin with a Bible and a friend: change the way people see the church. Paul to the Philippians: I thank my God every time I remember you.

    1. 46:12 Mountain, valley, shoreline

      • Josh thanks God for lonely mountaintop moments that change you forever, for valley ministry and healings, for shoreline faith tossing a line when you are not sure. God provides. He is not done with Chris. Carry him. In Jesus’ name, amen.
