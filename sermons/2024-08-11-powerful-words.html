Overview

  1. 00:05 Final week of Misquoted: a quick review

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 12:9

    • This is the final week of Misquoted, and Chris gives a quick review whether everyone likes it or not. Week one: “Jesus says not to judge,” which Josh did a wonderful job showing has been misquoted. Week two: “Follow your heart.” Chris has followed his heart right off a cliff; his feelings didn’t work out so well.
    • Week three: “God won’t give you more than you can handle.” Chris has always thought we got one word wrong: it should say God won’t give you more than he can handle. Chris has had more than he can handle many times, but in his weakness God’s grace was sufficient and his power was made perfect.
    • Week four: “I can be perfect if I try.” If someone says they can be perfect because the Holy Spirit is in them, Chris wants to interview those closest to them: spouse, kids, co-workers, best friends. He does not give permission to interview the people in his life, because he would hate for anyone to find out he wasn’t always wonderful, kind, gentle, and loving.

  2. 02:14 Standing in the middle of head and heart

    • The church holds a wide range of backgrounds, from very conservative to Pentecostal, and many landed here because they saw that whichever extreme they came from put too much emphasis on rules and legalism or on the gifts of the Spirit and feelings: one side heavy on head knowledge without heart, the other heavy on heart without head knowledge.
    • Here they stand in the middle: head knowledge through great teaching and discipleship, and heart and Spirit through loving one another, Crossroads Cares, and life groups. Today’s misquote generally happens on the Pentecostal side of the aisle, and Chris wants to show how it is dangerous and can actually push people away from the Lord.

  3. 03:31 The misquote: “My words have the power to create”

    Scripture: John 14:13–14 Mark 11:23–25 John 15:7 Luke 11:9–10

    • The misquote: “My words have the power to create.” If that isn’t familiar, you have probably heard it called manifesting, or name it and claim it. The belief is that our words have power to manifest or create, and whatever we ask or claim will happen through the power of Christ.
    • Chris reads the passages it draws on: John 14, “I will do whatever you ask in my name… You may ask me for anything in my name, and I will do it”; Mark 11, if anyone says to this mountain, “Go, throw yourself into the sea,” and does not doubt, it will be done; John 15, ask whatever you wish and it will be done for you; and Luke 11, ask and it will be given to you, seek and you will find, knock and the door will be opened.

    1. 05:01 A Pepsi, 20 pounds, and a Porsche

      Scripture: John 14:13–14

      • The theme is that if we ask for anything in Jesus’ name and don’t doubt, we will get it, and Chris would love that. Taking John 14 at its word, he prays for a Pepsi right now in Jesus’ name, for 20 pounds off his body (probably more like 30 or 40, but he doesn’t want to get greedy), and for his Chevy Cruze to turn into a beautiful blue Porsche, though he would settle for a Tesla.
      • He isn’t really being silly: he would like a Pepsi, he’s addicted, and who wouldn’t want a nice car? Then he gets serious, praying for everyone in the body with cancer, disease, or recovering from surgery to be healed, and for anyone struggling financially to see their finances turned around this week, in Jesus’ name.
      • These are prayers Chris and the entire staff lift up for the body. We know and believe God can and does answer them, but is it always with a yes, a healing, a financial turnaround?

  4. 07:07 Two personal stories

    • Chris shares two of his own experiences with words having the power to create, manifest, or name it and claim it.

    1. 07:16 The anointed wallet

      • When Chris and Holly lived in New Hampshire, one of her relatives pastored a Pentecostal church, a wonderful, sweet man Chris loved. Visiting one Sunday, Chris was unexpectedly called to the front, asked for his wallet, and watched the pastor anoint it with oil and pray over it and him for a financial blessing, promising that within a year they would see something big.
      • God has always provided and they have had what they need, but a year went by, then two, then more, and no big financial blessing came. The pastor was sincere, truly believed it, and asked in Jesus’ name, but it did not manifest. Some might say it was because Chris didn’t believe, but he wasn’t the one asking.

    2. 08:47 His mother’s cancer

      • When Chris’s mom was diagnosed with cancer, she had grown weak and her kidneys were failing; during treatment, mistakes introduced bacteria into her bloodstream. Family, friends, and the church were all praying for healing.
      • One of his uncles believed whatever you ask in Jesus’ name will be done, period. He prayed for her healing and told Chris’s dad he never had a doubt, because God was going to give him what he asked for. She died 12 days after being diagnosed.
      • His uncle was devastated; it rocked him to the core of his beliefs. Thankfully he wrestled with it, came to a greater understanding of God’s word, and made peace with what happened.

  5. 10:02 What the manifest preachers teach

    • That is what Chris wants the church to do today: wrestle with it, even though there isn’t time to go all the way down the road. What are the dangers of believing my words have the power to create, and how can it lead someone to fall away from the faith?
    • In a nutshell, the manifest preachers teach that my words create the family, the job, and the life I want, and also the terrible circumstances I am in: a miserable job, poverty, a terrible marriage, struggles with my kids, low self-esteem, or depression. If I pray for healing and don’t get it, it is because I don’t have enough faith, or worse, I am sick as punishment for some secret sin. Good or bad, you have created the world you live in by your words.

  6. 11:40 Creation is reserved for the Creator

    Scripture: Genesis 1:3, 6, 9, 14, 24 Genesis 1:26 Genesis 1:28

    • Chris isn’t saying words aren’t powerful; he will get to their true power. But we do not create or manifest things with our words. Creation is reserved solely for the Creator God, who created with his words.
    • In Genesis 1, verses 3, 6, 9, 14, and 24, each day of creation starts with “And God said,” followed by “Let there be”: let there be lights, let the water teem with living creatures, let the land produce living creatures. On the sixth day, “Then God said, ‘Let us make mankind.’” God spoke and creation just happened. Chris wishes he had been there.
    • We are made in his image, but we are not little gods. Verse 28 is where mankind got its marching orders: be fruitful and increase in number, fill the earth and subdue it, rule over the fish, the birds, and every living creature. If God intended us to create, he would have given us that mandate at creation; creating is reserved for God alone.

  7. 13:17 Why we got so far off course

    • Chris submits that name it and claim it fits very well with American ideals: you can be anything you want, do anything you put your mind to, be rich or famous or happy if you speak it into the universe and truly believe it. You see it every time you turn on the TV. It sounds an awful lot like New Age mysticism and Oprah-type religion, and like the Joel Osteens who tell you wealth, health, and happiness are yours if you just speak and believe it.
    • America truly is a land of opportunity, and hard work really can pay off.

    1. 14:15 Truth is the same for all people in all places

      • But biblical truth doesn’t care about geography, politics, language, social standing, or circumstances; it is the same for all people at all times in all places. If Christians can truly create with their words, that applies to believers all over the world.
      • Do Christians in China pray for freedom to gather without fear of persecution? Do believers in the Middle East pray not to be found by Muslim extremists who will murder them? Do starving believers pray for something to eat this week? Is something wrong with their faith? Do they not believe hard enough? Does God not care about them as much as people elsewhere?
      • We don’t even have to leave the room: the church is praying for people with cancer and disease and a dear brother fighting for his life in the hospital as Chris preaches. Is there not a single person asking in Jesus’ name with enough faith that God will heal them just because we want it?

  8. 15:52 How this misquote damages faith

    Scripture: John 14:13–14 Mark 11:24

    • Chris reminds the church what John 14 says: “I will do whatever you ask in my name… You may ask me for anything in my name, and I will do it.” The danger of not understanding these passages as part of the whole counsel of Scripture is that we could damage someone’s faith.
    • Imagine praying according to John 14, like his uncle praying for his mom, and not getting what you asked for. You begin to seriously doubt your faith: God didn’t answer because I didn’t believe I had received it, as Mark 11 says. My belief isn’t strong enough; I’m not a good enough Christian; or even worse, God must not be real or must not care about me.

  9. 16:58 John 14 in context

    Scripture: John 14:1–15 John 14:12 John 14:13–14

    • If Jesus isn’t saying we can get whatever we want by asking and believing, what are these passages teaching? In John 14 Jesus is speaking to his disciples, preparing them for when he won’t be around and answering their questions, so Chris reads verses 1–15 for context.
    • Do not let your hearts be troubled; my Father’s house has many rooms; I go to prepare a place for you. Thomas says they don’t know the way, and Jesus answers, “I am the way and the truth and the life.” Philip, not quite getting it, asks to be shown the Father: anyone who has seen me has seen the Father. Whoever believes will do the works I have been doing; I will do whatever you ask in my name; if you love me, keep my commands.
    • Jesus is comforting his disciples and speaking directly to them, not to us. Verse 12 says whoever believes will do the works he has been doing, but then Jesus gets specific: “I will do whatever you ask,” and “you may ask.” The words matter; if he meant whoever asks in his name, he would have carried the language of verse 12 into verses 13 and 14.
    • He also says several times in chapter 14 that if they love him they will keep and obey his commands, so anything they ask in his name will be in keeping with his teaching and what they observed him doing.

    1. 20:02 John 15: remain in me

      Scripture: John 15:7

      • John 15 continues the same conversation: if you remain in me and my words remain in you, ask whatever you wish. Just before this Jesus tells them he is the true vine and that they are to remain in him. The promise is conditional on remaining in him and his words remaining in them, so again their asking will be based on Jesus’ example and teaching.

    2. 20:41 Luke 11: the Father gives the Holy Spirit

      Scripture: Luke 11:9–13 Ephesians 1:14

      • Chris reads on in Luke 11: which father would give his son a snake for a fish or a scorpion for an egg? If you, though evil, know how to give good gifts, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him.
      • The disciples had asked how they should pray, and at the end of that passage Jesus is clearly talking about giving the Holy Spirit to those who ask, seek, and knock. God will not deny the Holy Spirit to those truly seeking him, and the Spirit is the greatest gift ever given, because according to Ephesians 1 he is a deposit guaranteeing our inheritance.

  10. 21:47 The genie in the bottle

    Scripture: Matthew 6:24 1 John 2:15–16 Matthew 6:19–21

    • There is more to this, and Chris looks at two more angles. The first is the genie in the bottle. If our words create whatever we pray for as long as we believe enough, then God is doing our bidding and serving us, not the other way around. If we follow the teaching that God wants me healthy, wealthy, and happy, we have turned God into a genie in a bottle and turned from seeking first the kingdom of God to seeking first my comfort and my kingdom.
    • Matthew 6:24: no one can serve two masters; you cannot serve both God and money. If our minds are on our health, wealth, and happiness, they aren’t centered on Christ; we must devote ourselves to being sanctified and looking more like our Savior.
    • Since when have sinful humans known what is truly best for ourselves? Isn’t that why we turned to Christ in the first place, because our way wasn’t working? (“How’s that working out for you?” Thanks, Dr. Phil.) God calls us to give up our desires and replace them with his. 1 John 2: do not love the world; the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life come not from the Father. Matthew 6: store up treasures in heaven, for where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.
    • Bottom line: if we are worried about health, wealth, and happiness and buy into a doctrine that says we can create whatever we want, our focus is not on Christ. We are striving in the wrong direction and need to change course.

  11. 24:32 The issue of suffering

    Scripture: John 14:14

    • The second angle is suffering. If Chris could pray for suffering and difficult times to end, he would. Does anyone like to suffer or be persecuted? He hates it. Frankly, he wishes John 14, “You may ask me anything in my name, and I will do it,” was meant for him, because he would pray his discomfort away every time.

    1. 25:02 A 28th anniversary and a bad back

      • A few weeks ago Chris and Holly went away for their 28th anniversary to reconnect. It was not suffering like some have suffered, but it was suffering for that week. Holly found a big-cat rescue just north of Kingman, because Chris loves big cats (the little cats can all get thrown out somewhere; he apologizes to cat lovers).
      • After a few days on a terrible, hard hotel bed, he swung his legs over one morning and literally could not walk, and both places they wanted to see required walking. He prayed seriously, all day every day, that it wouldn’t affect their time together. It didn’t go away; even the Sunday he came back he tried to stand in one place so no one would see him hobbling. He believed God could have taken it away, no question, but God chose not to.

    2. 27:16 If you follow Jesus, you will be persecuted

      Scripture: Matthew 5:10–12 2 Timothy 3:12

      • Scripture is full of passages about suffering and persecution far beyond Chris’s few days. If you follow Jesus, according to Scripture you will be persecuted. Matthew 5 starts with “blessed”: blessed are those persecuted because of righteousness; blessed are you when people insult you and falsely say all kinds of evil against you because of me; rejoice and be glad. We are blessed and should rejoice when persecuted, not try to get out of it.
      • Paul doubles down writing to Timothy: everyone who wants to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted. Not maybe, not pray that you aren’t; you will be. It goes against everything in Chris’s head, but when we are persecuted for his name’s sake, we are blessed and should rejoice.

    3. 28:59 James 1: trials produce perseverance

      Scripture: James 1:2–4

      • What about trials and difficulties? James has an answer for a lot of tough stuff. He gets a super short greeting in, then verse 2: “Consider it pure joy.” Chris thought it was going to be good, then: “…whenever you face trials of many kinds,” because the testing of your faith produces perseverance, so that you may be mature and complete, not lacking anything.
      • It is as if James knew what we were thinking: why do I have to go through this? To produce perseverance so we can become mature and complete. If we could speak and make trials disappear, would any of us ever reach maturity? Chris stands with James and says no.

    4. 30:20 People who haven’t been through anything yet

      Scripture: Romans 5:3–5

      • Have you met someone who hasn’t been through anything difficult yet, no lost loved one or job, even met their spouse in high school with no bad breakup? Chris isn’t making fun; you might be in here, and the tough stuff will hit, but it hasn’t yet.
      • He has noticed two things about such friends. First, they have no idea how to walk others through tough times; they mean well, but they reach for platitudes like “God works everything for good” or “It’ll all work out in the end.” Second, when something finally turned their life upside down, the blessing was having others who knew that pain walk through it with them.
      • If Christians never walked through trials or pain, we would have no idea how to care for the hurting in a world full of pain, and imagine how shallow our faith would be if it were never tested. Chris concludes the thought with Romans 5: we glory in our sufferings, because suffering produces perseverance, perseverance character, and character hope, and hope does not put us to shame. We have hope because of it.

  12. 32:51 The real power of the tongue

    Scripture: James 3:3–12

    • Chris has shown that name it and claim it isn’t what Scripture teaches, but he doesn’t want to leave it there. The power of the tongue is real, just in a different way than creating.
    • Back to James, in James 3: bits in horses’ mouths turn the whole animal; a very small rudder steers a large ship; a great forest is set on fire by a small spark. The tongue is a fire, a world of evil that corrupts the whole body; no human being can tame it. With it we praise our Lord and Father and curse human beings made in God’s likeness. Can fresh water and salt water flow from the same spring?
    • Simply put, our mouth steers us and can corrupt our whole body. Believers should take serious stock of what comes out of their mouths, even in jest. Are we using words to build up or tear down, to praise the Lord or to curse? According to James, it is probably both, and as James says, that shouldn’t be.

  13. 35:13 Out of the abundance of the heart

    Scripture: Luke 6:45 Luke 6:45

    • Why do our words mean so much? Luke 6: a good man brings good things out of the good stored up in his heart, and an evil man brings evil things out of the evil stored up in his heart, for the mouth speaks what the heart is full of. What comes out of your mouth is what is in your heart; Chris’s too.
    • You can pretend all day that the dirty jokes, the cuss words, or tearing others down “all in good fun” are just words and not who you truly are, but the word of God says that’s a lie. We are what comes out of our mouth.
    • Chris really likes the way the ESV puts it: “Out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.” What we speak isn’t the dark corners of our heart; it is the abundance of our heart. Whatever is coming out of your mouth is what you are filling your heart with.

    1. 36:32 Fill your heart with his word

      Scripture: Psalm 119:11 Proverbs

      • What should we fill our hearts with? Psalm 119: “I have hidden your word in my heart that I might not sin against you.” Like the passages where Jesus told his disciples to remember his words, his teachings, and what he did, his word should be in our hearts. We need to be reading and memorizing it, because the abundance of our heart should be the words of God.
      • To see the power of the tongue, read Proverbs; it is chock-full of warnings about the tongue. That is the church’s homework, since Chris doesn’t think Josh is giving them any for a few weeks.

  14. 37:30 Your words matter

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:29 Luke 6:45

    • Chris leaves the church with a verse that helps him with what comes out of his mouth, Ephesians 4:29: do not let any unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what is helpful for building others up according to their needs. He fails at that and has to ask himself what the abundance of his heart is.
    • Your words do not create; God alone is the Creator. But your words do have power: to build up, to tear down, to curse, and to praise. They matter to your spouse, kids, family, friends, and co-workers, to the hurting and the hopeless, and to God. For out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks.

  15. 38:37 Closing prayer

    Scripture: Psalm 141:3

    • Chris prays Psalm 141, “Set a guard over my mouth, Lord; keep watch over the door of my lips,” knowing his mouth reflects the abundance of his heart. He recounts what they learned: God alone is Creator, and suffering and persecution build character, bring hope, help us persevere, and make us more like Christ.
    • He asks God for an unquenchable desire for his word, so that the abundance of their hearts is God, his word, and the example and words of Jesus, and the words that come out of their mouths are uplifting and bring hope, peace, and joy, words that could only come from the author and Creator of life who died on a cross to give us salvation, flowing like crashing waves and a river.
