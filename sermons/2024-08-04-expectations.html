Overview

  1. 00:04 Expectations: relationship builder or relationship killer

    • Expectations are the ultimate relationship builder and the ultimate relationship killer. Properly communicated and properly answered, they build trust and deepen relationships: “I need this from you.” “Okay, I understand; let me work on that.”
    • When they aren’t communicated or responded to properly, they break trust and make relationships so fragile that sharing your deepest thoughts becomes a horrifying endeavor. Josh voices the unspoken version: “You should know I need this from you,” and until you figure it out, anger, resentment, and bitterness grow. “How dare you not know my deepest insecurities… my hidden and unspoken desires.”
    • He wishes he could slap a “Handle With Care” sticker on everyone’s expectations. Whether in a friendship, a marriage, or a pastor and his congregation, communicating expectations is vital.

  2. 02:31 What Josh expects of the church

    • “I expect you to try.” If you call this church home, look at its core values, evaluate yourself, and try to be more like Jesus, a daily exercise for the true believer.
    • When you fail, not if but when, grab on to God’s grace, ask him and anyone you hurt to forgive you, and keep walking with him and with them. Be humble, show mercy, own your actions, and live holy lives, because this is what God expects of his people.
    • He wants the church to be a place where sinners are transformed into dedicated Christ followers. There are plenty of places where people can go pretend; here we aim high, and when we fall short we rest in his grace and keep aiming high.

  3. 03:26 What the church should expect of Josh

    • First, that he be above reproach, or above criticism: he doesn’t bring criticism on the Lord, the church, or himself. That doesn’t mean no one disagrees with him on theology, interpretation, or how the church is governed; an army of people does, some have stormed out screaming obscenities, and he has haters.
    • Still, he would line up across from them any day and let them fire shots at his authenticity and character, because he has nothing to hide; no one can say worse things than he has already said about himself. Expect that level of transparency, especially from a teacher, and expect him to stay faithful to his wife: 13 years and counting.

    1. 05:00 Restraint, and the rest of the list

      • Josh distinguishes self-control, of the physical kind, from restraint, of the emotional kind, and says restraint is where their pastor struggles. He feels everything deeply; it makes for a great sermon, but cross him or anyone he loves and it makes for intensity. He asks the Lord all the time to help him manage it.
      • Expect him to be respectful, hospitable, and able to teach; not given to drunkenness, not violent but gentle. A good shepherd shouldn’t be afraid of a fight, but doesn’t go looking for one. Expect him not to love money, to manage his family well because that shows how he will manage this family of believers, and to have a good reputation, with consistency between who he is in here and out there.

  4. 06:18 What to expect from the preaching

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 2:1 Romans 1:16

    • In these 40 to 45 minutes each week, Josh isn’t interested in crafty and creative things; he isn’t concerned with cuteness or charisma, though he loves a good illustration. His job is to repeat God’s unchanging truth: focus on the text, find the timeless truth embedded in it, and challenge listeners to inspect their lives and move toward implementation.
    • Paul was similar, telling the believers in Corinth he did not come with eloquence or human wisdom. The church can address finances, marriage, sexuality, and everything else, but the true church is never ashamed to return to the gospel every week, because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes.
    • That power fits in a few words: Christ shed his blood for sinners like you and me, not because we deserved or earned it, but by his great love and mercy. The promise is eternal life, the only hope that gives true purpose, meaning, and comfort. So find Jesus on every page and let him reach into your life and fix what is broken; the Scriptures tell of a Savior who died, rose, and will return to complete salvation for those who wait for him.

  5. 08:26 The Sermon on the Mount: a sermon of expectations

    Scripture: Matthew 5 Matthew 5:1–12 Matthew 5:6 Matthew 5:10

    • Josh began with expectations because Jesus also discusses them. In Matthew 5 he gives the Sermon on the Mount, quite literally a sermon of expectations for those seeking the kingdom of God.
    • In the first 12 verses, the Beatitudes, Jesus discusses our attitudes. Verse 6: blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness; something joyful happens in us when we desire and seek what is right and good. Verse 10: blessed are those persecuted because of righteousness; there is even more joy when our desire for good causes those around us to treat us poorly. But this isn’t a sermon on the Beatitudes, so he keeps moving.

    1. 09:22 Salt that loses its saltiness

      Scripture: Matthew 5:13

      • In verse 13 Jesus moves to difficult concepts: you are the salt of the earth, but if the salt loses its saltiness it is good for nothing but to be thrown out and trampled.
      • Salt in Palestine, especially around the Dead Sea, was contaminated with gypsum and other minerals until it wasn’t really salt anymore, and in the home it would be thrown out. Jesus isn’t saying, as many teach, that you can lose your salvation, but that you can lose your effectiveness in leading others to salvation, the purpose for which you were created. The world can contaminate you until your word carries no weight and you aren’t believable or trustworthy.

    2. 10:35 Not a new law but clarity: murder and anger

      Scripture: Matthew 5:21–22 1 John 3:15

      • People flocked to Jesus because they heard about his healing, then quickly ran away after hearing his teaching. He doesn’t bring a new law but insight and clarity into the old one.
      • On murder: you have heard it said, you shall not murder, but anyone who is angry with a brother or sister will be subject to judgment. Is Jesus really saying there is no difference between murder and anger? John seems to think so: anyone who hates a fellow believer is a murderer, and murderers do not have eternal life in them.
      • The alternative is to settle our issues with one another quickly. Reconciling with a fellow believer should matter more to you than your worship, because worship laced with anger is not really worship.

    3. 11:47 Adultery and the eyes

      Scripture: Matthew 5:27–28

      • Jesus builds a foundation of Christian morality by connecting adultery to your eyes: anyone who looks at a woman lustfully has already committed adultery with her in his heart. To the man who says he isn’t cheating on his wife by looking at porn, Jesus teaches that he is.
      • Josh almost lost his marriage because of this. He pleads with men and women to stop trading flesh and blood and true intimacy for a pixelated version: you rob yourself, rob God of his glory, and set yourself up for greater sins by learning a sexual standard of morality equal to that of a French Bulldog.

    4. 12:44 Back up your words: yes or no

      Scripture: Matthew 5:37

      • Citizens of heaven back up their words: all you need to say is simply yes or no; anything beyond this comes from the evil one. Think over your words and promises carefully. Stop drowning everyone in paperwork and disclosures so long no one can read them; be clear about your intentions and agenda.

    5. 13:10 Love your enemies

      Scripture: Matthew 5:43–47 Romans 5:10

      • One of the hardest points starts in verse 43: love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you, that you may be children of your Father in heaven, who sends sun and rain on the righteous and unrighteous. If you love only those who love you, aren’t even the tax collectors doing that? If you greet only your own people, don’t even pagans do that?
      • Jesus teaches the difference between human love and divine love, and then walks it out on Calvary; he is not all talk. Paul says in Romans 5 that while we were God’s enemies we were reconciled to him through the death of his Son. He loved us while we were still enemies of God.
      • We share that divine love by blessing those who curse us. When we pray for those who persecute us, God helps us love them, and the more we pray, the easier it gets. Josh is convinced this is one reason Jesus spent so much time in prayer: he had a lot of enemies.

  6. 14:45 Jesus raises the bar

    • Throughout the chapter Jesus pulls back the curtain on the kingdom of God, revealing a standard of behavior that far exceeds human standards. He uses human morality to introduce divine morality; he is raising the bar, or rather showing that it was higher than we thought.

    1. 15:05 The leap of faith

      • In Josh’s eight years as a youth pastor, a favorite moment was the leap of faith: students, and sometimes leaders like his wife, climb a telephone pole, stand on top, and jump for a pull-up bar hanging about five feet away. You win if you grab it and hang on, with bonus points for a pull-up.
      • On the ground it isn’t difficult, but 25 feet in the air the bar looks so far away, and so does the ground. When the standard is raised higher than you think possible, it’s easy to freeze on the pole or talk yourself into climbing down because it’s too big a leap.

    2. 16:08 “Be perfect, therefore”

      Scripture: Matthew 5 Matthew 5:48

      • Reading Matthew 5, the bar gets higher and further out until your legs are shaking, your heart is beating, and there is sweat in your eyes. Then Jesus throws out the line at the end of the chapter: “Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect.”
      • If we tried to summarize the Sermon on the Mount, or even Jesus’ entire ministry, it would be this phrase: the purpose of Christ’s work for us and in us, the telos. Murder, lust, hate, deception, pride, and vengeance don’t describe God, and Jesus made clear that God did not lower the bar to accommodate us; he kept it at his holy and righteous standard.

  7. 17:24 The misquote: saved people no longer struggle with sin

    Scripture: Matthew 19:26 Romans 1:17

    • Here is the misquote: many Christians wrongly assume that because Jesus saved us from our sins, we will no longer struggle and wrestle with our sinfulness. That is unfortunate and false.
    • Two crowds form around the command to be perfect. Some press back on Jesus: why command what he knows we can’t do? Easy: he wants you to rely on him to do it. As Jesus would later teach, with human beings this is impossible, but with God all things are possible. If God gives a command, he provides the power to accomplish it; he isn’t cruel, setting a mark you can’t reach and laughing as you try.
    • Human righteousness is possible: just be better than the guy next to you. Because it is so reachable, it is like filthy rags in God’s eyes. God’s righteousness is impossible: just be equal to God in all things. That is why it is holy and perfect. But the impossible mark becomes possible for anyone who trusts in Jesus, because he gives them his righteousness; as it is written, the righteous will live by faith.

  8. 19:01 Moving the target

    • Josh illustrates it on stage. Jesus commands us to be perfect, and we answer, “That’s not possible, so I’ll do this instead,” and change the target to one with a huge bullseye. “Now I’m feeling confident. Look, Ma.” It seems ridiculous pointed out, but it is common, and because we do it we stop caring about hitting the mark he laid out and only worry about getting closer than the next person.
    • It sounds like this: “I’m a pretty honest person, definitely more trustworthy than her.” “Yeah, I look at porn, but at least I’m not running out on her.” “Yeah, I have a bad temper, but I’d never hurt anyone.” But the Christian goal isn’t to be better than them; it is to be like him.
    • Jesus points to his target: that’s it; aim for that. When you miss, not if but when, confess it to God and to everyone you hit in the process, and Jesus grabs the axe and says, “Try again.” The believer’s power isn’t in the accuracy of the throw but in the freedom to try again, and again.
    • Jesus wants you to see your inability to hit the mark every time, which leads to the meek and poor attitudes of the Beatitudes, often called spiritual bankruptcy. You need divine assistance to reach the divine standard.

    1. 21:40 The crowd that thinks it’s hitting the mark

      • Then there is the crowd that thinks it is hitting the mark, calling “Bullseye!” on a clear miss. Josh isn’t sure which is worse; the blind guy makes the better comedy sketch, but both are sad and both miss the point.
      • The aim of perfection is good and right. The problem is believing you can do it on your own, or that being more accurate than your neighbor matters in God’s eyes. It does not; those are unholy add-ons that don’t belong.

  9. 22:31 Two crowds listening to Jesus

    • Two crowds listened to Jesus preach this, and there are probably two here today. The non-Christian hears the Sermon on the Mount, realizes he doesn’t live up to the standard, and Jesus shows him he needs a Savior and presents himself as the one sent to save.
    • The disciples were there too, having already dropped their nets and devoted their lives to following him. The standard hits both crowds: one can’t love like that because they haven’t experienced God’s love through Christ, and the other has the power to love like that but isn’t being faithful with what they’ve been given. The message is for all of us.

  10. 23:27 Sinless perfection

    Scripture: Matthew 5:48

    • This landed in the Misquoted series because a small group hears “Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect” and takes it to mean the sinless life is within their reach.
    • Josh describes sitting around a table with godly men he respects and does life with, men who love Jesus, fall short, ask for forgiveness, and walk in grace upon grace, when one man, with a witness he brought along, says he no longer sins. “It’s weird.” Josh has witnessed the claim only once, and once was enough.
    • The false doctrine of sinless perfection isn’t widespread, but it is worth addressing so the church is prepared to answer biblically. Achieving moral perfection is actually a great goal; God commands it. But to say you’ve achieved it is another matter.

    1. 24:54 Salt in the cookies

      • As the series has shown, false teaching is usually biblical truth mixed with something else. It sounds true and nice and looks good on the surface, but something extra in it doesn’t taste right.
      • It is like baking cookies and using salt instead of sugar: you won’t know until you bite in, and then the whole thing goes out, unless you’re weird and love salt cookies. The chocolate chips, eggs, and brown sugar could be top-notch; one thing went really wrong. That is sinless perfectionism: all the right ingredients and one thing that went wrong.

    2. 25:50 What the perfectionist claims, and what Scripture says

      Scripture: Titus 2:11–14 1 John 1:8 Hebrews 4:15

      • They will say Christians can completely defeat sin in this life and live perfect lives like Jesus did. It even sounds holy. They will walk you through Titus: the grace of God has appeared, teaching us to say no to ungodliness and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in this present age while we wait for the blessed hope.
      • Josh can get along with such a person a long time before realizing something is horribly wrong, because they agree that all Christians should seek holy lives and kill sin. But why would Jesus teach the disciples to pray for forgiveness if they didn’t need it? And John tells us that if we claim to be without sin, we deceive ourselves and the truth is not in us.
      • What’s worse than ignoring sin? Pretending you’ve defeated it. Christians who claim a higher spiritual level than everyone else are extremely dangerous; anyone who denies the sin in their life misunderstands their condition and the Spirit’s ongoing work of sanctification. Jesus was the only human being who could claim to be without sin: we have a high priest who was tempted in every way just as we are, yet did not sin.

    3. 27:34 Possible, just not yet

      • Sinless perfection is possible, just not until believers receive their glorified bodies in heaven. Josh can’t wait and is excited for that day. He understands the desire to see it now (“Come, Lord Jesus”), but we must not try to open doors the Lord himself has not opened yet.

  11. 28:18 The other ditch: not caring about sin

    Scripture: Galatians 6:9

    • Josh understands why the perfection crowd leans this way: so much of the church is filled with people who don’t seem to care about their sins anymore. The other attitude says sin is just a natural part of life and there is nothing you can do about it. No: there is nothing natural about it, and there is something you can do about it.
    • Part of the problem is no one wants to put in the work; many have done what Paul said not to do and grown weary of doing good. Churches have softened their stance on sin, afraid of being called unloving or legalists, so they say nothing. Because so many pastors won’t preach the hard stuff, churches are full of people with a master’s degree in grace who are high school dropouts in repentance. God doesn’t even do grace without repentance, yet here we are offering it.
    • Anyone, even a professed believer, who seeks to cover up sin is in the depths of spiritual darkness and deception. Christians don’t do cover-ups. Josh is frustrated by half-cocked apologies from well-known pastors (“Well, we didn’t commit a crime”); just be honest and humble before the Lord.

    1. 30:01 No such thing as a secret sin

      Scripture: Romans 2:16 Romans 2:6 Romans 1:17 1 John 1:10

      • There is no such thing as a secret sin. It is known by God and will one day be known by all, and on that day there will be surprises. Paul tells us God will judge people’s secrets through Jesus Christ and will repay each person according to what they’ve done. The righteous will live by faith, but if we claim we have not sinned, we make him out to be a liar and his word is not in us.
      • Christian cover-ups seem good on the surface, perhaps meant to spare feelings or protect people, but most of the time they are about protecting yourself, and they never end well.

    2. 31:01 True believers confess

      Scripture: 1 John 1:9 Proverbs 28:13

      • Whether we ignore our sin or deny it is there at all, we are fooling ourselves. True believers don’t deny the sin in their lives or their tendency to fall into it, because if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness.
      • True believers are open and honest about sin, confess it to the Lord, and seek forgiveness from those they have sinned against. Proverbs 28: whoever conceals their sins does not prosper, but the one who confesses and renounces them finds mercy.

  12. 31:39 What perfectionism gets right

    Scripture: Matthew 5 Matthew 5:46–47 Matthew 5:48

    • Give perfectionism credit: the desire to be without sin is good, right, and holy, a godly ambition more Christians should have. We realize we all fall short, but we aren’t happy about it; we stumble and fall, but we don’t excuse or defend it. “I’m a sinner, I failed, I came up short, and praise God for his mercy.”
    • You expect a newborn on a bottle for a while, but not a five-year-old walking around with one; eventually the child eats solid food. You expect the child who throws bedtime tantrums to eventually handle the routine: “Go brush your teeth and go to bed. Get out my face.” (Just kidding.)
    • In Matthew 5 Jesus is clearly raising the bar: aren’t even tax collectors doing that? If you only say hi to your homies, don’t the pagans do that? The kingdom of God doesn’t look like the world, and there is greater responsibility as you grow in the faith: be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect.

  13. 33:15 More is expected because our claims are big

    Scripture: 2 Peter 2:2 Matthew 5:16

    • The Lord expects more of us because Christians make big claims, and a claim that something has happened to you should be supported with strong evidence.
    • The Christian claims to be a new creation, and the skeptic asks, “Oh, really? What’s new about you?” The Christian claims to be awakened to the evil of sin: “Is there less of it in your life?” The Christian claims Jesus rescued them: “Do you have more joy than before? Why do you only go to church once a month?” The Christian claims eternal rewards and a crown in heaven: “Are they telling other people how to get there?”
    • If you say you’ve received it, your life should provide proof. Claiming to be an adopted child of God while living in unholiness is the greatest hypocrisy; Peter says many will bring the way of truth into disrepute, and you slander the name of God. If you are a new creation being made holy, show it: let your light shine before others, that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father in heaven. If you are still your old self, stop claiming to be a new creation.

  14. 35:06 More is expected because we have something more in us

    Scripture: John 3:3

    • Every believer in Christ is born again: not a make-believe Christian idea but a true, living experience. We have gone from death to life, and God dwells in us by his Spirit.
    • Jesus told the Pharisee Nicodemus that no one can see the kingdom of God without being born again. Nicodemus was confused, thinking about a second trip through the birth canal, but this isn’t like the first birth. The second birth begins something new; the new life produces good fruit, more than the previous life. The expectations are higher, but in Christ we are enabled to reach further than ever before.
    • That is why a believer can never say, “Josh, you don’t understand, there’s no way I can drop that habit or kick that addiction.” You have the Spirit of God in you, the third person of the Trinity; God is all-powerful, so how can you set a cap on his power in your life? We can burn down addictions, tear down idols, take our sin out back, shoot it, bury it, and never see it again. God gives us victory through Jesus Christ.

    1. 36:59 Reach for the goals he set

      Scripture: Matthew 5:48 Philippians 2:13

      • Christians, stop setting goals for yourselves and start reaching for the goals he set for you: be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect. This is our divine calling. We reach together, we reach imperfectly, but we never stop; we set our eyes on Jesus, the highest standard man has ever known, and lean in no matter the cost.
      • For it is God who works in you to will and to act according to his good purpose. Let him work, let us strive, and may we never settle for less than perfect.

  15. 37:47 Are you actually a Christian?

    Scripture: Matthew 7:21

    • Josh fears some here believe in Jesus but are living in sin. We all have sin in our lives, but some seem to enjoy it; he isn’t placing judgment, because he has been there. Others say, “I believe, so I can live how I want and be saved.”
    • Meanwhile Jesus says not everyone who says to him “Lord, Lord” will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only those who do the will of his Father. Jesus doesn’t rescue us so we are free to sin; he rescues us so we can be freed from sin.
    • Ask yourself: are you actually a Christian, or are you getting really good at sounding like one? Do you really believe what Jesus has done for you, what he says about you, and what he calls you to do? Are you aiming at your own target or his? Jesus is way more concerned with your walk than your talk.

    1. 39:21 Build on the rock

      Scripture: Matthew 7:24–27

      • Josh prays they encounter Jesus today: confess your sins and ask him, “God, nail my sins to the cross of Christ. I want peace with you. I want to live a holy life. I’m ready to listen. I’m ready to obey.”
      • Everyone who hears these words and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock; the rain, streams, and winds beat against it, yet it did not fall. Everyone who hears and does not put them into practice is like a foolish man who built on sand, and it fell with a great crash.
      • Obedience is evidence of true faith. Has your life really changed since you came to Christ? Is there godly fruit? Are you confident in the Lord or blown around in every storm? Just come to Jesus today, trust him, and he will help you.

  16. 40:39 Closing prayer before communion

    • Josh prays as the church prepares to hold the elements that represent Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection, reminders of his sacrifice so we can be forgiven and also walk in grace. Walking in grace is an interesting journey: we expect to fail, but failure never keeps us from the effort; we get beaten down, but there is always a way back to him if we humble ourselves and seek his face and his help.
    • He prays for anyone who has fallen into a routine of acting like a Christian, nailing the behavioral standard as man designates it but not walking in faith. He asks that people would bring their addictions and failures and lay them at Jesus’ feet so he can grow their lives into a tree that bears good fruit, which only happens when we are connected to and led by him.
