Born Again?

Chris DeWald · Crossroads Church

Overview

  1. 00:40 Welcome, worship, and On Mission

    • Chris greets the church, celebrates hearing the congregation worship the God who cares for them, and invites newcomers and longer-term attenders to On Mission to learn Crossroads’ mission, vision, and values.

  2. 02:00 Trouble, gratitude, and a practiced response to anxiety

    Scripture: John 16:33 John 16:33 1 Thessalonians 5:16-18

    • Thanksgiving prompts Chris to admit a difficult season of frustration, doubt, and struggle. Following Christ does not promise ease: Jesus promises trouble, peace in himself, and victory over the world.
    • A study about anxiety and gratitude becomes the practical bridge. God gives continual reasons for gratitude—relationship through Jesus’ sacrifice, adoption, care, family, Christian community, food, shelter, and freedom to worship.
    • First Thessalonians gives his working response: rejoice always, pray continually, and give thanks in every circumstance. Chris urges listeners facing hard holidays to practice that response rather than give anxiety uncontested room.

  3. 07:36 Nicodemus meets the one who speaks truth

    Scripture: John 3:1-2 John 3:3 1 Chronicles 16:36 Ephesians 3:20-21 John 3:3 John 3:4

    • John 3 introduces Nicodemus, a Pharisee and ruling-council member who comes at night, calls Jesus Rabbi, and recognizes that his signs show God’s presence. Jesus bypasses formality: no one sees God’s kingdom without being born again.
    • Chris pauses over Jesus’ opening ‘Very truly.’ First Chronicles and Ephesians show amen following praise or a doxology; in the Gospels Jesus places it before his words. Others respond to truth, but Jesus speaks with binding authority and certainty.
    • Nicodemus reads rebirth physically and asks how an old person could enter the womb again. Live-birth and newborn-size humor exposes the impossibility of that literal reading.

  4. 14:04 Born of water and Spirit, not remade by legalism

    Scripture: John 3:5-6 Ezekiel 36:25-27 Numbers 19 Ezekiel 36:27 Exodus 20:8; Deuteronomy 5:12 Romans 14 Romans 14:21 Matthew 22:36-40 Romans 13:10

    • Jesus says entry into the kingdom requires birth of water and Spirit. Ezekiel joins cleansing water, a new heart, and God’s Spirit within his people; Numbers 19 supplies the law’s cleansing background.
    • Nicodemus and the Pharisaic system preserved formal cleansing while missing the promised Spirit. Chris warns churches against multiplying rules because black-and-white lists feel safer than spiritual discernment.
    • Freedom in Christ is not permission to indulge every desire. Romans 14 calls believers to limit freedom rather than make another stumble. The law’s heart is love for God and neighbor, because love fulfills the law.

  5. 19:04 The wind, the Spirit, and Nicodemus’ limits

    Scripture: John 3:7-8 John 3:9 John 3:10-15

    • Jesus compares those born of the Spirit to the wind: its sound and effects are experienced although its source and destination cannot be controlled. The Greek wordplay uses pneuma for wind and spirit.
    • Nicodemus asks how this can be. Jesus rebukes Israel’s teacher for missing what he should know, identifies the Son of Man as the one from heaven, and says he must be lifted up as Moses lifted the snake so believers receive eternal life.
    • If Israel’s learned teacher cannot grasp this by human wisdom, ordinary hearers appear hopeless on the same terms.

  6. 21:59 God’s wisdom and the meaning of new birth

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 1:18-31 1 Peter 1:23 Titus 3:5-7 2 Corinthians 5:17 Romans 5:20 Titus 3:5 2 Corinthians 5:17

    • First Corinthians answers the apparent hopelessness: the cross is foolishness to the perishing but God’s power to those being saved. God chooses the foolish, weak, lowly, and despised so no one boasts; Christ becomes wisdom, righteousness, holiness, and redemption.
    • Chris asks the controlling question: have you been born again? First Peter, Titus, and Second Corinthians describe imperishable birth, merciful washing and renewal by the Spirit, and a new creation in Christ.
    • The law exposes sin but cannot save. Jesus lived perfectly, paid sin’s penalty, and sends the Spirit to remake people. Chris asks whether confusion about rebirth is keeping anyone from giving life to Christ.

  7. 27:24 Questions are not disqualifying

    Scripture: Mark 4:1-9 Mark 4:10-13 Mark 4:33-34 1 Corinthians 1:25-30

    • The disciples also struggled. After the sower, they asked about Jesus’ parables, and he privately explained what they did not understand. Nicodemus and the disciples both showed up and asked rather than hiding their confusion.
    • Chris gives questioners three truths: they are not alone; pastors, elders, and staff want to help; and this is an eternal decision too important to surrender to embarrassment.
    • Human beings may be foolish, but God is wise and supplies what they lack.

  8. 30:22 Nicodemus moves from night to the cross

    Scripture: John 7:45-52 Matthew 4:12-16 Isaiah 9:1-2 Isaiah 9:1-2 John 19:38-42 Mark 15:43

    • John 7 shows guards marveling at Jesus while the Pharisees mock them. Nicodemus challenges his peers to hear a man before condemning him, a courageous step that suggests movement toward belief without proving where he yet stands.
    • Matthew’s quotation of Isaiah answers the claim that no prophet comes from Galilee: light dawns in Galilee of the Gentiles. Chris clearly labels as speculation his hope that Nicodemus investigated that prophecy.
    • John 19 brings Nicodemus publicly to the cross with Joseph of Arimathea. They receive, prepare, and bury Jesus’ body; Mark says Joseph went boldly to Pilate. Nicodemus has moved from showing up at night to showing up at the cross.

  9. 34:27 Ask courageously and live faith openly

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 1:25 Romans 1:16

    • First appeal: ask the questions that need answers. God is not threatened by them; his supposed weakness surpasses human strength, so no question can overwhelm him.
    • Second appeal: stop keeping faith secret. Joseph and Nicodemus eventually showed courage. Romans 1 grounds the call to live faith out loud because the gospel is God’s saving power.
    • Visible faith may prompt another person’s questions and lead to shared eternity with Christ. If others do not know whom a Christian trusts, they have no reason to ask about that faith.

  10. 37:23 Communion and a prayer for visible faith

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:23-26 Romans 1:16 Matthew 5:14-16

    • Those who have not trusted Christ may let the communion trays pass. Believers remember Jesus’ broken body, shed blood, and willing self-giving through the bread and cup.
    • Chris prays for courage to ask questions, confidence in Christ’s cross, and lives marked by love, grace, humility, and care rather than shame of the gospel.
    • He asks that the church be a city on a hill whose light is not hidden and closes with the desire that heaven be full rather than people remain eternally separated from God.
