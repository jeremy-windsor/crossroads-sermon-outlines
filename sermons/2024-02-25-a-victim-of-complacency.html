Overview

  1. 00:05 Asking for grace

    • Coming off the nine o’clock service, Josh tells the church it was really hard for him to get up and preach today. He has no explanation; he is hurting, it is emotional, and he thinks it may be grief sneaking up on him. He asks for their grace and patience, and thanks God that he doesn’t need Josh to do this; he can do it despite Josh’s condition.

  2. 01:19 Genesis 34: a depressing chapter

    Scripture: Genesis 34 Genesis 28 Genesis 34:1–5 Genesis 34:5–12 Genesis 34:13–31

    • It is week eight of a nine-week series in Genesis, and the church will be in Genesis 34. Josh’s disclaimer: this chapter is depressing.
    • From Genesis 28 we read about Jacob starting a family that, as the church heard last week, is pretty dysfunctional. One definition of a dysfunctional family is one in which an individual’s conflict and misbehavior occur continually, leading other members to accommodate them. Everyone is picturing an individual right now, but often our response to that individual deepens the dysfunction rather than relieving it.
    • Josh’s dad recapped the families of Genesis beautifully last week. Genesis 34 is one of the most important stories in the book, about a woman named Dinah, in three parts: verses 1–5, the rape of Dinah; verses 5–12, the offer; and verses 13–31, revenge.

  3. 03:07 Jacob, a passive husband and father

    Scripture: Genesis 29–30

    • Dinah is Jacob’s only daughter, but first Josh looks at how Jacob leads his home. As husband and father Jacob is extremely passive. If you think your marriage is hard, he was married to two sisters. Josh is a one-woman man until death, though he once broke up with a girl for her sister: “Don’t judge me. I wasn’t being sanctified yet,” and it didn’t go well.
    • In Genesis 29 and 30 the sister wives compete in the Olympic event of who can have the most children to earn their husband’s affection, and Jacob is completely passive: “Yes, babe. Whatever you want, dear.”
    • Jacob did reconcile with Esau, but not because of his leadership; Esau was stirred with love and forgiveness. They made plans to meet down the road, and Jacob ghosted him. He plays fast and loose with relationships: if there is nothing in it for him, he won’t even put it on his Google Calendar.

    1. 05:20 Fight the urge to mentally slap someone

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 32:35

      • Many things today may bring a person to mind. Fight the urge to mentally slap them. You are here to evaluate yourself against the Scriptures, not others; if they don’t do that work, that is between them and God. Trust God when he says in Deuteronomy, “It is mine to avenge; I will repay.”
      • That doesn’t mean complacency or never calling anything out. It means that when we call it out and can’t win our brothers and sisters over, we trust the Lord with what we can’t win.

  4. 06:10 Living in Shechem instead of Bethel

    Scripture: Genesis 35

    • Dinah is somewhere between 13 and 17, the only girl living with ten brothers, with one more “due” in Genesis chapter 35. The family has settled in Shechem, and last week showed why that is a problem: Jacob promised God he would live in Bethel, where his grandfather Abraham worshiped, but he wrongly settled for Shechem.
    • Many people base their view of God on the failures of so-called godly people. But God can’t be properly understood by looking at godly men and women; we bear his image, but only partly. He can only be properly understood by looking at his word, his Son.

    1. 07:21 Pack up and leave for Bethel

      Scripture: Revelation 2:5

      • Jacob let his comfort overrule his commitment, and you never sin alone; the people around you always suffer. Many of you are living in Shechem right now and need to pack up and leave for Bethel.
      • Josh echoes Jesus’ words to the church in Ephesus: consider how far you’ve fallen, repent, and do the things you did at first. Don’t pitch a tent; get up and go. Stop being passive, take back your family, your home, and your marriage, and get back to the basics.

    2. 08:10 Not one mention of God

      Scripture: Genesis 34

      • One more detail: chapter 34 doesn’t mention God once. No request for help, no casting anxieties on him, no asking for wisdom or guidance, just impulse after impulse. Let it be a lesson: just because you think it or feel it doesn’t mean it is from God.

  5. 08:47 Part one: Dinah goes out

    Scripture: Genesis 34:1

    • Dinah, Leah’s daughter, went out to see the women of the land. It seems innocent: a kid in a new town, stuck in a house of boys, looking for friendship. Josh remembers moving to Glendale at 16, to a street called Utopia across from a basketball court, and skipping the U-Haul unloading to go shoot hoops and make friends.
    • But Josh was a six-foot, 200-pound 16-year-old. A teenage girl at 85 pounds soaking wet takes a brother with her. Men, one of our roles in the home and the church is to protect, and we need to take it seriously.

    1. 09:55 Ladies, please be careful

      • Josh recently had to call his wife out. She stays for both services, often working in the office during second service. One day she was alone in a dark back room, opened the door to a strange man, and escorted him through an empty parking lot to the lobby. He was harmless, there to pick up things for Crossroads Cares, and Josh loves her heart, but he firmly told her never to open that door alone again.
      • Normally he wouldn’t advise it, but maybe the ladies need to watch more scary movies: never walk alone at night, never open the door alone for a strange man, and never hide in the abandoned garage behind the wall of chainsaws. Josh wonders whether, had Dinah been Rachel’s daughter, there would have been a protective detail.

    2. 12:04 Know the people they’re with

      • Technology like Life360 can help parents and grandparents track inexperienced teenagers, but knowing where they are isn’t enough; you need to know the people they are with. Dinner with the boy isn’t enough; grill the family, and if the dad is passive like Jacob, drag your daughter out of there. She may hate you, but you tried to protect her when she wasn’t interested in protecting herself.
      • Don’t send your daughter to his home unless it is rooted in the word, not just aware of it. Even if they came to Easter, Good Friday, and Christmas Eve, inspect that home to see if the man leading it is a contender for Christ or a pretender.

  6. 13:15 Shechem seized her

    Scripture: Genesis 34:2

    • When Shechem, the prince of the land, saw her, he seized her, lay with her, and humiliated her. Some read it as a mutual seduction, but that reads our context into the text; sex wasn’t casual back then. Josh is in the camp that Shechem raped Dinah.
    • Part one shows three things: passivity, stupidity, and depravity. Passive parents create aggressive sinners. Parents need to teach daughters the danger of boys and, just as important, teach sons how to treat a lady, “cuz this ain’t how.”

  7. 14:34 “He spoke tenderly to her”: give love to get sex

    Scripture: Genesis 34:3

    • Shechem’s soul was drawn to Dinah, he loved her and spoke tenderly to her. “Well, I bet he did.” Every parent with a daughter must discuss this with her at the right time, and don’t play fast and loose with the timing. You are setting the example of what she is looking for from birth, and depending on the school, sexuality may come up as early as second grade. Many children share classrooms with kids who have Jacobs and Hamors for parents; prepare them.
    • Not every guy is like this, and praise God for men doing their job at home. But many young worldly men give love to get sex, which is how women end up married to abusers, addicts, and perverts: as long as he says he loves you and promises he will change, they believe it. On the flip side, young women often give sex to get love. Protect daughters until they are ready and teach sons how to be ready, because sex isn’t the pinnacle of intimacy; commitment is.

  8. 16:42 Watch how he treats his parents

    Scripture: Genesis 34:4

    • If you want to know what kind of man he’ll be, watch how he treats his mama. Observe Shechem in his natural environment: “Get me this girl for my wife.” Josh’s kids learned please and thank you young; his “Josh 2024 translation” of Shechem’s words is, “Daddy, please give me a spanking.”
    • The most shocking part is that now he wants to marry her. Many women still fall for the thinking that since we’ve already been together, we should get married and that will fix it. It won’t. He stole something you can never get back. Don’t excuse and enable him; execute the relationship, take it out back and shoot it: not him, the relationship.
    • Guys, marrying someone because you crossed the line doesn’t fix anything either. Your only shot is leaving your life of sin and running to the cross of Christ.

    1. 18:49 Ladies, inspect his life

      • Men like Shechem are wicked; they want what they want now and don’t care who they hurt. Prepare your daughters, teach your sons, and, ladies, inspect his life. Does his definition of love look anything like Scripture’s? Does he know God and have a personal relationship with Jesus? Is there any fruit of being a new creation? Don’t just listen to what he says; watch what he does.

  9. 19:36 Jacob held his peace

    Scripture: Genesis 34:5

    • Jacob heard that Shechem had defiled Dinah, and he was furious? No, it doesn’t say that. His daughter has been raped by the king’s son and is in his home, and because his sons were in the field, Jacob held his peace; one version says he kept quiet until they came.
    • Maybe there is room for different approaches; some fathers would sit quietly or talk to Hamor. Not Josh: he would go straight to Shechem’s front door and say, “You stole something that didn’t belong to you. You sinned against God and my family and my daughter. Repent, stay out of her life, and give her back. I’m taking her home.”

    1. 20:44 Leadership exists for crisis

      • New people often ask Josh about the church’s governance. He is lead pastor and also an elder; the elders aren’t on staff, so they delegate day-to-day work to him. At their level, church leadership exists for crisis. If Josh gets hit by an ice cream truck, the church will be grateful for amazing men ready to lead, “and y’all better take care of my family.” Strong leadership shows up in crisis.
      • Dinah is in crisis and the family is in crisis. This is no time for silence or complacency; the father needs a plan, now, not only for justice but for his daughter’s recovery.

    2. 22:23 Don’t forget the wounded

      • The victim is often overlooked, because people aren’t comfortable with negative emotions and, in crisis, are consumed with their own needs. The military trains troops never to leave a man behind, while the church gets in the car and drives past the wounded on the side of the street, maybe even running them over. The church is so bad at restoring fallen and wounded people. If crisis knocks on your door, don’t forget to treat the wounded.

  10. 23:15 Sins of omission and the hole passivity leaves

    • There are sins of commission, like Jacob deceiving his father last week, and sins of omission, doing nothing at all. Jacob is making a habit of omission: living in Shechem, letting his daughter walk around alone, and making no plan for this crisis.
    • Men, passivity creates a hole in the family. Adam was passive, so Eve stepped in; Abraham was passive, so Sarah made plans. “That went well.” If your wife is wearing the pants, it is likely she saw you weren’t willing to. Wake up, step up, and lead your home like Christ; a wife in Christ will be happy to let you lead.

    1. 24:26 To single parents

      • Josh has watched both his parents balance single parenting, and his heart goes out to those carrying that burden. Don’t try to fill both roles; you weren’t created for that. Find creative ways to expose your children to godly men, or, for single dads, godly women: youth group, Sunday sermons, Crossroads Cares, mission trips. The church is more equipped to fill that void than you are, so ask for help.

  11. 25:24 The brothers step in

    Scripture: Genesis 34:6–7

    • In this story it isn’t the wife who steps in. Hamor goes out to speak with Jacob, and Jacob’s sons come in from the field as soon as they hear: immediate action. Even if you choose not to lead in crisis, you are leading; your silence speaks volumes, often more than words could.

    1. 25:54 Righteous anger

      • The men were indignant and very angry. There is nothing wrong with anger; there is healthy anger and righteous indignation. Any emotion can be negative, but anger can be extremely beneficial. God gets angry; Jesus got angry. If someone rapes your sister or molests your daughter and you aren’t furious, Josh is concerned for you.

    2. 26:38 “Such a thing must not be done”

      Scripture: Genesis 34:7 1 Corinthians 6:3 Matthew 7:5

      • They were angry because Shechem had done an outrageous thing in Israel, and such a thing must not be done. Here Moses inserts commentary, writing later with knowledge of God’s law. Fun fact: this is the first time Israel is mentioned as a nation, Israel meaning one who fights victoriously with God.
      • Isn’t calling things what they are what the church is supposed to do? People now want to rename pedophiles “minor-attracted persons”; Josh calls them sick and twisted sexual deviants who need Jesus Christ, because you can’t provide care without starting at the truth. Morality isn’t subjective; there is one code, and yours doesn’t exist without God’s; you borrowed it and then rejected him.
      • Christians say, “Who am I to judge?” You’re right, no one should care what you say; they should care what God says, and if you don’t tell them, how will they know? Paul tells the Corinthians we should judge matters pertaining to this life, and Jesus teaches us to deal with our own eyes first so we can help with others’. The sons rightly judged that something was done that must not be done.

  12. 28:43 Part two: the offer

    Scripture: Genesis 34:8–9

    • Next we hear from the father of the boy who lacked self-control. Hamor says Shechem longs for their daughter: give her to him, intermarry with us. In other words: “I know my son raped your daughter, but he really likes her. Let’s have a wedding, actually several weddings. This family will be a dynasty.”

    1. 29:33 Commitment comes first, feelings later

      • Back then the whole family approved a marriage. We rightly frown on arranged marriages, but statistics don’t lie: divorce rates are much lower where they are practiced, partly because families have known each other for years.
      • One person in an arranged marriage said they learned commitment comes first and feelings come later, while we do it backwards, basing everything on feelings and hoping to learn commitment. Josh disagrees with some of what they do, but that is beautiful; and he believes it is your heavenly Father who knows who he has for you, and your job is to wait, listen, and watch.

    2. 30:59 Not honorable but heinous: power and control

      Scripture: Genesis 34:23

      • One commentator tried to make Hamor’s proposal honorable. It is heinous. An unbeliever rapes a believer, and his father pushes for a wedding. “If your sons rape young girls, I can only imagine how well you’ve done raising young ladies.”
      • It is about power and control: Hamor sees an opportunity to rule over Israel and will use Dinah’s pain to gain it. Verse 23 shows his motive as he tells his city, “Will not their livestock, their property, and all their beasts be ours?” Jacob stays quiet, Hamor builds a name, and who is caring for Dinah? The lack of leadership is astonishing.

    3. 32:27 Men who protect

      • It blows Josh’s mind that voices in this country, including some Christian women, are trying to demonize Christian men. Yet as this pagan king trades women like baseball cards, who stands in the way to defend their dignity? God-fearing, promise-believing men. Wait until there is an enemy on our shores; leadership shows up in crisis.
      • Josh calls the idea of treating women as equals ridiculous: we treat them better than equals, like the treasures they are. That is why men would take a bullet for them, let women and children board the life raft first, and don’t require women to register for the draft, not because women can’t do it, but because we value their lives more than our own.

  13. 33:53 Throwing money at the problem

    Scripture: Genesis 34:10–12

    • “Could we get the rest of your daughters too?” Jacob is still in coward mode, and the boys are about to step up. In verse 10 Hamor offers land, trade, and property, and Shechem says, “Ask me for as great a bride price and gift as you will, only give me the young woman.” They are throwing money at the problem, trying to buy their way out.
    • Has anyone noticed what these sorry excuses for men haven’t done? Apologized. They haven’t owned their actions, and no one wants to hear your solution to a problem when you are part of the problem.

  14. 34:58 Guilt and defilement

    Scripture: Genesis 34:13 1 Corinthians 6:18

    • Jacob’s sons answered deceitfully because Shechem had defiled Dinah. Josh wonders where they learned to be deceitful: “They’re using Daddy’s tactics.”
    • When we sin we feel guilt; when someone sins against us we feel defiled and violated. Almost every time we sin against someone, we prioritize our own guilt over the defilement we caused. The king and his son care more about feeling better than about the one they hurt; they came to negotiate and left Dinah alone in the city instead of returning her.
    • Sexual sin is brutal; it makes us feel dirty and unclean. Paul says the sexually immoral person sins against their own body, and this is worse because it wasn’t Dinah’s choice. She has to carry it for the rest of her life, and victims of sexual immorality often struggle to rebuild their identity. Some in the room know that feeling all too well.

    1. 37:03 Forgiven and purified

      Scripture: 1 John 1:9

      • Josh points to an often-overlooked treasure in the gospel: 1 John 1:9, if we confess our sins he is faithful and just to forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness. Two things are happening: you are forgiven for what you’ve done, and purified from what others have done to you.
      • If you have been defiled, Jesus can purify that defilement. Your identity isn’t in what someone did to you but in what Jesus has done for you, and he will cleanse you, heal you, and make you a new creation.

    2. 37:59 A right thing for wrong reasons

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 22:28–29

      • You can do a right thing for the wrong reasons. Moses writes with the law in his head, though these people don’t have it yet. Josh shows God’s law: a man who rapes an unpledged virgin must pay her father 50 shekels of silver, marry her, and never divorce her. It is possible to do a right thing for the wrong reasons, and a wrong thing for the right reasons.

  15. 38:43 Part three: revenge

    Scripture: Genesis 34:14–17 Genesis 17 Genesis 34:18–19

    • The brothers say they can’t give their sister to an uncircumcised man; only if every male is circumcised will they intermarry and become one people. Abraham was the first circumcised, in Genesis 17; it is the sign of the covenant, showing you belong to God and take him at his word, much as we relate to Jesus through baptism. The sons are preaching, deceitfully: “You need Jesus if this plan’s going to move forward.”
    • Update on Jacob: still hasn’t said a word. Hamor and Shechem are pleased: “All I got to do is go to church, and I can have your daughter.” They are pumped, because they are monsters.

    1. 40:08 Church attendance isn’t enough

      Scripture: John 15

      • Church attendance doesn’t qualify a boy for one-on-one time with your daughter. Test him on the law of God; if he loves God he will know it, because when you love someone you learn what they love and hate.
      • He gave his life to the Lord at church camp and raised his hand for ministry? Slow down. Watch him for three months to see if he is faking it. Anyone can buy a Bible, go to church, get baptized, pray a prayer, raise their hands, cry, and join a study; Josh has been in church his whole life. But you can only bear the fruit of the Spirit if you are in Christ, and Jesus says that fruit will last, in John 15. Wait, watch, listen, and test it.

  16. 41:20 Simeon and Levi’s vengeance

    Scripture: Genesis 34:20–24 Genesis 34:25–29

    • Hamor and Shechem take the deal to the men of their city: land, trade routes, daughters as wives, all for the small price of circumcision, with verse 23 as the big selling point. The men agree.
    • On the third day, while they were sore, Simeon and Levi took their swords, came against the city while it felt secure, killed all the males, saved Hamor and Shechem for last, took Dinah, and plundered the city door to door. This isn’t justice; it is vengeance.

    1. 42:29 Jacob finally speaks

      Scripture: Genesis 34:30–31

      • Guess who finally chimes in? Jacob tells Simeon and Levi they have brought trouble on him and made him stink to the inhabitants of the land. They answer, “Should he treat our sister like a prostitute?” Josh says at least check on your daughter before rebuking your boys; “a little triage, man.” Jacob won’t be writing books on parenting anytime soon.

  17. 43:15 Where are the good people?

    • This story kills Josh because every man in it sins. No one seeks godly counsel like David, pauses to pray like Nehemiah, leads by example like Joshua, or shows patience like Mary. They follow the first impulse of the flesh, and Dinah will never be the same.
    • Our culture seems to have three types of people: passive like Jacob, perverted like Shechem, and unhinged vigilantes like Simeon and Levi, angry with no wisdom or discernment in exercising justice.

    1. 44:20 God’s call: stop being passive

      Scripture: Micah 6:8

      • God is calling people to drop their nets and follow Jesus: to stand up for the defenseless and against the dangerous, to act justly, love mercy, and walk humbly, to speak truth into chaos, weep for the lost, pray in the closet, call others to faith, call out false teachers, and live lives of commitment. He calls men and women who don’t value their reputation above his, who prepare people for the future, celebrate all the gifts, keep watch, wash feet, fish for men, stay near the foot of the cross, and repent and repeat.
      • If you want to look like Jesus, read about Jesus; if you want to be a man of God, follow the God-man. Stop being passive. It is time to take a stand.

  18. 45:40 Communion and reflection

    • Communion can look different for different people: kneeling with the elements in hand, bringing your sin to the Lord and trusting Christ’s sacrifice and his purification; worshiping without worrying about the person beside you; or, for those holding on to everything as theirs, giving a gift to God for the first time. Anyone who kneels at the front will be taken as a sign they want the pastors and elders to come pray with them: “You’re not alone.”

    1. 47:33 Simeon and Levi became priests

      Scripture: Genesis 49

      • This morning Josh found encouragement fast-forwarding to Genesis 49, where Jacob blesses his sons; it wasn’t a good day for Simeon and Levi. Yet despite their anger, and despite being the worst tribe in the nation, God made Levi a priesthood with oversight over his people and his house. Josh was humbled: “Who am I to steward this?” He loves that God uses broken, jacked-up people to do his work; otherwise Josh wouldn’t be here.
      • The church needs to stop being reckless, stop running past the wounded on the side of the road, get out of the car, help them, and look to restore them.

    2. 49:01 Closing prayer

      • Josh asks the Father to send his Spirit to flood the room, to convict, encourage, and comfort where needed, and thanks him for his Son Jesus and for salvation: “I’ll sing forever, and I’ll follow you anywhere.”
