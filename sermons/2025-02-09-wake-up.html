Overview

  1. 00:18 Part four: the upside-down kingdom

    • Josh opens in prayer, asking that everyone in the room would see God’s power, love, and compassion.
    • This is part four of an eight-part series through Mark, investigating major themes, especially the kingdom of God and its upside-down values. Again and again this reverse kingdom loves the marginalized instead of lording it over them, notices the vulnerable even as the powerful scream accusations, and calls the worst of us to follow a new king.

  2. 01:15 A king who leads us to victory

    Scripture: John 8:58 John 3:16 John 14:6

    • This king has led us to victory on a battlefield we didn’t know we were on: life and death, sin and shame, darkness and light. Death came through sin, but 2,000 years ago a miracle child was born in Bethlehem, and around 33 he claimed he came to bring eternal life. He said, “Before Abraham was, I am,” that God sent him so that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life, and that he was returning to the Father to prepare a place for his brothers and sisters: come follow me.
    • Don’t we need directions? He says, “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.” Death through Adam, life through Christ. If we take up arms against this enemy without Christ we will be defeated, but if we stand in his righteousness, we will certainly die, yet death will be unable to hold us down.

  3. 02:47 What will heaven say when I arrive?

    • Now there are generations of God’s people who don’t live like everyone else. The world trains us to acquire wealth, build a name, and worry about our legacy: what will they say about you when you’re gone? This upside-down kingdom isn’t worried about wealth or reputation on earth but about whom it will bring to heaven. Forget what they think when I’m gone; what will heaven say when I arrive?
    • When people flip their mindset from the temporary to the eternal, a fork develops in the road, and no one is without excuse. The wide road to the left looks familiar: no leaving your comfort zone, free to “do you.” The narrow road to the right looks very different: where you chased success you embrace sacrifice, where you aimed for notoriety you grow in humility, and instead of your own glory you are trained and convinced to focus on God’s glory and making great the name of Jesus.

  4. 04:32 We Believe: the creeds of the church

    • Josh just finished a big, thick book called We Believe, a collection of creeds, catechisms, and confessions from the second to the late 16th century; he admits he nerded out. Among his favorites was the Scots Confession. He warns readers that “Kirk” is the Scottish word for church; the first time he ran into it he had a mini identity crisis wondering whether it meant Kirk Cousins or Captain Kirk. Others in his top three are the Westminster Confession of Faith and the Apostles’ Creed.
    • We don’t pause enough to appreciate how blessed we are to pull all these up on a phone and see the core of the Christian faith preserved over centuries. Josh can’t even get “empty the dishwasher, Madison” communicated to his kids, yet the faith has been preserved for thousands of years. He is encouraged that his views of God and salvation align with some dude from Scotland in the 1600s, maybe a Christian Viking; if Josh had to live in any other era, he would want to be a Viking and train a dragon.

    1. 06:57 Sola Scriptura, and the creeds kept

      • The battle cry of the Protestant Reformation was sola scriptura. The Holy Spirit convicted God’s people to return to Scripture and shed many unbiblical traditions the Roman Catholic Church had imposed, returning to divine revelation as the final, only infallible rule of faith. But the Reformers didn’t throw the baby out with the bathwater; they kept these statements of faith for a reason, and Josh thinks more Christians should occasionally take a second-century theology class.

    2. 07:33 A second-century theology class: the Apostles’ Creed

      • The Apostles’ Creed is one of the oldest and most widely accepted statements of Christian faith, a summary of core beliefs traditionally attributed to the apostles’ teaching, though not written by them. Josh invites the church to say it with him: “I believe in God the Father Almighty, maker of heaven and earth, and in Jesus Christ his only begotten Son our Lord,” through “the resurrection of the body and the life everlasting. Amen.”

    3. 08:48 Rooted in Scripture

      Scripture: Deuteronomy 6:4 1 Corinthians 8:6 Isaiah 53:5

      • Our beliefs about God are rooted in Scripture, but that doesn’t mean the creeds lose value. Deuteronomy 6:4: “Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God, the Lord is one.” Paul references this to the church in Corinth: there is but one God, the Father, and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom all things came.
      • When the fullness of time came, God sent his Son into the world, the promised Messiah we call Immanuel, truly God and truly man in one person. Born under the law, he met its every demand where we couldn’t, and instead of taking the victory for himself he made a way for us to wear a crown too. He absorbed God’s wrath toward sinners; he was pierced for our transgressions, and as Isaiah would say, by his wounds we are healed.
      • Jesus wasn’t done: he rose again for our justification and destroyed the author of death. His enemies tried to cover it up, but eyewitnesses testified, and many gave their lives to protect the truth.
      • Josh goes on: he believes in the Holy Spirit, whom he sees working in his life and theirs; in the church, not a call out to the Roman Catholic Church but to the universal church, the Lord’s church, whose brothers and sisters are his; in the forgiveness of sins; and in the promise of eternity.

    4. 11:01 “Christ is risen”

      • Josh feels a spiritual connection with the brothers who wrote the creed; he doesn’t know them, but he knows them. To honor them, he asks those who believe to shout with him, “Christ is risen,” teasing that the room will go nuts when Pat Mahomes tries for a three-peat.
      • That is the most central confession of the Christian faith. If it didn’t happen, everything we believe is built on a lie. If it did, nothing can stay the same.

  5. 11:45 One more example of heaven’s Advocate

    • If Jesus can do all that, what Josh shows today won’t be a stretch. It is one more example of heaven’s Advocate stepping into the lives of the vulnerable and leaving them forever changed. Josh has been praying all week that people would become the next testimony of God’s grace, some for the first time and many for the hundredth: he is knocking, and Josh prays they answer.

  6. 12:15 Mark 5: two very different people in need

    Scripture: Mark 5 Mark 5:21–43

    • Josh wants to introduce the wideness of Christ’s love and mercy. In Mark 5 there are two very different people in need, and he reads the whole text before breaking it down, starting in verse 21.
    • Jairus, a synagogue leader, falls at Jesus’ feet pleading for his dying little daughter, and Jesus goes with him. In the pressing crowd a woman who had bled for 12 years, spent all she had on doctors, and grown worse touches his cloak, thinking, “If I just touch his clothes, I will be healed.” Her bleeding stops. Jesus asks, “Who touched my clothes?” She falls trembling at his feet and tells the whole truth, and he says, “Daughter, your faith has healed you. Go in peace.”
    • Messengers arrive: your daughter is dead, why bother the teacher? Jesus says, “Don’t be afraid; just believe.” At the house, to the wailing mourners, he says the child is not dead but asleep, and they laugh. He takes her by the hand and says, “Talitha koum,” which means, “Little girl, I say to you, get up!” She stands and walks around; she was 12. They are completely astonished.

  7. 14:59 Two daughters

    • These two stories reveal a powerful truth about the coming kingdom of God: victory over shame and death. There is also something beautiful to see: a daughter who has a father to advocate for her, and a daughter who must advocate for herself. Jesus steps into both situations differently and uniquely, and Josh hopes we can learn to be more like him here.

  8. 15:29 Where the text lies: the crowd that begged Jesus to leave

    Scripture: Mark 5:17

    • Before that, Josh points to where the text lies. Chapter 5 begins with Jesus sending demons into a herd of pigs, and then the people began to plead with Jesus to leave their region. Jesus had just saved a man’s life; why would they want him gone? Two reasons. First, they were afraid of his power: their pagan gods were easy to manipulate with a gift, but Jesus saw through the gift to the heart of the giver, and that freaked people out. Mostly, though, they saw dollar signs as the pigs drowned; they cared more about their livelihood than the man’s needs.

    1. 16:18 Crossroads Cares, and we can’t please everyone

      • Eli and Andrea had planned to share stories from the Crossroads Cares ministry, but Andrea is home sick, so it will wait until next week. Considering how many needs the church has met, Josh feels blessed that God gave a vision; he doesn’t say it enough, but he is proud of this church and the way it loves each other and strangers.
      • In ministry we can’t please everyone. Josh finds encouragement that one crowd can’t wait for Jesus to leave while another can’t wait for him to come. Sometimes God takes people out of our lives to clear space for new people he wants us to bless. Josh is still learning this and preaches to himself: don’t sweat the people who want you gone; spend your energy preparing for the people who hop on.

  9. 17:43 No upper or lower class at Jesus’ feet

    • Jairus approached Jesus first, but the woman received help first. In the kingdom of God there isn’t upper class and lower class. Jairus was an important synagogue official; the woman was an anonymous nobody; Jesus welcomes them both.
    • Jairus was about to lose a daughter who brought him joy for 12 years; the woman was about to lose an affliction that brought her suffering for 12 years. Jairus was no doubt wealthy, but no money could save his daughter; the woman was bankrupt from doctors. He had every resource and people ready to do whatever he asked; she had simply run out of options. Both found everything they needed at the feet of Jesus.

  10. 18:45 Verse 23: an urgent rescue

    Scripture: Mark 5:23

    • Josh loves how the two stories weave together. In verse 23 Jairus falls at Jesus’ feet: “My little daughter is dying.” This is urgent: she is not close to dying; she is dying. Instead of staying by her bedside in her final moments, Jairus took a massive risk and ran to find Jesus, and Jesus said yes. Fathers can feel the rush of adrenaline: my sweet girl might still have a chance.

    1. 19:38 “Who touched my clothes?”

      Scripture: Mark 5:30

      • There isn’t much time, the crowds are chaotic, and then Jesus realizes power has gone out from him and asks, “Who touched my clothes?” The word for power is dynamis, where we get “dynamite”; he experiences an explosion of power and knows there has been a healing. The rescue mission grinds to a halt in the middle of the street.
      • Imagine Jairus’s heart hitting the floor, going from leading the charge to a miracle at home to standing still listening to this woman’s testimony. Everyone is confused: Jesus went to seminary, not medical school, but triage! She has had a chronic issue for 12 years and can wait 12 more minutes; the child is acute. In our day Jesus would probably get hit with a malpractice suit.

    2. 21:19 “Don’t be afraid; just believe”

      Scripture: Mark 5:35 Mark 5:36

      • The key is that Jesus didn’t misunderstand the situation; everyone around him miscalculated his power. While Jesus was still speaking, people came from Jairus’s house: your daughter is dead, why bother the teacher anymore? Everything around Jairus said the window had shut; start preparing to bury your daughter.
      • Josh tells believers to cling to this and never let go, no matter what trouble they face: overhearing them, Jesus told him, “Don’t be afraid; just believe.” In other words, “Jairus, trust me.”

  11. 22:31 Wrestling with God’s timing

    • The timing of God can be difficult. It rarely matches our preferred timetable, and we all have a different sense of time, which Josh knows because he is married.

    1. 23:00 Forty-five minutes late to the altar

      • Josh’s wife made him wait 45 minutes at the altar. Having grown up with two older sisters, he imagines every bridesmaid taking turns telling his fiancée it’s not too late to sneak out the back, with a chopper on standby, ending with his future mother-in-law’s final appeal, “Are you sure?”, and everyone redoing their makeup after each appeal. That, he speculates, is why weddings run late.
      • But 45 minutes late for his wife is actually right on time. When he saw her, she looked amazing, the wait was worth it, and she has only gotten more amazing; he’ll show up late anywhere with her. He is slowly learning that just because he says they are late doesn’t mean they are; timing is relative.

    2. 25:35 The Lord’s delays are divine preparations

      Scripture: Psalm 27:14

      • Everyone has an opinion about the right time, but the Lord’s timing is always perfect. Even when we can’t see him working, we must trust that he is, and even in the waiting we can find blessing. A tested faith is a blessing in itself: assurance of faith and growing patience by submitting to his plans.
      • Spurgeon, the Prince of Preachers, said the Lord’s delays are not denials but divine preparations. We keep trying to see what he is working on when our job is not to see it but to believe it. Psalm 27 says wait for the Lord, be strong, take heart.

  12. 26:36 Jesus speaks to all of us

    Scripture: Exodus 14:14

    • Josh believes something global and spiritual is happening in Mark 5. When Jesus implores Jairus to faith, he encourages the disciples, Jairus’s friends, and the multitude of believers who will one day search his word to meet him, like this woman and like Jairus. He speaks directly to anyone who feels trapped the words Yahweh spoke to Israel when they saw no way out: the Lord will fight for you; you need only to be still.
    • To Jairus: I will fight for your daughter. To the woman with no advocate: I will fight for you too; I don’t see you like they do. From ceremonially unclean to daughter, crushed under the law and trapped beneath a burden you can’t carry, but with even just a little faith, he can work miracles.

  13. 27:56 Not everyone has the same degree of faith

    • We don’t talk enough about the fact that not everyone has the same degree of faith. Jairus, a prominent man, humbles himself and approaches Jesus without caring what his synagogue buddies think, and they don’t like it. Josh argues he displays incredible faith and takes on the most risk. The woman wants to fly under the radar; what we see from her is best described as superstition with an inkling of faith. But that is all it takes.
    • Jesus responds to faith no matter how small. Others were certainly bumping against him in the chaos, yet only one healing is reported, because she had faith and no one else did. Josh reminds those who make Christianity an intellectual exercise that it is one thing to know everything about Jesus and another to place your life in his hands.

  14. 29:40 Why did Jesus deal with the woman publicly?

    • Our culture would ask why Jesus didn’t protect her privacy and dignity when she wanted to keep it to herself. Josh gives a $5 answer and a $50 answer.
    • The $5 answer: God’s power in your life doesn’t belong in a closet. Show discernment, but by nature a witness must testify. Part of Josh’s testimony is that the Lord took a lust-filled man and made him a more loving husband, turning his lusts into love for his bride. He shares it only in appropriate spaces, and wouldn’t on a family Sunday, but he shares it because the power that changed his life, marriage, and home can change yours too. Call on the name of Jesus and meet him at his feet.

    1. 31:03 The $50 answer: for her

      • The $50 answer has three reasons: he did it for her, for Jairus, and for the gospel. Jesus is more than a healer; he is a Savior and a friend. He wanted to look into her eyes, let her witness his loving kindness, and restore her reputation. By the end of the conversation in the street he called her “daughter.” This was a physical, spiritual, and social healing, a plus one for the kingdom: we just watched our sister come to Jesus.

    2. 31:51 For Jairus

      • Jairus really needed encouragement. Some can’t walk and chew gum, but Jesus is a great multitasker: he knew Jairus’s elevated heart rate and distress, and knew he was devastated by the news of his daughter’s death. Watching Jesus restore this woman may not have restored Jairus’s hope, but it had to encourage him.
      • Josh loves that Jairus hung around. Most people in the church these days would already be out, because many of us won’t stick around for someone else’s miracle if we don’t get ours first. But Josh has learned by experience: with or without your miracle, the best place for you is right next to Jesus.

    3. 33:02 For the gospel: let the redeemed say so

      Scripture: Psalm 107:2, 20–21

      • Third, she could share her testimony and glorify the Lord. Psalm 107 says let the redeemed of the Lord say so; he sent out his word and healed them. Mark doesn’t say it, but Josh assumes it was quite the night in that town, her testimony echoing through the streets: I touched Jesus, he ended 12 years of suffering, and he said my faith healed me.

  15. 33:50 Down the street: “Sweet girl, it’s time to get up”

    Scripture: Mark 5:39 Mark 5:42 Mark 5

    • Down the street the vibes are very different. Twelve years of joy had just ended in a family; the professional mourners wouldn’t have been called if the girl were alive. Yet Jesus’ first words on arrival were, “Why all this commotion and wailing? The child is not dead but asleep.” Jairus and his wife wouldn’t have to wrestle with that for long; he showed what he meant. “Sweet girl, it’s time to get up,” and immediately she stood up and walked around.
    • In Mark 5 Jesus is forming a view of death very different from anything anyone imagined. For Jesus, pulling someone from death is as simple as waking them from a nap.

  16. 34:58 What do you think happens when we die?

    Scripture: Ecclesiastes 3:11

    • No one really talks about mortality anymore; we are afraid of it. Josh thinks Christians need to be aggressive with this conversation, and nothing gets to the point quicker than asking, “What do you think happens when we die?”
    • There are two leading views. The secularist or humanist, like Richard Dawkins and Bertrand Russell, believes death is the absolute end of existence. The theist, like Thomas Aquinas and C. S. Lewis, believes God made us eternal beings and that something is very wrong about death, which is why it terrifies us and why Josh believes Jesus weeps at the tomb of Lazarus. Which view do you hold?
    • In Mere Christianity, C. S. Lewis argues that our yearning for eternity points to our being created for it. Fish don’t complain about being wet; they flop around when they are out of the water. If I find a desire nothing in this world can satisfy, the only logical explanation is that I was made for another world.
    • Josh puts it this way: conflict in the head, confidence in the heart, a gift from God. Ecclesiastes 3 says he set eternity in the human heart. The body is more than an accidental biological machine; it was designed for eternity, and it kicks and screams because we aren’t where we are supposed to be. Meanwhile Jesus sits next to your bed: “Hey sleeper, it’s time to wake up.”

  17. 37:46 Do you see his power?

    Scripture: 1 Kings 17 Mark 5

    • Jesus isn’t calling on anyone for help or reciting anything. Josh urges the church to read 1 Kings 17 this week, where Elijah raises a widow’s son, lying on him and breathing on him; it is still a miracle, but nothing like Mark 5. Jesus has faced storms, demons, diseases, and crusty religious leaders, and now he faces death: “Sweet girl, it’s time to get up.”
    • Death puts up no fight. It has no rebuttal and no cross-examination; it simply gives her back at Jesus’ command. When Jesus has you by the hand, nothing else gets to have you, overcome you, control you, or keep you. You are his forever.

  18. 39:12 Do you see his love?

    Scripture: Psalm 68:5–6

    • When the parents are powerless or gone, Jesus steps into the role: he is a father to the fatherless, a defender of widows, and he places the lonely in families.
    • Josh asks one thing: if Jesus has you by the hand, why are you so worried? Why are you in such a hurry? Where are you going? That is why he loves Jesus: when Josh gets anxious or angry, or runs ahead pursuing his own will, Jesus says, “Hey Josh, been here all along, man. You done yet?”

  19. 40:19 What it cost: “Not my will but yours”

    Scripture: 2 Corinthians 13:4 Luke 22:42

    • You know what it took and what it cost to make the extension of his hand possible. Jesus was forsaken so that every time we feel separation we can come back and reconnect. Paul tells the Corinthians he was crucified in weakness yet lives by God’s power; he became weak so we could be strong.
    • Jesus knew the only way to resurrection life was through death. In Gethsemane he asked if there was another way, and the answer was no. He didn’t push back, negotiate, or complain; he said, “Not my will, but yours be done.”
    • Josh calls the church to adopt this mindset: conform to his ways, journey with Jesus without worrying about his methods or timing, take his hand, and say, “Lord, do what you will. I know you love me. I will follow you to the grave because I know you made a way out on the other side.”

  20. 41:34 Closing prayer

    • Josh thanks the Father for his grace, mercy, compassion, power, and love, and for this moment in Scripture where Mark shows how he stands before different people in different situations and provides whatever their need. He prays that whether someone has an advocate or is advocating for themselves, they would hear about the great heavenly Advocate, a friend of sinners, full of grace and truth.
    • He asks God to purify the church and help it be holy, to put down the things of this world and honestly wrestle with whether it is all just over or whether there is something next. He believes there is a next, that God is its king, and he comes to his feet.
