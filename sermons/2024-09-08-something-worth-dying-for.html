Overview

  1. 00:05 Not a car guy: the transmission fluid

    • Steve is not a car guy. He needs something to get from point A to B with good gas mileage, sliding doors for the kids, and seats that fold down for trips to Home Depot; yes, he is the proud owner of a minivan.
    • Almost 20 years ago, newly married, he set out to prove to his new bride that he was the man of the garage by changing the oil in their Isuzu Rodeo. He pulled the bolt and watched fluid the color and consistency of cough syrup stream out: he had drained the entire transmission.
    • With no specialized pump, he used an empty two-liter bottle of Dr Pepper to squeeze the transmission fluid back in from underneath while the car ran. An hour and a lot of mess later the Isuzu was running, and he virtually got a free transmission flush. What began as an embarrassing mistake became a source of pride: “I was now Unstoppable. Brake pads next week, honey. I’ve got this.”

  2. 02:45 Setbacks become opportunities for growth

    Scripture: James 1:2

    • The story reminds Steve of the mess Satan was trying to create at the dawn of the church: poking holes, unscrewing mystery bolts, attempting to drain the joy out of God’s people as they carried the message of Jesus through Jerusalem and beyond.
    • Storms, trials, mishaps, and attacks can come at any time. For the unbeliever they become a source of ultimate failure or of self-pride: look what I overcame. For the believer who calls Jesus both Lord and Savior, they become opportunities to test faith, stretch trust, and grow in reliance on the one who upholds the universe and every one of your cares. That is why James can open his letter, “Count it all joy when you meet trials of various kinds.”

  3. 03:46 Acts 5: “We must obey God rather than men”

    Scripture: Acts 5 Acts 5:27–28 Acts 5:29–30

    • It was no different for the early church in the book of Acts, the focus of the current series, Unstoppable. So far they have seen the Spirit at Pentecost, Peter’s powerful preaching, believers committing to each other, and imprisonments and confrontations followed by miracles and healings.
    • In Acts 5 the apostles are arrested and an angel rescues them overnight, and the council is perplexed: how do we stop them? The high priest says, “We strictly charged you not to teach in this name.” Should they reply, “Pinky promise, back to our church for a holy huddle”? No. Peter and the apostles answer, “We must obey God rather than men,” and they don’t just defend themselves; they double down. It’s a hook and an uppercut.

    1. 05:16 The anthem of the early church

      • Those words in verse 29 become the anthem of the early church, the steady tune playing for 2,000 years behind every great accomplishment, advancement, or sacrifice for the kingdom of God. Everything God’s people have today has come at a price: men and women willing to lay down their lives for the gospel and God’s word, who gain the title of martyr.
      • Today’s text unfolds one of the first such sacrifices, a young man whose name Steve happens to share: Stephen.

  4. 05:57 Acts 6:1: the disciples were increasing

    Scripture: Acts 6–8 Acts 6:1 Acts 9 Acts 11

    • The time in the word surveys Acts 6 through 8, starting at 6:1: “In those days when the number of disciples was increasing.” The members of the early church were called just that, disciples or believers; not until Acts 9 are they called the Way, and in Acts 11 at Antioch they are first called Christians.
    • They were increasing: some estimate 8 to 10,000 so far, some scholars twice that, close to 20,000. No small group here.

  5. 06:48 Complaining in the church: the enemy switches playbooks

    • The Hellenistic Jews complained that their widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution of food. Shocking, Steve says, complaining in the church. But this is another form of the enemy’s opposition. Arrests, imprisonments, beatings, threats, and warnings have not worked; like Wile E. Coyote chasing the Road Runner, every scheme works to his own demise.
    • So the enemy switches playbooks to an inside play: stir up strife and mistrust from within. In an oversimplification, the Hebraic Jews were the native population of Israel and the Hellenists had adopted more Greek practices, but both were true Jewish converts who believed Jesus is Messiah. This isn’t a Jew-versus-Gentile issue, which comes later in Acts; it is a squabble between brothers and sisters in Christ.

  6. 08:14 Verse 2: the Twelve choose seven

    Scripture: Acts 6:2–4

    • The Twelve say it would not be right to neglect the ministry of the word of God to wait on tables, so the disciples should choose seven men known to be full of the Spirit and wisdom, while the apostles give their attention to prayer and the ministry of the word.

    1. 08:36 Not “we will not” but “we cannot”

      • Steve has read this in the past and thought the apostles were acting high and mighty, too good to serve tables. But the Greek word for tables can also refer to money, a table for counting or accounting. This job was overseeing the funds to purchase and distribute food to everyone in need, a big administrative job.
      • The apostles made a “we cannot,” not a “we will not,” statement. They needed to focus on teaching the word and prayer, what God called and equipped them to do. Nothing was beneath them, but some things were outside other people’s giftings and responsibilities.

    2. 09:33 His dad on the plant floor

      • We all appreciate leaders like that. Steve’s dad was a plant manager in manufacturing in California and Arizona, and his employees loved him, because when someone called in sick from the assembly line he wasn’t afraid to work a full shift on the floor beside the people who called him boss. He wouldn’t ask anyone to do something he wasn’t willing to do, and that has become Steve’s working definition of a true leader.
      • The Twelve wisely realized they needed more hands to expand the ministry, which is what church leaders are called and expected to do.

    3. 10:21 Equip the saints for the work of ministry

      Scripture: Ephesians 4:11–12

      • In just over three years as Crossroads youth pastor, Steve has struggled with the idea that he must do and be everything. Fifteen years as a classroom teacher taught him that the teacher is also coach, event planner, disciplinarian, counselor, Band-Aid distributor, test administrator, newsletter writer, and gum scraper; if you don’t do it, no one will.
      • Not so in the body of Christ. We all have diverse skills, passions, and abilities; we must be willing to step up and use them, and to step down and let others use them. He is thankful for the youth team that frees him to focus on teaching and leading.
      • Paul reminds the church in Ephesians 4 that Christ gave apostles, prophets, evangelists, shepherds, and teachers to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ. Steve asks his students what his job is: yes, to teach and lead them, but more importantly to equip them for ministry themselves.

  7. 11:50 Verses 5–7: the solution, and a progress report

    Scripture: Acts 6:5–6 Acts 6:7

    • The proposal pleased the whole group, and they chose Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit, along with Philip, Procorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolas, and the apostles prayed and laid hands on them. All seven have Greek names, representing the Hellenistic people who brought the complaint. Laying on hands reminds us that all work done in Christ’s name is kingdom work, worthy of honor and prayer.
    • In verse 7 Luke gives one of his frequent progress reports, about ten of them in Acts: the word of God spread, the disciples in Jerusalem increased rapidly, and a large number of priests became obedient to the faith. Crisis averted; the enemy’s plan spoiled once again.

  8. 12:55 Stephen: full of grace and power

    Scripture: Acts 6:8–10

    • Now a case study of one who was filled with the Holy Spirit. Stephen performed great wonders and signs, and when members of the Synagogue of the Freedmen argued with him, they could not stand up against the wisdom the Spirit gave him as he spoke.

    1. 13:29 An ordinary guy in an ordinary role

      • Read the chapter backwards and you might be shocked to discover Stephen wasn’t anything special: an ordinary guy who stepped up to fill an ordinary role. Yet God had bigger plans for this servant-hearted young man. He was so on fire for Jesus and so full of the Spirit’s wisdom that the most learned people were no match for him. These conversations no doubt focused on Jesus’ death and resurrection and on Jesus as the promised Messiah of the Old Testament. Stephen was a bold witness for Christ.

    2. 14:07 “I don’t know what to say”

      Scripture: Luke 12:11–12

      • The youth have begun a 32-week series called Witnesses. Asked why they don’t regularly share their faith, students gave two top answers: I don’t want to be made fun of, treated differently, or rejected; and I don’t know what to say. Steve will come back to the first.
      • To the second: the transfer of souls from the kingdom of darkness to the kingdom of light belongs to Jesus only. At the summer high school camp at Biola University, a structure over the parking garage reminds them that Jesus saves. If Jesus saves, the pressure is off. The success of sharing Jesus isn’t reliant on the outcome; the success is simply sharing Jesus.
      • Scripture also promises that when we are faithful to start such conversations, we don’t have to worry about carrying them on. Jesus told his disciples in Luke 12 not to worry how they would defend themselves before synagogues, rulers, and authorities, for the Holy Spirit will teach you at that time what you should say. Stephen is seeing that verse play out live: saturated with Scripture and ready to be an instrument of God’s power, an unstoppable combination.

    3. 16:14 Created players with 99s across the board

      • We are quick to forget we have the Spirit of the living God living in us. Millennials who grew up on EA Sports games remember creating a player with 99 in every stat, the tallest, strongest, fastest player on the field, with your name on the jersey, dominating every CPU team. That is what living in full surrender to the Holy Spirit and his empowerment is like: a powerful, unstoppable advantage.

  9. 16:59 Verse 11: false witnesses and a charge of blasphemy

    Scripture: Acts 6:11–14

    • With no other route to victory, Stephen’s opponents adopt trickery and deceit: they plant false witnesses, stir up the people, and bring him before the Sanhedrin to make a case for blasphemy. They accuse him of speaking against the temple and the law, the two things they hold most dear; any threat to them is a threat to their livelihood and religious interest.

    1. 18:07 Déjà vu from Jesus’ trial

      Scripture: Mark 14:58 John 1:17 John 5:45

      • Stephen was simply restating the words of Jesus, and the chief priests had played the same lines at Jesus’ trial: “We heard him say, I will destroy this temple made with human hands, and in three days will build another not made with hands.” Jesus did say that, but he spoke of his own body, destroyed by them and raised three days later by God.
      • Stephen was also apparently elevating Jesus above Moses, more déjà vu. John wrote, “For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.” Jesus was more direct with the religious leaders in front of him: “Do not think I will accuse you before the Father. Your accuser is Moses, on whom your hopes are set.” Ouch. To people living by the law, any disruption of works-based righteousness was a real threat.

    2. 19:27 Truth mixed with lies, then and now

      • Their accusations carry some truth taken out of context, truth mixed with lies, and Christ followers today are no strangers to it. “You Christians are too exclusive.” We believe Jesus is the only way, truth, and life, but also that he died for all. “You hate women and their rights.” No, we defend the innocent unborn, made in the image of God. “You’re intolerant and hateful.” We desire everyone to come to a saving knowledge of Jesus; we do hate sin and evil and are intolerant of injustice.
      • Some Christians in some churches couldn’t make that defense; they are exclusive, brash, and hateful. Be like Stephen: don’t blur the truth with an agenda of your own. Take personal preferences and passions out of it and let people be confronted with God and his word, because God will defend both quite effectively apart from us.

    3. 20:55 Grace stands opposed to pride

      • Why was Christianity so opposed then, and why now? Quite simply because it is grace-based, and human nature says, “I want to stand on my own. I don’t need your charity. I’ve got this.” Pride stands in opposition to grace, and the religious are the worst in the pride department.

  10. 21:20 A face like the face of an angel

    Scripture: Acts 6:15 Psalm 34:5

    • The Sanhedrin saw that Stephen’s face was like the face of an angel. Not the chubby cherub of a 90s Christian bookstore: Luke means Stephen was completely calm, his face pure and bright, reflecting the presence of God.
    • When we are falsely accused, are we cool, calm, radiant, and reliant? David said in Psalm 34:5, “Those who look to him are radiant, and their faces shall never be ashamed.” God’s people, whose future is secure and whose hope is undiminished by circumstances, should be nothing short of radiant. The bigger question: are you looking to Jesus? In the middle of this mess, Stephen no doubt was.

  11. 22:29 Chapter 7: “Are these charges true?”

    Scripture: Acts 7 Acts 7:1

    • Steve won’t cover all of chapter 7, but shows how Stephen’s defense of the gospel starts and ends. The high priest was most likely Caiaphas, the same one who presided over Jesus’ trial, which makes “Are these charges true?” more sinister. It isn’t an open door for a nice reply; in modern legal speech it means, “How do you plead?” And Stephen, the layman hired to oversee food distribution, was ready to reply.

    1. 23:16 Apologetics: a reason for the hope

      Scripture: 1 Peter 3:15–16

      • Stephen’s speech is commonly called his defense or apology, not as in saying sorry and paying the fine, but apologetics, from the Greek apologia, an answer given in reply. We apologize when we admit we are wrong; apologetics states an argument that proves you are right.
      • Peter was likely standing nearby and had Stephen’s boldness in mind when he wrote: in your hearts revere Christ as Lord, always be prepared to give an answer for the hope that you have, with gentleness and respect. Notice he doesn’t say know everything about Jesus and the Bible or memorize every scientific argument and counterargument; be prepared to give a reason for your hope. That’s all there is to it.
      • Steve tells his students they don’t need all the answers, only to be ready to share their hope when the time comes. God opens the doors and prompts you with what to say; you just obey the Spirit’s nudge.

  12. 25:10 Stephen’s defense and its ending

    Scripture: Acts 7:2–50 Acts 7:51–53

    • Verses 2 through 50 are homework: a masterful, detailed defense of the Christian faith drawing a line from the Old Testament to the rejection of Jesus, calling out the very men who rejected him to the point of death.
    • Stephen finishes: “You stiff-necked people… you always resist the Holy Spirit… now you have betrayed and murdered him.” Stephen had clearly never read How to Win Friends and Influence People. He calls them out for resisting the Spirit and rejecting the true purpose of the law they claimed to follow.
    • Paraphrasing pastor and commentator David Guzik: Stephen wasn’t interested in hearing “not guilty” from men; he wanted to hear “well done” from God his Father. He had to know by their growing agitation that things would not end well.

  13. 26:48 Stephen sees the glory of God

    Scripture: Acts 7:54–56 Acts 7:2

    • The Sanhedrin were furious and gnashed their teeth, but Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, looked up and saw the glory of God and Jesus standing at the right hand of God: “Look, I see heaven open.”
    • Something cool, whether Luke intended it or not: Stephen opens his speech in verse 2 with “the God of glory” and ends by seeing the glory of God. He was interested in nothing but God’s glory, the two bookends of his speech and the core of his mission.

    1. 27:38 Where is the Holy Spirit?

      Scripture: Acts 17:28

      • Many in the church wonder why they don’t feel the fire and passion they once felt; Steve has been there too. If we truly want to be filled with the Spirit, we need to step into something that requires his filling: things God calls us to that are beyond our strength, ability, knowledge, and vocabulary. We play it safe and never leave our comfort zones, so there is no need for the Spirit to be anything more. We can’t expect God to equip us with something we have no intention of using.
      • He says it tongue in cheek, since he needs God’s Spirit to do anything and everything (have you sat in a school pickup line lately?). It is in him that we live and move and have our being. But God longs for more for his church when it comes to being bold witnesses.

  14. 28:54 The stoning of Stephen

    Scripture: Acts 7:57–60 Leviticus 24:16 1 Corinthians 15:55 2 Corinthians 5:8

    • They covered their ears, rushed at him, dragged him out of the city, and stoned him, laying their coats at the feet of a young man named Saul. Stephen prayed, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit,” and “Lord, do not hold this sin against them,” and he fell asleep.
    • Stoning was the law’s punishment for blasphemy, but it was no peaceful death, and this was no organized execution of deserved justice; it became mob violence against an innocent man who had spoken in the name of God. Some people go from cupping their ears to hear the truth to plugging them when it brushes against their pride.
    • Stephen prays for his attackers and falls asleep, a euphemism for the death of a believer, because death is not final: “Death, where is your victory? Death, where is your sting?” To be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord.

    1. 30:37 Just like Jesus

      • The parallels with Jesus’ death: Stephen stood trial before false accusers on similar charges, criticized the religious elite, saw God in heavenly glory, was taken outside the city and condemned to death, committed his spirit to hands beyond his own, and prayed forgiveness for his enemies. Talk about a “What would Jesus do?” moment. Stephen became the first martyr for Christ, a man worthy to hear, “Well done, my good and faithful servant.”

  15. 31:26 Persecution in the church

    • Before this the apostles had experienced persecution, but now it resulted in death and the stakes were raised. Recall the students’ answers: “I don’t know what to say” can be overcome by the Spirit’s power and time in God’s word. The first answer was “I don’t want to be made fun of or treated differently.”
    • The American church doesn’t expect or experience much persecution, and we wrongly equate it with any wrong treatment: the guy must have cut me off because of my Christian bumper sticker; they wouldn’t take my expired Kohl’s Cash, they must hate Jesus; I can’t play Christian music loud in my cubicle; my boss makes me work Sundays. Join the club.

    1. 32:46 Real persecution, and why to expect it

      Scripture: John 15:18–20

      • Believers around the globe know real persecution. Data shows one in seven Christians worldwide face high levels of persecution, up from one in eight three years ago, a big difference across millions: hostility, restrictions, intimidation, discrimination, harassment, torture, arrest, children taken, imprisonment, and death. On U.S. soil we worry someone might think we are weird or awkward.
      • Persecution is to be expected; it is evidence of authentic faith, because the gospel will always be opposed and tested. Leaving the upper room on his final night, Jesus said in John 15, “If the world hates you, keep in mind that it hated me first… If they persecuted me, they will persecute you also.”
      • If you face no opposition for your faith, something may be off. Steve isn’t telling you to sell your house and move to North Korea, but you may need to step outside your comfort zone and start bold conversations with lost people in your life.

  16. 34:44 Acts 8: scattered, and the word spread

    Scripture: Acts 8:1 Acts 8:4

    • Right after Stephen’s death a great persecution broke out in Jerusalem, and all except the apostles were scattered through Judea and Samaria, exactly what Jesus wanted, and those scattered preached the word wherever they went. The enemy threw everything he had at the church, but Stephen’s death and the persecution that followed were only fuel on a blazing fire.
    • Like that hot transmission fluid Steve could only watch drain out, the good news of Jesus was out and unstoppable, and every attempt to squash it only spread it further. It is still true: Christianity is thriving where persecution is greatest. An estimated 13 Christians die every day for their faith, but the spread of the gospel is way more than that.

  17. 36:04 Missionaries and martyrs

    • No comic books for Steve as a kid; he grew up reading stories of missionaries and martyrs, starting with Stephen. Most of the original apostles became martyrs, and even as a kid he realized there was something special about the message they would sacrifice everything for: Hudson Taylor in inland China, John Wycliffe burned at the stake, Dietrich Bonhoeffer, victim of the Nazi party.

    1. 36:58 Nate Saint and Jim Elliot

      • One of the most impactful for him is jungle pilot Nate Saint, his friend Jim Elliot, and three other missionaries murdered in January 1956 trying to take the gospel to the Waodani in Ecuador. Their bodies were found on a remote river beach beside their damaged yellow Piper; they had guns but clearly chose not to use them.
      • Through Gates of Splendor by Elisabeth Elliot and End of the Spear by Steve Saint, Nate’s son, tell how tragedy turned into triumph. No pain, loss, or tears were wasted as God brought salvation to the Waodani and other tribes, and the places those five vacated were filled many times over by young people moved by their sacrifice.

    2. 38:18 His grandparents’ calling

      • Steve knows that to be fact. While the headlines announced five men missing, his grandparents, David and Joan, were in Panama waiting for visas to Venezuela with a toddler, Steve’s dad, and another baby on the way, called to take the hope of Christ to the Orinoco River valley. The news did nothing to deter them; missionaries then expected to spend the rest of their lives on the field, prepared for persecution and death.
      • They weren’t perfect, but they faithfully followed God’s call, dropping the 1950s pursuit of the American dream for the kingdom. Both died a few years apart when Steve was in his 20s, and he knows they entered their Savior’s presence with “Well done, good and faithful servant.” Not martyrs, but witnesses to the very end, servants and ambassadors of the Lord.

  18. 39:41 Embrace it: the fellowship of his sufferings

    Scripture: Philippians 3:8–11

    • So how about you? Steve prays that from this look at the first Christian martyr the church is encouraged not to run from persecution but to embrace it, as weird as that sounds, because it is a sign you are walking hand in hand with the Spirit.
    • In Philippians 3 Paul, the same Saul who kept watch over the coats while men stoned Stephen, writes that he counts everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus, that he may know him and the power of his resurrection. Yes, Lord. But wait, there’s more, and it’s not as much fun: “and may share his sufferings, becoming like him in his death.”
    • Truly following Christ and sharing his victory requires sharing his suffering, and Christians across the world embrace it. Are we prepared to stand firm in Christ if and when it comes to our doorstep?

  19. 41:22 “He is no fool”

    • Steve closes with the words of Jim Elliot, a reminder to prioritize the eternal over the temporal and seek first the kingdom of God: “He is no fool who gives what he cannot keep to gain that which he cannot lose.”

  20. 41:45 Closing prayer

    Scripture: John 16:33

    • Steve thanks God for a word both encouraging and convicting and prays the church would be motivated to share its hope: no guilt in life, no fear in death, the power of Christ living in us. In this world we will have trouble, but take heart, he has overcome the world.
    • He asks God to help them tell real trouble from fake trouble, to be counted worthy of the call to reach the lost, not all as missionaries or evangelists but as ambassadors for Christ ready to give a reason for their hope, remembering he is always with us, even to the end of the age.
