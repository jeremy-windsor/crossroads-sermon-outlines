Overview

  1. 00:17 Mark 10: a loaded chapter

    Scripture: Mark 10 Mark 10:13 Mark 10:35

    • The church continues through Mark in chapter 10, and it is loaded: Jesus discusses marriage and divorce, blesses the little children, speaks to a rich young man, predicts his death a third time, teaches the disciples about serving, and heals a blind beggar.
    • Josh didn’t launch this eight-week series to go verse by verse; the math doesn’t work. His goal is to show major themes about the kingdom of God and embrace Mark’s unique perspective. Over and over God’s kingdom has flipped social and cultural norms, and in chapter 10 power, position, importance, and influence are flipped too.
    • The reversal stands out in two sections. The first starts in verse 13, “The Little Children and Jesus,” where 95% of the message will be spent. The second, briefly, is in verse 35, which Josh affectionately calls “the ambitious anglers.”

  2. 01:57 Dependence and sacrifice, vertical and horizontal

    • Josh warns that the text will confront the church as it confronted him, and it demands an attitude adjustment from the off-balanced believer. The kingdom of God values dependence and sacrifice, not only in our vertical relationship with God but in our horizontal relationships with each other.
    • If you sacrifice for God but hold back from your neighbor, you violate not only the second greatest command but also the first. You are also out of balance if you grow dependent on relationships with believers but don’t stand strong in your personal relationship with the Lord.

  3. 02:36 Everyone wants to be a theologian; no one wants to wash feet

    Scripture: Mark 10

    • Two things stand out in chapter 10. First, today we look to influencers, scholars, and seminary graduates to guide our faith, but in Mark 10 Jesus says we should be more like children and slaves.
    • Josh suggests the church has a pandemic: so much content that professional Christians walk around with YouTube diplomas and no desire to sacrifice for others. Everyone wants to be a theologian and no one wants to wash feet. Christians have their AirPods jammed in so deep they will never hear the person screaming next to them. You watched an eight-hour podcast? You could learn more about following Jesus by asking a stranger if you can pray for them.
    • If people tune out the rest of the message, Josh asks them to get this: you will grow more in your faith through five minutes of obedience than in eight hours of observation. Take the YouTubers out of your head and you might hear from Yahweh. Listening has its place, but doing and sacrificing can’t begin until you stop listening. You don’t become a better Christian by hearing 18 sermons a week but by applying one. We have a content problem, 90% absorption and 10% action, and we need to flip it.

    1. 04:52 Start being doers in your marriage

      • The first place to be a doer of the word is your marriage. Some Christians aren’t scared to pray for a stranger but fail to pray for their spouse. Do you think the Lord cares how well you lead people in his house if you refuse to lead the people he has placed in yours?
      • Josh’s favorite testimonies are married couples sticking it out, with a drug addict going clean for Jesus a close second. The lobby scares him, but he goes there out of love, and he always seeks out couples. Last week he met couples married over 50 years and told them he wants to be like them when he grows up, because they have been through it, seen some junk, and are still praising Jesus.
      • Run the race; stop watching others run it. Don’t become an addict of Christian content; become a doer of the word in your home, marriage, workplace, and neighborhood.

  4. 06:43 Children are a blessing

    • Second, unlike today, in Jesus’ day most people, especially the Jews, considered children an incredible blessing. Josh finds it infuriating that our culture sees them as a burden; back then being childless was a disgrace, and now it is celebrated as freedom. If we truly believed children were a blessing, our culture would encourage people to have more babies.
    • He loves how many babies are in the church; they are thinking about expanding the nursery, and anyone who loves babies can sign up to help so moms and dads can hear the word. He wants a new Uncle Sam campaign telling people to go make babies, says he would double the child tax credit, and would pull all funding from abortion clinics and give it to new moms and dads. To be clear, he loves babies but doesn’t want to hold yours; that is a lot of responsibility.
    • When a baby cries in the service, it doesn’t bother Josh one bit; he is more likely to call out the person giving you the stink eye, who probably left his phone on last service. We’re all hypocrites. Josh loves the little children, and so does Jesus.

  5. 08:52 “Let the little children come to me”

    Scripture: Mark 10:13–16

    • Josh reads verses 13–16: people bring little children for Jesus to place his hands on them, the disciples rebuke them, and Jesus is indignant: “Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them, for the kingdom of God belongs to such as these. Truly I tell you, anyone who will not receive the kingdom of God like a little child will never enter it.” He took the children in his arms, placed his hands on them, and blessed them.

    1. 09:22 Jesus was indignant

      Scripture: Mark 9:36–37

      • Parents customarily brought children to a rabbi for a blessing, so they brought children of all ages to Jesus, some in their mothers’ arms and some running around, and he welcomed them all.
      • Jesus’ rebuke was heated and public. He was angry with the disciples, rightly, because he had just gone over this in Mark 9: whoever welcomes one of these little children in my name welcomes me. His rebuke is clear: why can’t you get with the program?

    2. 10:16 Were the disciples just protecting his schedule?

      • Kind people might defend the disciples: they were just helping Jesus manage a busy schedule. Maybe so, but according to Jesus, if you are going to save him time, the cuts won’t come from the children; more likely they will come from the hypocrites.
      • Jesus says these children are better kingdom examples than the adults. Everyone tells children to grow up and act their age, but to enter the kingdom of God you need to be more like a child.

    3. 11:08 The great sin of becoming a roadblock

      • In the lead-up to this text you can’t miss what a great sin it is to become a roadblock that keeps anyone from Christ, the only way of salvation from the wrath of God. What kind of person would keep someone from Jesus, add obstacles, or be content watching them go the other way? Not a relative of Jesus; his relatives know he has no favorites and desires all to be saved. Someone who keeps another from Jesus must not know Jesus at all.

    4. 12:01 Are your hands spotless?

      • If we don’t defend the disciples, some of us want to stand by Jesus pointing the finger, because then we don’t have to look at ourselves. So Josh asks: are your hands spotless? Is it possible you have hindered someone from coming to Jesus through repentance and faith, stood between them and their Savior, or sent them away instead of pointing to Jesus? It is a terrible thought, but he thinks far too many of us have done just that.
      • He will point out eight ways Christians build walls instead of parting the sea. If Jesus was angry at the disciples, he is certainly angry at you and me. We must not keep them from him.

  6. 13:24 Eight ways Christians build walls instead of parting seas

    • Josh names eight ways Christians keep people from Jesus, building walls instead of parting the sea.

    1. 13:24 1. Hypocrisy

      Scripture: Galatians 5:22–24 Galatians 6:8

      • If professing Christians live in a way that contradicts the gospel, it drives people away. The Holy Spirit gives power to live holy lives, which Paul calls fruit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control; those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the sinful nature.
      • The problem is the outspoken Christian with no joy, no gentleness, no self-control. One believer claims the Spirit and keeps living in sin; another claims the Spirit and lives a changed life. The outsider asks why the drastic difference, what the point of being a Christian is if nothing changes, and why he would run to Jesus if he can’t really help.
      • Hard as it is to see, if we don’t keep in step with the Spirit, we may be a stumbling block that people stumble over right into hell. Paul says a few verses later that those who sow to please their sinful nature will reap destruction. The heavy reality: if you reap destruction, how many are you taking with you?
      1. 15:24 Repentance, confession, restoration
        • That is why repentance, confession, and restoration are so important in the local church. Josh admits he has sinned against some of them; ask Chris, the executive pastor, who besides his wife spends the most time with him. Josh is a sinner, aware of his sin and its impact on him and others, but that awareness is the work of the Spirit, not Josh. He is quick to humble himself, some days better than others, a work in progress.
        • You become a stumbling block when you live in secret sin but claim to be showered by God’s grace. Why live in secret if you are covered in grace?
      2. 16:31 Attending your own funeral

        Scripture: Galatians 6:7

        • Josh couldn’t shake a picture all week of a burial site. As their pastor, he says many are attending their own funeral, willing participants buried beneath their shame, climbing into their own tomb, closing the casket, and welcoming every spoonful of dirt: “I deserve that.”
        • Meanwhile Jesus is outside saying, “Get up! I didn’t do this so you could live in shame. Shake it off and live a life of victory in my name. I died for your freedom.” Josh is frustrated not at them but for them. He rebukes Satan’s lies in Jesus’ name: Satan wants you to believe you are buried under something you can’t climb out of, but you have all the help you need; just reach out and grab him.
        • This is how we end hypocrisy and put it to death. Listen to the great apostle: do not be deceived; God cannot be mocked; people reap what they sow.

    2. 18:29 2. Harsh and judgmental attitudes

      • The second wall is a fruit of hypocrisy: harsh, judgmental attitudes. A sinner is saved by grace, receives the Spirit, and God begins the work of sanctification, conforming us to the likeness of his Son, only for that person to take God’s masterpiece, erase his name, and sign their own.
      • Josh hears, “I led someone to Christ today.” Praise God for that soul, but is this your postgame interview or his? God rescued you, taught you, sent you, put you next to that person, and gave you the words; it sounds like God led someone to Christ through you. We must make that distinction because we give him all the glory, and the byproduct is that you stay humble.
      1. 20:01 “I’ll go first”: childlike, not childish
        • Josh goes first: there is nothing good in him apart from Christ. He is naturally a selfish husband, and the Spirit nags him to love his wife, lead like a man of God, and lead like he would die for her. He would be a deadbeat dad if not for the Spirit nagging him to discipline his kids even when it is hard: you think you are loving them by being soft, but you are hating them.
        • The Christian with a superiority complex doesn’t part seas for others to see Jesus; they send people away. Anything superior in you is there by God’s grace alone, so stop pretending you built that.
        • We enter God’s kingdom by faith like little children, helpless and totally dependent on God’s mercy, and we enjoy it the same way, believing the Father loves us and will meet our every need. When a child is hurt, they run to Mama and Daddy. Many are settling for running to each other; run to him. Community has value, but not when it dismisses the community you need with him. In our relationship with the Lord we learn from little children, but with one another we can’t afford to be childish.

    3. 22:03 3. Preaching a false gospel

      Scripture: 1 Timothy 1:15 Romans 3:23 1 Corinthians 15:3–4 Romans 6:23 Philippians 2:11 Ephesians 2:8–9 James 2:26

      • Man’s efforts to make the gospel more friendly lead people away from Jesus, and so do efforts to make it more exclusive. We lead people away whenever we widen the road or narrow it further.
      • Some say the gospel is simple; others say it is complicated. Josh says it is both. It is simple enough that salvation can happen in a single phrase: Christ died to save sinners, of whom I am the worst; call on the name of Jesus and be saved. It is complicated in that you could spend a lifetime dwelling on it and find nothing but joy, hope, fulfillment, and love.
      • We all fall short of the glory of God, but there is one without sin who saves, Jesus Christ. Paul passed on what he received as of first importance: Christ died for our sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day. He had to die because the wages of sin is death; he absorbed God’s wrath, was raised for our justification, ascended, and sits at the Father’s right hand as our heavenly Advocate and only mediator. Every tongue will confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, he will return and make all things new, and Jesus reversed the curse, for it is by grace you have been saved through faith.
      • Faith without works is dead, but works without faith is filthy; phony faith is just as bad as empty religion. You need to run to the feet of Jesus and confess your sins to him every day, and he will forgive you. If he died for you, you can kill your sin for him. The gospel doesn’t just touch lives; it changes them forever.

    4. 25:22 4. Lacking love and compassion

      Scripture: John 13:35

      • The ways are all linked. If we fail to show the love of Christ through our actions, we make the gospel unattractive. Jesus tells the disciples everyone will know who they are if they love one another. Love is the magnet that pulls people to Christ; selfishness is the stench that keeps them away.
      • Josh admits this may sound grumpy; when he takes a week off he gets hyped, and he has sat with this for two weeks. But the distinctions matter.
      1. 26:24 What it means to become like a little child
        • To enter the kingdom you have to become like a little child, but it is a metaphor, because there are many things children are that you don’t want to be. A child just says what’s on their mind, while an adult uses discernment and self-control, takes every thought captive, and would never use the tongue as a weapon. Jesus isn’t giving permission to be spiritually childish; he says be childlike, not childish.
        • Children are dependent. The gospel needs a helpless response: not just knowing Jesus paid your debt, but depending on him completely to earn what you couldn’t. Children don’t come in with their own part to play. Your child won’t walk in like Boss Baby with a briefcase and offer to get a job because raising him is expensive; parents of 26-year-olds at home know they need everything from you.
        • But here is the distinction: children expect to be loved. A four-year-old walks in convinced he is the only one with anything important to say. You need a child’s dependence to love others like Jesus, but if you have too high an opinion of yourself, you will never love like him, because you will be too busy getting everyone’s attention.

    5. 29:12 5. Expecting the cleanup before the confession

      Scripture: 1 John 1:9

      • This one infuriates Josh. If your advice to someone seeking Jesus is that they need to stop drinking, smoking, or sexting to meet him, you’re the problem. It is like telling someone they might want to shower before they get baptized.
      • 1 John 1:9: if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us and purify us from all unrighteousness. Step one is forgiveness; step two is purification. Forgiveness is immediate; purification is not.
      • Getting uncomfortable, Josh shares that the Lord called him into ministry with tobacco in his lip and lust in his eyes, and the first time he led a teenager to Christ he was still an addict. He isn’t those things now, and if he stumbles he will bring it to the church. Love people where they are, not where you wish they would be. If we baptize the alcoholic, we must be willing to preach the gospel to him on Tuesday when he relapses. No shame in this place.

    6. 30:48 6. Misrepresenting the character of God

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:6

      • Josh wasn’t sure how the church would react to an eight-point sermon, but so far so good; three to go. The sixth is a big one. We like to present God the way we think a person needs to see him, when we should show them all of him and let them wrestle with God. Someone looking for justice sees God’s justice without his mercy; someone looking for affirmation sees his loving kindness without his holiness.
      • God is holy, eternal, invisible, immense, immutable, self-existent yet offering life, perfect, sovereign, all-powerful, all-knowing, ever-present, incomprehensible, uncontainable, indescribable; loving, good, faithful, merciful, gracious, righteous, just, truthful, forgiving, patient, and jealous, because there is no one like him. He hates sin and will punish the guilty. Josh’s favorite: he is the rewarder of those who diligently seek him.
      • Since it is your job to tell people about him, it is your job to know him. Do you know him?

    7. 32:44 7. Pleading the fifth

      Scripture: 1 Peter 3:13–16

      • The world condemns the church when it speaks out; heaven condemns her when she stays silent. Who are you playing for?
      • It is scary, but have your testimony loaded and ready; it is your weapon, so don’t leave home without it. Share what God has done, let people ask questions, let them hate you for it if they want, and invite friends and neighbors to church. You are never alone; he promised to be with you to the very end. Pray for strength and wisdom, write your testimony out, be vulnerable about your struggles, and don’t worry about the outcome; just be faithful.
      • Peter says: who will harm you if you are eager to do good? Even if you suffer for what is right, you are blessed. Revere Christ as Lord and always be prepared to give the reason for your hope, with gentleness and respect. However it goes, pray for them and keep sharing the word. Spurgeon said it best: if sinners be damned, at least let them leap to hell over our dead bodies, and if they perish, let them perish with our arms wrapped around their knees, begging them to stay.

    8. 34:37 8. Causing division in the church

      • This last way takes the bulk of the time. Josh grew up in a household that loved ministry, the son of a pastor who was the son of a pastor, and for most of his life thought the worst enemies of the gospel were out there. He was wrong: the worst enemies of the gospel are those who profess it yet bring shame on it.
      • Scripture is full of warnings against causing division; if he quoted one he would have to quote them all. It has always amazed him that the message of reconciliation can become a deadly weapon in our hands, a message of healing causing wounds. One reason: instead of childlike dependence, we have developed childish demands.
      • Division is a result of the flesh, not a fruit of the Spirit. Before his crucifixion Jesus prayed that we would be one, and that prayer was answered, because we are all one in Christ. Our problem isn’t oneness with Christ but oneness with each other, and we fail each other in two areas: socially and doctrinally.
      1. 36:28 Socially: stop gossiping
        • Stop gossiping. Ninety-eight percent of our issues would be covered if we stopped talking to the wrong people about someone else; repent and move on. If you have an issue with someone, go directly to them; if it’s with Josh, come to him. Your life group isn’t the place to dump frustration about the thermostat settings.
        • Stop telling others how to manage their family when yours is a hot mess; get your priorities straight first. If you are quick to see others’ flaws but ignorant of your own, you probably don’t spend enough time with the Lord, because he is the one who shows you your flaws. The moment you find yourself gossiping, turn those words into prayer and intercede for them.
        • Josh expects to make some friends: among the ladies the issue is predominantly gossip, and among the men mostly doctrine. Men gossip and women care about solid doctrine, but women talk more and men flex more. Both are childish, not childlike. Whatever your 23rd pair of chromosomes, the message is the same: stop talking about them and go to them.
      2. 38:24 Doctrinally: preferences or foundational truths?

        Scripture: Galatians 5:22–23

        • In a church with so many backgrounds, a frequent issue Josh encounters is that many were taught denominational preferences instead of foundational truths, with tradition elevated over truth.
        • Some, his wife included, were raised in churches teaching that the only evidence of the Holy Spirit is tongues. Josh rejects this entirely: Paul told the Galatians about many fruits of the Spirit, none of them tongues, and the ones Paul mentions, like kindness, goodness, and gentleness, all build others up. He isn’t sure how uninterpreted utterances in the hallway build up anyone but yourself. But we can disagree on this without breaking unity; it is a secondary issue.
      3. 39:57 Essential issues
        • There are essential, secondary, and tertiary issues, and Josh walks through them as the church’s elders see them. Essentials include the Trinity, one God in three persons; the deity of Christ; the gospel, salvation by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone; the resurrection (if you say you don’t believe in it, you’re not a Christian); the return of Christ; and the authority of Scripture.
        • That last one is interesting: the inerrancy and authority of Scripture isn’t an issue of salvation, but it is still essential. You can be saved with a wrong view of God’s word, which is why we need good shepherds and teachers to correct us. These core doctrines are foundational, and most are necessary for salvation.
      4. 40:54 Secondary issues
        • Secondary issues are incredibly important but don’t affect salvation, though they lead to denominational differences. Josh firmly believes Christians can differ greatly here and still celebrate one another’s faith, take communion together, and worship together. The On Mission class covers these more fully.
        • Examples: baptism by immersion or sprinkling, and infant or believer’s baptism (the church practices believer’s baptism by immersion, but Josh would welcome someone sprinkled as a brother); church governance (Crossroads is elder-run, and Josh is the only staff member who is also an elder, so they are his peers and his boss, which is awkward, but they make it work); cessationism versus continuationism; end-times views, premillennial, amillennial, postmillennial, which many in this church need to hear are secondary; and the role of women in ministry, complementarian versus egalitarian.
        • These may lead you to another church, but they don’t have to. If we disagree on women elders, Josh isn’t wondering whether you’re saved; he is questioning your view of the authority of Scripture, and you can work through it together. The closer you look, the more a non-essential can start to feel essential, but even then be charitable and show grace and mercy.
      5. 43:21 Tertiary issues

        Scripture: Romans 14

        • Josh’s least favorite are the tertiary, non-essential issues. You don’t have to be my twin to be my brother; we don’t have to see eye to eye to walk hand in hand. If you need a kick in the rear or feel judgy, read Romans 14 this afternoon.
        • Worship style: hymns or contemporary. The church likes both, and if you came from a church that hates instruments, they rather like them; Eli the worship guy claps. In the hands of God’s servants they make music that pleases the Lord, so they will sing and play until their throats hurt. Bible translations: there are bad ones, but someone once left the church because Josh said he preferred the NIV over the ESV. He likes the ESV too, just not that much. Christian liberty demands a different approach. Views on creation, young earth or old earth; Josh jokes that he thinks the earth is flat.
        • The last two tertiary issues cause the most division: dietary and lifestyle choices, and politics. That includes alcohol and whether to burn the Easter Bunny at the stake. Go back far enough and everything is pagan; that doesn’t mean it can’t be robbed for God’s glory. On drinking, ask before you order. Josh and his wife enjoy wine together, but he won’t pop champagne with someone holding a 90-day recovery token. Use common sense and don’t cause your brothers and sisters to stumble.
      6. 46:12 When secondary becomes essential

        Scripture: Titus 3:10

        • Josh lays this out to point out that most of the division he has experienced came when people elevated tertiary and secondary issues to essentials. Disagreeing with the preacher’s end-times view or your life group leader’s view of immigration policy isn’t wrong; being outspoken in a way that causes disunity, or going behind their back, makes you the problem. Knowing what is essential allows respectful theological discussion and unity while keeping doctrinal integrity.
        • Each person is responsible for building a culture of unity; it isn’t Josh’s job any more than theirs. But he speaks for all the elders with confidence: Titus 3 says to warn a divisive person once, then a second time, and after that have nothing to do with them. Tolerating divisive people doesn’t promote unity, and the elders are committed to church discipline when necessary, even among themselves.

  7. 47:52 The ambitious anglers and the suffering servant

    Scripture: Mark 10:42–45 Mark 10

    • As promised, a little time on the last section. We have a choice: be like the ambitious anglers or like the suffering servant. James and John are lobbying for position and causing division among the disciples, and Jesus says those regarded as rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, but not so with you: whoever wants to become great must be your servant, and whoever wants to be first must be slave of all, for even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve and give his life as a ransom for many.
    • Be more like a slave. Be more like a child. That’s Mark 10.

  8. 48:50 The high cost is worth it

    Scripture: Hebrews 6:10–12

    • Josh understands if this kingdom makes people uncomfortable: the first will be last and the last first, the powerful weak and the weak powerful, the rich poor and the poor rich. Count the cost and you’ll see it is extremely high, but the high cost of following Jesus is worth it.
    • Hebrews 6 says God is not unjust; he will not forget your work and the love you have shown him as you have helped his people. Show the same diligence to the very end, not becoming lazy, but imitating those who through faith and patience inherit what has been promised.
    • Christian, if you’re building walls, watch out, because Josh will be right behind you with a sledgehammer.

  9. 49:58 Prayer at the Lord’s Table

    • Josh tells the Lord he loves him with all his heart; God knows everything about him yet loves him. This is his favorite moment of the week, when believers fellowship as they commune with God, a picture of kingdom connectedness, love, sacrifice, joy amid trials, and impossible peace unknown to the world.
    • He asks that, whatever it costs, God would make the church attractive to those who don’t know him, not for their sake but for his, in workplaces, marriages, and communities, rebuking Satan’s lies daily and clinging to his promises. Holding the bread and the juice, they remember that he took their place so they could know and be known, no longer just image bearers but his children, his holy family set apart forever.
