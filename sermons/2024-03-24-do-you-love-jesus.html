Overview

  1. 00:06 The final week of Road to Redemption, on Palm Sunday

    • Josh welcomes the church to the final week of Road to Redemption, a short series on Peter’s denial and restoration. Peter’s journey from cowardly fisherman to courageous apostle offers tremendous hope. Week one saw Peter’s denial; in week two Pastor Steve looked at Christ’s work on the cross and the atonement; today the church witnesses the result of that work changing Peter’s life forever.
    • It is also Palm Sunday, so two roads to redemption intersect. Most of the time will be spent on Peter, but you can’t speak of roads and redemption on Palm Sunday without thinking of the triumphal entry.

    1. 00:59 The triumphal entry

      Scripture: Matthew 21:1–11

      • For those who didn’t grow up in church: the triumphal entry is when Jesus came into Jerusalem the Sunday before his crucifixion, and all four Gospels mention it. Josh reads Matthew’s account: Jesus sends two disciples for a donkey and her colt to fulfill the prophet’s words, “See, your king comes to you, gentle and riding on a donkey.”
      • The crowds lay down coats and palm branches, shouting “Hosanna in the highest.” The whole city is stirred and asks, “Who is this?” and the crowds answer, “This is Jesus, the prophet from Nazareth in Galilee.”

  2. 02:04 A project for Holy Week

    Scripture: John 12–19

    • Josh usually gives homework; this week he gives a project. He spent his time off building a popsicle-stick bridge with his son (blame the school district) and kept thinking he needs to hand out more projects to the church.
    • Most churches think an amazing Holy Week takes pageantry and performance, early mornings and multiple services. Nothing is wrong with those, but a church that doesn’t plead with you to read the word of God is a church that doesn’t love the word of God.
    • The project: read from the triumphal entry to Good Friday, John 12 to John 19, eight chapters in six days, and do it together as a household, whether one or eleven. Read a chapter or two and pray over it; bring in family by Zoom or FaceTime, even Grandma in Michigan. Josh thinks it could be one of the best Holy Weeks they’ve ever had.

  3. 04:12 From the denial to the shore

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:3 Romans 10:9 John 20:29 John 20:31

    • Last time Peter denied knowing Jesus and ran away weeping bitterly. In between, Jesus stands trial; Pilate finds him not guilty but hands him over to be crucified; he dies, is laid in the tomb, and is raised on the third day. An easy way to remember it: DBR, death, burial, and resurrection, which Paul calls of first importance. Paul also says if you declare with your mouth “Jesus is Lord” and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.
    • After that comes the work of God’s Spirit in our hearts, our highest boast, and the next step is baptism. Drea believes in her heart and has declared it with her mouth, and she will be baptized after the sermon.
    • Then Jesus appears to Mary Magdalene, to the disciples, and a week later to the disciples again with Thomas there, and says what Josh considers the most amazing sentence ever uttered: “Because you have seen me, you have believed; blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed.”
    • It is like a divine game of whack-a-mole: where will Jesus pop up next? Why was John so willing to risk and lose his life to write down those 40 days? He tells us in the very next verse: these are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and by believing have life in his name.

  4. 06:34 Where’s Peter? Back to the fishing hole

    Scripture: John 21

    • Picking up in chapter 21, Jesus has appeared to the disciples twice, and you can’t help but wonder where Peter is. He has returned to what was familiar: he failed Jesus, so he went back to the thing he always found success doing, fishing.
    • Josh can relate. You are all in for Jesus, then you get pressured and cornered and do what you know you shouldn’t. Instead of running to Jesus, falling face down in prayer, and repenting, many of us let the failure lead us to the fishing hole. Shame drives you to silence, and the whispers of discouragement come: “I’m a failure. I’m too far gone. There’s no way he can use me now. I might as well go back to the way things were.”
    • But God works out victory differently than you might think. It isn’t always a first-quarter blowout; often it is a nail-biter coming into the fourth quarter with bumps and bruises. God isn’t interested in the fresh, confident, hyped pregame you; he is waiting to use the tired, weak, humiliated, fourth-quarter, blood-and-grass-stained you. If you have felt like there’s no way God can use a bum like you, chapter 21 is for you.

  5. 08:43 “It is the Lord”: Peter jumps into the water

    Scripture: John 21:4–7

    • Jesus appears a third time. Peter is with them, and they’re fishing, or really sailing, because they’re catching nothing but wind. Jesus calls from the shore, “Friends, haven’t you any fish?” and tells them to throw the net on the right side. They can’t haul it in for the number of fish; the disciple Jesus loved says, “It is the Lord,” and Peter wraps his garment around him and jumps into the water.
    • Some say Peter is still trying to prove something, to be the greatest disciple: John recognized him, but Peter swam. Maybe. Or maybe he is carrying the weight of his denial so heavily that nothing else matters but the Lord’s forgiveness. It isn’t as if Jesus needed the fish; he has been appearing at people’s dinner tables for days.
    • The key to following Jesus with longevity is hustling to him after failure. Peter gives a pure, unrehearsed display of what a personal relationship with Jesus looks like. Sadly, many of us hide from him instead, which reveals a lack of faith: if you believed he would forgive you, you’d be in the water too.
    • Adam and Eve hid from God in the garden, but Jesus made a way for sinners to stop hiding and start running to him. That doesn’t mean we don’t fear or revere God; it changes how we interact with him about our sin and failures.

    1. 11:05 “This man welcomes sinners”

      Scripture: Luke 15:2

      • The religious elite try to discredit Jesus for the company he keeps, prostitutes, tax collectors, and sinners: “This man welcomes sinners and eats with them.” The word isn’t a casual “hi, come on in”; it is prosdechomai, an eager welcome, the host waiting at the door for you to arrive. There is something personal and intimate about it. Praise God, Jesus welcomes sinners.
      • As you battle that nagging sin, don’t forget that word. Jesus isn’t sitting with arms crossed saying, “Really, Josh, this again?” He is pleased that you call on him for help and smiling when you trust his forgiveness. He wants to wage war with you, not shame you for getting your face punched in.
      • Josh knows at least a dozen people who decided in the last 30 days to dethrone an idol. Satan will toss haymakers at this church because he sees affections stirred for Christ and people making distance swims to Jesus. So when the whispers come, “You’re a failure, you’re too far gone,” remember: forgiveness is eager to welcome you; forgiveness is on the shore. You can stay in the boat and drift, or you can dine with Jesus.

  6. 14:06 “Come and have breakfast”: do you love me more than these?

    Scripture: John 21:12 John 21:15

    • Jesus says, “Come and have breakfast.” For the food lovers, Josh counts three instances of Jesus eating after he was raised, and judging by the menu, the Lord would enjoy a night at Pappadeaux.
    • When they finish eating, Jesus asks, “Simon son of John, do you love me more than these?” The question demands an answer from everyone, not just Peter: do you love Jesus? Set all other questions aside until you answer it, because no other question matters more.

    1. 14:53 Debate centers instead of care centers

      • Josh fears churches have become intellectual powerhouses with few signs of true love, more debate centers than care centers. Calvinists want more Calvinists, old-earthers want more old-earthers; are you a cessationist or a continuationist, an egalitarian or a complementarian? Josh says, “Who cares?” Shouldn’t believers ask the questions Jesus asked: do you love Jesus, and why?
      • We want to align with people before we fellowship with them, and Christ condemns it. Unity isn’t uniformity; we won’t agree on everything. Meanwhile Jesus is asking Peter, “Do you love me?”
      • Anyone can say yes; does your life verify your love? God sees what is done in secret. Josh has met people whose claim to fame is 40 years of studying God’s word and a well-organized theology, yet who seem unable to discern between their own will, God’s will, and Satan’s lies. Head knowledge means nothing without love, and understanding what it means to be a Christian doesn’t make you one.

    2. 17:14 Love is the greatest because love is eternal

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 13:13 1 John 4:8

      • Three virtues mark true believers: faith, hope, and love, and the greatest is love. Love is greatest because it is eternal. Faith won’t be forever, because we will see and experience Christ’s return; hope won’t be forever, because it will be seen fully. Love remains, because God is eternal and God is love: whoever does not love does not know God, because God is love.

    3. 18:13 The size of the question

      Scripture: Matthew 10:37 Philippians 1:21

      • “Do you love Jesus?” means: are you a Christian, a disciple, saved? It isn’t “Am I in your top five?” but “Do you love me more than these?” Josh recalls the words he thinks best separate the committed Christian from the consumer Christian: anyone who loves father or mother, son or daughter, more than me is not worthy of me.
      • This isn’t the shallow, half-devoted love we promote today; it is love willing to walk away from anything that would harm your relationship with him. Celebrate Jesus as Savior, but also revere him as Lord. You don’t become a Christian by raising a hand, filling out a card, saying a prayer, or walking an aisle. Salvation is by faith alone apart from works, and genuine saving faith develops an unshakable commitment: to live is Christ, to die is gain.
      • We are called to love family, but our commitment to Christ must never yield to anyone else. You don’t have to sell your possessions or abandon your family to follow Jesus, but would you? Don’t trick yourself; search your heart: do you love Jesus more than these?

    4. 20:39 Feelings aren’t the work of the Spirit

      Scripture: Psalm 139:23–24

      • Many have been deceived: they felt something when the lights changed or the haze hit right during a favorite Chris Tomlin song, or a preacher’s passion moved them, and mistook it for the work of the Spirit. What happens when the emotions fade, on Monday morning?
      • In ten years of youth ministry, Josh saw the fourth night of CIY play out the same way every time: everyone crying, repenting, calling home, going up front, for the same reason everyone wore a mask when COVID broke out. First we deceive ourselves, and then Satan grabs the lie: “But you said a prayer; you went up front.” Belonging to a church on earth doesn’t mean you belong to him; having your name in the church’s system doesn’t mean it is written in the Lamb’s Book of Life.
      • Self-examination is vital, and Satan hates that hearts are being searched. But your own search is faulty at best, so delegate it to God. Make Psalm 139 your prayer: search me, God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts; see if there is any offensive way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.

    5. 22:41 He is waiting patiently

      Scripture: 2 Peter 3:9 Acts 4:12

      • The first time Josh told his future bride he loved her, she looked very uncomfortable, and it was one of those “right back at you, you’re great too, gotta go” moments. As hard as that was on his pride, it was amazing when she finally said it. He loved her when she didn’t love him, and he waited, and it was well worth the wait.
      • Jesus loves you, and he knows whether you love him; you aren’t fooling anybody. But he is waiting patiently. He wants no one to perish and everyone to come to repentance, to turn from sin to faith in God. Here is the gospel according to Peter in Acts, well after his restoration: salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven by which we must be saved.
      • Don’t just answer “Yes, Jesus, I love you” because you’re here and it’s the right answer. Be sure your love is genuine; Josh can’t think of anything more devastating than a falsely professed love.

  7. 24:46 “Feed my sheep”

    Scripture: John 21:15–17

    • “Yes, Lord, you know that I love you.” “Feed my lambs.” A second time: “Take care of my sheep.” The third time Peter is hurt: “Lord, you know all things; you know that I love you.” “Feed my sheep.”

    1. 25:17 Restoration in light of the denial

      • The setting matters: Jesus chose a setting like the denial, around a fire. He also uses Peter’s own words. “More than these” recalls Peter’s boast, “Even if all these deny you, I won’t,” where “these” were the other disciples. Jesus wants the restoration seen in light of the denial; it isn’t time to forget it yet.
      • He asks three times, not to re-humiliate Peter, but to show the performance-driven guy that the only performance that counts is already done. That is what makes the gospel so good: your performance is not the basis of your acceptance before God. Jesus welcomed sinners and rebuked the self-righteous.

    2. 26:37 It’s our strengths that keep us from being used

      Scripture: Matthew 5:3

      • It isn’t our weakness that keeps us from being used by God; it is our strengths. Based on performance, John seems the most qualified to preach at Pentecost, but it was Peter, not because of his qualifications but because of his failures. Peter was the most educated in the grace of Christ; he had a PhD.
      • Many Christians waste their education in grace. They hit rock bottom, get up, and wonder whether to keep it secret or what people will think. But your education in grace is your license to preach; it is part of denying self. Jesus said blessed are the poor in spirit. Nearly everyone teaches us to be rich in spirit, “You’re awesome, go do you,” but Jesus says bankrupt that thing.

    3. 28:20 Josh’s own education in grace

      • Some of Josh’s biggest critics came because he has been open, publicly and consistently, about his struggle with pornography and nicotine, neither of which has reared its head since he last shared. He can’t take credit; the Lord keeps it from him. He has shared the pain it caused his family and the harm to his marriage; he thought he would lose it.
      • He knows these stories don’t elevate people’s impression of him, and some have left the church thinking it disqualifies him. But he asks how many have decided to go to war against a sin since joining this church, and gives praise to God for the stories of men and women who have. By opening up about his failures, others have been freed to open up about theirs, and Christ and the church have loved them through it.

    4. 30:05 Discernment, and no lower-tier testimonies

      • Discernment is crucial when sharing a testimony. Josh gives kudos to a woman who noticed his son nearby before church and exercised discernment, and recalls a man who shared his story in great detail in front of his kids. If kids are near, it is okay to say you’ve been down some dark roads and the Lord brought you out; spare the gory details.
      • Don’t compare testimonies, either. Some consider theirs weak because they never had a big period of rebellion. But growing up in church, around churched people, and never falling away is an amazing testimony; you don’t need to overcome a heroin addiction to have one. The truly buried testimony belongs to someone who struggles to believe they are forgiven.

    5. 31:56 Agape and phileo

      Scripture: John 15:13

      • In the Greek, Jesus and Peter use different words for love. Jesus uses agape, a giving, sacrificial love; John 15 defines it: greater love has no one than this, to lay down one’s life for his friends. “Peter, will you lay down your life for me?” Peter answers with phileo: “We’re buds, Jesus.” It is like Kristen to Josh: “I love you.” “I like you too, man.”
      • Before getting out the Peter-bashing hammer, remember this is humble Peter. He remembers overestimating himself at the Last Supper, and now he is eating breakfast next to the guys he said he was better than. So he plays it safe.

    6. 33:43 Be honest about where you’re at

      Scripture: Matthew 21

      • It is better to be honest and say you aren’t sure you’re all in than to pretend. In the parable of the two sons in Matthew 21, one said he wouldn’t, then did; the other said he would, then never did. Jesus gave credit to the one who was honest and changed his mind.
      • Be real; the Lord is patient and will help you. Talk is cheap. The church is full of mouth warriors; the Lord is looking for action heroes who mean what they say and say what they mean.
      • Peter wouldn’t claim sacrificial love, but he was waiting for his chance to show it: “Stop asking me to tell you; give me an opportunity to show you.” And he did, preaching at Pentecost, suffering for Christ, and, when Nero blamed Christians for the fire, according to church tradition asking to be crucified upside down, counting himself unworthy to die the same way Jesus did.

  8. 35:26 Verse 18: you’ll get your chance

    Scripture: John 21:18–19

    • Jesus tells Peter that when he was younger he dressed himself and went where he wanted, but when he is old he will stretch out his hands and someone else will lead him where he doesn’t want to go, indicating the death by which Peter would glorify God. Then: “Follow me.”
    • Josh loves Jesus’ ministry to Peter. When Peter was arrogant and boastful, trying to be the greatest disciple of all time, Jesus predicted his denial. When Peter is humble and underconfident, Jesus tells him he will one day have the courage to die for him, the courage he thought he had before. In other words, “You’ll get your chance, Peter.”

    1. 36:24 Three lessons from the master

      • Peter learned that his influence doesn’t come from his strength but from his surrender; that no one should lead others unless they love the Lord; and that the evidence of true love is obedience.

  9. 36:53 How do you know if you really love God?

    • Josh gives several ways to tell.

    1. 36:56 You’ll delight in him

      Scripture: 1 John 4:19

      • We love because he first loved us; someone who loves God meditates on his love and falls more in love with him. You enjoy fellowship and talk to him; a vibrant prayer life always shows up in a loving relationship. If Josh said he loved his bride but never talked to her, or called her his best friend but never went to her in crisis, you would call him a liar.
      • You spend time in his word, not because Josh makes you feel bad, but because you long to; that longing is from God. You want him to speak into your life and direct your hard decisions.

    2. 37:59 You’ll look forward to worshiping with your church

      • No one has to drag you out of bed or coax you in from the lobby by the second song; you feel off when you miss it. Your worship isn’t just Sunday; Monday morning you wake up still singing, opening his word, and thinking about his commands. Got cut from the choir? Sing in the shower where the Lord can still enjoy it.

    3. 38:55 You’ll invest in his church

      • You give financially to the mission of your church home, not grudgingly but cheerfully. Giving is worship as much as praying and singing are.

    4. 39:11 You’ll love what he loves

      Scripture: Psalm 119:97

      • Psalm 119 declares, “Oh, how I love your law; I meditate on it all day long.” A person who loves God carries his word in their head and heart, not just their hands, and seeks to know and memorize it.
      • You will also love his church. The line “I love the Lord but I can’t stand Christians” doesn’t work. If you love the Lord, you love his people however messed up they are: you stick it out, reconcile over disagreements, accept church discipline (which goes both ways), don’t run to the hills, and restore those who fall, as Jesus restored Peter.
      • You will love believers and unbelievers, and this week you can love unbelievers by inviting them to church.

    5. 40:41 You’ll hate what he hates

      Scripture: Proverbs 6:16–19 Psalm 97:10

      • Someone recently told Josh he shouldn’t use the word hate when preaching. Says who? God hates pride, lies, hands that shed innocent blood, schemers of wickedness, feet that rush into evil, and those who stir up dissension among brothers. Psalm 97 says let those who love the Lord hate evil.

    6. 41:15 You’ll long for his return

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 4:8

      • There is a crown of righteousness in store, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will award on that day, not only to Paul but to all who have longed for his appearing.

    7. 41:33 You’ll obey his commands

      Scripture: John 14:21 Daniel 9:4 John 14:15 1 John 5:3

      • Jesus says whoever has his commands and keeps them is the one who loves him. Daniel, pouring out his heart in prayer, confesses to the great and awesome God who keeps his covenant of love with those who love him and keep his commandments. John echoes Jesus: if you love me, you will keep my commandments.
      • If that feels heavy, so many commandments and so hard, John adds that his commands are not burdensome.

  10. 42:13 Sit by the fire with Jesus

    Scripture: Isaiah 6:8

    • Many need to sit by the fire with Jesus today and be restored as Peter was. Jesus restored Peter in front of everyone so he would have credibility to help lead the early church, and many need a public restoration today. The Lord has kingdom work set aside for you, but far more important is that you recall where you failed and ask his forgiveness.
    • Many have stopped doing it his way and fallen in love with their old ways. Don’t stay on that boat. Tell the Lord you’re ready to change, admit where you were wrong, say you want to do it his way, and echo the prophet Isaiah: “Here am I. Send me.”

    1. 43:11 Get out of the boat: a public restoration

      • Some come in heavy, some light. Josh asks believers carrying a nagging thing, floating along, to get out of the boat and be restored publicly, as Peter was. Anyone who knows there is something to turn away from and give to the Lord is asked to stand, and Josh stands first. We cannot sin better than God can forgive.
      • Turning from sin to faith in God is an amazing act of faith, and the church is meant to recognize it. Josh asks those around each person standing to gather, lay hands on them, and speak hope: “I know what it’s like to feel like I can’t be used, like I have no future, like I’m too far gone.” Many need the church’s help to catch them.

    2. 46:08 Prayer: we swim to you

      • Josh prays that, in a sheer act of faith, they jump out of the boats of stubbornness, pride, sin, and self-righteousness and swim to the Lord, because what they have isn’t working and isn’t enough; they need his righteousness. He thanks God for every heart standing up with Peter publicly to turn something over to Jesus, and for communion, which remembers that they are covered by his blood, it is sufficient, and they are safe, saved, and his.
