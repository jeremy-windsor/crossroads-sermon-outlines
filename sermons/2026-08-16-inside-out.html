Detailed chronological outline

The outline begins with communion, follows the sermon’s verse-by-verse movements through Psalm 51:6–9, and preserves the closing application and prayer. Scripture tags jump to the verified reference table.

  1. 00:36 The Lord’s table calls believers to true self-examination

    • Josh opens from Crossroads’ weekly practice of communion and says no sermon has ministered to him as consistently as the Lord’s table.
    • Holding the signs of Christ’s body and blood leaves nowhere to hide and demands true confession before communion.

    1. 00:36 Communion exposes the worshiper

      • The Lord’s table confronts the communicant with Christ’s sacrifice and requires confession rather than concealment.

    2. 01:10 Paul tells Christians to examine themselves

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:27-32

      • Josh reads Paul’s warning that believers should examine themselves before eating and drinking, discern Christ’s body, and avoid bringing judgment on themselves.
      • He stresses that Christians—not merely unbelievers—are under the microscope in the passage and may approach the meal in an unworthy manner.

    3. 02:06 Unworthy and worthy approaches to communion

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:27-32

      • Going through the motions, treating the ceremony itself as saving, harboring bitterness, unforgiveness, hatred, criticism, or refusing repentance are presented as unworthy approaches.
      • The worthy approach is worshipful attention to the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit and genuine love for God and fellow believers; otherwise, Josh says, let the meal pass.
      • Celebrating the cross that removed sin while carrying unconfessed sin is hypocrisy, and Paul’s warning is described as fatherly discipline.

    4. 03:33 Table manners and the call to wash before the table

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 11:27-32 Psalm 51:1-5

      • A mother correcting ordinary table manners illustrates that every table has a right and wrong approach.
      • The familiar question “Did you wash your hands?” becomes the bridge from Paul’s command to examine oneself to walking through Psalm 51.

    5. 04:19 Why preaching helps the preacher and the church

      Scripture: Psalm 51:1-5 Mark 10:45 Hebrews 10:25

      • Preaching first forces Josh to examine his own wounds before asking the congregation to examine theirs; unlike the perfect Jesus, he must address his own imperfections before helping others.
      • He also values preaching because the Spirit can use any text, illustration, or statement in an unexpected listener’s life.
      • Two recent testimonies of heart and life change reinforce the value of gathering in person rather than forsaking congregational life.

    6. 06:20 The series slows down at Psalm 51:6

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6

      • After a thirteen-week passage through Hebrews, this series has six weeks for nineteen verses.
      • The riding-mower turtle and rabbit settings illustrate the deliberate “turtle mode” pace before verse 6 is read.

  2. 07:13 God delights in truth within, not surface cleanliness

    Scripture: Psalm 51:6

    • Psalm 51:6 reveals the contrast between God’s delight in inward truth and David’s life of falsehood.
    • The sermon moves from external washing to the heart as the source of defilement and applies that distinction to parenting.

    1. 07:13 Psalm 51:6 states God’s inward desire

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6

      • Josh reads that God delights in truth in the inward being and teaches wisdom in the secret heart.
      • He refuses to treat the verse as a mere transition and gives it sustained attention.

    2. 07:30 David moves from confession to diagnosis

      Scripture: Psalm 51:1-5 Psalm 51:6 2 Samuel 12:1-23

      • Verses 1–5 are recapped as David’s confession after Nathan confronted his adultery and murder: David knows his transgression, admits sin against God, and acknowledges sin from conception.
      • David sees that God desires truth while he has been living a lie; the problem is not that facts leaked but that truth within him stopped ruling.
      • Rather than asking only how to repair events, David keeps digging and asks what has gone wrong in him that he would act this way.

    3. 09:09 Jesus locates defilement in the heart

      Scripture: Matthew 15:1-2, 16-20

      • In Matthew 15, Pharisees object to the disciples eating without ceremonial washing, but Jesus turns attention from hands to hearts.
      • Josh quotes Jesus that what enters the mouth passes through the body, whereas evil thoughts, murder, adultery, sexual immorality, theft, false testimony, and slander come from the heart and defile a person.
      • A story about Josh’s young son using the passage to resist washing his hands shows the danger of detaching a text from its context.

    4. 10:50 Parents must watch what comes out of children’s hearts

      Scripture: Matthew 15:1-2, 16-20

      • Josh addresses mothers, grandmothers, aunts, and others with influence over children, affirming their extraordinary influence over the next generation.
      • Careful monitoring of food, sugar, allergies, snacks, water, and vitamins is contrasted with neglect of anger, entitlement, disrespect, dishonesty, cruelty, and foolishness.
      • The church supports parents, but Josh assigns parents responsibility for training children in the Lord.
      • The goal is not merely public behavior, achievement, scholarships, or a career; a well-mannered, successful child without a new heart may still reject Jesus.

    5. 12:37 Lois and Eunice model sincere faith and Scripture in the home

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 1:5 2 Timothy 3:15

      • Paul’s words about Timothy are used to show that sincere faith lived first in his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice before it was evident in his ministry.
      • Timothy knew the sacred Scriptures from childhood, and those Scriptures could make him wise for salvation through faith in Christ.
      • Lois and Eunice taught more than worldly success; they taught truth that led Timothy to Jesus.

    6. 13:07 Parents are asked to examine the sincerity children see

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 1:5 2 Timothy 3:15

      • Mothers and grandmothers are asked whether children see sincere rather than flawless faith in them.
      • Concrete tests include confessing wrongs, owning mistakes, opening Scripture, seeking wisdom, praying together, and praying for grace, forgiveness, and wisdom rather than only safety, success, grades, and food.
      • They are asked whether loving Jesus visibly matters more than teams, scholarships, or an impressive life.
      • The answer is not to make Bible reading one more item on a chore chart.

    7. 14:18 The problem is beneath behavior, at the root

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6 Matthew 15:1-2, 16-20

      • Rules alone cannot repair what is wrong because the problem lies below the surface.
      • David does not merely ask for rotten fruit to be removed; he asks God to address the root producing it.
      • What repeatedly comes out reveals what lives within, and God seeks inward truth and secret-heart wisdom rather than an externally clean appearance.

    8. 14:55 God’s commands are good, but Psalm 51 names his delight

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6

      • Josh contrasts inward truth with rule-keeping, appearance management, behavior control, prohibitions, reputation protection, and avoidance of visible consequences.
      • He affirms that God’s commands are good and glorious, while insisting that believers ask not only what God forbids but what he delights in.
      • Psalm 51 answers that God delights in truth.

  3. 15:40 David’s false world exposes the need for inward truth

    Scripture: Psalm 51:6

    • David’s failure is traced beyond lust to the false narrative built to hide it.
    • The same inward-truth demand is applied to hypocrisy, image management, secondhand faith, and personal confession.

    1. 15:40 Psalm 15 joins dwelling with God to truth from the heart

      Scripture: Psalm 15:1-2

      • Josh quotes David’s question about who may dwell in God’s sanctuary and the answer that such people speak truth from their hearts.

    2. 16:04 Lust was the trailer; falsehood was the movie

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 11:2-27

      • The audience chooses whether lust or falsehood best describes David’s sin with Bathsheba.
      • Josh says lust initiated the episode, but falsehood became the larger story, and he assigns 2 Samuel 11 as homework.
      • David sees Bathsheba, takes her, learns she is pregnant, and begins constructing a narrative to conceal the truth.

    3. 16:34 Narrative-building recruits others and should be resisted

      Scripture: Proverbs 18:17

      • A side application warns that quickly building a narrative about another person requires talking to someone else about that person.
      • Proverbs says the first case sounds right until it is examined by another; Josh counsels pausing, praying, and waiting rather than joining the narrative.
      • Quick narrative-building is associated with something being covered.

    4. 17:07 David’s cover-up becomes murder and public spin

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 11:2-27

      • David brings Uriah home to sleep with Bathsheba, gets him drunk when he refuses, and finally sends him back carrying the order that brings his death.
      • After Uriah dies, David takes Bathsheba as his wife, allowing the public story to look like a gracious king caring for a fallen soldier’s widow.
      • The public spin looks noble, but the deed displeases the Lord; David lives inside the manufactured false world for about a year.

    5. 18:30 God seeks actual inward truth, not truth-telling as performance

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6

      • God is not after advantageous truth-telling alone but truth in the inward parts: the unseen and unsaid realities.
      • A polished bucket cannot fix a poisoned well, and religious appearance cannot cover inward corruption before God.
      • The demand is for true repentance and actual faith, not their shadow, picture, sound, or vocabulary.
      • Hypocrisy may deceive a church, family, friends, or even the hypocrite, but it never deceives God.

    6. 19:27 Image management fragments the self

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6

      • Modern image management produces public, work, church, social, social-media, and marriage selves alongside a hidden raw self.
      • Josh identifies that hidden self as the place where God wants to begin working.
      • “Wisdom in the secret heart” means truth must reach the being, not remain only in the brain.

    7. 20:02 Knowing doctrine is not the same as being conquered by truth

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6

      • A person may know doctrines of grace without being conquered by grace, and accurate theology may coexist with accusation and harshness.
      • One may explain the cross without kneeling before it or defend Christ’s character without delighting in him.
      • Doctrine is precious because it identifies God truly, but it is not a hiding place; knowing the language of repentance is useless without repenting.

    8. 21:21 Secondhand Christianity cannot save

      • People may borrow testimonies, songs, phrases, joys, and sorrows from other Christians and sound Christian without personally knowing those realities.
      • Words and Christian-sounding language do not save or substitute for nearness to Christ.
      • Josh calls for personally confessing sin, fleeing to Christ’s cleansing blood, loving Jesus, talking and walking with him, and being known by him.

    9. 22:18 The test is whether truth has reached and changed the heart

      Scripture: James 2:19

      • The demons’ knowledge and trembling show that mere factual recognition is not enough.
      • Josh asks whether truth has reached the inward parts, humbled the hearer, changed anything, and made Jesus precious.
      • Friends, family, a pastor, oneself may be fooled; God searches the heart and is not interested in the successfully presented version of a person.

    10. 23:00 A testimony displays grace through confessed ugliness

      • A believer’s recent testimony encouraged Josh because public honesty about Josh’s own lies, pride, selfishness, and addictions had helped another believer return to the Lord.
      • He praises God for taking truths people hide and bury, cleansing them, and making their lives public displays of grace.
      • Amazing grace means freedom to stop pretending, put down the mask, and stop defending an image because God already knows everything and sent his Son to save.

    11. 24:36 God enters the place where the mask is managed

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6

      • Hearers are asked where they perform, manage, polish, or say “I’m fine” when they should admit wretchedness.
      • David’s confidence is that God enters the hidden place and works exactly where humanity’s fall has corrupted it.
      • The transition is made from teaching right and wrong to God teaching wisdom.

  4. 25:13 Wisdom is truth possessed and lived

    Scripture: Psalm 51:6

    • Truth names reality; wisdom rightly lives according to it.
    • Scripture written outside a person must be inscribed by the Spirit upon human hearts so believers become living letters.

    1. 25:13 Truth and wisdom are distinguished

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6

      • Knowing what is right does not guarantee doing it; both truth and wisdom are needed.
      • Truth is defined as knowledge of reality, while wisdom is rightly living according to that truth.

    2. 25:38 Wisdom puts true claims into action

      • Truth says sin destroys; wisdom runs from it.
      • Truth informs people what is real; wisdom navigates that reality.
      • Truth says the resurrected Christ is enough; wisdom builds a life on him.
      • Wisdom is summarized as truth in action and truth lived out.

    3. 26:14 Romans names reality; Jesus commands abiding life

      Scripture: Romans 6:23 John 15:5

      • Romans states that sin’s wages are death and God’s gift is eternal life in Christ Jesus.
      • Jesus’ vine-and-branches saying shows wisdom as abiding, staying with him, speaking to him, and asking from him because apart from him nothing can be done.

    4. 26:58 Truth on a scroll becomes truth written on a person

      Scripture: John 21:25

      • Josh contrasts truth written on a scroll for reading with wisdom as God writing truth on a person for others to read.
      • John’s closing statement that the world could not contain all the books about Jesus’ works is proposed as a picture that may remain open as Christ continues writing his story in changed lives.

    5. 27:45 Believers are letters from Christ written on hearts

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 3:3 Exodus 20:13-14, 17

      • Paul’s words to Corinth identify believers as Christ’s letter, written not with ink but by the living God’s Spirit, not on stone tablets but human hearts.
      • Truth may be carved in stone, memorized, recited, and defended while the soul remains unchanged.
      • David knew the commands against adultery, murder, and coveting his neighbor’s wife, but knowledge outside him did not keep him from violating them.

    6. 28:12 David asks that known truth become lived wisdom

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6 2 Corinthians 3:3

      • David’s problem was not a failure by God to write truth but David’s failure to live what was written.
      • Josh voices David’s plea for God to take external truth and inscribe it on his heart until he becomes a living letter of grace.

    7. 28:52 Changed lives become living volumes of Christ’s work

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 3:3

      • Every cleansed sinner, straightened heart, and restored life becomes another living declaration of what the Lord has done.
      • The congregation is invited to stand as testimony that Jesus has changed them and they are not returning to what they were.
      • The visible response is named as lives washed and cleansed by Christ.

  5. 29:40 “Purge me with hyssop”: God cleanses from the inside out

    Scripture: Psalm 51:7

    • Psalm 51:7 introduces hyssop as a biblical sign of purification and blood.
    • The sermon traces hyssop from Passover and purification rites to Jesus’ crucifixion, then unfolds David’s confidence that God can make him truly clean.

    1. 29:40 David asks to be purged and washed beyond snow

      Scripture: Psalm 51:7

      • Josh reads David’s request to be purged with hyssop and be clean, washed and made whiter than snow.

    2. 29:51 Hyssop serves purification in Exodus, Leviticus, and Numbers

      Scripture: Exodus 12:22 Leviticus 14:4-7 Numbers 19:6, 17-18

      • In Exodus 12, hyssop carries Passover blood to the doorposts.
      • In Leviticus 14, the priest uses hyssop in sprinkling the person being cleansed from leprosy.
      • In Numbers 19, cedarwood, hyssop, and scarlet wool are thrown into the red heifer’s fire; the ashes are mixed with fresh water, and hyssop is dipped into the cleansing water to sprinkle a tent, its furnishings, and persons defiled by contact with death.

    3. 30:36 David identifies with captivity, exclusion, disease, and death

      Scripture: Psalm 51:7 Exodus 12:22 Leviticus 14:4-7 Numbers 19:6, 17-18

      • By asking for hyssop, David identifies himself with captives, the outcast leper, and people rendered unclean through disease or death.

    4. 30:56 Hyssop appears at Calvary

      Scripture: John 19:29-30

      • Josh explicitly turns to John 19:29 and reads of wine vinegar on a sponge lifted to Jesus on a hyssop stalk.
      • He continues through Jesus’ declaration “It is finished,” his bowed head, and the giving up of his spirit.

    5. 31:26 The plant traces cleansing history to the cross

      Scripture: Exodus 12:22 Leviticus 14:4-7 Numbers 19:6, 17-18 John 19:29-30

      • The congregation is told it stands on holy ground as hyssop is traced from Egypt’s doorposts and the tabernacle to the crucifixion outside Jerusalem.
      • There the Son of God gives himself to rescue his people from sin and death.
      • Josh asks the congregation to see and smell the plant and let it preach to their souls in anticipation of a later embodied worship response.

    6. 31:57 The outcast is restored, and David expects appointed purification

      Scripture: Psalm 51:7 Leviticus 14:4-7

      • The hearer is asked to imagine an excluded leper rejoining the assembly and singing again, welcomed among God’s people through Jesus.
      • David is described as confident that God’s appointed provision will finish purification for his people.
      • His language is not “I might be clean” but “I shall be clean,” trusting God’s power despite his own filth.

    7. 32:51 God’s grace can make cleaner than fresh snow

      Scripture: Psalm 51:7

      • Fresh snow is David’s brightest imaginable comparison, yet he asks for a cleansing even whiter than snow.
      • Snow can cover a filthy field without changing the field beneath it; God does better by working from the inside out.

    8. 33:12 God’s purity is inward and enduring

      Scripture: Psalm 51:7

      • Snow gathers dirt and melts under heat, but God can give an enduring purity that survives judgment.
      • David asks not merely to appear clean but actually to be clean where only God sees.
      • The requested cleansing reaches memory, motives, imagination, nightmares, and every inward place touched by sin.

    9. 33:53 The gospel removes the stain rather than concealing it

      Scripture: 1 John 1:7

      • The blood of Jesus is said to wash away sin rather than merely hide its stain.
      • Memories, consequences, and scars may remain, but when God cleanses the sinner, the stain does not remain.

    10. 34:30 The accuser points backward; faith points to the cross

      Scripture: 1 John 1:7

      • The accuser says “Look at what you’ve done” and calls the person filthy, while faith answers “Look at what the Lord has done.”
      • The sermon quotes that Jesus’ blood cleanses from all sin and says Jesus names his people friend, brother, sister, and co-heir rather than by their worst moment.

    11. 35:01 Old labels no longer name the whole story

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:11

      • Adulterer, drunk, liar, addict, hypocrite, failure, divorced, abandoned, damaged, and dirty may describe a chapter but do not name the book of a person washed by Jesus.
      • Paul’s “such were some of you” is followed by washed, sanctified, and justified in the Lord Jesus’ name.
      • Grace does not deny the old life; it makes that life no longer the truest fact about the believer.

    12. 35:54 The blood, not shame or the past, names the cleansed person

      Scripture: Psalm 51:7 John 19:29-30 Isaiah 1:18

      • Hearers are told to stop letting the accuser introduce them and to reject the names supplied by shame, failure, the past, or their own former self-description.
      • They are named washed, forgiven, justified, clean, and white as snow by Jesus’ blood.
      • Isaiah’s scarlet-to-snow promise is joined to David’s faith and to Jesus’ completed work at Calvary, sealed in the sermon’s image by hyssop touching Jesus’ lips.

  6. 36:40 Grace restores joy while discipline rescues

    Scripture: Psalm 51:8

    • Grace is pictured as a wave that takes guilt away while bringing joy.
    • Psalm 51:8 asks not for pain or consequences to vanish but for broken bones to rejoice.

    1. 36:40 Grace brings an undeserved gift as it removes guilt

      • Josh calls grace a wave that always washes something up while washing something away.
      • Grace removes guilt and restores joy.

    2. 37:05 Psalm 51:8 asks to hear joy and gladness

      Scripture: Psalm 51:8

      • Josh reads David’s request to hear joy and gladness and for the bones God has broken to rejoice.
      • Sin promised pleasure but produced misery, so David reaches toward restored joy.

    3. 37:20 David seeks help carrying memories of the harm he caused

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 11:2-27 Psalm 51:8

      • Josh voices David asking to hear something other than the cries he caused and to see something other than the death order sent through Uriah to Joab.
      • The memory remains, but David asks God for help carrying it.
      • The accusing voice may pester people not only about deeds but even about sinful thoughts.

    4. 38:22 Believers may ask God to restore peace and joy

      Scripture: 2 Samuel 12:1-23 2 Samuel 11:2-27 Psalm 51:8

      • David believes God can silence accusing noise and fill him with joy, and Josh tells believers they can pray the same way and ask for peace.
      • Sin kills joy and overpromises while underdelivering; Bathsheba’s promised happiness yielded adultery, anxiety, fear, lies, manipulation, murder, silence, depression, and a dead child.

    5. 38:57 Psalm 32 and Psalm 51 connect wasted and rejoicing bones

      Scripture: Psalm 51:8 Psalm 32:3 Hebrews 12:6

      • Psalm 32 says David’s bones wasted away while he kept silent; Psalm 51 asks that the bones God broke rejoice.
      • Josh states twice that God wounded David to rescue David.
      • Cultural praise for military toughness and hardship is contrasted with discomfort at the biblical claim that God disciplines those he loves.

    6. 39:58 A loving Father intervenes against self-destruction

      Scripture: Hebrews 12:6

      • A loving God comforts people in pain but does not watch passively while his children destroy themselves.
      • As a good Father, he steps in even when his children do not want the intervention.

    7. 40:17 Pray for rejoicing through pain, not merely removed consequences

      Scripture: Psalm 51:8

      • Those who need joy are told to pray during the week for renewed joy and gladness.
      • David does not ask for consequences or even brokenness to be removed; he asks for joy and gladness through the pain.
      • The image concludes with broken bones learning to dance.

  7. 41:04 What David cannot hide, God can blot out

    Scripture: Psalm 51:9

    • Psalm 51:9 reverses David’s failed concealment: unable to hide sin from God, David asks God to hide his face from it.
    • Confession receives mercy because Christ cancels the condemning record at the cross.

    1. 41:04 David asks God to hide his face and blot out sin

      Scripture: Psalm 51:9

      • Josh reads the request for God to hide his face from David’s sins and blot out all his iniquities.
      • For more than a year David tried and failed to hide sin from God; now he asks God to hide it from himself.
      • David’s attempted covering failed, while God’s covering works.

    2. 41:41 God exposes concealment and covers confession

      Scripture: Psalm 51:9 Proverbs 28:13-14

      • The sermon states the wisdom as a contrast: what people conceal, God exposes in love; what they confess, God covers in love.

    3. 41:58 Proverbs locates mercy at confession and renunciation

      Scripture: Proverbs 28:13-14

      • Josh announces “Proverbs 28:3,” but the words he reads are Proverbs 28:13–14.
      • Those who conceal sins do not prosper; those who confess and renounce them find mercy.
      • The reading continues that those who tremble before God are blessed, while hard hearts fall into trouble.

    4. 42:11 Blotting is pictured as annihilating the legal record

      Scripture: Psalm 51:1-5 Psalm 51:9 Colossians 2:13-14

      • Josh notes that Psalm 51:1 and 51:9 both use “blot,” which he says can be rendered “annihilate,” and describes David as wanting the record gone.
      • Colossians 2 is read: God makes the dead alive with Christ, forgives sins, cancels the legal indebtedness that condemned them, takes it away, and nails it to the cross.

    5. 42:45 Jesus’ finished work answers David’s prayer

      Scripture: John 19:29-30 Colossians 2:13-14

      • David’s prayer is paraphrased as “Annihilate my sin record.”
      • Jesus’ last-breath declaration from the cross is invoked as finished, done, dead, dealt with, and paid.

  8. 43:06 God supplies in Christ what he delights in

    • The standards of inward truth, wisdom, purity, and righteousness expose what sinners cannot produce.
    • Psalm 51 restores hope because God produces what he delights in through his Son.

    1. 43:06 God’s desires expose human inability

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6 Psalm 51:7

      • People lack the inward truth God delights in and cannot manufacture secret-heart wisdom.
      • They cannot cleanse themselves into the purity God requires and stand guilty before his demand for righteousness.
      • These requirements appear hopeless until Psalm 51 shows that God produces what he delights in.

    2. 43:47 Christianity presents God’s standard and God’s Son

      • The message is not “Here is God’s standard; try harder,” but “Here is God’s standard, and here is God’s Son.”

    3. 44:02 Jesus embodies everything Psalm 51 says God desires

      Scripture: Psalm 51:1-5 Psalm 51:6 Psalm 51:7

      • Jesus is named as truth, perfect wisdom, perfect purity, and perfect obedience, without hypocrisy, deceit, hiding, pride, or selfishness.
      • From birth Jesus is everything in which God delighted, while human beings are sinners from birth.

    4. 44:20 The great exchange gives peace with God

      Scripture: Colossians 2:13-14 Romans 5:1

      • The perfect God-man takes shame, guilt, sin, debt, past, and crimes upon himself and gives peace with God in exchange.
      • David prays for what Jesus provides.

    5. 44:53 God’s hiding and blotting prevent judgment from falling on the sinner

      Scripture: Psalm 51:9 Colossians 2:13-14 Romans 5:1

      • Josh says that if God does not hide his face from sin, he must hide his face from the sinner; if he does not annihilate sins, he must annihilate sinners.
      • The congregation praises God for making a way and confesses peace with God through the Lord Jesus Christ.

    6. 45:19 The risen Christ reigns and will return

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 15:55 Ephesians 1:20-22 Romans 8:34

      • The Savior is not on the cross or in the tomb: the stone is rolled away, death defeated, the grave conquered, and Jesus alive.
      • Josh quotes the taunt asking death where its sting and victory are.
      • Jesus is described as ascended, seated at the Father’s right hand, ruling, reigning, and interceding, and as the king who will come again.
      • Josh anticipates dancing when he reaches God’s holy mountain and sees the King.

    7. 46:10 Closing calls to ask boldly for inward renewal

      Scripture: Psalm 51:6 Psalm 51:7 Psalm 51:8 Psalm 51:9 Psalm 51:10, 12

      • Until Christ returns, believers are called to ask him to teach, purge, wash, restore joy amid chaos, and hide his face from remembered sin.
      • The week’s bold prayer is for God to annihilate anything within that he does not delight in, prayed with readiness to release it.
      • The next sermon will continue David’s requests to create, restore, and uphold.
      • Hearers are urged to ask God to go as deep as needed, make them clean and new in the secret heart, and renew them.

    8. 47:36 Final prayer for cleansing and confident freedom

      Scripture: Psalm 51:7 Exodus 12:22 Leviticus 14:4-7 Numbers 19:6, 17-18 John 19:29-30

      • Josh prays as one who knows both hiding and crying out, especially for those afraid of what is truly inside them or unable to look at themselves.
      • He asks the Spirit to flood them with the truth that God can wash it away so they may walk under salvation in freedom and confidence, saying “I shall be clean.”
      • As the congregation smells, holds, and sees hyssop, sings, and comes around the table, he asks that the Old-to-New-Testament cleansing connection minister to them and remind them what Christ has done.
      • The prayer closes by asking that they call on God in need and walk with him every day.
