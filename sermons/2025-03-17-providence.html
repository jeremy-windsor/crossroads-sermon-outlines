Overview

  1. 01:14 The heroes who built up your faith

    • Josh asks the church to call to mind the Christians who have had the greatest impact on their walk with Christ: celebrities, politicians, war heroes, athletes, or a less flashy list of Mom, Dad, Grandma, Grandpa. He was raised in a family of faith, flawed people who are a mess, but a mess that loves God and others.
    • Outside his family he thinks of Eric Liddell, the 1920s Olympic gold medalist who refused to run on Sundays. Given the choice, Josh would trade the 2020s for the 1920s to pastor: a century ago athletes ditched their sport for church; now we ditch church for the sport.
    • Reggie White, the Packers’ “Minister of Defense,” was a pastor and a Super Bowl champion. Today there is Tim Tebow (Josh was once accused of flexing while preaching; it is involuntary), Steph Curry, Tony Dungy, and Kurt Warner.
    • Pastoral advice: Christian athletes often float side to side with culture instead of standing against it. Just because a Christian is visible doesn’t mean they are reliable. Test everything.

  2. 04:29 Presidents and Amelia Earhart

    • Among politicians Josh thinks of George Washington and Jimmy Carter, who answered what happens when a Sunday school teacher becomes president. JFK, the first Catholic president, said, “Ask not what your country can do for you, but what you can do for your country.” Josh’s challenge to the modern American church: ask not what your church can do for you, but what you can do for your church.
    • From left field: Amelia Earhart, the first woman to fly across the Atlantic. Josh isn’t sure where she stood with the Lord; her impact on him was social, cultural, and leadership. She didn’t care what the men of her generation said; she flew up and grabbed her dreams.
    • To the teenage girls in the room, and the way he speaks to his almost-19-year-old daughter: why care so much about how the world views you? If you have a dream, go get it. Don’t waste energy convincing people you are capable; just go do it.

  3. 06:27 Your top biblical heroes

    • Josh asks for one hero from the New Testament, not counting Jesus, and one from the Old. He polls the room: Mary, Peter, John, James, and Stephen (Josh was alone on that one); then Moses, David, Joshua, Elijah (Kirby), Daniel, and Eve, which he was just kidding about.

  4. 08:01 Ezra: he knew, believed, and obeyed God’s word

    Scripture: Psalm 119

    • Did anyone have Ezra? Of course not. He isn’t well known, but he was a faithful and courageous man of God: a priest, a scribe, and a gifted leader whose name means “help,” and he dedicated his life to serving God and helping God’s people. Tradition says he wrote most of 1 and 2 Chronicles, Ezra, Nehemiah, and Psalm 119, and helped lead a council of about 120 men who compiled the Old Testament canon.
    • What Josh loves most is that Ezra not only knew God’s word but believed and obeyed it, with incredible reverence. God’s word humbled him and drove him to obedience and worship, unlike Christians who study the word to satisfy curiosity and boast in head knowledge. The word is meant to humble us, not puff us up.

  5. 09:47 The second Exodus

    Scripture: Nehemiah

    • Ezra is also known as the second Exodus. In the first, Israel grew into a massive people in Egypt, Pharaoh oppressed them, they cried out to God, and Moses, reluctantly, led them out to the promised land. Later Babylon conquered Judah and exiled many Jews, and Ezra focuses on God’s promise, prophesied by Jeremiah, that they would return.
    • The second Exodus comes in three waves: first under Zerubbabel, a descendant of David and grandson of Judah’s last king before the exile; then Ezra 58 years later; then the third wave in Nehemiah, the subject of a series a couple of years ago.
    • For anyone who thinks God offers believers a cushy, comfortable life, tell that to Zerubbabel, Ezra, and Nehemiah. Many must have shaken their heads, but God’s hand is all over the second Exodus.

  6. 11:25 God calls us to hard things

    Scripture: Romans 5:3–5 Romans 8:31

    • Doing easy stuff is easy, and it polishes our ego: I brushed my teeth, made my bed (some of you are animals), made coffee, got to church on time, read my Bible. But we often feel betrayed when God asks us to do hard things, and he calls us to hard things to grow us. Paul says suffering produces perseverance, character, and hope.
    • God isn’t interested in you just sounding like Jesus or regurgitating what he said; he wants to help you be like Jesus.
    • God is calling his people home as he said he would, but rebuilding the temple and city and restoring Jewish community will take faith, courage, and sacrifice. Ezra knew God said he would do it; the Lord is leading from start to finish. We can only respond like Paul: if God is for us, who can be against us?

  7. 13:08 How the series will go, and coffee with your pastor

    • Josh may look at certain events several times through different lenses. Today he highlights God’s providence at work in two events: the release and the return.
    • The church is a healthy mix of seasoned and new believers, as long as the seasoned pour into the new instead of lording it over them. If the church stays faithful to being disciple makers, Josh believes the Lord has great years ahead. He doesn’t want to drop big theological words without explaining them, and if anyone has questions, he wants to get coffee.
    • That is part of how he wants to change the way people see the church: knowing people, being prayerful over them and available. You can get your book signed at Joel Osteen’s stadium or get coffee with Josh, and he sees a big difference. Shepherds should pray, P-R-A-Y, over their flock, not pry, P-R-Y, and not use people to get a book deal.

  8. 15:11 What is providence?

    Scripture: Colossians 1:16–17 Romans 8:28 Matthew 10:29–30

    • Providence is God’s sovereign, purposeful control over all things, past, present, and future. Nothing happens by accident; everything occurs according to his perfect wisdom and plan.
    • Start with Colossians 1: in him all things were created, not some things, all things; he is before all things, and in him all things hold together. Or Romans 8: in all things God works for the good of those who love him.
    • Josh rejects coincidence and thinks luck is silly. Everything happens under God’s providential rule, even the small stuff: not one sparrow falls outside the Father’s care, and the hairs of your head are numbered.

    1. 16:36 Providence in suffering

      Scripture: Job 1:21 Genesis 50:20

      • If God is behind the joyful stuff, he is behind the joyless stuff too. Josh is handling a Crossroads Cares need for a woman in Mayer, Arizona, who lost her son in September and learned right before the funeral that his widow was pregnant with a baby boy. She is on SSI and needs help buying tires to attend her future grandbaby’s shower.
      • He asks those who have met a need before to pray instead and leave this one for someone who has never met a need through Crossroads Cares; if it is taken, text him and he will find another way to help.
      • She was crying through her suffering, tears from her eyes and praise from her lips, preaching to Josh about God’s faithfulness. Our suffering is not meaningless or random; God uses it to refine, grow, build, and sanctify. Let Job preach: the Lord gave and the Lord has taken away; may the name of the Lord be praised. Or Joseph, sold by his family, who told his brothers, “You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good.”
      • Hard things are part of God’s plan. Christians pray for rain and get mad at the mud, pray for sunshine and get mad at the sunburn. Hard things shape us, like the potter using heat to keep the mold.

    2. 19:28 Providence in salvation

      Scripture: Ephesians 1:4–5 John 6:44 Proverbs 3:5–6

      • Salvation isn’t random either. He chose us in him before the creation of the world and predestined us for adoption. If you haven’t trusted Christ but God chose you, he will get you; you can run but you can’t hide. Or, if you prefer, he is waiting for you, doesn’t want you to perish, and is patiently presenting himself. How much more must he show you? What are you waiting for?
      • No one can come to Jesus unless the Father draws them. The Father, Son, and Spirit drew up an eternal plan to save souls before time began, and we are witnesses to it unfolding. God’s providence should lead to praise. Have you responded? Trust in the Lord with all your heart. We can have peace in trials, confidence in God’s plan, and assurance he is working all things for his glory and our ultimate good.

  9. 21:15 The release: Ezra 1:1–4

    Scripture: Ezra 1:1–4

    • Josh launches the series with the captives’ release, reading Ezra 1:1–4: in the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, to fulfill the word of the Lord spoken by Jeremiah, the Lord moved Cyrus’s heart to proclaim that the God of heaven appointed him to build a temple in Jerusalem, that God’s people may go up and build it, and that their neighbors should provide silver, gold, goods, livestock, and freewill offerings.

    1. 22:19 Three phases of destruction

      Scripture: Isaiah 6

      • About 150 years earlier Isaiah warned that Judah would be taken captive by Babylon and punished for their sins (Isaiah 6, for later), and it was fulfilled. Three phases of destruction, to be matched by three phases of reconstruction: in 605 Nebuchadnezzar took the royal family and temple treasures; in 597 he exiled 10,000, mostly skilled workers and fighters; in 586 he destroyed Jerusalem and the temple and exiled the rest, leaving only the poorest.

    2. 23:12 Cyrus’s new policy

      • Industry standard was to conquer and assimilate. That is what makes Ezra 1 and Cyrus the Great so compelling. The superpowers were punching their way to world domination, Assyrians, then Babylonians, then Persians, and Cyrus is now king of the world, lowercase k. But in 538 he signs an executive order letting the peoples of his kingdom go back home.

    3. 24:08 Isaiah named Cyrus

      Scripture: Isaiah 44:28–45:5

      • This was also prophesied by Isaiah, and Josh reads it all because it is awesome: God says of Cyrus, “He is my shepherd,” who will say of Jerusalem, “Let it be rebuilt.” The Lord takes hold of his anointed’s right hand to subdue nations and strip kings of their armor, goes before him, and summons him by name for the sake of Israel, though Cyrus does not acknowledge him.

    4. 25:23 The writing on the wall

      Scripture: Daniel 5 Judges 10 Daniel 5:25–28

      • For stripping kings of their armor, read Daniel 5: Belshazzar’s drunken feast and a hand writing on the wall. God will not be mocked.
      • Before reading the inscription, Josh is grateful that what Judges 10 describes being done to people who mispronounce things, from his Bible reading yesterday, no longer happens. Mene: God has numbered the days of your reign and brought it to an end. Tekel: you have been weighed on the scales and found wanting. Peres: your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians. Daniel interprets it, and that night Belshazzar dies and the kingdom is taken over.
      • God weaves his plan through prideful, drunk, arrogant kings.

    5. 26:53 Did Cyrus read about himself?

      Scripture: Isaiah 44 Jeremiah 25 Jeremiah 29

      • Is it a stretch to think Cyrus first learned of his edict by reading it in the scroll of Isaiah? Maybe Daniel pointed out Isaiah 44 and Jeremiah 25 and 29 and they read it together: “Welcome to church, Cyrus. Yahweh mentioned you 150 years before your birth. Want to know what he said you’d do?”

    6. 27:29 The Cyrus Cylinder

      Scripture: Daniel 5

      • There is strong archaeological evidence for Cyrus’s edict: the Cyrus Cylinder, a football-shaped baked clay cylinder inscribed in Akkadian and found in the ruins of Babylon. It confirms two things in Ezra: Cyrus took Babylon without a fight and was welcomed as a hero (anything was better than Belshazzar), and his policy was to let peoples the Babylonians captured return home and rebuild their temples. Archaeology again confirms the Bible can be trusted.
      • Many call it the first declaration of human rights, and a replica sits at the UN. Critics once said no king would conquer territory and then send everyone home, and then the cylinder was uncovered. God keeps answering questions through findings over time, and Josh wonders what discoveries people will point to in a hundred years.

  10. 29:50 Five truths from what Cyrus did

    • What Cyrus did 25 centuries ago forces us never to forget some important spiritual truths, and Josh shows five.

    1. 30:00 1. God is faithful to his word

      Scripture: Joshua 23:14

      • Jeremiah spent decades pleading with Judah’s leaders to repent and surrender to Babylon to preserve the temple and homeland. They called him a traitor, and the city and temple were leveled. Jeremiah and Daniel both confirmed the captivity would last 70 years; whatever your timeline, the predictions and fulfillments demand praise. Whether God intends to rebuke or bless, he is faithful to his word.
      • Joshua’s final words are among Josh’s favorites: you know with all your heart and soul that not one of all the good promises the Lord gave you has failed.
      1. 31:08 Why Josh raises his hands
        • Josh has been in the church his whole life and seen her dirty little secrets; he loves her, but she is a little cuckoo sometimes. One concern: he isn’t convinced our worship is always a response to God’s faithfulness, but it should be.
        • That is why he raises his hands and embraces his family during worship: “We’re right here where we need to be, under the blood of Jesus. This is where we’ve always come.” He will never stop praising or lifting his requests in prayer, because God is faithful and doesn’t know how to fail.

    2. 32:53 2. God is faithful to his covenant

      Scripture: Genesis 12 John 4:22 Romans 3:2

      • They were a disobedient, sinful, stiff-necked people, but God’s chosen people, children of the promise made to Abraham in Genesis 12. They broke the covenant, but the Lord remained faithful. Anybody grateful we are saved by faith, not works?
      • God called the Jewish nation to bless all the earth, and no one, not even his own people, can get in the way. Through them the world knows the one true God, has his word, and meets the Savior: salvation is from the Jews, they were entrusted with the very words of God, and the Messiah was a Jew. It is the most beautiful mess of all time.

    3. 34:25 3. God is in control of the nations

      Scripture: Isaiah 41:25

      • Many American Christians need to hear this. The Lord raised up Nebuchadnezzar to punish disobedient Judah, then Cyrus to defeat Babylon and establish Persia. Josh notes the Persians are credited with inventing crucifixion and the Romans perfected it: God put in place the empire that would develop the method used to execute his Son for the sins of the world.
      • Isaiah says he treads on rulers as if they were mortar, as a potter treads the clay. The living God is sovereign over all nations and can hold the most powerful rulers to his will.

    4. 35:31 4. God is more powerful than the powerful

      Scripture: Daniel 4:34–35 Exodus 9 Romans 9 Esther 2 Kings 19 Luke 2 Acts 12 2 Chronicles 20:6

      • Nebuchadnezzar tore the holy city to shreds, but the Lord brought him to his knees. In Daniel 4 he says his sanity was restored (who took it, and who returned it?) and he praised the Most High, whose dominion is eternal and whose hand no one can hold back.
      • Believer, if wars and rumors of wars have you shaking, or every election has you curled in the fetal position, stop. Look what God did with Pharaoh in Exodus 9 (or the short version, Romans 9), with Ahasuerus in Esther, Sennacherib in 2 Kings 19, Augustus in Luke 2, and Herod Agrippa in Acts 12.
      • Listen to the king who got it, Jehoshaphat: “Lord, the God of our ancestors, are you not the God who is in heaven? You rule over the kingdoms of the nations. Power and might are in your hand, and no one can withstand you.”

    5. 37:22 5. God uses who he chooses

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 1:27

      • God uses people who fail, because it is really his only choice; only one man in all history was fully dedicated to God. God chose the foolish things to shame the wise and the weak things to shame the strong. He can even use people who don’t acknowledge him; he just did it with Cyrus.
      1. 38:04 God doesn’t need Joe Rogan

        Scripture: Philippians 2:9–11

        • It drives Josh nuts when Christians go on about needing more Christian governors, senators, and presidents, or watch the Joe Rogan Experience imagining what God could do if Rogan joined the team with all his influence. God doesn’t need Joe Rogan; Joe Rogan needs God. God already exalted Jesus and gave him the name above every name, at which every knee will bow.
        • God doesn’t need your status, influence, clean record, denomination, preferences, traditions, money, attention, or even your obedience. He can do everything without you and is allowing you to join his work. You need God. If you won’t give yourself to him, he has plenty of faithful servants lined up, and he will reclaim everyone he said he would, with or without you.

  11. 40:06 The return: hearts God had moved (Ezra 1:5)

    Scripture: Ezra 1:5 Philippians 2:13

    • Josh closes with verse 5: the family heads of Judah and Benjamin, the priests and Levites, everyone whose heart God had moved, prepared to go up and build the house of the Lord. It wasn’t only Cyrus’s heart that was moved; God gave some Jews the desire to return. It sounds like Philippians 2: it is God who works in you to will and to act.

    1. 40:41 A small remnant against the crowd

      • Studies show our natural propensity to stick with the crowd; we find comfort being concealed in the masses and going with the flow. To overcome that, God had to move some hearts.
      • The remnant was few. Most exiles stayed in the comfort of Babylon, and you can’t blame them: a long journey to a destroyed home and temple, then sweat and toil to rebuild as people try to kill you. It was difficult, some would say impossible.

    2. 41:40 Too comfortable in Babylon

      Scripture: Jeremiah 29:5–7

      • When they first settled in Babylon they received Jeremiah’s letter: build houses, plant gardens, marry, increase in number, seek the peace and prosperity of the city, because you will be there 70 years. Make the most of it.
      • But by the time of Jesus over a million Jews still lived in Babylon. They got a little too comfortable; fewer than 50,000 felt led by the Holy Spirit to go back.

  12. 42:39 Have you gotten comfortable in exile?

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 5:24

    • Before communion, one question for believers: have you gotten comfortable in exile? Have you fallen in love with the world around you, or are your eyes fixed on the new heaven and new earth? Not only are you a citizen of this world or of heaven, but where would you prefer to reside?
    • Following Jesus isn’t an easy journey; it is a massive distance from enemy of God to child of God, leaving the comfort of the known for the great unknown. The rebuilding project of your life can be overwhelming and scary, but the one who calls you is faithful, and he will do it.
    • Is he calling you, trying to move your heart? Don’t ignore it. He isn’t calling so you can respond tomorrow or next week but right now. Go to the lobby, get a T-shirt, say you want to be baptized, and get in the water. What are you waiting for?

  13. 44:29 Don’t walk away

    • It must have been horrible to watch the temple destroyed, or to be born in exile knowing only the memory of a temple and God’s presence. Far more devastating would be someone here ignoring the move of God’s Spirit in their heart and walking away, because each time you walk away it gets easier. Don’t let your heart be hardened.
    • Jesus presents God’s gospel in two commands: repent and be baptized. Turn from sin, make Jesus Lord, go and sin no more, and if you do sin, God offers grace through his Son. All of this will come crumbling down, even this building, but Jesus will rebuild it all, and the only way to see that holy reconstruction is to put your faith in Jesus. Don’t run off to other things until you run to him.

  14. 46:32 Closing prayer for the young

    • Josh prays for his younger brothers and sisters in Christ in the room, remembering his own rebellious years and the youth listening in while their youth pastor is away. He asks that they would hold their faith with confidence in this culture, not swayed side to side, relying on God in the storms and seeing that even in the bad he has something good.
    • “We submit, we give our lives to you, and we say, have your way.” He prays for faith to walk out into the wilderness and say, “This seems big, but our God is bigger.”
