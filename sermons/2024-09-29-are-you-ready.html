Overview

  1. 00:50 The coolest job in the world

    • Josh is convinced he has the coolest job in the world. It isn’t always easy, but it is amazing that God chose a twisted mess like him to serve the church. Even sitting down on Tuesdays with a blank piece of paper, he feels blessed to be in that space.
    • The God of the universe, who formed you in your mother’s womb, dug the canyons and formed the mountains by his might, paused and gave you a message. All of creation was assembled around this message; the creator of all things is shouting at us through what has been made.

    1. 01:56 Without excuse: the invisible made visible

      Scripture: Romans 1:20

      • Paul says in Romans 1 that since the creation of the world God’s invisible qualities, his eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly seen, so that people are without excuse. Whether you are a Christian or not, everyone here is a witness and without excuse before hearing a single word about God.
      • That is why Josh loves his job: he gets to argue for God and show that the God you can’t see took on flesh so you could see him and hear from him. In Jesus we see God’s kindness, justice, gentleness, love for children, desire for thriving marriages and homes, desire to know us personally, power to heal, and generosity, and his hatred of hypocrisy, pride, and division, and his desire for unity and love in the body.
      • The invisible became visible and taught us about righteousness and salvation; the eternal became mortal and rose again. He promised that those who love him will hear his voice and have eternal life. God’s plan to redeem humanity and raise dead sinners to life is a man, and his name is Jesus, and Josh wants them to see him as he does.

  2. 04:17 Check everything with the word of God

    • Exercise caution. There is no shortage of people declaring who Jesus is, but far too many, more and more even in the church, venture away from Scripture to describe him. Check everything you hear with the word of God, even Josh; he gives permission to be a skeptic of him. Go home and test what he said against the word.
    • Why get your information about God from a third party? Take it from him. Become a student of the word and a disciple of Jesus; listen to Christ teach as if he is teaching you.

  3. 05:21 An invisible friend and an invisible enemy

    • Just as there is an unseen creator who worked to save your soul, there is an unseen destroyer who seeks to devour it: an invisible friend and an invisible enemy. As God’s qualities are seen in what he made, the enemy’s qualities are seen in the product of his work. Wherever truth has a chance to sink roots, the enemy isn’t far behind, trying to uproot it before it grows.

  4. 06:00 The parable of the sower

    Scripture: Mark 4:3–8

    • Josh is drawn to the parable of the sower; as a pastor he clings to it because it is so important to get right that not everyone who hears will produce fruit from a changed life. The seed is God’s word, the sower is the servant faithfully sharing it, and the soil is the heart that encounters the truth about God.
    • He reads it: seed on the path eaten by birds, seed on rocky places that sprang up and withered for lack of root, seed among thorns that choked it, and seed on good soil that produced a crop thirty, sixty, a hundred times. It is timeless: there are still four kinds of hearts that respond to God’s message four ways.

    1. 07:36 The hard heart

      • Some have a hard heart that resists the word, an easy target because Satan swoops in and snatches it away. The path is hard because it has been trampled by influences and opinions: this friend, this media outlet, this coworker, this second cousin. You make room for everyone, and there is no room for Jesus.
      • The only way to let Jesus get deep into your life again is to blow up that path: grab the jackhammer and destroy it. It might mean change, or in some extreme cases destroying some relationships in your life.

    2. 08:34 The shallow heart

      • The shallow heart has a little soil but no depth, the feelings response the church sang about in “Christ Be Magnified”: I won’t be led by feelings. This person accepts God with excitement but doesn’t count the cost of following Jesus, so when resistance or difficulty comes, the joy fades. Josh calls it the camp response.

    3. 09:09 The thorny heart

      • The thorny heart hears but never makes space for the word to flourish. You plant your preferences, career, wealth, lust, politics, and habits, then try to plant Jesus, and he has no room because you didn’t pull the weeds. This person loves Jesus as Savior but ignores Jesus as Lord, living as if to say, “I’d like eternal life, but please don’t touch my stuff.”
      • Jesus doesn’t stay where he isn’t wanted. Josh’s dad got him hooked on the Gaither Vocal Band, whose song “Second Fiddle” says God don’t play second fiddle. He is first chair or nothing.

    4. 10:48 The good soil

      Scripture: Matthew 7:16 2 Corinthians 5:17

      • This is the heart Josh has been praying every person in the church would cultivate, the heart of a true believer. Jesus says you will know them by their fruits. It isn’t a blend of old and new; Paul says if anyone is in Christ, the old is gone and the new is here. Every genuine Christian will have evidence of fruit.

  5. 11:26 Launching the series: Ephesians 2

    Scripture: Ephesians 2:1–3

    • Josh launches the series with this parable because the enemy has different strategies based on the condition of your heart; he comes at you differently depending on where you are.
    • Ephesians 2 matters too: you were dead in your transgressions and sins, following the ways of this world and the ruler of the kingdom of the air, the spirit now at work in the disobedient. There is the invisible enemy identified. We all lived among them, gratifying our sinful nature, and were by nature deserving of wrath. The Pope calls us all children of God; Paul calls us all children of wrath. Somebody should tell him.

    1. 12:24 Three enemies: the world, the devil, the flesh

      • Christians have three enemies: the world, the devil, and the flesh. Josh wants them to see the battlefield clearly. God created the world to reveal himself, love us, meet our needs, and be in intimate fellowship with us, and to grow our fellowship with each other, and at one point it was good. The enemy convinces us to love the gifts instead of the giver; we have turned the gifts into God. Business is booming; he is good at what he does.
      • God created our pleasure centers. Satan didn’t add them to make life hard; they are wonderful gifts to help us enjoy God, each other, and creation. But the enemy robs us of that, and we become pleasure centers thinking only of the next high, the next gift, and forget the one who gave the gifts.

  6. 14:06 The Thessalonian letters: Paul the pastor

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 2 Thessalonians 1 Thessalonians 3:12

    • Josh hopes this six-week series through First and 2 Thessalonians builds the church’s faith and strengthens believers, which is why Paul wrote. It was one of his first letters, around AD 51, to a church established only two or three months earlier, and he builds their faith by correcting misconceptions about the resurrection of the dead and the second coming of Christ.
    • The series skips much of the first half, where Paul is being their pastor: thanking God for their faith and for their reputation in the city, which matters to God and should matter to us. He has been in their living rooms, sent Timothy, misses them, and longs to be with them. He prays things like “May the Lord increase your love,” the kind of prayer we should pray over each other, so they can be blameless before God when Jesus comes. We like Paul the apostle, but this is Paul the pastor, and he loved and served his people well.

  7. 15:51 Chapter 4: do this more and more

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 4:1–2

    • Chapter 4 begins with Paul reminding them how to live in a way that pleases God, as any decent pastor does regularly. They are already living this way; now he urges them to do it more and more. The message to the believer living to please God: keep going. Keep pressing in, keep doing good, and don’t grow weary of doing good.

    1. 16:59 Who do you live to please?

      Scripture: Romans 15:1 Galatians 1:10

      • Not the church answer: who do you actually live to please? Yourself? Romans 15 says we who are strong ought to bear with the failings of the weak and not to please ourselves. Others? That sounds Christian, but Galatians 1 says if I were still trying to please people I would not be a servant of Christ. Imagine being tasked with pleasing everyone; Josh would be an accountant.
      • The aim of the Christian life is to please God, but pleasing God is more than doing God’s will. Jonah did God’s will, but his attitude toward the people of Nineveh was trash. He was a whiner; God still accomplished it, but Jonah was miserable the whole time.

    2. 18:37 Are you miserable?

      • Have you met Christians who seem miserable? Are you? Their hearts just aren’t in it. You can check all the boxes, but if you pout as you do it, it isn’t from the Spirit and it certainly isn’t pleasing to God.
      • Josh wants them to hear this: the one who asks you to live a holy life will help you live a holy life. When did you last get on your knees and ask God to help you be holy and ready for what is coming? Have you opened his word to learn what he loves and hates? We get to know his heart by reading his word, singing his promises, serving one another, and spending time with other believers.

  8. 19:57 God’s will: that you be sanctified

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 4:3–8

    • Paul wanted the Thessalonians to know God’s will: that you should be sanctified. For this text, one word defines Paul’s Greek word hagiasmos: purity. Josh reads on: avoid sexual immorality, learn to control your own body in a way that is holy and honorable, not in passionate lust like the pagans who do not know God, and wrong no brother or sister, for God did not call us to be impure but to live a holy life; anyone who rejects this rejects God, who gives you his Holy Spirit.

    1. 21:00 God created sex; humanity hijacked sex

      • Put simply: God created sex; humanity hijacked sex. Notice how helpful, simple, and selfless God’s design is, and how complicated, harmful, and selfish man’s redesign is.
      • Don’t tune out. The room holds people from different walks of life, families, and parents who taught different things, but we all worship the same God, so we should start to look more alike. Experiences and views can dominate life but aren’t meant to, so believers should all take a big fat humble pill before discussing biblical sexuality.

    2. 22:09 Even this guy

      • Josh considered asking everyone who has struggled with sexual immorality of any type to stand, but wisdom kicked in: everyone would be standing except the liars. From 15 to 23, not one of his relationships could be described as pure; even he and his wife at the beginning did not choose purity, and none of it pleased God.
      • Praise God, they asked him to forgive them and set a new standard of purity and faithfulness, 13 years and counting. At any moment you can call on the name of Jesus and he will lift you from impurity and make a way to walk in purity again. If he did it for one of the worst, he can do it for you. He doesn’t just forgive good people; Josh thanks God daily that it is the sick who need a doctor, because he is sick.

    3. 23:47 The hijacking

      • The hijacking has been exacerbated over the past 60 years or so. Society has severed any connection between morality and sexuality and turned freedom of sexual expression into a god it will worship at all costs; even the threat of deadly disease couldn’t slow it. If it’s sensual, it’s acceptable. Children used to be off limits, but that is slowly changing as people attempt to rebrand pedophiles as minor-attracted persons. The aim of modern sexuality fits one phrase: personal gratification at all costs.

    4. 24:54 “Nothing but mammals”

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:13

      • Some will remember the MTV song: “You and me, baby, ain’t nothing but mammals, so let’s do it like they do on the Discovery Channel.” Josh used to laugh because surely it was a joke, but that is exactly what they think: sex is merely a biological function, so no moral restrictions; we are animals doing an animal thing.
      • Paul argues this with the Corinthians in chapter 6. They said sex is no different from eating: food for the stomach and the stomach for food. Paul responds that the body is not meant for sexual immorality but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body. Yet we use it however we want. Shame on us all.

    5. 26:25 No test drives

      • Fulfilling sexual desire has become goal number one. Culture has even made marriage, the most beautiful union we have, all about sex, and now young people are convinced they should take a test drive.
      • To the young people, especially young ladies: if a boy wants a test drive, hold out for one who knows that is between you and him for marriage. Let him earn it. No test drives. Josh doesn’t know why society doesn’t say this more to protect young women. Trust the Lord; good men who love Jesus and have self-control are out there, and it can wait. The hijacking breeds confusion that captures young communities, when God intended beautiful intimacy.

    6. 27:46 Not so different from Thessalonica

      • The church Paul wrote to had issues much like ours: fornication, adultery, homosexuality, men dressing like women, pornography, surrounded by tolerant governments and tolerant religious organizations. Sound familiar? And this ancient church would have been filled with Christians rescued from sexual immorality, just like this church.
      • Josh loves Paul’s heart as a pastor and wants to show up the same way, keeping urgency in front of the people: don’t let your guard down for a moment; don’t begin to think you stand, you might fall; guard your heart; break away from all sexual immorality. It is not God’s will for you to live impure lives.

    7. 28:51 God’s user manual

      Scripture: Genesis 1:28 Hebrews 13:4

      • Sorting through Satan’s lies is difficult; presenting God’s truth is easy. God created sex and gave the user manual from the beginning: marriage is a sacred union between one man and one woman for one life. That’s it. It is for reproduction, which let man obey God’s first command to be fruitful and multiply, and also for connection and for pleasure, a gift to be enjoyed within marriage.
      • That is why the porn industry is straight from the devil himself, and going by the numbers, some invite the devil into their home weekly. It has trained urges and desires toward a prerecorded stranger when those God-given impulses are meant to drive you to the marriage bed. Hebrews 13 says marriage should be honored by all and the marriage bed kept pure, for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral.
      • Godly sexuality is real, satisfying, and good; Satan’s counterfeit is empty and destructive. The world always offers alternatives. Josh’s one piece of advice: flee the offers of counterfeit intimacy. Young ladies especially: no intimacy is better than counterfeit intimacy. Flee.

  9. 31:03 Love more and more

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 4:9–12

    • Paul says they have been taught by God to love each other; a good pastor knows God is doing work too, and it isn’t all on him. They already love all the believers in Macedonia, yet he urges them to do so more and more. That is the answer to the Christian who wants to go deeper: we are called to do the same thing faithfully every day until he comes, more and more.
    • Throughout the New Testament, if God is teaching you to love, there is always more. If your love for others comes out of his love for you, you will always be supplied.

    1. 32:17 A quiet life isn’t “shut up and pray”

      Scripture: Micah 6:8

      • Verse 11 says make it your ambition to lead a quiet life, mind your own business, and work with your hands. Some use this to teach that Christians should shut up and pray and not speak into the issues of the day. That misses it, since the Lord has shown us what is good: to act justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with God. A quiet life means a life of order, peace, and confidence that persists in faith no matter what is going on.
      • The context: young Christians were quitting their jobs to wait for Christ’s return. They weren’t working, and believers and unbelievers alike were impacted by freeloaders. Paul says no: our reputation matters. Don’t stir up trouble; live together and contribute to your communities; stop being idle.
      • What do you want the Lord to catch you doing when he returns: working hard for your family and supporting the work of the church, or neglecting your family and needlessly draining the church’s resources? It is God’s will that we live holy, obedient, hardworking lives, get our hands dirty, contribute to our communities, and take care of our brothers and sisters in Christ.

  10. 34:09 Verse 13: hope, not an intellectual exercise

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18

    • Much has been said about these verses. Many in the church, including well-known pastors, are so infatuated with the end times that they have abandoned their own spiritual growth and passion for evangelism to arrange their end-times argument. Josh calls them solar eclipse Christians, and shows their posture.
    • Christ’s second coming isn’t an intellectual exercise; it is meant to motivate us to prepare others and ourselves for the judgment to come. Paul’s words encourage young believers to hold on to a hope the world has never known. Josh reads it: we do not want you to grieve like the rest who have no hope; the Lord himself will come down from heaven with a loud command, the voice of the archangel, and the trumpet call of God; the dead in Christ will rise first; we who are alive will be caught up with them to meet the Lord in the air; and so we will be with the Lord forever. Encourage one another with these words.

    1. 35:59 What happens to those who have died?

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 1:10 1 Corinthians 15:52 John 5:28–29 1 Corinthians 15:54–55

      • The Thessalonian believers, most of them new to faith, wonder what will happen to friends and family who died before Christ’s return. We don’t get the full picture in one place; God’s revelation is gradual until it is complete. Paul tells Timothy it has now been revealed through the appearing of our Savior Christ Jesus, who destroyed death and brought life and immortality to light, so we look to Christ and the apostles he entrusted.
      • Paul says in a flash, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet, the dead will be raised imperishable and we will be changed. That aligns with Jesus in John 5: a time is coming when all who are in their graves will hear his voice and come out. Our Savior has conquered death, so we don’t need to fear the future: death has been swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your victory? Where is your sting?

    2. 37:24 “Sleep” is not soul sleep

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:27–28 2 Corinthians 5:8

      • As the series unpacks the invisible enemy, you will learn that Satan doesn’t need much room to insert a lie; he’s a wiggly dude. Paul uses the word sleep for believers who died, as Jesus did of Lazarus, who was clearly dead. Soul sleep, the belief that the soul sleeps until the resurrection and final judgment, is not biblical; sleep simply describes how a dead body looks.
      • Hebrews 9 says people are destined to die once and after that face judgment, and Christ will appear a second time to bring salvation to those waiting for him. For believers, to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord: at death the spirit goes to be with the Lord, and God will bring with Jesus those who have died in him. Forget the bucket-list Northern Lights; this will be way better. Christ’s return is the believer’s ultimate comfort.

    3. 38:58 Not a timeline, but a pastor’s encouragement

      • Knowing exactly when the dead will be raised relative to the other events of the second coming isn’t as important as remembering why Paul wrote. It isn’t about putting together your little eschatological timeline. This is a pastor encouraging his people to hold on tight to their hope, so they can encourage one another when they lose a loved one or are uncertain of the future.

    4. 39:37 Five events at Christ’s return

      Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17

      • Five significant events surround Christ’s return. First, three sounds: Christ himself coming down with a loud command, the voice of an archangel, and the trumpet call of God.
      • Then the dead in Christ will rise. The grave is not the end: the body dies, the soul goes to be with the Lord, and when he returns he brings the soul and raises the body in glory, uniting body and soul forever. Like the risen Christ appearing to 500, eating fish with people, and letting Thomas see his wounds, we will have glorified bodies just like our Lord.
      • Next, living believers will be caught up. The word rapture isn’t used here; the Latin rapto, to carry off, is where we get the English word.

    5. 40:40 Alive or asleep, either way

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:14 Philippians 1:20–21

      • In some places Paul seems to hope he will be alive when Jesus returns. Who could blame him? Who here wants to be alive when Jesus returns? At other times he thinks he may die first, as in 1 Corinthians 6: by his power God raised the Lord from the dead, and he will raise us also.
      • It’s okay to desire to be among the living, as long as you aren’t devastated to be among the dead. It’s okay to wrestle with this, but we need more Christians who will end the race well. Paul wrestled in Philippians 1: Christ will be exalted in my body, whether by life or by death; for to me, to live is Christ and to die is gain.
      • For everyone in Christ, both remain possible: we will either rise or be caught up. Either way we have nothing to fear, because Jesus is coming with us or for us. Maranatha. Come, Lord Jesus.

  11. 42:37 “So we will be with the Lord forever”

    Scripture: 1 Thessalonians 4:17 John 17:24 John 14:3

    • Josh loves verse 17: we will be with the Lord forever. He needs to see more smiling faces; this meeting will be way better than any weekly staff meeting. We will have glorified bodies.
    • Jesus prayed for you in John 17: “Father, I want those you have given me to be with me where I am, to see my glory.” It isn’t only Josh who wants you to be with Jesus; Jesus wants you there too. His return fulfills his promise in John 14: if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come back and take you to be with me, that you also may be where I am.

    1. 43:47 The judgment seat of Christ

      Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:10 Revelation 20

      • In 2 Corinthians 5 Paul says we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ to receive what is due for the things done in the body, good or bad. Don’t confuse it with the great white throne of Revelation 20; they are two different things.
      • Christ’s judgment seat is like steps, like the choir risers, or the rewards stand at the Olympics: a judgment of believers’ works, with rewards given for what they have done. It is where every true believer longs to hear, “Well done, good and faithful servant.”

  12. 44:38 Are you ready?

    Scripture: John 14:1–2 Job 13:15

    • Death is a fact of life, unless Christ returns while you are alive. Either way you have an appointment with him. Are you ready? Are you prepared? What might you say face to face? Christians have hope because of his resurrection and his promise to come back for us. Do you have this hope? If not, Josh invites you today to put your hope and trust in Jesus Christ. He doesn’t disappoint; Josh put his hope in many things and saw only disappointment until he put it in Jesus, and he has never been the same.
    • Jesus says, “Do not let your hearts be troubled. Trust in God; trust also in me. My Father’s house has plenty of room.” Jesus makes room for those who love him. The only question is whether you will make room for him, not just today but every day until he comes back. Whether he gives or takes, will you trust him, obey him, and wait for him?
    • Josh loves what Job says: “Though he slay me, yet will I hope in him.” We aren’t crazy; we’re Christians. Our hope can’t be shaken, because we know God is in control.

  13. 46:30 Closing prayer

    • Josh prays as the church comes before God grateful that he saved them and humbly seeking to serve him as King and Lord. He confesses that all of them have let the invisible enemy creep too close; he goes first and says he has given up too much ground. It is time to take it back, take a stand, and live holy lives pleasing to God, and he pleads for help and for the Spirit to keep convicting them after they leave.
    • He prays that men would lay their desires and ambitions at God’s feet, their lives a living sacrifice and a testimony of his grace, mercy, and goodness, with leadership that changes homes, and that wives would lay down their ambitions too. Pursuing holiness means loosening our grip on what we hold tightly, and in the releasing there is peace and joy. God has given many good gifts, but by far the greatest is his Son, whom they remember now.
