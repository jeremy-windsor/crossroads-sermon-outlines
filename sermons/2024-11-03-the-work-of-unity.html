Overview

  1. 00:06 Students, relational tension, and the danger inside the house

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3

    • Josh welcomes students who are in the service: he has something for them today. He doesn’t know of a greater tool Satan will use to break apart their faith than relational tension; he has seen it time and again. The invisible enemy is at work.
    • Opening 2 Thessalonians 3, Josh notes the series has focused on outside influences. There is evil in the world to push back against, but he pictures a Christian staring out the window while evil lurks behind them. The often-overlooked danger is the one inside the house.

    1. 01:35 A protector by nature

      • Josh isn’t known for his brains but for his passion; he is a protector by nature, especially of women and children. Scripture teaches they are weaker and that his God-given role as a man is to protect them. That isn’t popular, but he would stand between any woman and danger, even a feminist. He does it for his family, often unnoticed, and he does it as their pastor.
      • Some dangers in the church get past him too. This week he realized people had been gone three weeks before he noticed, something he feels he could have helped with. When we stare out the window, we miss what is right behind us.

  2. 03:16 The parable of the sower: not everyone is good soil

    Scripture: Matthew 13

    • The church opens its doors to everyone, because the gospel must be preached to all. But while everyone hears it, responses and obedience will vary. Josh points to the parable of the sower in Matthew 13 and urges people to memorize its principles: the seed of truth, that Jesus came to save sinners and will purify you if you repent, is scattered. Some do nothing with it, some a little, and a very small group experience complete life change.
    • What is often forgotten is that someone who does nothing with it doesn’t necessarily stop attending church. The overflow of faithful believers is so great it benefits even people who want nothing to do with Jesus as Lord.
    • Many Christians have a childlike optimism that everyone is living what they learn; relationships in the church quickly force you out of it. The church isn’t only good soil; some people are hard as a path, rocky, or thorny. Don’t assume everyone here is surrendered to Jesus, or always obedient even if they are.
    • You don’t need an emotional flak jacket for the next relational grenade, but this family is a messy place, with people in all different places in their walk with Christ.

  3. 06:35 Experienced Christians still in diapers

    • Bottle-fed, immature Christians aren’t always new Christians. Sometimes experienced Christians are still wearing diapers: they could riff on any biblical topic but forgot to apply any of it. Truth doesn’t just alter our thinking; it alters our living. We become vessels of the truth so people watching us know without a word. You can’t lead someone where you’re unwilling to go.

    1. 07:30 Going deeper means “kill it”

      • Many say, “I want to go deeper,” and Josh is afraid to ask what they mean. In his opinion the deeper Christian life is killing the things you needed grace for. Remember what you asked the Lord to forgive? Is it still around? Kill it. Jesus purifies you; now you make sure he doesn’t have to do it again. You won’t do it perfectly, but you try with everything. That is Christian maturity.
      • Mature Christians encounter truth and have no choice but to change: “I’ve got to get rid of this. Lord, help me.” They realize the thing is the very thing Christ was crucified for. True believers don’t view sacrifice as an obligation but an opportunity.

    2. 09:19 Truth without application never equals testimony

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:12

      • Write it down: truth without application never equals testimony. We have a quiet church because we have a church not applying the text. Our learning and living must align; if you know it but your life doesn’t show it, you don’t believe it.
      • Hebrews reminds us the word of God is alive and active. The Christian can put the Bible down, but it doesn’t put them down; it keeps working, sharper than any double-edged sword, judging the thoughts and attitudes of the heart.
      • The problem with the church today isn’t elder boards, failing pastors, governance models, denominations, worship, tradition, or the fog machine. It is the individual who shuts the word down the second there is discomfort.

  4. 10:41 Plumbing problems: calling out the stink

    • If your home’s plumbing stopped flowing, you would call a plumber, or at least leave a voicemail. If you didn’t, your neighbor would eventually notice the stink, and if they lost their sense of smell during COVID, the HOA would smell it on their drive-bys. Someone will notice the stink in your life even if you refuse to.
    • The church should be calling out stink in one another’s lives, but we stopped because it isn’t nice. Now we have a bunch of Christians with plumbing issues, and everything stinks.

    1. 11:57 Fine with God forming things for us, not reforming us

      Scripture: Hebrews 11:3 1 Corinthians 6:9–10 James 1:22

      • Hebrews 11 says by faith we understand the universe was formed at God’s command. Everything we enjoy exists because God spoke it into being, and Christians are fine with that, thanking him for meals, because it is about them. (Any atheist is welcome to go to lunch with Josh to discuss it.)
      • But the Lord entrusted Paul with the gospel, and Paul says the wrongdoers will not inherit the kingdom of God: neither the sexually immoral nor idolaters nor adulterers nor male prostitutes nor practicing homosexuals nor thieves nor the greedy nor drunkards nor slanderers nor swindlers. Something smells fishy: we are good with God’s word forming things for us, not so much when it starts reforming things in us.
      • When we don’t just listen to the word and deceive ourselves, God gets the glory, and your testimony fuels the church and builds the kingdom.

  5. 14:08 Paul’s prayer points

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:1–5

    • Anywhere God seeks to gain ground, you will find opposition. So Paul opens chapter 3 with practical prayer points. Josh reads verses 1 through 5: pray that the message of the Lord may spread rapidly and be honored, and that we may be delivered from wicked and evil people; the Lord is faithful and will strengthen and protect you from the evil one; may the Lord direct your hearts into God’s love and Christ’s perseverance.
    • Do you pray like this? When did you last pray that someone would encounter Christ the way you have? We need more prayer power behind the gospel in a resistant culture.

    1. 15:27 Where the gospel spreads, lies spread

      Scripture: Isaiah 55:11

      • Wherever the gospel spreads rapidly, lies spread rapidly. What spreads fastest now are new ideas, usually false ideas. God’s word isn’t being taught, and when the church isn’t fed, it starts eating each other.
      • When you abandon the word, you declare it doesn’t work, and that is a lie. God’s word always works, even when you don’t see the result and weeks pass with no one getting in the water; then someone does, and you learn they were listening all along. God’s word never returns void, as the Lord says through Isaiah. Who are we to decide how God is using it? How can the pottery criticize the potter? The problem is we think it depends on us.

    2. 17:20 Zero confidence in us, complete confidence in the Lord

      Scripture: Genesis 3

      • Josh has zero confidence that he or anyone can handle the invisible enemy by punching Satan in the face; Genesis 3 shows you can’t handle anything. But he has complete confidence the Lord can give strength to fight for a slipping marriage, protect our hearts as we forgive people we don’t want to forgive, give strength to say no to temptation and break addiction, and protect us from the enemy.
      • Pray for me that I would persevere in faith, and I’ll pray for you. Pray that the Lord would direct our hearts into his love, that Josh would be sensitive to the Lord’s leading, and that the church would not just listen but do the word. Nothing is more valuable to Josh than their prayers.

    3. 19:10 A commitment: we don’t want this place to stink

      • Josh asks for eye contact and a verbal commitment: we don’t want this place to stink. Given the choice between a Circle K bathroom and a country club bathroom, where are you headed, if they’ll let you in?
      • Paul lays out four things to keep the church from a stinky reputation: love the truth, protect the truth, live the truth, and speak the truth. If everyone does their part, this place will pass the sniff test, even in Anthem.

  6. 20:14 Keep away from the idle and disruptive

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:6

    • Josh reads verse 6: in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, keep away from every believer who is idle and disruptive and does not live according to the teaching. Three types are called out: idle, disruptive, and disobedient. The first two are the same Greek word used twice, which could be translated lazy and disorderly, though he likes the NIV here.
    • Church problems are like physical problems: early detection is key. Ignore it and the infection grows and gets gooey, sorry for the visual. Paul is dealing with people who quit their jobs to wait for the Lord, and someone lazy quickly becomes known for other things: gossip, slander, even clever teaching, which Josh sees as coming from laziness.
    • Paul had warned them before; now he commands in the name of the Lord Jesus: enough is enough. Bored Christians easily turn into bad Christians.

    1. 22:06 Our work for unity

      • Many think calling this out is the paid staff’s job. But Paul calls the entire church to work together, which is why Josh titled the message “our work for unity.” If it were just his, he’d have called it “my work for unity” and taken today off.

  7. 22:40 What Scripture teaches about work

    Scripture: Genesis 2:15

    • Many teach that the need to work is a result of sin. That is false; just read two chapters. Work was part of life before the fall: Genesis 2:15, the Lord put the man in the Garden of Eden to work it and take care of it. Sin turned work into hopeless toil, but work was created by God as a gift.
    • What were people doing when God called them? Moses was shepherding; Gideon was threshing wheat, hiding underground like a wuss but working; David was tending his father’s sheep; Peter was fishing; Paul was making tents. Abraham is the trick question: he was living off his father’s inheritance and worshiping false gods. The Lord loves to use hard workers, but he can wake up the lazy too.

    1. 24:24 Won’t work versus can’t work

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:10 1 John 3:16–17

      • There is a difference between someone who doesn’t want to work and someone who can’t: a physical handicap, an injury, or caring for family. That is why Paul gave the rule: anyone unwilling to work shall not eat. If a believer can’t find work and is in need, the church must step up.
      • Crossroads Cares is how the church practices what the Lord teaches. But Josh is clear: the church holds the needs of its own family much higher than those outside. Right now it can meet both, but it had a tight summer and needs a generous church; if times get tough, members come first in triage.
      • John says this is how we know what love is: Jesus laid down his life for us, and we ought to lay down our lives for one another; if anyone has possessions and sees a brother or sister in need but has no pity, how can the love of God be in them?

    2. 26:15 Title versus tenure

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:7–9

      • We are led not only by the word but by obedient men and women who lead by example. Josh reads verses 7 through 9: Paul worked night and day so as not to be a burden, to offer himself as a model to imitate.
      • Effective Christian influence comes when you can point to your life as an example of obedience. It is the difference between title and tenure. Josh’s legal title is “governing authority,” which makes him laugh, since without Jesus he couldn’t govern his own life; Jesus is the governing authority. Anyone can have a title, but tenure is earned. For over a decade he has confessed his darkest moments and how Jesus rescued and restored him, and the church accepted him. He doesn’t care about his title; he cares whether he lives in a way worthy of it.

  8. 28:29 “Not busy, but busybodies”

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:10–12

    • Josh reads verses 10 through 12: anyone unwilling to work shall not eat; some among you are not busy but busybodies; settle down and earn the bread you eat.
    • Teaching his kids hard work is a comedy sketch. Madison used to dodge work but is now a hard worker; Nolan, 10, is a work in progress. Pulling weeds after a June monsoon, Josh wrecks the yard while Nolan walks by the same weed fourteen times, as if he needs a friendship with the weed before uprooting its life. Parenting is God’s favorite way to build humility and patience; God asks how many years Josh refused to pick up his cross.

    1. 30:26 Without work and purpose

      Scripture: 1 Timothy 5:13–14 Proverbs 29:18

      • Paul told Timothy to encourage younger widows to marry, or they get into the habit of being idle, going house to house as busybodies saying things they ought not. This isn’t a male-female thing: without work and purpose you get a “desperate housewife” situation that is bad for you, whoever you drag into it, and the church’s reputation.
      • Proverbs 29: where there is no vision, the people perish. If you’re bored, get involved in ministry. Josh can’t wait to retire, but you don’t retire from ministry or spreading the gospel. Serve with teenagers or in elementary; “I don’t do kids” means you should.

    2. 32:03 Stop speculating; do the word

      • Unplanned, Josh calls out something specific to this church. Famous pastors on YouTube preach generally; he can preach specifically. Some people talk about Scripture in a fantastical, philosophical way with a heightened embrace of mystery unlike anything he has heard in a lifetime under godly men.
      • Then it clicked: they want to see beyond what they are supposed to see, insisting there is more when there isn’t. Here is how you go deeper: do it. Pick up your cross and do it. A philosophy degree isn’t building disciples; it is creating confusion. If God wanted you to know it, he would have told you; one day we’ll know fully. If you discovered something 1,900 years of faithful Christians never saw, that is a red flag; it probably isn’t Christian.

  9. 34:32 Never tire of doing good

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:13 Galatians 6:9 Psalm 37:21 Psalm 41:1 Acts 20:35

    • Are you content in the little things? Verse 13: never tire of doing what is good. Josh wants it on a T-shirt and quotes it to himself all week; Paul told the Galatians the same thing, let us not become weary in doing good. It is his battle cry for ministry and for Crossroads Cares.
    • This is one of the most generous churches Josh has ever seen; if he didn’t think so, he’d probably be gone. But they have helped pay people’s mortgages only to see them disappear: did we just get taken for a ride? Maybe, but we can’t let those who take advantage change our readiness to care for those who truly need us.
    • David says the wicked borrow and do not repay, but the righteous give generously, and blessed are those who have regard for the weak. In Acts 20 Paul recalls Jesus’ words: it is more blessed to give than to receive. Our job is to make sure the brother or sister next to us isn’t growing weary of the good work.

  10. 36:47 Church discipline: correcting love

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:14–15 1 Corinthians 13:5

    • Josh reads verses 14 and 15: take special note of those who don’t obey; don’t associate with them so they may feel ashamed, yet don’t regard them as enemies but warn them as fellow believers. Love keeps no record of wrongs, but it seems necessary to keep a record of wrongdoers.
    • No one talks about church discipline anymore; it isn’t “church cool.” A soft, passive stance blows Josh’s mind, because Jesus clearly addresses it. Many think it is a spiritual SWAT team of elders pouncing on anyone in sin. Should you live in sin as a believer? No. Should we kick you out right away? No; this is precisely where you need to be until you prove you don’t want to be here.
    • Many have a negative view because they sat under dictators, not pastors. Think of a good parent disciplining a child: correcting love.

    1. 38:30 Matthew 18: how it should go and how it usually goes

      Scripture: Matthew 18:15–17

      • Josh reads Matthew 18: go point out the fault between the two of you; if they won’t listen, take one or two others; then tell the church; then treat them as a pagan or tax collector. Whether you were wronged or did wrong, go to the person first and meet in the middle. How often does that happen?
      • From pastoral counsel: if you fear for your physical safety, do not go alone; sometimes you need to establish a testimony sooner. Use discernment and seek wisdom; that isn’t gossip when you are honestly trying to honor God in a complicated situation.
      • How it usually goes: you confide in someone close, build your case, then go to war. And instead of trying to win the sinner, which means restore them, many Christians try to win the argument: why be righteous when I could be right?

    2. 40:51 Handling doctrinal error

      Scripture: James 5:19–20

      • For essential doctrinal issues, ask: are they even in error? Go ask what they believe. Are they in error because no one has sat with them? Then sit and work it through, or find someone who can. Or do they keep ignoring believer after believer and church after church? Help them understand, but if they persist, rebuke them, as Paul called out Peter in Antioch.
      • If you need help deciding whether an issue matters, ask a pastor or elder. James 5: whoever turns a sinner from the way of error will save them from death and cover over a multitude of sins. The goal is to turn them back, not turn them off. Ignore them and you haven’t loved them well, and you’ll answer for that.
      • Nobody comes out a winner in church discipline, but no church discipline smells way worse. Nobody wants this place to turn into a Circle K bathroom.

    3. 43:18 Divisiveness and leaders who cut and run

      Scripture: Titus 3:10

      • Disagreements are fine; disagreements that build factions are a massive problem, and Josh will be right behind. Paul tells Titus to warn divisive people twice, then have nothing to do with them.
      • Josh asks every elder candidate how they ended things with their previous church. Leaving a church over a disagreement isn’t keeping the peace or taking the high road. Leaders need a track record of pushing through difficult times, kind, gentle, and patient with people who aren’t.

    4. 44:33 Cancel culture in the pulpit

      • If a man in the church sins, he sinned; if Josh sins, it is called moral failure and he is disqualified. Isn’t all sin moral failure? No wonder pastors are depressed and lonely when confiding sin could cost them their job; we create an environment where they are supposed to lie. “Find someone at another church”? What kind of church can’t minister to its own minister?
      • Repeat offenders must be dealt with, but faithful pastors of 40 years booted over one moment of weakness? Where is the healing for those who preach healing? A sinner is a sinner. If you fail to restore or forgive a repentant sinner while enjoying peace with God yourself, Josh is more concerned for you than for them; what you showed them will be shown to you.

    5. 46:28 Sinners are the mission field, not the battlefield

      Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:15

      • Sinners aren’t the battlefield; they’re the mission field. You aren’t out to execute them but to exonerate them, and the mission field is harder work than the battlefield.
      • Sometimes the best thing you can do for an unrepentant sinner is pull your relationship. If you keep the same fellowship with disobedient Christians as with obedient ones, you put your stamp of approval on their sin; if you don’t call them out, you might as well cheer them on. Whose sin are you approving by not saying the hard truth?
      • Issues in the church mean issues in people’s hearts. Some know they need help, some don’t, some refuse it, but we must always offer it, always forgive, always seek to restore, and always build up the church.

  11. 48:13 The Lord of peace be with you

    Scripture: 2 Thessalonians 3:16 Matthew 28:20

    • Paul’s final words: may the Lord of peace himself give you peace at all times and in every way; the Lord be with all of you. As we work out our salvation together awaiting his return, there will be good times, bad times, lasting and broken relationships, but when Christ is Lord we have peace through it all. One sentence, so much peace: he never leaves us or forsakes us, and he will be with us to the very end of the age.

  12. 49:25 A cross being carried

    Scripture: Matthew 16:24

    • Someone asked Kristen why her cross necklace was sideways; she didn’t know. Josh told her to say that’s what a cross looks like when it’s being carried. Many are sitting and watching when they should be carrying.
    • For some, carrying your cross and denying yourself means forgiving the unforgivable today; for others, realizing you are the one who needs to be forgiven. Deny yourself, pick up your cross, and follow me. Jesus gave up everything; why can’t you give anything up? There is a crown on the other side. Give it up, die to self, surrender.
    • Josh prays over what he calls a housekeeping message, a family meeting: the work is eternal and actions have consequences. He asks the Lord to help them live like him, change and soften them, forgive them, teach them to forgive others, and make this place shine.
