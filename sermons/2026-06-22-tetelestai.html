Overview

  1. 00:38 God Uses Human Words to Reveal Divine Reality and the New Covenant

    • Josh opens by marveling that the infinite God explains himself through limited human words and pictures, then uses that principle to introduce Hebrews’ comparison of the new covenant to a last will and testament.

    1. 00:38 Isaiah’s Creator-Creation Distinction

      Scripture: Isaiah 55:8–9

      • After greeting the church on Father’s Day, Josh quotes Isaiah to establish that God’s thoughts and ways transcend humanity’s as the heavens transcend the earth.
      • The wonder is not that human language can contain God completely, but that the all-knowing, all-powerful, ever-present God chooses to make himself understandable through it.

    2. 01:43 Every Analogy Is a Spotlight, Not a Complete Portrait

      Scripture: Isaiah 55:8–9 John 3:16

      • Josh explores apparently risky biblical comparisons: Jesus’ arrival carries a thief’s surprise without a thief’s character, while shepherd, slave, father, king, and husband imagery each illuminates one aspect of God and leaves others in shadow.
      • He playfully extends the thief image through a song in which the enemy thought he had the believer, but Jesus answers, ‘You are mine.’
      • Because no human word, picture, or experience fits God perfectly, hearers must ask what part of each comparison is intended and what part is not.
      • God’s condescension in using human language reflects the love that brought him down to humanity’s level.

    3. 04:40 The New Covenant and Its Four Better Promises

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:16–17 Hebrews 8:13 Hebrews 8:6–12 Hebrews 8:12

      • Hebrews reaches for another spotlight by comparing the new covenant with a last will and testament. Calling it new already declares the first covenant obsolete.
      • Recapping the preceding week, Josh says God no longer writes his will merely on external stone; by the Spirit he works inside believers so they begin to love what he loves and hate what he hates.
      • Josh gathers the better promises as grace, inward change, forgiveness, and eternal inheritance. God’s refusal to remember sin is not forgetfulness but the result of Christ’s blood washing it away.

    4. 05:52 Christ Bore Sin, Brought Peace, and Healed His People

      Scripture: Romans 5:1 1 Peter 2:24 Isaiah 53:5

      • Josh says the new covenant is Christianity’s heartbeat because it explains how God deals with sin and establishes peace through Jesus Christ.
      • He quotes Peter’s declaration that Jesus bore sins in his body on the cross so believers might die to sin and live for righteousness; Peter’s use of Isaiah identifies Jesus as God’s Messiah.
      • The result is more than pardon: through Christ’s wounds his people are healed and reconciled, which gives substance to songs thanking Jesus for his applied blood.

    5. 06:38 The Same Blood Purchases Justification and Sanctification

      Scripture: Romans 8:1 1 John 2:1–2 Galatians 5:22–23 Ephesians 4:30 Ephesians 1:13–14

      • The covenant also writes God’s law on hearts. Believers may still fall, but they hate sin because they know its cost and walk without fear of condemnation under Christ their advocate.
      • Jesus lived the life sinners could not and died the death they deserved; his shed blood purchased both justification and sanctification.
      • The Spirit produces love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control, seals believers for redemption, and guarantees their inheritance.

    6. 07:42 An Imperishable Inheritance Kept in Heaven

      Scripture: 1 Peter 1:3–5 Hebrews 9:24 Philippians 3:20 Matthew 6:20 2 Corinthians 5:7

      • Josh quotes Peter’s praise for new birth through Jesus’ resurrection into a living hope and an inheritance that cannot perish, spoil, or fade because God keeps it in heaven.
      • Houses, cars, bodies, health, and even memories fade; the inheritance bearing the believer’s name does not.
      • With sanctuary, Father, Savior, citizenship, inheritance, treasure, and hope in heaven, believers can walk by faith rather than sight and remain confident whatever happens here.

  2. 08:54 Hebrews 9: A Covenant Inheritance Activated by Death and Blood

    • Hebrews 9:16–22 compares covenant inheritance with a will, shows that both old and new covenants are ratified in blood, and leads Josh through Scripture’s long lesson that sin costs life and requires a substitute.

    1. 08:54 Hebrews 9:16–22: A Will Takes Effect at Death

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:16–22

      • Josh reads that a will becomes effective only after its maker dies, then follows Hebrews through Moses’ use of blood, water, scarlet wool, and hyssop to inaugurate the first covenant.
      • The reading culminates in the principle that nearly everything is cleansed with blood and without shed blood there is no forgiveness.

    2. 09:49 A Will and a Covenant Are Similar but Not Identical

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:16–17

      • A will is primarily a legal document, while a covenant is a relationship established by oath; Josh cautions the church to retain both the usefulness and the limits of the comparison.
      • The common point is that an inheritance required death: the blessings of the new covenant were activated by Jesus Christ’s death.

    3. 10:20 Jesus Names the New Covenant in His Poured-Out Blood

      Scripture: Luke 22:20 Hebrews 8:13

      • Jesus’ words over the cup explain the relationship: the new covenant is in his blood poured out for his disciples.
      • The new covenant’s arrival makes the former covenant obsolete, yet Hebrews now emphasizes not only difference but continuity.

    4. 10:45 Both Covenants Were Ratified Through Blood

      Scripture: Exodus 24:3–8 Genesis 15:9–18 John 19:30

      • Josh sends hearers to Exodus 24, where Moses sprinkled the covenant book and people with blood, and to Genesis 15, where God passed between divided pieces.
      • Ancient covenant promises carried death with them. Jesus’ blood therefore signs and ratifies his last will and testament.
      • God’s unified purpose spans history: saints before Calvary trusted the promise ‘I will do it,’ while saints after Calvary trust the accomplished declaration ‘It is finished.’

    5. 11:51 Leviticus Locates Life and Atonement in the Blood

      Scripture: Leviticus 17:11

      • Josh quotes Leviticus 17:11: a creature’s life is in its blood, and God gave blood on the altar to make atonement.
      • That text opens the recurring question of why Scripture contains so much blood, death, warfare, lambs, and sacrifice.

    6. 12:28 Eden Begins the Lesson That Sin Costs Something

      Scripture: Genesis 3:6–7, 21

      • From the beginning God taught humanity that sin has a cost. Adam and Eve tried to cover themselves with fig leaves, but human effort could not address guilt.
      • God clothed them with animal skins; Josh describes this as the first sacrifice coming from God and the first blood shed because of human sin, anticipating that the final sacrifice also comes from God.

    7. 13:06 A Sacrificial Line Runs Through the Biblical Story

      Scripture: Genesis 4:3–5 Job 1:5

      • Cain brought produce and Abel a sacrifice from the flock; Job sacrificed for his children; Abraham built altars; and Moses received exact instructions for a sacrifice-filled tabernacle.
      • Generation after generation, lambs died on altars. The repeated lesson is that sincerity, good intentions, and human effort cannot erase sin; a substitute is required.

    8. 13:53 Guilt Requires Payment, and Every Sacrifice Points to Jesus

      Scripture: Romans 6:23a

      • Josh appeals to sacrifice across human cultures as evidence of a widespread conviction that guilt requires payment, then states Scripture’s truth that sin’s wage is death.
      • Humanity senses that something—or someone—must stand between a holy God and guilty sinners.
      • Hebrews supplies the answer: every lamb, altar, and drop of blood pointed beyond itself and whispered the same name, Yeshua—Jesus.

  3. 15:00 The God-Man Dies, Rises, and Applies the Better Sacrifice in Heaven

    • Josh guards the will analogy through the hypostatic union, explains Christ as both the one who dies and the risen executor, and reads Hebrews 9:23–28 to show the once-for-all sacrifice applied in heaven itself.

    1. 15:00 Do Not Press the Will Analogy into Heresy

      • Every analogy has limits. Josh addresses the question ‘Did God die?’ with ‘yes and no’ and invokes the hypostatic union: Jesus Christ is fully God and fully man simultaneously.
      • God cannot die in the divine nature, so the Son took flesh and died in humanity’s place.

    2. 16:06 Jesus Died to Activate the Covenant and Rose to Execute It

      • The risen Jesus uniquely serves both sides of the testament: he dies to activate the covenant and rises to execute it.
      • He therefore personally guarantees every covenant promise and gives believers the Holy Spirit.
      • Old Testament believers were saved the same way as later believers; the cross reaches backward to them and forward to the church.

    3. 16:44 Crucified with Christ Across Time

      Scripture: Galatians 2:20

      • Josh quotes Galatians 2:20, a favorite verse he had his children memorize: Paul was crucified with Christ, no longer lived as the old self, and lived by faith in the Son who loved and gave himself for him.
      • Though Paul was not physically beside Jesus at Calvary, Josh uses the verse to say every believer—including David, Daniel, and Micah—shares union with Christ’s death.

    4. 17:16 Only Those Who Come Through Christ Receive the Inheritance

      Scripture: Hebrews 7:25

      • Josh raises the recurring question of the heirs and answers that only those who come to God through Jesus Christ receive the imperishable inheritance.
      • The controlling point remains simple: inheritance requires death, and God’s promised eternal inheritance required the death of his Son.

    5. 17:59 Hebrews 9:23–28: Christ Appeared Once and Will Appear Again

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:23–28

      • Before reading, Josh explains that Scripture’s repetition should be treated as bold, underlined, and highlighted because healthy churches must not forget the gospel.
      • Hebrews contrasts earthly copies cleansed by sacrifices with heavenly reality addressed by a better sacrifice. Christ entered heaven itself to appear for his people in God’s presence.
      • Unlike an earthly high priest, Christ did not offer himself repeatedly: he appeared once to put away sin, people die once and face judgment, and the once-sacrificed Christ will appear a second time to bring salvation to those waiting for him.

    6. 19:37 A More Costly Sacrifice Is More Effective

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:23–28

      • Both covenants were established through blood, but Christ supplies the better sacrifice and applies it in the better place.
      • To make the contrast concrete, Josh compares an iPhone 3’s obsolete specifications with an iPhone 17’s: once the superior model is available, no one would choose the slow, old one.
      • Josh’s side-by-side visual contrasts repeated sacrifice with one sacrifice, others’ blood with Christ’s own, covered sin with removed sin, Israel’s covenant administration with a saving invitation to sinners, and an earthly holy place with heaven itself.
      • The comparison is meant to stir praise for the new thing God has done.

    7. 21:05 Do Not Build Confidence on Things Made by Hands

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:24

      • Josh presses Hebrews 9:24 pastorally: confidence in God must not rest on anything that can break, burn, age, fail, or die.
      • The tabernacle faded, both Jerusalem temples were destroyed, genealogical records disappeared, and no functioning sanctuary remains for the old priesthood.
      • Rather than merely criticizing ancient Christians tempted to return to Judaism, present Christians should examine their own dependence on man-made religious structures.

    8. 22:00 Good Gifts Make Terrible Saviors; the Living Christ Does Not Perish

      Scripture: Acts 1:3, 9 John 1:29 1 John 2:1 Hebrews 9:27

      • Church buildings, denominations, baptism certificates, past prayers or altar calls, morality, giving records, and long attendance may be gifts, but they cannot save.
      • Christian hope rests in the risen Christ who left the tomb, entered the true sanctuary after forty days, never dies, and never needs to repeat his sacrifice.
      • Everything made by hands ends in a landfill, museum, or graveyard; salvation rests instead on the living advocate who represents sinners before God’s supreme court.

  4. 23:36 Peace While Waiting and the Cross’s Word Tetelestai

    • Jesus’ victory gives peace in present trouble and salvation at his return; his cry tetelestai announces a legal pardon, military victory, and fully repaid debt, leaving sinners to receive him and worship.

    1. 23:36 Take Heart Because Jesus Has Overcome the World

      Scripture: John 16:33

      • Josh quotes Jesus’ promise of peace in the midst of worldly trouble and asks whether the church truly believes Christ has overcome the world.
      • Because he has, believers can take heart rather than letting circumstances control their confidence.

    2. 24:00 Christ Will Return Carrying Salvation, Not Sin

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:28

      • The salvation believers possess now is real, but Hebrews promises a still greater fullness when Jesus appears again.
      • His first coming bore his people’s sin, accomplished death in humility, and secured them; his second will gather them in glory and bring their completed salvation.

    3. 24:22 The Blessed Hope Ends in Sight and the Church’s ‘Come’

      Scripture: Titus 2:13 1 John 3:2 Revelation 22:20

      • While waiting for Christ to fill the skies, Christians live godly lives in expectation of the blessed hope.
      • John says believers are God’s children now, though their final state remains mysterious; when Christ appears they will be like him because they will see him as he is.
      • Revelation closes the exchange: Jesus promises to come soon, and the church answers, ‘Amen. Come, Lord Jesus.’

    4. 25:31 Christians Worship Together While They Wait

      Scripture: Hebrews 9:28

      • Across the ages Christians share one posture: they worship while they wait.
      • The first coming brought Christ in humility to die for his people; the second brings him in glory to gather them.

    5. 26:06 John’s Eyewitness Record and the Meaning of Tetelestai

      Scripture: John 19:30 John 19:30

      • Josh traces the phrase ‘the finished work of Christ’ to Jesus’ final word in John 19: after receiving the drink, he said, ‘It is finished,’ bowed his head, and gave up his spirit.
      • John records the Greek word tetelestai. Josh unfolds it as completed, accomplished, paid in full, and finished, with force in legal pardon, military victory, and repaid debt.
      • He calls it one of the New Testament’s most important words and the fitting tagline for the new covenant.

    6. 27:12 If the Work Is Finished, Worship Is What Remains

      Scripture: Romans 5:1 Colossians 2:14

      • Tetelestai distinguishes Christianity from every religion built on unfinished human work. If Christ’s sacrifice was perfect and permanent, justification is by faith, and the debt was canceled at the cross, nothing remains to be added.
      • Josh’s answer is not passivity but worship: fall before the Savior, receive Jesus as Savior and Lord, and come for the forgiveness he pours out.
      • The forgiven worship; that response carries the sermon into Hebrews 10.

  5. 28:03 Hebrews 10: Shadows Cannot Perfect, but Christ Does God’s Will Once for All

    • The law’s repeated offerings expose their own insufficiency; Christ is the substance anticipated by every shadow, the obedient body promised in Psalm 40, and the once-for-all sacrifice through whom believers are made holy.

    1. 28:03 Hebrews 10:1–4: Repetition Cannot Remove Sin

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:1–4

      • Josh reads that the law is a shadow rather than the coming reality and that endlessly repeated annual sacrifices cannot perfect those drawing near to worship.
      • Josh explicitly marks this part of Hebrews’ argument as a transition to sanctification.
      • If those offerings had cleansed worshipers once for all, they would have stopped; instead they repeatedly recalled guilt because bulls’ and goats’ blood cannot take away sin.

    2. 28:43 The Day of Atonement Gave Temporary Rest and an Annual Reminder

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:1–4

      • Josh recalls the preceding week’s explanation of Day of Atonement preparation and distinguishes remission from reminder.
      • Each new year’s sacrifices proved the prior year’s were insufficient. Israel lived under temporary rest but never the perfect rest now available through Christ.
      • A thousand years of offerings still left another sacrifice due, and Josh asks hearers to feel that exhaustion.

    3. 29:25 Blueprint, Ring, and Trailer: Shadow Gives Way to Substance

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:1 Luke 22:20

      • Josh likens the old forms to a blueprint before the house, an engagement ring before marriage, and a trailer before the film.
      • Sacrifices, priesthood, tabernacle, Day of Atonement, and covenant ceremonies were shadows; Christ is the superior sacrifice and priest, brings access to the heavenly sanctuary, accomplishes actual atonement, and establishes the true covenant in his blood.

    4. 30:06 Hebrews 10:5–10: ‘A Body You Prepared for Me’

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:5–10

      • Josh reads Christ’s words on entering the world: God did not finally desire animal sacrifice and offering but prepared a body; Christ answers, ‘Here I am ... I have come to do your will.’
      • Offerings commanded by the law were performed correctly but were intentionally insufficient. The obedient Son sets aside the first covenant order to establish the second.
      • By that will, believers are made holy through Jesus Christ’s bodily sacrifice once for all.

    5. 31:05 Psalm 40 Preaches Christ a Millennium in Advance

      Scripture: 2 Timothy 3:16 Psalm 40:6–8

      • Josh connects Hebrews 10 explicitly to Psalm 40 and calls the relationship evidence of Scripture’s God-breathed character.
      • David spoke prophetically roughly a thousand years before Jesus’ birth; Hebrews identifies Jesus as the better, pleasing sacrifice who fulfills ‘Here I am ... I have come to do your will.’
      • Josh concludes, ‘The patriarchs are preaching the gospel.’

    6. 31:45 The Temple Became Obsolete; Christ’s Finished Work Never Will

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:10

      • Hebrews could point first-century readers to Jerusalem’s visible, smoking temple and still declare its order obsolete.
      • Christ’s work cannot become obsolete: his one bodily sacrifice has made his people holy once for all.
      • Whatever was, is, or will be needed for their salvation, Christ has done it.

  6. 32:20 The Pleasing Son Sits Down After One Sacrifice

    • Josh contrasts priests who stand through endless duties with the Christ who offers one sacrifice and sits down, then makes the contrast felt through exhausting repetition and a sunrise that outshines every old-covenant star.

    1. 32:20 From Creation’s ‘Good’ to the Father’s Pleasure in the Son

      Scripture: Genesis 1:3–31 Matthew 3:17

      • Josh recalls God’s verdict of goodness over creation and compares it with the Father’s pleasure in the Son’s redemptive obedience.
      • At Jesus’ baptism the Father declared, ‘This is my Son, with whom I am pleased’; the Son’s ‘Here I am’ and the work it anticipated pleased him.

    2. 33:04 Hebrews 10:11–14: The Standing Priests and the Seated Priest

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:11–14

      • Josh reads that every ordinary priest stands day after day to repeat sacrifices that never take away sins.
      • Christ, by contrast, offered one sacrifice for all time, sat at God’s right hand, and waits for his enemies to become his footstool.
      • By that one sacrifice he has perfected forever those being made holy.

    3. 33:43 Opposing Repetition Dismantles the Old Repetition

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:11–14 John 10:27

      • Hebrews speaks to a people formed by religious repetition; Josh says the letter answers exhausting repetition by repeating Christ’s once-for-all sufficiency until the sheep hear their Shepherd’s voice.
      • Hundreds of thousands of animal offerings could not achieve what Jesus achieved through one sacrifice forever.

    4. 34:16 Standing, Washing, Offering, Changing—Then Rest

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:11–14

      • Josh slows his delivery to repeat the priests’ cycle of standing, washing, offering, and changing until the language itself feels tiring.
      • The purpose of feeling the exhaustion is to celebrate the rest: this priest, one sacrifice, all time, every sin dealt with in one move.

    5. 35:27 The Testimony of a Seated Savior

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:12–14

      • Josh blesses the church with the image Hebrews supplies: a seated Savior.
      • His posture testifies that the atoning work is not waiting for another offering or another priestly shift.

    6. 35:32 When the Sun Rises, the Stars Fade

      Scripture: Malachi 4:2 Hebrews 9:12

      • Stars shine clearly before sunrise and disappear when a greater light appears—not because they were false, but because they are outshone.
      • Aaron, sacrifice, temple, priesthood, and Day of Atonement were stars; when Christ, the Sun of Righteousness, appeared, every shadow faded in his light.
      • They were weak and he is strong; his strength secured eternal redemption and silenced sin, death, and Satan.

  7. 36:41 The Spirit Testifies: Forgiven Sins Need No Further Sacrifice

    • The Spirit’s new-covenant testimony moves Josh from Jeremiah’s promise to the prodigal son, God’s decisive forgiveness, the believer’s recurring self-accusation, and Hebrews’ command to rest where sacrifice is no longer needed.

    1. 36:41 Hebrews 10:15–18 Returns to Jeremiah 31

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:15–18 Jeremiah 31:33–34

      • Josh highlights the whole Godhead at work: the Holy Spirit testifies through Scripture to Jesus’ finished sacrifice.
      • Hebrews returns to Jeremiah’s covenant promise that God will place his laws in hearts and write them on minds, then adds that he will remember sins and lawless acts no more.
      • Where forgiveness has occurred, another sacrifice for sin is unnecessary.

    2. 37:08 The Spirit Tells Families About Jesus Through the Word

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:17–18

      • Josh urges the church to enter the word and to bathe spouses, children, and grandchildren in it because there the Holy Spirit works and testifies about Jesus.
      • He repeats the promise that sins are remembered no more and no sacrifice remains necessary after forgiveness.

    3. 37:37 The Prodigal Was Treated as Forgiven

      Scripture: Luke 15:11–32

      • Jesus’ prodigal-son story pictures the Father’s forgiveness: the returning son was not placed at the table’s end, sent to the kitchen, or made to prove repentance over several weeks.
      • The robe, ring, feast, music, and celebration immediately restored him to family honor.
      • The story’s point for Josh is that the father did not merely say he forgave the son; he treated him as forgiven, just as God treats every sinner in Christ.

    4. 38:43 God Does Not Forgive the Way Humans Forgive

      Scripture: Luke 15:11–32 Hebrews 10:17 Isaiah 55:8–9

      • Humans forgive while remembering, recording offenses, keeping distance, and requiring trust to be re-earned; Josh calls people poor forgivers.
      • God says he will remember sins and lawless deeds no more. Even when an ‘older brother’ Christian refuses to forget, God’s ways remain higher than human ways.
      • Divine remembering no more does not mean ignorance; because of Jesus, God will never hold the sin against the person who belongs to Christ.

    5. 39:42 Stop Reopening Cases God Has Closed

      Scripture: John 19:30 Hebrews 10:17

      • Believers are skilled at remembering sins and weak at remembering promises. They replay failures, revisit regrets, and reopen cases God has closed, while Satan exploits the cycle.
      • God says ‘forgiven,’ ‘paid in full,’ and ‘I remember it no more,’ yet the conscience questions or insists on further payment.
      • Josh asks what right anyone has to hold against themselves what God no longer holds against them or demand payment for a debt Christ paid.

    6. 40:27 Sit Down and Rest Where Forgiveness Is Complete

      Scripture: Hebrews 10:18

      • Hebrews’ final line lands like a hammer: where sins are forgiven, sacrifice for sin is no longer necessary.
      • Christians still wage war on sin, witness, and love hostile neighbors, but concerning forgiveness Josh commands them to sit down and rest in their Lord.

  8. 41:04 Faithfulness Fixates on Christ’s Effort, Not the Believer’s

    • Because the crucified, risen, reigning Christ supplies everything salvation requires, Josh rejects anxious sin management, calls Christians to rest and trust, and invites unbelievers into forgiveness, baptism, communion, and the family feast.

    1. 41:04 Everything Needed Is Present in the Perfect Christ

      Scripture: Ephesians 2:14 Hebrews 6:19 Matthew 27:51 Hebrews 10:12

      • Jesus is peace, and believers possess everything needed in the perfect, crucified, risen, and reigning Christ.
      • Every sin is answered, debt paid, accusation silenced, promise guaranteed, enemy defeated, and hope anchored.
      • The altar is empty, blood shed, curtain torn, tomb empty, and Son seated; Josh asks why believers remain standing anxiously when their Savior has sat down.

    2. 41:44 Christian Faithfulness Is Not Sin Management or Endless Trying

      • Following Jesus is not a plan to try harder, perform better than another person, or hang on a little longer.
      • There is always another improvement to attempt, so effort-management cannot provide assurance.
      • Christian boasting rests instead in the Christ who will never let his people go.

    3. 42:22 Lay Down Human Effort and Fixate on Christ’s

      • Faithfulness lays down self-effort and fixates on Jesus’ effort. It surrenders trying, accomplishing, and self-generated strength.
      • The heart’s confession is trust in Christ’s power, perfection, resurrection, and promises because he alone can help, save, and free.

    4. 42:57 Look Only to Jesus and Breathe at the Throne of Grace

      Scripture: Hebrews 4:16

      • Josh warns that looking sideways in comparison steals joy, then tells Christians to stop looking everywhere else, lift their heads, and see Jesus seated on the throne of grace.
      • Because they can approach with confidence, they can exhale and rest rather than carry their own acquittal.
      • A shared breath in the room turns the theological claim into an embodied pastoral invitation.

    5. 43:34 An Invitation to the Unbeliever

      • Josh tells unbelievers to ask Jesus for forgiveness, trust that he will forgive, and ask him to add their names to the book of life.
      • Those who genuinely come should come to the table, enter the baptismal water, join the family, and enjoy the feast of new life in Christ.
      • Jesus has done what no priest, sacrifice, religion, or sinner could do for themselves.

    6. 44:10 Tetelestai, Church

      • Josh closes the proclamation by repeating the sermon’s title word: ‘Tetelestai.’
      • He then turns the gathered church toward prayer and communion.

  9. 44:27 Closing Prayer: Rest in the Promises and Worship While Waiting

    • Josh prays for weary people to leave discouraging self-preaching, rest in Christ’s promises, anticipate the world made new, and commune and worship together until he comes.

    1. 44:27 A Quiet Moment with the Father

      • Josh begins with silence, asking simply to sit with the heavenly Father for a moment.
      • The pause matches the sermon’s repeated command to stop striving and rest.

    2. 44:45 Rest for the Weary and Freedom from Self-Discouragement

      Scripture: Matthew 11:28

      • He acknowledges weariness in the room and asks God to help people get out of their own heads.
      • He prays that they would stop preaching discouragement to themselves and begin listening to God’s word.

    3. 45:13 Bathe the Church in Hope and a Better Future

      Scripture: Revelation 21:4–5

      • Josh asks that the church be bathed in promises of hope and a better future without death, mourning, sadness, or pain.
      • The former things will pass away, and God is making all things new; the church watches with anticipation.

    4. 45:36 Come, Lord Jesus; to Live Is Christ and to Die Is Gain

      Scripture: Revelation 22:20 Philippians 1:21 1 Corinthians 10:16

      • Whatever happens here, the faithful church says ‘Come’ and waits for the Lord.
      • Josh confesses that to live is Christ and to die is gain, then entrusts the church’s lives to the God they love.
      • He thanks the Father for Jesus and for the body’s communion and fellowship in the Savior’s blood and body, then prays that they remember and worship while they wait.
      • The prayer and verified sermon end with ‘Amen’ at 46:11.
