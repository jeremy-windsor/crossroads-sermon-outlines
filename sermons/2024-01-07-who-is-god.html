Overview

  1. 00:30 Start, launch, begin: Genesis means beginnings

    • Happy New Year. Start, launch, rise, begin, open, emerge, birth: there is something uplifting, optimistic, and positive about these words. It could be the start of a new year, a new day, the opening of a beautiful song, or the first day of a family vacation, like the problem-free philosophy in Lion King, full of hope, promise, and excitement as we imagine all the possibilities.
    • Genesis is the first book of the Bible, and it means beginnings or origin. And it is exciting.

  2. 01:28 Two books our culture hates: first, the Bible

    Scripture: Psalm 119:105

    • There are two books our culture desperately hates right now, and the Bible is one. It is a sad time in the history of the church when pastors seem to dislike the Bible: instead of defending it as God’s word, they try to redefine it, because they care more about how people look at them than how people look at God. Josh says he will show the power of God today, and he can’t figure out how we can see his power and then attempt to change or ignore what he says.
    • The leadership of this church, the pastors, elders, and teams, admire Holy Scripture and believe it contains the divine answer to the deepest needs of humanity. We live in a dark world, but we hold God’s word up and boast in it: “Lord, your word is a lamp to my feet and a light for my path.”
    • Josh can’t imagine where he would be without God’s word: without being taught his law of love, without knowing he is a sinner under the promise of grace. What kind of husband, pastor, or man would he be? He is wandering in the dark unless God shines his light.
    • The believer clings to the word. But our culture hates the very book that contains its origin story, because if they accept that, they have to accept it all.

  3. 03:53 Second, biology textbooks

    • God’s creation is by his design, not ours; we are discovering it, not co-designing it. Try an experiment on an island to create a flourishing society without men and you would have a tragedy on your hands. The rules of reproduction can’t be changed; God made them male and female.
    • Order, adaptation, and environmental response all point to an intelligent designer and to a beginning. Smart people can argue well on this, but the evidence for a Creator is convincing; it isn’t a shot in the dark. Faith and science pair well together; without faith in the church you wouldn’t have science.
    • If you hate the Bible and biology textbooks, Josh is glad you are here, but you are probably going to hate this series, because we are about to get a biology lesson and a history lesson from the source of all things.

  4. 05:36 The question is not whether God exists, but who God is

    Scripture: Genesis 1:1

    • Moses wrote Genesis, part of the Pentateuch, the first five books of the Hebrew Bible. A massive question is answered in just its first couple of verses: who is God?
    • Notice what the question isn’t. There is no proof of God in Genesis 1 and no apologetic approach; Moses simply assumes God exists and that it is foolish to assume otherwise. Genesis is all about God, all of history is about God, science is about God; there is simply a proclamation of his existence and presence: “In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.”
    • This is an ancient text, roughly three and a half thousand years old. Ideas, philosophies, religions, and trends have come and gone, and Genesis still remains. It is a great place to learn about God, but reader beware: it isn’t exhaustive. From chapter 1 to chapter 50 Moses covers about 2,000 years of human history, about as much as the rest of the Bible combined. It gives us what we need to sufficiently answer the question, who is God?

  5. 07:40 Creation is known by revelation and accepted by faith

    Scripture: Genesis 1:2 Hebrews 11:3

    • We can’t really argue the creation question one way or another, because none of us was there to see it go down. Besides common sense, verse 2 says the earth was formless and empty, with darkness over the deep and the Spirit of God hovering over the waters.
    • We can hypothesize through science and philosophy, but the only way to truly know what happened is not by guessing but by being told: by revelation. The only one there was God, so he tells the story only he can tell. Hebrews 11 says by faith we understand that the universe was formed at God’s command.
    • The origin of the universe has yet to be explained apart from faith. After centuries of speculation, people keep sharing notes and get no closer. Josh read quote after quote this week from scientists and philosophers at the end of their lives, and all he saw was frustration. Bertrand Russell wrote that we must conquer the world by intelligence, but if you can’t determine how it started, how do you intend to conquer it?
    • Josh takes it by faith that the universe was formed at God’s command; they take it by faith that it began by accident. Where do you stand?

    1. 09:51 Hollow and deceptive philosophy

      Scripture: Colossians 2:8

      • Paul warns believers about human tradition and clever arguments in Colossians: see to it that no one takes you captive through hollow and deceptive philosophy that depends on human tradition and the elemental spiritual forces of this world rather than on Christ. So be careful.
      • The nebula theory lasted about 100 years; the tidal theory led to the steady state theory; now there is the big bang theory. None gained universal acceptance among scientists, and even nonbelieving scientists call for their peers to reconsider evolution. No one agrees.
      • Josh thinks it is because the answers aren’t for us to find; they are for us to accept. If we refuse and just keep searching, we go from one theory to the next, over and over, because understanding is found by faith. That is how God always intended us to accept origins, and anything.

    2. 11:17 The Spirit who witnessed creation is still hovering

      Scripture: Genesis 1:2

      • In verse 2 the Holy Spirit was there among creation establishing order, and that same Spirit reveals God’s truth to the writers of Scripture so they can share what God says and how he is working. A key witness to the origin of the universe is the third person of the Trinity.
      • He is still hovering today to draw you near to God. Josh has been praying over this series and asks believers to join him in praying that nonbelievers who hear these messages would come to faith and by faith accept these things.

  6. 12:01 “In the beginning”: how old is the earth?

    Scripture: Genesis 1:1

    • So who is God, where does creation come from, and what does it all mean? In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. Talk about a loaded verse; someone asked Josh how long the series will be, and we could spend weeks right there.
    • Questions flow immediately from “in the beginning”: when was that, and how old is the earth? Josh polls the room: who thinks it is 4.5 billion years old, who thinks six to 10,000 years, and who won’t raise a hand for fear of getting jumped by their small group leader in the lobby.
    • Christians love to debate this, and Josh doesn’t know why, since the very next phrase literally says God created the heavens and the earth. God speaks and suddenly void becomes vibrant, and leave it to us to argue about how old it is. The Hebrew word for beginning means begin, first; it doesn’t say how old the earth is. God says, “I made it”; we say, “I must know when.” If God wanted you to know, wouldn’t he have told you?
    • In those conversations Josh figures out what the other person thinks and agrees fast so the conversation can be over. Old or young, he doesn’t care; he is glad you are here to worship and call him king.

    1. 14:22 “In the beginning God”: the uncaused causer

      • Part of this phrase shows that the one responsible for the beginning had no beginning. He is often called the uncaused causer, and if he is, then everything else is rooted in him. He has no beginning and no end.

  7. 14:42 “Created”: brought into existence

    • Genesis uses two different Hebrew words for creating, and in English, ESV or NIV, both are translated “create.” Josh asks what is more impressive: taking the pieces laid out on a table and building someone a delicious sub sandwich, or making a delicious sub sandwich appear with a thought. If he made sandwiches out of nothing, he would have a lot more friends.
    • The Hebrew word here, bara, means to bring into existence. God brought the earth into existence and set it up so we could move in, live here, and enjoy him and what he worked for. We jump over this too quickly; marinate in it. The eternal, always existent, sovereign God created something for your enjoyment.
    • Life, the earth, and creation come from God. The evolutionists are wrong; they needed an answer and came up with the first thing they could. We don’t pop up from nothing, by nothing, for no reason. The impersonal doesn’t make the person, nothing doesn’t make something, chaos doesn’t bring order, and what lacks intelligence doesn’t produce intelligence. We all come from God, the intelligent one.

    1. 16:51 A frog won’t grow up to be president

      • Josh can come around on microevolution, but macroevolution is ridiculous. Cleaning the pool a few months ago he found a living frog; his wife, with her big heart, demanded it be set free, not murdered, and said, “Handle it.” He tried to fling it over the wall with the dustpan, misjudged the trajectory, and the frog slammed into the wall to certain and immediate death. He felt bad.
      • No textbook will make that frog adapt to the wall. Maybe it could have adapted to his backyard, but a frog isn’t going to grow up to be president. When his brother-in-law’s baby girl number two comes, nobody is nervous the ultrasound will show a flamingo.
      • We don’t believe in evolution; we believe God created everything from nothing and gave creation order and intelligence. Not a meal goes by that Josh doesn’t thank God for everything he has provided for him, his family, and his kids, because it is all from him.

  8. 18:44 The heavens and the earth

    Scripture: Genesis 1:1

    • Look at what he made: in the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. Appreciate how little Moses probably knew about either. We are first introduced to the expanse of the universe and the complexity of our planet by a man with no expertise in the field, yet he is writing the very words of the God who created it.
    • Now we have the Hubble telescope and apps you can point at the sky, even down to see the stars on the other side. Moses probably didn’t even have a map, let alone a map of the stars.

    1. 19:33 God determining, the Son sustaining

      Scripture: Psalm 147:4 Hebrews 1:3

      • Moses wasn’t the only unqualified, Holy Spirit-empowered astronomer. David, filled with the Spirit, testifies to God’s hands-on approach in the cosmos: God determines the number of the stars and calls them each by name.
      • One of Josh’s favorite verses is in Hebrews chapter 1, which intimately connects God and Jesus in being and says Jesus is sustaining all things by his powerful word. We have God determining and God’s Son sustaining. What is Jesus sustaining? All things.

    2. 20:19 A universe precisely tuned for life

      • Within the last 20 years scientists have discovered that the universe has been precisely tweaked to support life; change almost anything by the smallest increment and we would cease to exist. If gravity, the force wrecking your knees and joints, were altered by more than one part in 10 to the 40th power, stars wouldn’t exist, and neither would we.
      • Josh shares Frank Turek’s illustration: stretch a tape measure across the entire known universe and set gravity at one inch mark. If it were different by one inch in either direction across a scale as wide as the universe, we wouldn’t be here.
      • Are we supposed to believe our planet just won the cosmic lottery? Third from the sun, in the Goldilocks zone: a little closer and we’d burn, a little farther and we’d freeze. Change the 23.5-degree axial tilt, the 24-hour rotation, or the size and distance of the moon slightly, and we don’t exist. His favorite: if Jupiter doesn’t hold orbit, we don’t exist. Without Jupiter and Saturn we’d need Bruce Willis from Armageddon blowing up asteroids every week.
      • The average distance between stars in our galaxy, about 30 trillion miles, is required for life on earth, and the stars are as many as the grains of sand on all the beaches on the planet.

    3. 22:54 Lift up your eyes

      Scripture: Isaiah 40:26 Isaiah 40 Psalm 103:11–12

      • “Lift up your eyes and look to the heavens: who created all these? He who brings out the starry host one by one and calls them each by name. Because of his great power and mighty strength, not one of them is missing.”
      • Homework: read the entire chapter of Isaiah 40. Isaiah is writing to a nation in turmoil and saying God’s got this; look at God’s sovereignty. So who is God? Instead of turning on Netflix tonight, go out and look up and get an education there.
      • Psalm 103 says as high as the heavens are above the earth, so great is his love for those who fear him; as far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us. Only two things compare to the infinite expanse of creation: God’s love and God’s mercy. And as amazing as creation is, there is only one thing he created in his image, but we’ll get to that.

  9. 24:04 Formless and empty, with the Spirit hovering

    Scripture: Genesis 1:2

    • The earth was formless and empty, darkness over the deep, the Spirit of God hovering over the waters. Tyndale, the first English translator, called it “formless and void,” and Josh loves “void”: a barren, uninhabitable wasteland. It is like an iPhone with no operating system. The earth had been created but wasn’t ready for God’s most precious creation.
    • Don’t forget the Holy Spirit is there; he witnessed creation and inspired Moses to write it down so we could know what happened too.
    • If you are hearing this for the first time: any time the Spirit of God is hovering, something amazing is about to happen. Without form, void, darkness: that was the condition of Josh’s heart before he was given the Spirit of God. But God’s Spirit doesn’t enter and leave things the same; he doesn’t send his Spirit so that things stay the same.

  10. 25:31 Day one: “Let there be light”

    Scripture: Genesis 1:3–5 Genesis 3 Exodus 20:11

    • God said, “Let there be light,” and there was light; he separated light from darkness, and there was evening and morning, the first day.
    • Josh believes these are literal days. He can’t start thinking the Bible says stuff it never says; he remembers somebody doing that in Genesis 3, and it didn’t go well. Scripture seems clear: Exodus 20 says in six days the Lord made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and all that is in them, but rested on the seventh day, and blessed the Sabbath and made it holy.
    • God speaks and creation listens; that is the theme of Genesis 1. It was dark, God spoke, and the sun rises. Don’t miss this: God speaks every day. He is involved in every sunrise and sunset. Do you wake up thinking, “God, you’re the master architect; you’ve done it again,” or do you think he set everything in motion and is taking a divine cosmic nap until the day of the Lord? The rising of the sun is the work of his hand.

    1. 27:01 Worship with a side of wine?

      • Sovereignty is huge here, and Josh challenges the church to praise the Lord for what he is doing around them every day. Are you going home and pouring some wine because you like it, or are you celebrating God in it? Do you take time to thank him for the fruit, the hands, the time, and the effort behind such an enjoyable treat? Is it worship with a side of wine, or just wine? This isn’t just about wine; apply it to anything.
      • If you don’t thank God for 24-hour days, thank goodness they aren’t longer. Josh and his wife are falling asleep by 10. If you aren’t grateful, volunteer for a youth all-nighter or be a camp sponsor; you’ll learn to appreciate it quickly.
      • Josh loves waking up to a brand new day, a blank canvas to write on. Some wake up on a different side of the bed, but he encourages them to change their mindset and learn to see God’s love in the rhythms he established for us to enjoy.

  11. 28:29 Day two: water in the sky

    Scripture: Genesis 1:6–8

    • God made a vault to separate the water under it from the water above, and called the vault “sky”; evening and morning, the second day. Some read that and say, “What?” You may live in Arizona, but there is water in the sky; they are called clouds.
    • Josh wondered this week how Moses got this information: a divine conference table taking notes with God? A Genesis highlight reel like on ESPN or NFL Network? He has no idea, but it is fun to think about.
    • On day two God didn’t create anything new; he ordered what he had already created. Praise God for his creative work, but also for his vision in the order he gives it. If we had nothing but oceans and clouds, you wouldn’t be enjoying the beautiful Arizona sunsets of the last three days. Thank God for day two more often.

  12. 29:58 Day three: “And it was so”

    Scripture: Genesis 1:9–13 Romans 11

    • God gathers the waters so dry ground appears, then the land produces vegetation, seed-bearing plants and fruit trees according to their kinds; God saw that it was good; the third day.
    • A theme repeats: God speaks, creation obeys, he looks it over and declares it good. Another phrase recurs: “and it was so.” In those brief phrases we learn a timeless truth: God’s intentions never fail. His speaking is his doing; everything obeys the word of God.
    • Someone may say, “But Josh, so many disobey his word.” Yes, but their disobedience is used for his glory. There isn’t time to blow your mind with it, but go read Romans 11. More homework.

    1. 31:31 Fruit on Tuesday

      • Day three is vegetation: fruit, berries, and veggies; God probably knew vegetables wouldn’t be a big hit. Josh could eat blueberries every day; his late sister used to freeze packages of them. His family loves fruit, and he has no objections to fruit unless God says don’t eat it. God made fruit on Tuesday; why? Josh doesn’t have an answer.
      • We didn’t have steaks until after the flood, over 1,600 years later; Josh asks if anyone praises God for being born after the flood so they could have a steak dinner. And before the vegans parade around church as more biblical than the rest of us, milk did a body good back then too: for a long time it was milk, nuts, fruit, and veggies. So the gluten-free crowd must be the most holy among us. Just kidding.

  13. 32:54 Day four: lights to govern day and night

    Scripture: Genesis 1:14–19

    • God made two great lights, the greater to govern the day and the lesser to govern the night, and the stars, to mark seasons, days, and years and to separate light from darkness; the fourth day.
    • Another debate, and Josh excuses his frustration, since God creates the universe from nothing and we turn it into foolish debate: when did God make the sun? Verse 1, day one with “let there be light,” or day four? Josh doesn’t think day four is when God literally created the sun but when he announced it. None of it matters much to him; all he sees is God sorting the cosmos like Josh sorts his son’s Legos. He doesn’t have questions to ask; he has songs to sing.
    • Maybe God needs to announce his creation, so that people worshiping the stars find out God made them and say, “Our bad, we’ll just worship you and skip the middleman.”
    • God cares about your quality of life. He wants you to sleep well and enjoy your day; he cares that you have time to work and to rest, to be with your spouse, kids, and grandkids. Far too many see God as a cruel overlord, but even despite our arrogance and rebellion he is gracious, kind, loving, and generous.

  14. 35:11 Day five: God blessed them

    Scripture: Genesis 1:20–23 Genesis 1:22

    • Day five has Discovery Channel written all over it: the waters teem with living creatures, birds fly across the sky, and God blesses them to be fruitful and increase; the fifth day.
    • Did you catch verse 22? God blessed them: the animals. Genesis talks about God pouring out blessing more than any other book in the Bible, over 80 times: God blessed, I will bless you.
    • So who is God? He is the uncaused causer, he is good, and he desires to bless you. If he rolls out blessings to the animals, how much more will he offer you?

  15. 36:27 Day six: in his image, and very good

    Scripture: Genesis 1:26–27 Genesis 1:31

    • On day six God finishes the animal kingdom and then does something amazing: “Let us make human beings in our image, in our likeness,” to rule over the fish, the birds, the livestock, and all the creatures. So God created human beings in his own image, male and female.
    • Up to this point God looked over what he had done and called it good. But as he closes day six, God saw all that he had made, and it was very good.

    1. 37:13 The image we bear is still his

      • Josh bets many, like him, have struggled with self-worth: things they don’t like about themselves, or things others would change. This week he accidentally shaved off his goatee, and when his wife saw him, her face said, “What have you done to my husband?” Laughing about it, he realized we all struggle with our image and our insecurities in different ways.
      • The truth is that God is pleased with how he made you. He made each of us for a reason, with a God-given purpose; you have value because he assigned you value.
      • We went and made a mess of things, and our hearts were deceived, but the image we bear is still his. We don’t wear that with the honor we should. We are his; we belong to him; we were bought.

  16. 39:17 Day seven: blessed when we rest

    Scripture: Genesis 2:1–3 Genesis 2

    • A lot of us don’t take time to rest; we’re bad at it. In chapter 2 Moses says the heavens and the earth were completed, and on the seventh day God rested from all his work, blessed the seventh day, and made it holy.
    • If God chooses to rest, you should too. Many rob themselves of joy and vitality by refusing to slow down or take a break, but God sets the example. We are blessed when we create, but we are blessed when we rest.
    • Genesis 2 continues with another account of creation; read it before next week. The homework: Isaiah 40, Romans 11, and Genesis 2. A busy week, but you can handle it.

  17. 40:19 Who is God?

    • He is the Creator, the Son, the Redeemer, the Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end. In Genesis we see a personal, infinite, eternal, just, loving, holy God who designed the universe and everything in it to reflect his glory, greatness, power, majesty, beauty, wisdom, justice, and mercy. He has no beginning, and he depends on nothing and no one. That is our God.

    1. 40:55 In the end: “I am making all things new”

      Scripture: Revelation 21:5 John 14:6

      • In the beginning God created; in the end God will separate. Revelation 21 says he who was seated on the throne said, “Behold, I am making all things new.” Has he made you new? Is he working on you?
      • Darkness covered the waters, but God spoke life. We turned from him and our hearts were covered in darkness like the waters, and God once again spoke life. The first time he painted the skies by his Spirit; when he spoke again, he sent his Son. Jesus says, “I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.”

    2. 41:57 It is never too late to turn to Jesus

      • Some think Jesus won’t welcome them, or hear from others that they have gone too far or fallen too far. It’s a lie. You have changed a thousand times over the years, but God has not changed once. God wants to give life, and the righteous will live by faith.
      • Josh pleads: believe in Jesus, trust him, follow him, join him.

  18. 42:41 Prayer before communion

    • Josh prays as the church heads into communion, the Lord’s Supper, a time of fellowship with God to remember what he did, approached with reverence. He wants to be like Jesus, who walked through tightly gathered crowds and paused and stopped when a sick woman who believed in him reached out and touched him; he asks God to show him when to pause and stop, and whom to care for and love so they can know him.
    • He thinks of the Last Supper and the patience and love that let a betrayer sit in the room, and asks not to be shaken by the betrayers in his life. Either God is sovereign or he isn’t; Josh believes he is in absolute control, so when God takes something, he doesn’t ask where God was but what he is working on.
    • He prays to be like the Son in the garden, about to carry the load of our sin and pay our debt in full, asking for a strong back to carry others as Jesus carried him, and to be faithful when all alone, as Jesus was on the cross. “You spoke it into existence and it is good.” He thanks God for loving, providing for, and saving him.
