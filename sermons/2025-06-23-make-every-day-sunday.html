Overview

  1. 03:43 Ephesians 5:1–20 and how chapter 4 ended

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:1–20 Ephesians 4:24 Ephesians 5:1–2

    • Chris dives into Ephesians 5:1–20, first reminding the church how chapter 4 ended. Last week Josh told us the sins to avoid, the sins that shouldn’t be part of our new self, which was created to be like God in true righteousness and holiness.
    • As we hit chapter 5, the narrative seems to shift: follow God’s example as dearly loved children and walk in the way of love, just as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us.

  2. 04:26 Read it with 4:32: forgive as God forgave you

    Scripture: Ephesians 4:32–5:2

    • When Paul wrote this letter he didn’t have chapters, so Chris doesn’t think these two verses were meant to be separated from the verse before them, and he reads all three together.
    • Paul has just listed the things we’re not supposed to do, but he knows we’re not perfect and we’re going to mess up sometimes. So he tells us to be kind, compassionate, and forgiving of one another, because God forgave us, and to love and support one another when sin gets the better of us.

    1. 05:33 Not a license to live in sin

      • Some might think, “Then no one can call me out for my sin or my lifestyle, because you’re supposed to love me and forgive me.” That’s not what’s being said at all. Context is important: Paul is talking about the believer who is growing in their walk with Christ and fighting their temptations, not the believer or false believer purposely living in sin and wanting everyone around them to accept or even condone it.
      • How does Chris know? “Because I kept reading.”

  3. 06:20 Ephesians 5:3–7: when we become idolaters

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:3–7

    • Chris warns that the next few minutes will be uncomfortable for some, and he won’t pull any punches, because Scripture doesn’t pull punches.
    • Here’s the context: when we are running after immoral, impure things and we are greedy, we become idolaters, and the Bible tells the rest of us not to partner with them. Idolatry means putting something else in the place of God and worshiping it.
    • Don’t mistake this for sin we are truly repenting of, praying about, and fighting to get out of our lives. Idolatry means we have purposely crafted it and set it up in our lives, and it has taken the place of God because we won’t remove it.

    1. 07:59 How do you know you’ve made an idol?

      • Do you make excuses for doing something Scripture clearly tells you to stop? When a message calls it sin, or someone who truly cares about you calls it out, do you get mad? Do you decide that’s not what Scripture is saying, point out that others are doing it too, or say, “What I’m doing may not be right, but it’s not as bad as what the person over there is doing”?
      • When convicted by Scripture, a sermon, or a loving brother or sister in Christ, instead of getting mad or defending yourself, ask why you feel convicted. Is the Holy Spirit convicting you of something you need to change? Then read God’s word on the subject of your conviction and devour it, pray that the Lord would reveal whether it’s an idol you need to destroy, and if it is, destroy it. Kill it.

  4. 09:16 Verse 3: not even a hint of sexual immorality

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:3

    • Some sexual immorality seems obvious in our society because it is in our face: the sin of homosexuality, the transgender movement, and pornography, which destroys intimacy and trust in our marriages and frankly often destroys the marriage itself.
    • But what about the sexual immorality that has become acceptable in our culture and frankly in many of our churches? We give it a quick wink and a nod but don’t call it out because it has become so normal. Chris means two things: sex before marriage and living together before marriage.

    1. 10:37 “It’s not that big of a deal”

      • The big fallacy about sex before marriage: it’s not that big of a deal, it’s just a physical thing, you’ve got to try it before you buy it.
      • If God says sex is to be saved for marriage, it doesn’t really matter what we think. Chris asks anyone to show him a time in human history when man did it his way and it worked out better than the way God designed it.
      • He wants to focus on living together before marriage, but everything he will read and apply applies to both premarital sex and living together.

    2. 11:38 God’s design at creation

      Scripture: Genesis 2:24

      • God created a man and a woman. Notice it doesn’t say leave his father and mother and live together and make sure things work out first.
      • Living together is not God’s design. There is no commitment before God and man that you are living in a covenant relationship for life.

    3. 12:09 How Jesus handled it

      Scripture: Matthew 19:4–6

      • For those who like to dismiss the creation narrative, Chris shows how Jesus handled this leaving, cleaving, and uniting.
      • Jesus just reiterates God’s command for marriage. When a man and a woman decide it’s time to live together, it’s because it’s time to get married.

  5. 12:55 Objections to waiting

    • There is much Chris wants to address, but he moves quickly through a few objections he has heard.

    1. 13:02 “We’re married in our hearts before God”

      • It sounds good and gives Chris all the feels, but it doesn’t work. You can still walk right out the door with no legal, or in a worldly sense moral, ramifications; nothing holds you responsible for anything done in that home, except if you have children.
      • Arizona has no common law marriage. You can live together for 50 years, walk out, and there’s nothing.

    2. 13:45 Why certificates of divorce?

      Scripture: Matthew 19:3 Matthew 19:7–9

      • If there was no need to officially get married, why did there need to be certificates of divorce under the law of Moses? What we just read was only part of Jesus’ interaction with the Pharisees, who asked whether it is lawful to divorce one’s wife for any cause, and then asked why Moses commanded a certificate of divorce.
      • These are difficult words from Jesus, and Chris isn’t there to get into divorce. What he wants us to see is that Jesus takes a man and a woman in a sexual relationship seriously, and the expectation is that they are married and intend to stay that way for life.
      • Living together doesn’t hold that same commitment, because it is too easy an out if things don’t go your way: “This isn’t working out. We tried it. Going to move on.”

  6. 15:24 1 Corinthians 6–7: becoming one flesh

    Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:16–7:9

    • A final passage relates to premarital sex, which, let’s be real, is happening if you’re living together. Chris reads 6:16–20: the one who unites himself with a prostitute is one with her in body; flee from sexual immorality; your bodies are temples of the Holy Spirit; you were bought at a price, so honor God with your bodies.
    • In Corinth prostitution was rampant. There was even a term, “Corinthianize,” that meant engaging in sexual immorality; it was so bad they had a word for it.
    • If Paul equates meaningless sex with a prostitute with becoming one, and you can’t get any more meaningless than that, then it stands to reason that anytime it happens we become one flesh with that person. We are called to be one flesh with one person for life. That is the biblical standard.

    1. 17:09 Better to marry than to burn with passion

      Scripture: 1 Corinthians 7:1, 8

      • Chris finishes the passage through 7:9, noting there is a little marriage material in it that he won’t talk about.
      • Paul says in verses 1 and 8 that it is good to remain single, stay a virgin, or, for a widow, abstain from sex, so that our only focus is on Christ and telling the world about him. It isn’t wrong to be married. But if we are burning with passion and being tempted by sexual immorality, we should get married.
      • To those living together, “shacking up,” as Dr. Laura used to call it on her show: get married. Paul made it really clear that we should remain abstinent, and if we can’t, we need to be married.

  7. 19:24 Getting away with it doesn’t make it right

    • Your life might be great living together and your relationship might be awesome, but you’re outside of God’s design for your life.
    • Chris asks how many have told a lie to parents, at work, or to a spouse to keep from getting in trouble, and how many got away with it. He has, more times than he cares to admit. But just because he got away with it and the outcome he wanted happened, that didn’t make his lie okay. If you’re having sex outside of marriage, and maybe even living together, that’s not God’s design, and no human reasoning can change that.
    • His challenge to those living together: get married, or, though it can seem insurmountable, one of you move out until you decide what you actually want to do is get married. When you’re ready to live together, get married.

  8. 21:04 Parents, teach your children God’s design

    • Chris asks parents to teach their children God’s design for sex and marriage, and the dangers and pitfalls they may have fallen into and the consequences they now deal with. Children need to know that each person they have sex with will stick with them for life. It’s not like shaking somebody’s hand and going to wash it off.

    1. 21:53 You can wait

      • Chris loves the honesty of the pastors about their sexual issues and other sins; what the church sees on stage is the same thing he sees in the offices. It isn’t a show.
      • So he wants to be honest: you can wait for marriage, and you can teach your children they can too. Chris waited, and he was able to because he decided what he would do before he ever got to the situation. His main reason was that God says sex is only for marriage, but he also wanted his wife to wait for him, so how could he not wait for her? He also prayed that they would wait for each other.
      • His wife, looking at his senior-year student ID picture, thought no girl would ever have come near him. She’d be wrong. In high school he only wanted to play baseball, but in college he had opportunities, and every time, the decision he had made beforehand and all the reasons for it came flooding to mind. God’s word would intrude, and he would walk away.

    2. 24:10 Decide before the moment

      • If you wait until you’re in the moment to decide, chances are you’ll make the wrong decision, because desire and temptation are so powerful.
      • Parents, teach your children God’s design. Challenge them to know why they believe what they believe and to reason out ahead of time why they are going to wait for marriage. Either you teach them about sex or the world will. Do you want them to get it from God’s word or from a world that says sleep with every person you possibly can? It’s your choice.

  9. 25:05 Verse 4: what comes out of our mouths

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:4

    • Josh said it last week, and Chris jumps on the bandwagon: stop with the profanity, the coarse words, and the joking.
    • If we say we have Christ in us and are filling our hearts and minds with his word, if we are truly disciples growing and walking with him, how can crap continually flow from our mouths? He apologizes for saying “crap,” but they had just talked about sex.

    1. 26:01 A barrage of Scripture on the tongue

      Scripture: James 1:26 1 Peter 3:10 Luke 6:45 Matthew 15:18–20 Colossians 3:8 James 3

      • Chris reads a string of verses about what comes out of our mouths: James 1:26, 1 Peter 3:10, Luke 6:45, Matthew 15:18–20, and Colossians 3:8.
      • Has the Bible made its point? He didn’t use anything from Proverbs or Psalms, or everybody’s favorite, James 3, on the tongue causing a great fire that no one can tame. We could go all day. Simply put, what’s in your heart comes out of your mouth.

    2. 27:40 Tuesday night softball

      • Chris plays softball every Tuesday and hears some of the filthiest stuff. No one on his team is a believer, so what comes out of their mouths is what’s in their hearts.
      • If the same came out of his mouth, you’d be right to say his heart is no different from theirs. Why would it change just because he’s around them? It shouldn’t. Because of God’s mercy, grace, and transformation in his life, what comes out of his mouth sounds very different.

    3. 28:26 “Words aren’t containers”

      • Chris addresses an idea he has heard from multiple people: words aren’t containers. A word is like a puzzle piece whose significance comes from how it fits with other words, context, and speaker intent. He grants that language has nuance; English is a lazy language that reuses words, like “bank” for money or the side of a river.
      • But when it comes to coarse language, obscenities, and crude jokes, no matter what other pieces you throw at it, it’s not okay for a believer. He says “I hate you” to someone up front in several tones, as sweetly as he can, but the word is still hate and still carries a connotation. There are much worse obscenities he could say in no way that would make anyone okay with it; he wouldn’t have a job in five minutes.
      • Words matter and have meaning, or we wouldn’t need dictionaries. Telling someone you love that their hair is “round” or that they have a nice chair on their head makes no sense.

    4. 31:31 What do others hear?

      Scripture: Luke 6:45

      • There are two ways to see what’s going on in somebody’s life: the fruit in their life and the words that come out of their mouth. So what’s coming out of yours?
      • A terrifying thought: ask those around you what they hear and what they think is in your heart based on your words. Do you have the courage to ask? Does Chris? Because the mouth speaks what the heart is full of.

  10. 32:07 Ephesians 5:8–20: children of light

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:8–20

    • No more one verse at a time: Chris reads verses 8–20, from “you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord” through “always giving thanks to God the Father for everything in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.”

    1. 33:14 Once darkness, now light

      Scripture: Ephesians 5:5 Ephesians 5:8

      • In verse 5 Paul says no immoral, impure, or greedy person, an idolater, will inherit the kingdom, and we’re not to partner with them. Then in verse 8 he reminds us that we were once in darkness: we were idolaters, sexually immoral, greedy, and impure.
      • But now, because of Christ, we are light in the world, to live as children of light and please the Lord. Do we look like the world, or have we been changed by Christ’s sacrifice on the cross and the Spirit who lives in us? We are called out of the darkness we once wallowed in, the sin whose wages bring death, and into the light to bear fruit.

    2. 34:11 Does your life show the fruit of the Spirit?

      Scripture: Galatians 5:22–23 Ephesians 5:11

      • Does your life show the fruit of the Spirit, or do we look like we did before Jesus took over? Do you still basically look like you did before you called yourself a Christian? Are you chasing the same things? Is keeping your money and lifestyle, your sexual immorality, your crude language, your idol more important than shining the light of Christ?
      • Verse 11 says darkness produces fruitless deeds, too shameful to mention. Are your deeds fruitless, or Chris’s?

    3. 35:12 Good news: expose the darkness

      Scripture: Ephesians 5:13–14

      • Verses 13 and 14 bring Chris so much hope. Expose the darkness; rip open the areas of your life producing fruitless deeds. Don’t just sit there and hide your sin in secrecy and shame. Plenty here have had to expose their darkness and secrets, and they are here to walk through it with you, not condemn you.
      • There’s nothing like God taking our darkness and turning it into light. Something you never thought you could defeat or kill is destroyed when you expose it to the light, and you become a beacon for the world to see Christ’s transforming power.

    4. 36:19 No shadows between light and darkness

      Scripture: Revelation 3:15–16 Ephesians 5:14

      • Remember what was written to Laodicea: because you are lukewarm, I’m about to spit you out of my mouth. There’s no fence riding in the kingdom of God. You can’t hang out in shadows between light and darkness, because there aren’t any. You’re either in the light or you’re not.
      • Verse 14: wake up, sleeper, rise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you. There’s nothing like emerging from darkness into the sunshine, as morning people know; Chris wouldn’t, because he can’t get up that early.

  11. 37:30 Live wisely: read your Bible

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:15–17 2 Timothy 3:15–17

    • With everything exposed to the light, Paul reminds us how to live. How do we live wisely and understand the Lord’s will? Someone in the room held up a Bible: that’s right. Chris reads 2 Timothy 3:15–17 and urges anyone who doesn’t know it to memorize it.
    • He has no amazing new thing to wow everyone. He’ll say what they’ve heard a million times, because some need to hear it a million and one: you live wisely by reading and studying the Bible.
    • It couldn’t be easier to access on our phones, but our phones seem more important to us than our Bible. We panic when the phone is lost, broken, or out of battery. Do we have the same reaction when we haven’t read God’s word in a day, a week, a month? Is this service the only time you see what’s in God’s word because it’s on the screen? If you are not regularly in the pages of Scripture, you are unwise and foolish.
    • Chris cites a study he saw but forgot to note the source of: reading the Bible one to three times a week changes your life statistically the same as never reading it. Four or more times a week, the change skyrockets, in everything from sexual immorality to what comes out of your mouth to how you treat people, because the word of God is getting inside your heart and that’s what comes out.

  12. 40:44 Verse 18: don’t get drunk; be filled with the Spirit

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:18

    • The placement is interesting: Paul has just talked about living in the light and confirms what we all know, drunk people do stupid things.
    • Chris confesses he has never been drunk; all his poor decisions he made perfectly sober, which probably makes it worse. He isn’t holier than thou: he hates the taste of alcohol and can’t stand being out of control, even on strong pain medication. Being around drunk people irritates him, and he can’t count how many times he’s heard, “I would never have done that if I was sober.”
    • Hence Paul’s warning. We don’t make good decisions when we’re drunk. Instead, we should be filled with the Spirit.

  13. 42:33 Make every day Sunday

    Scripture: Ephesians 5:18–20

    • After all the talk of what not to do, sexual immorality, greed, drunkenness, idolatry, darkness, foolishness, we hear what brings love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control: be filled with the Spirit, speak psalms, hymns, and songs, sing to the Lord, and always give thanks.
    • Chris knows they covered tough things: sexual immorality to bring into the light, mouths to clean up, idols to kill, and darkness in his own life to expose. But when you let the Spirit fill you, every day should look like today, when we speak hope, love, joy, and peace, pray for each other, and praise God at the top of our lungs. Why does it look different the other six days of the week? It shouldn’t.
    • Call a brother or sister and speak hope and life into them. Don’t know what to say? Paul just told you: speak a psalm or hymn and pray it over them. Sing your lungs out in the car or the shower; if you’re praising the Lord in the car, you won’t be cussing out the driver who cut you off.
    • And thank him. Whatever is going on in your life, you can be thankful to God for sending Jesus to die on the cross so you can have eternity with him. Life is hard, but in eternity we will have all the fruit of the Spirit without even trying, and all the hard stuff will be gone. Go kill those idols and make every single day Sunday.

  14. 46:01 Closing prayer

    • Chris thanks the Father for all he has done. Our sin gets the better of us, and idols are so easy to craft and build; the Israelites were barely without God for five seconds before they built a golden calf, and we just have different idols.
    • He prays that they would go kill those idols, ask the people around them what they hear coming out of their mouths, and let God expose their darkness so it turns into light. He thanks God that when they built idols and said they didn’t need him, and then turned and said they were wrong, he was right there to make them his children, because that is why he sent his Son.
