Overview

  1. 00:17 One of the best-known stories in Scripture

    • Today the church encounters easily one of the best-known stories in Scripture. Jesus is the most popular figure in the Bible, but Samson has to be in the conversation for the top five; even people who have never opened a Bible have some idea who he was. Asked for the first word that comes to mind, many say Delilah.
    • Josh thinks of Arnold, not the grumpy politician but the chiseled Mr. Universe. Scripture doesn’t indicate Samson was shredded, and that isn’t important. Samson wasn’t meant to picture peak male perfection, but God’s strength working through man by the power of his Holy Spirit; for all we know he looked like Captain America before the experiment.
    • Samson is the last judge mentioned by name in Judges, with three chapters, one devoted entirely to his birth story. Unusually for Josh, they will cover a ton of content: a ton of dysfunction, and then at the end something beautiful. He asks the church to hang in there.

  2. 02:00 A divine demonstration of how God saves

    • Samson’s story is bigger than Judges; it reaches across all of God’s word as a divine demonstration of how God saves his people.
    • Israel isn’t just having a bad week. The cycle of disobedience, discipline, distress, and deliverance is inevitable: there is peace while a judge lives, then he dies, Israel forgets Yahweh, and it begins again. They were slaves to sin in desperate need of a Savior, and the symbolism in Samson’s story is remarkable.

  3. 02:45 Forty years under the Philistines

    Scripture: Judges 13:1

    • Josh starts in Judges 13:1: again Israel did evil in the eyes of the Lord, so he delivered them to the Philistines for 40 years. He loves Judges because of what that phrase reminds us: the story never ends with our disobedience, though it seems like it should. Because of his great love, it always ends with his faithfulness, and that is why we praise him.
    • Forty years is the longest oppression yet; the number usually indicates finality and judgment. The Philistines were the world’s superpower, superior in military, infrastructure, and architecture, while Israel was still figuring out how to pitch a tent in a cave. They took what they wanted, castrated and impaled the men, kept the women and children, got drunk on beer and fat on bacon.
    • The text never says Israel cried out to God during those 40 years. Whether they were content or afraid to make things worse, it is frightening and humbling how easily God’s people accept the way things are instead of calling on him.

  4. 04:42 Manoah of Zorah, a border town

    Scripture: Judges 13:2

    • Verse 2 introduces Manoah of Zorah, a Danite whose wife was childless. Zorah is like Florabama: one step you are in Israel, one step you are in Philistia. As we’ll see, Samson hopped back and forth, worshiping with God’s people one day and partying with the Philistines the next. His lifestyle bounced between both worlds.

  5. 05:30 “You are barren and childless, but…”

    Scripture: Judges 13:3

    • In verse 3 the angel of the Lord says, “You are barren and childless, but you are going to become pregnant and give birth to a son.” Only God can have sentences like that. It is the first time in Judges a judge is promised before birth; Ehud, Deborah, Othniel, and Gideon were all alive. God refuses the assist from man and saves in the face of impossibility.
    • They didn’t cry out, had nothing to offer, and had not just given up but assimilated. Their future was hopeless, but God wasn’t done. His salvation doesn’t come from tears of desperation; you can’t earn, contribute to, or buy it. God creates salvation out of nothing, so you are never too far gone, whether you wandered four years or 40.
    • It isn’t 90% done for you to add the finishing touches; it is finished. And if he saved Josh without any help of his own, he will keep him too, because if it were up to Josh to keep his salvation, there is no doubt he would lose it.

  6. 07:18 A Nazirite from the womb

    Scripture: Judges 13:4–5 Numbers 6

    • The angel tells her to drink no wine or fermented drink, eat nothing unclean, and never let a razor touch the boy’s head, because he will be a Nazirite dedicated to God from the womb and will begin to deliver Israel from the Philistines.
    • Numbers 6 describes the Nazirite vow: set apart, consecrated, and devoted to God, no cutting hair, nothing from the vine (not even Welch’s grape juice, so warm lumpy milk or water), and touching nothing dead. People normally took it for a period of time, like a fast. This one is unique because his mother kept it during pregnancy and Samson from birth; by age 12 Josh imagines serious male Rapunzel vibes.
    • The picture behind the vow is of a greater Samson who would one day be set apart, holy, and sinless.

    1. 08:53 He will begin to deliver

      Scripture: Judges 13:5 1 Samuel 17

      • Josh loves that the angel says Samson will begin to deliver Israel. A story’s end usually discusses a conclusion, yet Judges wraps up about to witness a beginning. For the conclusion, flip to 1 Samuel 17, where David, another type of Christ, finishes off Goliath, a type of the serpent whose scale armor would have looked serpentine. David crushed him, again pointing to Christ, who would come and finish the ultimate job.

  7. 09:43 Manoah wants specifics

    Scripture: Judges 13:8

    • Manoah prays for the man of God to come again and teach them how to bring up the boy. Skipping the detail, Josh notes Manoah wants specifics beyond his wife’s report: what will the boy do, what will his life look like? He wants to know the future.
    • We are not unlike Manoah; we want the details and how things will shake out. But God just wants obedience.

  8. 10:27 “We are doomed to die”

    Scripture: Judges 13:20–23 Psalm 2:11

    • Manoah offers the angel a meal, but the angel only accepts an offering, then ascends in the flame from the altar while they fall on their faces. Manoah says, “We are doomed to die… We have seen God.” His wife answers that if the Lord meant to kill them, he wouldn’t have accepted their offering or shown and told them all these things.
    • Samson’s parents interact with the Lord oddly, but Josh asks, aren’t you glad we don’t inspect your home verse by verse? (“That’s our next series, by the way.”)
    • Despite the odd couple, both display a timeless truth. Manoah was right in his fear of God, and the church needs more reverence, but all reverence and no comfort isn’t healthy, so his wife balances out the home: if Yahweh wanted us dead, we’d be dead. All fear and no joy is a nasty recipe for a Christian, and all joy and no fear is just as gross. Psalm 2 gives the balance: serve the Lord with fear and celebrate his rule with trembling. We can smile and tremble at the same time.

  9. 12:09 Names matter

    Scripture: Judges 13:24–25 Matthew 1 Matthew 1:21

    • The woman gives birth, names him Samson, the Lord blesses him, and the Spirit of the Lord begins to stir him in Mahaneh Dan between Zorah and Eshtaol.
    • Today we don’t care about names (“you just named your son Joab because that was the trend”), but names mattered in ancient cultures. Matthew 1 shows naming describes our Savior’s mission from birth: the angel tells Joseph to give him the name Jesus because he will save his people from their sins. With every Hebrew name to choose from, God chooses Yeshua, Joshua. What did Joshua do? He took down Jericho. And the Lord will take down the city of darkness. His name defines his mission to save.

  10. 13:34 Samson, named for the sun

    Scripture: Malachi 4:2 Revelation 21:23

    • Who was Samson named after? The little-g god Shamash; his name compared his strength to the power of the sun. Ancient rabbis used it as a cautionary tale: the same sun that gives life and healing can scorch and burn. Samson’s God-given strength was a blessing to God’s people, but misused it could be destructive.
    • The sun imagery recalls the last prophet, Malachi: “But for you who revere my name, the sun of righteousness will rise with healing in its rays.” Samson is a type of Christ, a Nazirite savior who fought Israel’s enemies, but like the saviors before him he would fade and die: a temporary savior and a possibly dangerous light.
    • That takes Josh to Revelation 21 and the unceasing light of the New Jerusalem, which doesn’t need the sun or moon, for the glory of God gives it light and the Lamb is its lamp. See how much encouragement God’s people can gain from a name. This isn’t just a library of 66 books; it is the living, breathing word of God.

  11. 15:22 Four problems: compromise, impulse, entitlement, pride

    Scripture: Judges 14:1

    • Four main problems cause Samson’s light to burn instead of heal: compromise, impulse, entitlement, and pride. Today’s activity: see if you can spot them all.
    • Judges 14:1: Samson went down to Timnah and saw a young Philistine woman. Josh asks for hands from everyone who has argued in the kitchen with their kids about dating decisions. Parenting a kid with a bad picker is like watching a horror movie for the second time: you already know the villain, but everyone tells you to stay quiet and eat your popcorn. Most kids date like we drive, fast and reckless; we lose sleep because we want what’s best for them, but God’s plan is better than ours. “Lord, help me.”

  12. 16:40 “Get her for me”

    Scripture: Judges 14:2–3

    • The next line may sound like a recent kitchen conversation: “I have seen a Philistine woman in Timnah; now get her for me as my wife.” His parents ask whether there isn’t an acceptable woman among their own people. Samson says, “Get her for me. She’s the right one for me.”
    • Josh thinks the parents hit a home run here; the text never indicates they failed to uphold their vow. Despite being raised to honor the Lord, Samson ignores them and decides to marry a Philistine, a clear violation of God’s law. Living by sight, not by faith, he let lust lead him away from the law; he didn’t care about pleasing the Lord or his parents, only himself.
    • If believers are warning you about the path you are on, take notes on how things go for Samson. And don’t forget, God will accomplish his plans with or without us, the easy way or the hard way.

  13. 18:16 Verse 4: God seeking an occasion

    Scripture: Judges 14:4

    • Verse 4 is hugely important: his parents didn’t know this was from the Lord, who was seeking an occasion to confront the Philistines, who were ruling Israel. Samson should have been going to war to deliver Israel, but he was making his way to a wedding.
    • This doesn’t mean God supported the decision to intermarry or underwrote Samson’s sin; it means God uses his decisions to accomplish his will anyway. Just because Scripture describes something doesn’t mean God supports it or prescribes it for your life. God’s will is that you marry a believer. Period.

  14. 19:01 A shortcut through the vineyard

    Scripture: Judges 14:5

    • In verse 5, as Samson and his parents approach the vineyards of Timnah, a young lion comes roaring toward him. No serious Nazirite takes a shortcut through a vineyard; it is like a Christian stopping by a strip club on the way home. The goal isn’t to see how close you can get to sin without sinning; it is to get as far away as you can. Run.
    • Did God send the lion as a warning, the second warning that Samson was going down the wrong path? Josh thinks so.

  15. 19:51 He tore the lion apart, then liked her

    Scripture: Judges 14:6

    • The Spirit of the Lord comes on him in power and he tears the lion apart barehanded as he might a young goat (apparently tearing young goats was normal; “don’t act like you don’t have weird family traditions”). He tells his parents nothing, then goes and talks with the woman, and he likes her.
    • That is how impulsive Samson is: he plans the marriage, then gets to know the girl. Josh tells the young guys she won’t be hot forever. Get a girl who loves Jesus; stop picturing her in bed and start picturing her at 70. Stop pleasing yourself and please the Lord: find a Christian, make babies, raise up more Christians.

    1. 21:09 The lion in the vineyard

      • There will be times you wander into a vineyard of temptation after committing to the Lord in baptism to deny yourself, take up your cross, and follow him, and you find yourself toeing the line between obedience and complete disaster. Josh has been there more than he cares to admit, and it feels overwhelming.
      • Our power isn’t in our upbringing, our strength, or what our parents taught us, but in the Spirit of God, through whom we can do all things. Walk away; say no; you have it within you. You may be face to face with a vicious lion looking to devour you, but the lion in the vineyard is no match for the lion God has raised up in you.

  16. 22:19 Honey from the carcass

    Scripture: Judges 14:8–9

    • Going back to marry her, Samson turns aside to look at the lion’s carcass. Why visit it again? It drives Josh crazy, but he promises it gets better at the end. Samson scoops honey from a swarm of bees in it, eats as he goes, and gives some to his parents without telling them where it came from.
    • If you think Samson was chilling in vineyards without partaking, Josh thinks he was getting down on some wine; if so, he has now violated two-thirds of the Nazirite vow. His sin began with the lust of the flesh, “I want this,” then the lust of the eyes, “I want that,” and now the pride of life.
    • Breaking your vow and feeling bad is something we can all work with. It is another thing to drag down others, especially those who gave you your foundation, and then turn your disobedience into a joke. Samson didn’t take his broken vow seriously; he turned it into a riddle to entertain his wedding guests.

  17. 23:51 The riddle and Ashkelon

    Scripture: Judges 14:18 Judges 14:19–20

    • He gives the guests seven days to solve it for 30 sets of clothes. On the fourth day they threaten to kill his bride unless she gets the answer, and she turns manipulation up high: “You don’t love me. This marriage is full of secrets.” (Josh adds, “I’m calling your mother.”) Samson caves, and when they solve it he says, “If you had not plowed my heifer, you would not have solved my riddle.” Josh doesn’t advise calling your wife a heifer.
    • With no self-control, on a terrible path despite everyone including God warning him, Samson loses his temper. The Spirit of the Lord comes on him in power; he strikes down 30 men in Ashkelon, gives their clothes to those who explained the riddle, and returns home burning with anger, while his wife is given to his companion from the feast.

    1. 25:28 The Spirit doesn’t settle your vendetta

      Scripture: Judges 14:4

      • Josh pauses to prevent confusion: the Spirit of the Lord didn’t come on Samson to help him do something petty and vindictive. Remember verse 4: the Lord’s purpose was to move against the Philistines, and even Samson’s hot temper is useful to Yahweh. He killed 30 Philistines to pay the riddle debt.
      • Samson was so ticked he didn’t even consummate the marriage, and meanwhile his father-in-law gave his wife to his best man. As a pastor, Josh wonders how to approach marriage counseling here: dead on arrival.

  18. 26:20 “This time I have the right to get even”

    Scripture: Judges 15:1–2 Judges 15:3 Judges 15:11

    • At wheat harvest Samson takes a young goat to visit his wife; these days we apologize with flowers (“Fellas, if you try this and it works, shoot me a text”). Her father won’t let him in, says he thought Samson hated her and gave her away, and offers her more attractive younger sister. Everybody is so messed up.
    • Samson says this time he has the right to get even; he didn’t before, but that didn’t stop him. A passion for revenge is taking over his life, summed up in the motto of 15:11: “I merely did to them what they did to me.” An eye for an eye.
    • When Josh thinks of David, he thinks of a man who took up arms against the giant in the name of the Lord. Notice the difference between David, who fights in the name of the Lord, and Samson, who fights only for himself.

  19. 27:57 Three hundred foxes

    Scripture: Judges 15:4–5

    • Samson catches 300 foxes, probably jackals, since foxes are hard to catch and don’t run in packs, ties them tail to tail with torches, and burns the Philistines’ grain, vineyards, and olive groves. Josh says the only way the story gets better is with cats, and apologizes to the many cat people offended at first service.
    • Violence breeds violence: the Philistines retaliate by killing her and her father. Notice how the Philistines seem to secure a win but end up losing; they solved the riddle, but 30 of their own died to cover the bet. Josh invites the church to enjoy the humor of Yahweh’s salvation, which only gets better.

  20. 29:32 The jawbone at Lehi

    Scripture: Judges 15:7–8 Judges 15:11–13 Judges 15:14–15 Judges 15:16

    • Samson swears he won’t stop until he gets revenge (Hebrew for “you’re all going to die”), slaughters many, and stays in a cave in the rock of Etam. The Philistines demand Judah hand him over, and 3,000 men of Judah ask, “Don’t you realize the Philistines are rulers over us?” He answers, “I merely did to them what they did to me.” Judah doesn’t recognize the peace they have or that the enemy has no clue what to do with Samson, and Samson has power but uses it only for himself.
    • They bind him with two new ropes and lead him to Lehi, where the Spirit of the Lord comes on him; the ropes become like charred flax, and with a fresh donkey’s jawbone he strikes down a thousand men. Josh reads the King James: “With the jawbone of an ass, heaps upon heaps, with the jawbone of an ass have I slain a thousand men.” Another translation puts it, “With the jawbone of an ass, I have piled them in mass,” and then a mic drop with the donkey’s dentures.

  21. 31:34 Stop being sad for the Philistines

    • Josh pleads with the church to stop being sad for the Philistines; no one else is. Think how an Israelite brother would react after 40 years of oppression. Laugh a little. The whole Samson story is one big joke on the Philistines: every move they make looks successful, but because of this chaotic, sinful savior from Zorah it becomes a disaster. He is playing with them, mocking them.
    • Josh has no interest in presenting Samson as a cleaned-up Sunday school hero. He is a mess heading down the wrong path hard, but he is taking a ton of Philistines with him. It isn’t irreverent to find humor in the Bible. The Philistines become Israel’s laughingstock so we see the inevitable destruction of being an enemy of Yahweh. Every moment they look like winners, the Spirit falls on this wild man and reveals their stupidity, and Israel gets a front-row seat to God making footstools of his enemies.

  22. 33:28 Appearing to be a Nazirite

    Scripture: Judges 16:1

    • Unfortunately the story doesn’t end at Lehi. One day Samson goes to Gaza, sees a prostitute, and spends the night with her. Of course he does.
    • Josh breaks our temptation to look down on him: Samson is not that different from modern Christians. He now only cares about appearing to be a Nazirite rather than keeping the vow; the only part left is the hair. By outward appearance he seems upright while he lies with a prostitute. Is the church different? We made it to church, memorized our doctrine, and sound Christian, while blatantly sinning against the one we say we love.
    • Just a few verses earlier a thirsty Samson whined in the desert that he would die, and the Lord provided water. To Samson and many Christians, they love his water but not his commands; they drink of the good but don’t care about the requirements. Grace is not a license to sin.

  23. 35:13 Carrying off the gates of Gaza

    Scripture: Judges 16:2–3 Matthew 16:18

    • The people of Gaza surround the place and wait all night at the city gate to kill him at dawn. In the middle of the night Samson tears loose the doors of the city gate with the two posts, bar and all, and carries them to the top of the hill facing Hebron. To possess your enemy’s gate was checkmate: certain defeat.
    • Samson, the sinful savior of Israel, draws our attention to the words of our blessed Savior to Peter in Matthew 16: “I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.” Through his death and resurrection Jesus ripped off the gates of hell and carried them off in victory. Amazing how we can see Jesus in this subpar savior.

  24. 36:26 Delilah and cash for kryptonite

    Scripture: Judges 16:4–5 1 Timothy 6:10 Judges 16:6

    • Then Samson falls in love with Delilah in the Valley of Sorek; he has a bad picker, and his parents tried. The Philistine rulers each offer her 1,100 shekels of silver to learn the secret of his strength so they can tie him up and subdue him. The love of money is a root of all kinds of evil: cash for kryptonite.
    • Delilah asks, “Tell me the secret of your great strength and how you can be tied up and subdued.” Josh rereads it, “because we’re not all stupid,” and waves the relationship red flag; you don’t build on this foundation.

    1. 37:33 An exhaustive study on sexual temptation

      Scripture: Genesis 39:12

      • For the men of God, Josh offers a completely exhaustive biblical study on handling sexual temptation: Genesis 39:12, where Potiphar’s wife caught Joseph by his cloak and said, “Come to bed with me,” but he left his cloak in her hand and ran out of the house. Streaking isn’t a sin if you are running from sexual sin. Run, Samson, run like Joseph; this woman is bad news.
      • How embarrassing that an unmarried man fled sexual sin better than most married men today.

  25. 38:20 The razor, the Lord gone, and Dagon praised

    Scripture: Judges 16:16–17 Judges 16:21 Judges 16:23–24

    • Three times she tries to find his kryptonite, prodding him with such nagging day after day until he is tired to death, and he tells her everything. Delilah gets her silver, the razor hits his head, the consecrated man is no longer perfectly consecrated, and his strength is gone. He wakes to find the Lord has left him.
    • The Philistines seize him, gouge out his eyes, and imprison him in Gaza. Josh paints the picture: they praise Dagon, their god of grain, saying, “Our god has delivered our enemy into our hands,” likely thinking of the burned fields and believing their god got justice. What a humbling moment for Samson, to see that because of him Yahweh’s enemies thought Dagon had triumphed over Yahweh.

  26. 39:41 “Sovereign Lord, remember me”

    Scripture: Judges 16:25–30

    • They bring Samson out to entertain them. He asks the servant to put him where he can feel the pillars. The temple is crowded with all the rulers, and about 3,000 men and women watch from the roof.
    • Samson prays, “Sovereign Lord, remember me. Please, God, strengthen me just once more, and let me with one blow get revenge on the Philistines for my two eyes.” Bracing against the central pillars, he says, “Let me die with the Philistines,” pushes with all his might, and the temple comes down. He killed many more when he died than while he lived.

  27. 40:45 Talk about finishing well

    • Josh doesn’t want to turn Samson into a hero, but talk about finishing well and the incredible faith he displayed. Some will push back that his prayer was for personal vengeance, but he was never taught to love or pray for his enemies; to Samson destroying the enemy was the call of his life, and God’s mercies allowed him to finish like a hero.

  28. 41:23 It’s never too late to cry out to God

    • Josh laid all that out to say one thing: it is never too late to cry out to God. It can all stop right now. They took his eyes and tossed him in a dungeon, and bald, alone, and embarrassed, he grew closer to God than he had ever been and experienced the miracle of repentance. His hair started to grow back; he was returning to God.
    • Samson proves that in the kingdom of God, when you are weak, you are strong. You can’t take back your sins or the ways you hurt those you love; your past is written. But you can repent, take responsibility, and run into God’s grace. It is never too late for that.

    1. 42:40 Jesus was bound for our sin

      • Samson was in chains because he was prideful and arrogant, chased the lust of his flesh and eyes, and wanted vengeance. Unlike Samson, Jesus was not bound for his own sin, but for ours. That is good news, because many of us are like Samson: our past isn’t pretty, and we have lived to please ourselves with lust, power, sex, and money.
      • Jesus also tore something down when he died: the idea that God is behind a curtain, accessible only once a year. He replaced it with a new dwelling place, his people, and when he dwells in them they become strong where Samson was weak.
      • However far you have been from God, through a failed marriage, disobeying parents, broken promises, or sexual sin so deep you can’t see the light, don’t let shame, guilt, or pride keep you from the most amazing blessing there is: God answering a sinner’s prayer. Run to him now and borrow Samson’s plea: “Please, God, help me. I need you.” If he could turn Samson’s life around and use it for victory, he can turn your story around too.

  29. 44:48 Closing prayer: from the temple roof to Pentecost

    • Josh thanks the Lord, his Savior and treasure, saying that watching him work through time is the honor of his life and explaining it to his people is his greatest joy. When he sees Samson’s arms spread out, all he can see is Christ, God’s only begotten Son, strung out for his sin, and he is in awe of the way God tells his story.
    • He thinks of the 3,000 on the roof when Samson brought down that false house of worship, a temporary relief while the problem persisted. Then he fast-forwards to a locked room in Jerusalem where eleven men who had seen what the world wasn’t ready for were filled with courage when the Spirit came down like fire. The first time human ears heard that Jehovah took on flesh and died for the sins of man, 3,000 were baptized: not temporary relief, but forever relief. “We simply say thank you.”
